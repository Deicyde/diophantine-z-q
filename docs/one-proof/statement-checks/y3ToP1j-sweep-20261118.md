## Comparison, clause by clause

### 1. Polarized print versus unpolarized objects

The fixed algebra, order and involution agree with Baba–Granath p. 739. The missing polarization is not dismissed as a generic-only convention: Buzzard §1, p. 3, supplies existence and uniqueness of the principal polarization for the fixed Rosati involution, including CM points. An \(\mathcal O\)-equivariant isomorphism pulls the canonical polarization back to one with the same involution; uniqueness makes it polarization-preserving.

This recovers the fixed-type polarized moduli problem from the unpolarized pair problem. It does not assert that every possible principal polarization at a CM point has that type. The latter would be a stronger and unjustified reading of Lin–Yang's simple-only converse.

### 2. Level-three quotient and the orbit dictionary

Printed: Baba–Granath supplies \(V\), its four-element normalizer/Atkin–Lehner quotient, and \(V_W\simeq\mathbf P^1_{\mathbb Q}\). Milne supplies finite-level quotients and the rationality of the translation maps.

Manuscript derivation, `diophantine_zq.tex:425–436`: \(N\) normalizes \(K_3\), \(|N/(\mathbb A_f^\times K_0)|=4\), central finite ideles act trivially, and the entire level-three curve has the indicated quotient. The local argument is correct: at split primes the normalizer is the centre times maximal-order units; at the two division primes the quotient records the parity of the reduced-norm valuation. At 3, the ideal \(3\mathcal O_3\) is stable under conjugation, so its congruence kernel is normalized.

Further dictionary, written in the tree: `docs/research/y3-p1j.md:120–129,177–189` labels this step ON THE SPOT. Its content can be made explicit:

- The global elements \(i\), \(j+ij\), and \(\mu\) normalize \(\mathcal O\) and have reduced norms \(-2,-3,6\). Their local parity classes realize all four normalizer classes. Hence \(N=N_{B^\times}(\mathcal O)\mathbb A_f^\times K_0\).
- Skolem–Noether gives \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal O)=N_{B^\times}(\mathcal O)/\mathbb Q^\times\). Inner twists by \(\mathcal O^\times\) are trivial up to isomorphism of the underlying pair. A unit \(1+i\) has norm −1, so reducing from \(\mathfrak H^\pm\) to \(\mathfrak H\) does not leave an extra orientation factor.
- In the lattice uniformization \(A_z=\mathbb C^2/\phi(\mathcal O)v_z\), twisting the action by conjugation with a global normalizer element corresponds, by its linear map on the lattice, to the normalizer action on \(z\). This is the required translation between twists and Atkin–Lehner orbits, not merely equality of their generic orbit sizes.
- The markings of a fixed pair form a torsor under \((\mathcal O/3\mathcal O)^\times\). This is elementary for \(\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathcal O}(\mathcal O/3\mathcal O,A[3])\); `Structures.lean:371–385,510–514` proves its algebraic form from the torsion-count input. It concerns all residue-ring units, not an assumed surjection from global units.

Thus forgetting the marking and then taking the normalizer orbit is exactly the Lean condition “some \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal O)\)-twist of the underlying pair is isomorphic”. Larger stabilizers at CM points do not invalidate an orbit biconditional.

The construction is
\[
Y_3/(K_0/K_3)=V,\qquad V/W=V_W\simeq\mathbf P^1_j.
\]
The combined acting quotient is \(N/(\mathbb A_f^\times K_3)\). Milne's construction uses all components, and \(K_0/K_3\) includes both reduced-norm residues modulo 3; no extra determinant-component factor is omitted. The notation \(\operatorname{Sh}_N\) in the research note should be understood as this successive quotient, not as an application with a noncompact level.

The scheme maps are over \(\mathbb Q\), by the canonical-model translation statement and Baba–Granath's rational quotient. Their geometric-point interpretation is unchanged on passing between \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and \(\mathbb C\). The orbit dictionary is a further consequence step; it is not printed verbatim as the leaf.

### 3. CM biconditionals and the incorrect table locator

The biconditional is a consequence, rather than just a forward substitution of three named values:

- A maximal-order CM embedding of discriminant −4 or −3 yields the elliptic fixed points of orders 2 or 3 on \(V\); these are the pairs \(z_2,z'_2\) and \(z_3,z'_3\) described on p. 739.
- For discriminant −24, the image of \(\sqrt{-6}\) has norm 6. It is a unit at all split primes away from 6 and normalizes the unique local maximal orders at 2 and 3, so its Atkin–Lehner class is \(w_6\). Its fixed points are \(z_6,z'_6\).
- These pairs become one point each on \(V_W\). The normalized Hauptmodul is an isomorphism on that quotient. Every point above one of these values is therefore in the same normalizer orbit as the corresponding CM representative.
- Twisting the \(\mathcal O\)-action leaves its image, hence its commutant, unchanged; isomorphism and forgetting a marking also preserve the CM predicate. This proves the reverse implications for all geometric representatives, not merely generic points.

The use of maximal CM orders is important: the roots in the Lean definitions force these orders, not an unspecified order in the same quadratic field.

The numbers 4, 6, 2 are orbifold orders. They are not the three coarse ramification indices of \(V\to V_W\), which are 2. The leaf contains no assertion of those indices, so this distinction causes no wrong-index clause in `Y3ToP1j`. Interpreting them as indices of a torsion-free level cover requires the separate torsion-free-cover argument, not the table alone.

### 5. Degree arithmetic and distinct-point bound

`diophantine_zq.tex:437–440` prints
\[
\deg(Y_3\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1_j)
\leq4| (\mathcal O/3\mathcal O)^\times |\leq4\cdot81=324.
\]
Baba–Granath does not state this level-three bound.

It was independently checked in the stated order, without using the earlier computation as an oracle. For coefficients \((a,b,c,d)\) in the integral basis, the reduced norm modulo 3 is
\[
\operatorname{nrd}(a,b,c,d)
=(a+2c)^2+(b+2d)^2\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3.
\]
Both squares must vanish for the sum to vanish. There are 9 nonunits among 81 residue elements, and 72 units. Enumeration of all possible two-sided inverses using exact rational quaternion multiplication independently returned the same count; the norm distribution is 9 of norm 0 and 36 each of norms 1 and 2.

Consequently
\[
4\cdot72=288\leq324.
\]
There is no arithmetic error. The upper bound is deliberately loose. Each quotient fibre is an orbit, and a finite morphism of degree \(d\) has at most \(d\) distinct geometric points in any fibre. This supplies exactly the finite-set/cardinality clause used by Lean.

The scalar units modulo 3 have size 2. Together with the manuscript's normalizer index, the quotient group has order \(4\cdot72/2=144\). This confirms why the bound has room to spare, but an exact degree of 144 additionally requires a generic-stabilizer check. That exact-degree claim in the research notes was not needed and is not certified here merely from the group-order calculation.
