## The Lean, unfolded

`R16Thm1.BShStandardModel` (`R16Thm1.lean:133`) takes:

* `N ≥ 2`;
* every integral `X` over `Spec Q` with `IsCurve X` (`ZInQKernel/HDecomp/ModCurves/Defs.lean:149`) and
  `IsX1Gen N X jX tX` (`AfTateLambda.lean:438`);
* every rational point `P₀`, abelian variety `J` over `Q` (`ZInQKernel/HDecomp/Objects.lean:162`), and
  `aj : X → J` with `IsJacobian` (`Defs.lean:188`, the Albanese property).

It asserts that there exist `tp : Primes → End J` and `δ : (Z/N)ˣ → End J`, with `End` the `Q`-endomorphisms
(`Objects.lean:220`), satisfying:

* `IsHeckeT N jX tX aj p (tp p)` for every prime `p` (`AfTateLambda.lean:498`). On classes of the points `x_τ`:
  * if `p | N`: `T_p([x_τ] − [x_τ']) = Σ_{i<p} ([x_{(τ+i)/p}] − [x_{(τ'+i)/p}])`;
  * if `p ∤ N`: the same, plus `[x_{γ(pτ)}] − [x_{γ(pτ')}]` for every `γ ∈ SL₂(Z)` with `N | c` and `d ≡ p (mod N)`.
* `IsDiamond N jX tX aj d (δ d)` for every `d` (`:511`): `[x_τ] − [x_τ'] ↦ [x_{γτ}] − [x_{γτ'}]`, for
  `γ ∈ SL₂(Z)` with `N | c` and lower-right entry `≡ d`.

The model and its points:

* **The model.** `IsX1Gen` says `Q(X) = Q(jX, tX)`, and the integer relations of `(jX, tX)` are those of
  `(j(τ), f₁(τ))`.
  * `f₁(τ) = weber(⟨1, τ⟩, 1/N)` (`ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/WeberField.lean:781`). On the generic branch this
    is `(g₂g₃/Δ)·℘(1/N)` for the lattice `Z + Zτ` (`ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/RingClassLattice.lean:74`).
  * So `X` is Diamond–Shurman's `X₁(N)` over `Q`: the form of the moduli problem `(C/(Z+Zτ), 1/N)`, i.e.
    `Z/N ↪ E`.
* **The points.** `x_τ` is defined by `AtTau` (`AfTateLambda.lean:458`): the rational functions take the limit
  values of `P(j, f₁)/Q(j, f₁)` at `τ`.

The Lean statement asserts only that such operators exist over `Q`. It does not mention Shimura's model or his
`ξ_p`. Those enter only through the docstring.

## Comparison, by the row's own split (B1–B4)

**B2 (rationality over `Q`). Printed, with no citation in the sentence (Q2).**

* The flag's "the Hecke ring is rational" paraphrases the print, which says "the elements of 𝔗 ∩ End(J) are
  rational over **Q**".
* The page does cite [11, §7.3] for the standard model, two paragraphs earlier (Q1). Where the paper argues the
  rationality of a correspondence elsewhere, it uses [11, Prop. 7.2] (Q7).
* The sentence covers `ξ_p` for every prime `p`, including `p | N`: each `ξ_p` is an element of `End(J)` lying in `𝔗`.
* The sentence does **not** directly cover `{Γσ_dΓ}`. That needs `{Γσ_dΓ} ∈ 𝔗`, which takes two steps, neither in
  this paper:
  * the Hecke relation `ξ_p² = ξ_{p²} + p{Γσ_pΓ}` for `p ∤ N` (Shimura 1971 Ch. 3; Diamond–Shurman Prop. 5.2.4;
    numbers UNVERIFIED). Through (1.3) and the injectivity of `δ` on `End(J)⊗Q`, it reduces to the same identity on
    `S₂(Γ₁(N))`;
  * Dirichlet's theorem, for `{Γσ_dΓ} = {Γσ_pΓ}` with `p ≡ d (mod N)`.
* Alternatively, the rationality of the diamonds follows from [11, Prop. 7.2] directly. I did not read that.

**B3 (a `Q`-rational correspondence gives a `Q`-endomorphism of `J`). Implicit in the print.** The print treats the
`ξ_n` as elements of `End(J)` and asserts their rationality. Moving them to the kernel's `J` is routine: an
isomorphism `X ≅ V_S` over `Q` (B1) and the uniqueness of the Jacobian (`IsJacobian.unique`, `Defs.lean:205`).

**B1 (the kernel's `IsX1Gen` model is Shimura's standard model, compatibly with `τ ↦ x_τ`). Not printed in this
paper, but its locator improves.** The flag says that the model is "defined by citation of Shimura 1971". The print
also fixes the group (Q6): `S = Q^× U_N Γ` with `U_N = {x_p ≡ [* *; 0 1] (mod N)}`, the model being `V_S` of
[11, p. 155].

What is still needed is that `V_S`, with its uniformization, is `ℚ(j, f₁)` with `f₁ = weber(⟨1,τ⟩, 1/N)`. That
needs [11, Ch. 6]: the Fricke functions `f_a`, the action of `GL₂(Z/N)` on `F_N`, `k_S`, and `V_S` on p. 155. I did
not read [11].

The data are consistent with the identification under the convention I recall from [11], namely `f_a^u = f_{au}`
for row vectors `a`, with `diag(1,d)` acting on Fourier coefficients by `ζ_N ↦ ζ_N^d`:

* Under that convention, the image `{[s b; 0 1]}` of `U_N` fixes `f_{(0,1/N)}`, since `(0,1)·[s b; 0 1] = (0,1)`.
  So `k_S = Q(j, f_{(0,1/N)})`, and `f_{(0,1/N)}` is a rational multiple of the kernel's `f₁`. The identification
  would hold, compatibly with the points.
* The print's own computations agree with this convention (Q7). `σ_q^{−1}y ∈ U_M` for `y = diag(1, q)` at `p | M`,
  since `σ_q^{−1}y ≡ diag(q, 1)`. And `σ(y)` sends `ζ_M ↦ ζ_M^q`.

**A caution on the research note's sketch for B1.** `docs/research/r16-thm1b.md` §3 proposes: "both function fields
are the functions with rational q-expansion at ∞". This cannot work for this `Q`-form.

* The `q`-expansion of `f₁` at `∞` has coefficients in `Q(ζ_N)⁺`, and they are not all rational once `N = 5` or
  `N ≥ 7`.
* For example, `℘(1/N; Z+Zτ) = (2πi)²(1/12 + ζ/(1−ζ)² + O(q))` with `ζ/(1−ζ)² = −1/(4 sin²(π/N))`. Since
  `g₂g₃/Δ = −(2πi)^{−2} q^{−1}/2592 + …`, the leading coefficient of `f₁` is `−(1/12 − 1/(4 sin²(π/N)))/2592`.
* The `Q`-form whose functions have rational expansions at `∞` is the `μ_N` form. The identification should go
  through the generators instead (the fixed field of `U_N` is `Q(j, f_{(0,1/N)})`), or through the expansion at a
  cusp that is rational on this model.

**Whether the Lean is stronger than the print.** Modulo B1, it is not.

* The existence asked for is over `Q`. It covers every prime `p` (including `p | N`) and every `d ∈ (Z/N)^×`.
* It is asked for every `X` with `IsX1Gen`. Any two such `X` are `Q`-isomorphic compatibly with the `x_τ`, because
  the function field is pinned as a subfield of the meromorphic functions on `ℍ`.
* The Lean asks only for existence, not for uniqueness or for agreement with Shimura's `ξ_p`.
