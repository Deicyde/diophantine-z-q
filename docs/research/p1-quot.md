### 2.3 Glue C1 from Edixhoven's theorem (helper `QuotientP1/FixedDisjoint.lean`)

New leaf, over genuine definitions:

```lean
def EdixhovenFixedSmooth2 : Prop :=
  ∀ (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] (𝒴 : Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of (Zloc p)))), Smooth 𝒴.hom →
    ∀ (σ τ : Aut 𝒴.left) (hσ : σ.asIso.hom ≫ 𝒴.hom = 𝒴.hom) (hτ : τ.asIso.hom ≫ 𝒴.hom = 𝒴.hom),
      Finite (Subgroup.closure ({σ, τ} : Set (Aut 𝒴.left))) →
      ¬ p ∣ Nat.card (Subgroup.closure ({σ, τ} : Set (Aut 𝒴.left))) →
      Smooth (pullback.fst (equalizer.ι (Over.homMk σ.asIso.hom hσ : 𝒴 ⟶ 𝒴) (𝟙 𝒴)).left
          (equalizer.ι (Over.homMk τ.asIso.hom hτ : 𝒴 ⟶ 𝒴) (𝟙 𝒴)).left ≫
        (equalizer.ι (Over.homMk σ.asIso.hom hσ : 𝒴 ⟶ 𝒴) (𝟙 𝒴)).left ≫ 𝒴.hom)
```

The source is B. Edixhoven, *Néron models and tame ramification*, Compositio Math. 81 (1992), 291-306, Prop. 3.4
(locator UNVERIFIED: no network). It says: for a smooth `S`-scheme `X` and a finite group `G` of `S`-automorphisms of
order invertible on `S`, the fixed-point scheme `X^G` is smooth over `S`.

- Here `S = Spec ℤ_(p)` and `G = ⟨σ, τ⟩`.
- `X^G` represents `T ↦ X(T)^G`. That is the fibre product over `X` of the fixed schemes of the two generators,
  each the equalizer of the automorphism and the identity in schemes over `S`.

Compiled consequences (namespace `HDec.MCRQuot`):

How `disjoint_closure_of_two` is proved:

1. A common point `z` of the two closures lies in `X^{⟨σ,τ⟩}`. That scheme is smooth (Edixhoven), hence flat, over
   `ℤ_(p)`.
2. Flat maps are generalizing (Mathlib `Flat.generalizingMap`). So `z` has a generization `ζ` in `X^{⟨σ,τ⟩}` over the
   generic point of `Spec ℤ_(p)`.
3. In `X^σ` the stalk at `z` is a domain, so `ζ` and the point `x` of `y` have a common generization `ξ`. If
   `ξ ≠ x`, the closure of `ξ` (inside `X^σ`) would carry infinitely many `σ`-fixed `ℚ̄`-points. Hence `x ⤳ ζ`.
4. The same holds for the point `x'` of `y'` and `τ`, so `x' ⤳ ζ`.
5. `ζ` lies in the generic fibre, so `ζ = x` and `ζ = x'`, a contradiction.

In C1 the two automorphisms are the stabilizer elements of the two CM points. They lie in `fibreAut`, which is
finite of order prime to `p` (glue A). The old proof needed cyclic inertia (L6) to make two *involutions* agree and
then L5. The fixed scheme of the group they generate removes both, and also the need for involutions.

**`CMStab24` (not proved).** The commutant's units are `±1`, so the stabilizer must come from an Atkin–Lehner twist
`α = conj(w)` with `w` in the normalizer of `𝒪`, `nrd w = 6` (SPECULATIVE: e.g. a `w` with `w² = −6`). `T` fixes `[P]` iff some `ψ ∈ Aut(A)` has
`ψ ι(a) ψ⁻¹ = ι(w a w⁻¹)`. The candidate is `ψ = −ι(w)φ/6`, with `(ι(w)φ)² = 36`. It lies in `End(A)` iff
`ι(w)φ` kills `A[6]`. That holds, SPECULATIVE (not compiled), because:

- `φ(A[6])` and `ker ι(w)` are `𝒪`-submodules of `A[6] ≅ 𝒪/6𝒪` of order 36;
- such a submodule is unique, since `𝒪` is ramified at 2 and 3.

It needs degrees, kernels and factorization through `[6]` in the kernel's abelian-variety layer, which are not
there. In print this is the moduli form of "the Atkin–Lehner involution `w_m` fixes the CM points by orders
containing `√−m`" (Ogg 1983, *Real points on Shimura curves*; Rotger 2004; locators UNVERIFIED). The print states it
on `X₀^D(ℂ)`, not on the objects `(A, ι, η)`. So `CMStab24` is the one project glue left under the field.

(An earlier attempt used only the `𝒪/3𝒪` structure; `scratch/p1-quot/py/conj.py`, VERIFIED by computation: for the

## 5. Abelian-scheme form (R1fpPropsAb) and Mumford's rigidity

The brief asks whether the field's consumer can be stated with `R1fpPropsAb`, which would drop Mumford's rigidity
leaf from this proof.

**Result (VERIFIED, compiled): the rigidity leaf is dropped without restating the field.**

- In this proof the only route to rigidity was `HeckeIrreducible`. The chain proves it from
  `deligneComponentsY3PrintedAb` by first converting that leaf to its `R1fpProps` form with `mumfordRigidityCore`
  (`deligneComponentsY3Printed_iff_ab`, TailZ23).
- `QuotientP1Proof.DeligneAb.heckeIrreducible_of_ab : DeligneComponentsY3PrintedAb → HeckeIrreducible` avoids the
  conversion:
  - the Deligne witness `Y₀`, with its abelian-scheme clause list, is connected: the chain's orbit computation
    `y3Connected_of_deligne` copied with the clause list cut to the one clause it uses, a `ℚ̄`-point;
  - for any triple `(Y, Φ)` with `R1fpProps`, `R1univProps Y` applied to the abelian scheme of `Y₀` gives
    `g : Y₀ → Y` with `Φ₀ = Φ ∘ g` (`phi_part'`). An abelian scheme is a `QMFamily` by
    `r1famProps_of_r1famAbProps`, which needs no rigidity;
  - `g` is onto (`Φ₀` onto, `Φ` injective), so `Y` is connected, hence irreducible.
- So `quotientP1Coordinate_of_fields` assumes `deligneComponentsY3PrintedAb` but not `mumfordRigidityCore`. The
  universal property used is that of the *target* triple (`R1univProps`, on `QMFamily`s), which `R1fpProps` carries.

**Restating the field in abelian-scheme form** (findings by reading, SPECULATIVE where marked):

- Elsewhere the proof uses `R1fpProps Y Φ` in model-glue's `exists_genFib_to_Y` (TailL L1645). That lemma applies
  `R1univProps` to the base change of the family `𝒜 : QMFamily 𝒴.left` of `IsShimuraModel`.
- `R1univAbProps` classifies only abelian-scheme families. So a field stated with `R1fpPropsAb` would also need
  `IsShimuraModel` with a `QMAbScheme` family. That is the more faithful reading of the good-reduction field
  `shimuraGoodReductionNoGuard`, whose published form is a moduli scheme of abelian schemes.
- SPECULATIVE (not compiled): the model-glue lemmas of TailL that take `R1fpProps` would have to be restated.
- Rigidity is used elsewhere in the chain as well (`r1R2fpRep_iff_ab`, `shimuraFamily_iff_ab`). The same trick
