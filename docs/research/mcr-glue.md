**Do the hypotheses already give a coordinate on the model? No.**

- `IsShimuraModel` gives moduli clauses on field points and Hecke automorphisms of `𝒴` over
  `ℤ_(p)`.
- The triple's `π` lives on `Y` over `ℚ`.
- The rational function `π` on `𝒴` is pinned by `CoordFibres` (model-glue: Möbius rigidity). But
  it need not be a morphism on `𝒴`, and need not have transcendental reduction, unless the three
  CM sections stay disjoint mod `p`.

Elementary example (by hand; it is not the Shimura curve):

- Take `𝒴 = P¹_{ℤ_(p)}` in the coordinate `s`, with `G = {s ↦ ±s}`.
- The `G`-invariant function `π = p s²` has the `G`-orbits as its fibres on the generic fibre.
- It is not a morphism, since it is indeterminate at `s = ∞` mod `p`.
- Its reduction at the special generic point is `0`.
- The quotient coordinate is `s² = π/p`. With only two branch points nothing fixes the scaling.

So the content is the tame quotient `𝒴/G' ≅ P¹_{ℤ_(p)}` together with the disjointness of the CM
sections, as the outline in the target's docstring says.

**How `modelCoordinateResidue_of_quotient` works.** Put `ρ = ρ' ∘ q`. At `ξ`, `κ(ξ)` is not
algebraic over `𝔽_p` (`specialFibreTranscendence`). Suppose `q ξ` were outside the affine chart
`ρ'.U`. Then `g` vanishes at `ξ`, every section over `ρ'.V` evaluates into `𝔽_p`, and `κ(ξ)` is
algebraic, because `q` is integral and `κ(ξ)` consists of quotients of evaluations. That is a
contradiction. The same argument with `f` in place of `g` shows that the residue of `ρ` is
transcendental. Only the surjectivity of the chart maps is used.

**How `not_isAlgebraic_residueField_of_smooth` works.**

1. If `κ(ξ)` were algebraic over `𝔽_p`, the prime of `ξ` in an affine chart would be maximal.
   This is `ker_isMaximal_of_isAlgebraic`, through `Subalgebra.isField_of_algebraic`.
2. So `{ξ}` is locally closed.
3. Its point `ξ'` on the special fibre `𝒴 ×_{Spec R} Spec κ_R` is then closed. The special fibre is
   smooth of relative dimension 1 over a field, hence Jacobson, and `pullback.fst` is a closed
   immersion, hence injective.
4. The stalk at `ξ'` is a quotient of `𝒪_ξ` in which `p = 0`. Since `𝔪_ξ = (p)`, it is a field,
   of Krull dimension 0.
5. The kernel's `LeafQM.AV.Pin.smoothStalkDimOne` says the stalk at a closed point of a smooth
   curve over a field has dimension 1. Contradiction.

The DVR hypothesis of `SpecialFibreTranscendence` is not used.

**Truth review** (SPECULATIVE where not marked; the docstrings carry the arguments).

- L1–L4 are the textbook facts of the quotient construction.
- L5: a tame fixed scheme is smooth (Edixhoven). If it has finitely many `ℚ̄`-points, it has no
  component of relative dimension 1. So it is étale and proper over `ℤ_(p)`, hence a disjoint
  union of spectra of semilocal Dedekind rings, each with a one-point generic fibre.
- L6 was checked for every kind of `w`:
  - closed points of the special fibre: faithful action on the local ring of the special fibre
    (by the fixed-locus argument), then Serre's cotangent-character argument;
  - closed points of the generic fibre: the same argument in characteristic 0;
  - generic points: the group is trivial by faithfulness.
  - The hypothesis "nontrivial elements fix finitely many `ℚ̄`-points" is needed. Without it,
    `H₁ × H₂` acting on `𝒴₁ ⊔ 𝒴₂` is a counterexample.
