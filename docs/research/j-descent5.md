# Short coordinates and the double pole on the specified CM period

**Main result.** `cmPeriodShortCoordinatesDoublePole_of_genusOne` proves the
coordinate comparison from `AbVarFunctionFieldGenusOne`.
`cmjDegree_of_print_of_genusOne` then gives the live CM degree row from
the genus comparison and `CMjDegreePrint`, the already stated reading of
Fowler. There is no additional comparison or literature input. The genus
itself is outside the scope of this contribution.

## Sizing and proof

**VERIFIED (source).** The starting point is
[`abVar_identityPoleSpaces_of_genusOne`](../../ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/JDescent3FunctionField.lean#L190).
It identifies the rational place of the actual generic stalk and supplies
the positive pole-space dimensions and exact poles from genus one.
Tau Ceti supplies valuation and pole-space arithmetic. Its Weierstrass
function-field files start with an equation, so they cannot construct this
one. For regular sections, the vendor identifies the local rings at closed
points with valuation rings in the generic field, and Mathlib supplies
generic-stalk injectivity and sheaf gluing.

**VERIFIED (Lean).** In the sixth pole space, the functions
$1,x,y,x^2,xy,x^3$ have distinct orders and form a basis. Expanding $y^2$
in this basis gives a relation with nonzero $x^3$ and $y^2$ coefficients.
Rescaling and completing the square and cube retains the pole orders.
A repeated root of the short cubic lies in the ground field; dividing $y$
by $x-r$ would give a function with only a simple pole at the identity,
contradicting the one-dimensional first pole space. The resulting $x,y$
glue on the complement of the identity. Inversion preserves the identity
place, and its action on the second and third pole spaces fixes $x$.
The degrees of the subfields generated by $x$ and $y$ are two and three.
Thus they jointly generate the function field; since inversion acts
nontrivially on the period, it must send $y$ to $-y$.

**VERIFIED (source and Lean).** The regular-function clause in
[`HDecomp/LeafPush2/R22.lean`](../../ZInQKernel/HDecomp/LeafPush2/R22.lean#L50)
gives holomorphy and open evaluation domains for the very witness used by
`IsPeriodAt`. The stronger `IsUniformization.coords` chart derivative in
[`HDecomp/Analytic/Uniformization.lean`](../../ZInQKernel/HDecomp/Analytic/Uniformization.lean)
belongs to a different uniformization interface with a similar holomorphy clause.
Here the identity
$t^2x=u$ is realized on an algebraic neighborhood, where $t$ is a
uniformizer and $u$ a unit. The evaluation $f$ of $t$ is holomorphic,
vanishes at zero, and does not vanish identically there because the
evaluation of $u$ is nonzero at zero. Using the existing dual-number
calculation for multiplication at the identity, we prove
$[2]^*t=2t+t^2g$ in the identity stalk. Restricting to a smaller
neighborhood and evaluating gives
$f(2z)=2f(z)+f(z)^2 g_{\mathbb C}(z)$.
If $f$ first vanishes to order $n>0$, then $2^n=2$, so $n=1$.
[`hasDoublePoleAtZero_of_localParameter`](../../ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/JDescent2Analytic.lean#L25)
now gives the exact analytic double pole. The existing lattice-coordinate
comparison in `JDescent2.lean` and coefficient lemmas in `JDescent.lean`
supply the descent of the modular invariant and the row.
