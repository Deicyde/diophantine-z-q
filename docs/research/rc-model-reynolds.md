# Invariant algebras for the open integral model of Y₀(N)

The finite-group and degree-zero quotients commute with arbitrary scalar extension. In particular, if a finite group of invertible order preserves a grading on an algebra $S$, the degree-zero part of its invariant algebra can be formed before or after passing to the geometric special fibre. Over an algebraically closed field the finite quotient identifies orbits. A homogeneous unit of nonzero degree gives the same point-lifting and orbit statement for the grading. The degree-zero ring of the short Weierstrass chart is $R[j]$ when $6$ is a unit. These results supply the invariant-theoretic part of the stack-free model behind the specialization in (O.7).

The Lean files are under `ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/RC/ModelInvariants/`. The results work over an arbitrary commutative base $R$, subject to the indicated hypotheses. Lean uses its canonical scalar-first tensor algebra $R'\otimes_R S$. The action is on the $S$ factor, as in the scalar-last formulation via the tensor commutor. A point in the grading theorem is an $R$-algebra map whose evaluation on each homogeneous summand transforms with that summand's weight.

The local lemmas `fieldPoint_eq_of_ker_eq` and `fieldPoint_ker_smul` express that $k$-algebra maps to $k$ are determined by their kernels and that those kernels transform equivariantly. In the grading proof, `homogeneousUnit_inv_mem` and `homogeneousUnit_zpow_mem` give the degrees of the inverse and integer powers. `positive_homogeneousUnit` uses the inverse if the original degree is negative. `degreeZero_not_in_span` proves $S_0\cap(\delta-1)=0$ for a positive-degree unit $\delta$, using the lowest and highest degrees of a nonzero multiplier. `units_zpow_surjective` takes a nonzero integer root in $k^\times$. In the compatibility proof, `degreeZeroBaseChange_equivariant` checks equivariance on tensors and `fixedEquivOfEquivariant` restricts an equivariant algebra isomorphism to invariants.

In the chart proof, `LocalizedRing` is the full localization and `localizationInclusion` maps the homogeneous localization into it. `discriminant_expand` expands its two coefficients and `fraction_mul_denominator` clears the denominator of a homogeneous fraction. `isUnit_a6Coefficient` and `isUnit_jCoefficient` prove that $-432$ and $(-48)^3$ are units when $6$ is. `aeval_j_surjective` and `aeval_j_injective` prove the two halves of the polynomial evaluation isomorphism. `adjoinRoot_pow` gives the power equation in a root adjunction and `adjoinRoot_pow_algebraMap_injective` gives the injection of the base for these monic polynomials of positive degree.

## Provenance

The manuscript gives the required special-fibre geometry at 8465–8479 and uses it for (O.7) at 9101–9113. The stack-free construction is the chosen route to the model. The reductions below are **ON THE SPOT (c)**. All print locators in this report are **UNVERIFIED**. The module adds no printed-form leaf.

- Finite invariants and geometric orbit fibres are standard affine quotient facts. The Reynolds average acts identically on invariants, and the tensor of the inclusion and retraction is again split. On an invariant tensor the extended average acts identically, proving surjectivity of the comparison map. The prime transitivity step uses the pinned `Algebra.IsInvariant` theorem. Standard reference placements are Grothendieck et al., *Revêtements étales et groupe fondamental* (SGA 1, 1971), Exposé V §1, and Mumford, Fogarty and Kirwan, *Geometric Invariant Theory* (3rd ed., 1994), Ch. 1 §2.
- The degree-zero projection is the Reynolds operator for the multiplicative group. Its base-change proof tensors the grading decomposition. For geometric points, set a homogeneous unit of degree $m>0$ equal to one. The resulting algebra is integral over $S_0$ since the $m$th power of each homogeneous image comes from $S_0$. For orbit uniqueness, the points have the same zero values on homogeneous elements. In each degree detected by a nonzero value, the ratio of the two values is independent of the element. The detected degrees form a subgroup of `ℤ`, and the ratios form a character on it. Extract a root of its value on a generator to realize the character by one scaling. The reference placement for linear reductivity is Mumford, Fogarty and Kirwan, *Geometric Invariant Theory* (1994), Ch. 1 §2.
- The finite-generation proof is the Noether and Artin–Tate coefficient argument, with the last step implemented via the Noetherian submodule theorem on the invariant algebra. References are Atiyah and Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra* (1969), Exercise 7.5 and Proposition 7.8, respectively. The finite-group descent toolkit in the repository handles effectivity of groupoid descent and gluing. The affine proofs use the orbit-polynomial and prime-transitivity results in the pinned Mathlib.
- For the short Weierstrass chart, a monomial of weight $12n$ has exponents divisible by $3$ and $2$ respectively. This gives the two generators. The discriminant relation eliminates the second generator when $6$ is invertible. To see that $j$ satisfies no polynomial relation over $R$, extend $R[t]$ injectively by adjoining roots of two monic polynomials that realize $\Delta=1$ and $j=t$. Evaluating the chart in that algebra gives the injection. This is a direct calculation **ON THE SPOT (c)**. The short Weierstrass discriminant and $j$ formulas are standard, with reference placement Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves* (2nd ed., 2009), Ch. III §1.
