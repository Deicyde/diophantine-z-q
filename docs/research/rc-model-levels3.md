# Integral short-level quotient at the detector

For $N\ge2$ and prime $\ell_0\ge5$ with $\ell_0\nmid N\varphi(N)$, Lean constructs the smooth affine model `Pinned.Y0Level` over $R=\mathbb Z_{(\ell_0)}$. Its geometric points over every algebraically closed $R$-field are isomorphism classes of pairs $(E,C)$ with $C$ cyclic of order $N$. This includes $\overline{\mathbb F}_{\ell_0}$ for the supersingular point reading. The coordinate algebra commutes with arbitrary base change, is flat and finitely presented over $R$, and is finite over its actual $j$-line. Its field fibres are reduced and normal and have dimension at most one. The comparison with the fixed rational chart remains open.

Write $A=R[a_4,a_6,\Delta^{-1}]$, $S=S_N$ for the exact-order algebra over $A$, $H=(\mathbb Z/N)^{\times}$, and $B=(S^H)_0$. The detector exclusions at tex 8366–8368 permit the tame hypotheses for fixed $N$. The construction uses generators of a cyclic subgroup and the geometric $H$-action on them. The grading on the whole short locus has weights $4,6,2,3,-12$ on the coefficients, the marked affine coordinates and the inverse discriminant. The generator action preserves it, and the proper-divisor complement idempotents map to degree-zero elements on the exact-order cover.

**ON THE SPOT (c).** These reductions use the short change of variables and the pinned exact-order readings. At a field point choose a transcendental scaling unit in the fraction field of the Laurent polynomial ring. A relation remains zero at the scaled point, so its distinct Laurent coefficients vanish, then reducedness of $S$ detects their vanishing in $S$. The same argument tests whether an element is homogeneous by testing its scaling at field points. Multiplication by an integer commutes with each scaling isomorphism, which is the test for preservation of the grading by the actual $H$-action. On the exact-order localization each proper-divisor complement idempotent maps to one. The separate weight-zero assertion on the full affine torsion chart would use the same field test there. Multiplication by the divisor level is compatible with scaling, so field points detect constancy of each complement idempotent under scaling.

**ON THE SPOT (c).** In the discriminant-one slice the relations for $j$ and the discriminant reduce the fourth coefficient's cube and the sixth coefficient's square to polynomials in $j$ with unit denominators. Thus the six combinations of exponents $0\le a<3$ and $0\le b<2$ suffice for weighted monomial reduction. The finite exact-order cover stays finite on the slice. An element of weight zero in the ideal $(\Delta-1)$ is zero, by the two extreme weights of a putative multiplier of $\Delta-1$. The pinned degree-zero subalgebra therefore embeds as an $R[j]$-submodule of the finite exact-order slice. The Hilbert basis theorem, Atiyah and Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra*, 1969, Theorem 7.5 UNVERIFIED, supplies Noetherianity and the implication from finite type to finite presentation.

**ON THE SPOT (c).** The normality descent uses the composite of the two Reynolds projections, linear over the quotient. The localized retraction descends the domain property by annihilators and integral closure by divisibility. This uses the normality of regular local rings through the unique factorization theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum, *Unique factorization in regular local rings*, 1959, main theorem UNVERIFIED. The passage from normality in dimension at most one uses Atiyah and Macdonald, 1969, Proposition 9.2 and Theorem 9.3 UNVERIFIED.
