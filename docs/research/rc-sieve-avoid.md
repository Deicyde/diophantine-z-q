# Appendix F: keep the coefficient support out of the tests

**The old-level, inertia, and Hecke interfaces now use the manuscript domain with the type unchanged.** No Appendix F Hecke reading requires a sieve prime: tex 9670–9674 excludes all of them, and tex 9919–9926 chooses fresh representatives. **Scoped stop:** the separate conclusion that the reduced trace is controlled by the retained type still needs a proof at extra sieve primes that the type does not detect. Tex 9745–9750 allows those primes to ramify. The formal interfaces must not use the manuscript's full stage type and a coarser prepared type interchangeably. Away from the retained support and the active primes, the existing type and bit characters already kill inertia. Thus the unchanged type quotient would force `SieveInertia` there; conversely, the new Kummer proof derives the quotient comparison from that local condition and the restricted formwise inertia. This locates the missing step on the actual reduced trace orbit, without asserting that any extra inertia survives for that orbit.

**Order of choices.** Tex 2016–2025 defines the full type after fixing the support of the coefficient source, explicitly including the current reduction prime during an odd stage. Tex 9447–9468 and 9675–9680 require the finite budget before the actual stage; tex 10579–10585 fixes the abstract data before the stage and chooses prime representatives for its full type afterwards. The prepared fixed data and fixed part of the filter stay fixed. The full odd-stage data need not be independent of the stage: `LabelData.T₁ θ` already has modulus `m₁ * ℓ₀ θ`. Re-reading *only* to exclude primes leaves the retained labels and symbol on surviving tests unchanged; adding squareclass coordinates refines the types, and changing the coefficient sieves or varying the moving condition can change which indices are tested. Merely adding to the stationary label support does not resieve the intrinsic source (tex 2026–2029, 9481–9483, 10589–10596). There is no license to reinterpret arbitrary previously chosen prime representatives at the new full modulus.

## Meaning of the premises

- `GoodPrime` and `GoodSet` just specify a test domain (tex 9471–9483, 9670–9674). They specify no values or operators. `HeckeAt` is a single-prime full Fourier identity. `Hexcl'` is strictly weaker than `FormwiseHecke'`: it says nothing at permitted ambient primes (tex 9660–9674). `FormwiseHecke'` supplies only trace/Fourier comparisons to the reading theorems; it contains no valuation, omission, or dictionary identities.
- `BlockLevels'` and `LevelSetup'` express divisibility and nonintersection of finite sets (tex 9497–9555, 9754–9760). They contain neither a constituent expansion nor an attachment and are insufficient for the inertia or Hecke conclusions. `AtLevel'` concerns individual sources at permitted coprime occurrence levels (tex 9647–9654, 9762–9797). It lacks the sum over the active coordinates, the stable lattice, and the coefficient-one reading. `InertiaOn'` concerns individual coordinate inertia only away from `Scoeff ∪ U` (tex 9745–9750); it contains no labels or trace identities at Frobenius.
- `SieveInertia`, and its specialization `WindowSieveInertia`, concern only inertia at the extra sieve primes for the indicated reduced trace stabilizer. This is weaker by itself than determining the elementary trace quotient on every Galois element: it says nothing about good-prime inertia away from the coefficient support, the support classification, or the label characters. It is weaker than requiring characteristic-zero inertia there to act trivially on each constituent. It is the extra condition needed to use the coarser type in the step at tex 9745–9750, not an assertion made by those lines. Once the other ramification and character hypotheses are supplied, it is enough by the existing odd-radical ramification criterion. The common-space builder retains the original model type, whose quotient field ranges over every `T.Good` root in budget; its premise is indexed over those roots, using the window’s zero-extended forms. The actual reading ports only use allowed roots.
- `BlockModularity'` asserts the separate complex expansions (tex 9647–9648), before a common inventory or Galois representations are chosen. `OldStageLaw'`, `MovingSieveLaw'`, and `UnaryStageLaw'` are the old premises at the same explicit levels with a weaker level-setup record. They concern pre-product weight 3/2 expansions of one source or its transforms, with characters; they give neither the weight-two expansions nor their constituent realizations without further input (tex 8421–8428, 8938–8988, 9497–9555, 9618–9625).
- `StageValuation'` specifies the support and valuation meaning of `R`, and the congruence with the source coefficient on allowed indices (tex 9675–9704). It supplies no trace or Fourier comparison. `ThetaData'` adds the exact source and pre-reduction data on the allowed domain (tex 9638–9704, 9905–9918), but contains neither the same-vector local comparison nor the resulting trace reading and omission identity. `LocalInput'` specifies the local blocks and their individual at-one operator comparison (tex 9799–9864); it cannot supply the readings without the actual coefficient identities and the common trace orbit.
- `Inputs'` is the intermediate pair of actual readings on the given model (tex 9670–9704, 9905–9926). `Inputs'` is a conclusion of the two source theorems, not a replacement assumption for either one. The final dictionary propositions `O12Reading'`, `FixedUFactorization'`, and `PrimaryReading'` are conclusions; none is assumed in its proof.

## Known issues

- The generic base interfaces conflate absence from the type modulus with absence from the actual coefficient support. A prime that is `T.Good` and divides the old level refutes the old `old_good` field. Putting it in the ambient avoidance set removes it from the good Hecke domain but could formerly send it to the excluded-prime branch.
- By `ThetaCoeff.no_good_mem_stationary`, under the existing type and good Frobenius assumptions, a `T.Good` prime cannot lie in a radical support detected by the retained character. In particular, applying the old `formwise_kummer` with `support := Scoeff` fails for a sieve prime that remains `T.Good`. Choosing fresh representatives does not remove a radical from an elementary quotient. There is no claim here that extra sieve inertia actually survives for a specific lift.
- No manuscript or printed-source error was established. In particular, the full type of tex 2016–2025 records squareclasses at all of its coefficient support; the restriction alone is not a substitute for reading those squareclasses if the formal type is coarser. No page or theorem locator is supplied from memory. The analytic and source risks recorded in the prior old-level report are unchanged.
