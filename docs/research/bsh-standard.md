# The standard-model Hecke bridge

**Scoped stop: the prime Hecke branch comparison is open.** One must
identify the whole family of *marked* cyclic quotients of the generic
elliptic curve with the triangular Weber branches, including the image
of the level point. The j-invariant alone does not identify the
required points of X₁(N). The fixed row
`R16Thm1.BShStandardModel` (definitionally the same as
`AfTateLam.HeckeJ1DS`) is not discharged. No new leaf or fold.

The bridge between Shimura's standard model and the kernel's model is in
neither the manuscript nor its citations. The recorded reading in
[r16-thm1b.md](r16-thm1b.md) confirms that Shimura's 1973 paper supplies
the rational Hecke ring on its own standard model, and defines the
curve by referring to his earlier book. The printed rationality on
that model does not compare its points and operators with the fixed
`IsX1Gen`, `AtTau`, `IsHeckeT`, and `IsDiamond` predicates.

## ON THE SPOT (provenance (c)): diamonds

For a matrix with bottom row `(c, d)`, multiplication by `cτ+d`
takes the lattice at `gτ` onto the lattice at `τ`. The image of `1/N`
is `d/N + (c/N)τ`, and replacing `d` by any lift of the same residue
adds another period. The first coordinate is unchanged. On the
generic Weber branch, represent the point on
`y² = x³ − Gx/4 − G/4`, where `G = g₂³/g₃²` and
`x = 46656 f₁/(j−1728)`. Multiplying by the lift `d` replaces `x`
by the ratio of the division polynomials `Φ_d(x)/ΨSq_d(x)`. The
denominator cannot vanish there: the translated `1/N` is outside
the lattice, by homothety and `N ≥ 2`.

Clearing denominators in the universal polynomials puts the new
Weber coordinate in the field generated by the two given functions
over ℚ. The equality holds in the punctured germs at every parameter
since the generic Weber tests hold eventually. Localizing the
polynomial evaluation yields a map on the given function field, with
the correct finite values after pulling back by any representative
of the residue. By properness and separatedness this map extends
and specializes to the specified points. The Albanese property
applies after subtracting the value at the rational base point;
that translation cancels in every difference.

## ON THE SPOT (provenance (c)): remaining prime calculation

The smallest arithmetic input is a comparison, as multisets with
multiplicities, in punctured meromorphic germs at each parameter.
With `K = ℚ(X)` and the given embedding into those germs, take
an elliptic curve with generic `j = j_X` and a marked point up to
sign having Weber coordinate `t_X`. Over an algebraic closure of
`K`, use each subgroup `C` of order `p` whose intersection with
the subgroup generated by the mark is trivial. The quotient
`E/C` and the image of the mark have a pair of intrinsic
coordinates `(j_C, w_C)`. After embedding into an algebraic
closure of the meromorphic germ field, those pairs must agree
with `(j, f₁)` pulled back along `(τ+i)/p` for `0 ≤ i < p`, and
`g(pτ)` when `p ∤ N`, with `g` as in `IsHeckeT`. This statement
concerns both coordinates in the same branches.

The lattice description predicts this comparison: the index `p`
superlattices are obtained by adjoining `(τ+i)/p` or `1/p`. The
kernel of the latter kills the order-`p` part of the mark exactly
when `p ∣ N`. When `p ∤ N`, scaling that lattice by `p` gives
the lattice of `pτ` and changes the mark to `p/N`; this is the
good-prime diamond correction. What is missing in Lean is the
compatibility of the algebraic quotient *and marked-point map*
with the analytic superlattice map. The vendored
`ModularPolynomialData.eval_jLattice_eq_zero_of_isAddCyclic` and
`ModularPolynomialData.fibrePoly_j_eq_prod_veluQuotient_j` control the
target j-invariants. The vendored
`exists_veluPointHom_oddOrderSummingSet_of_isAlgClosed` gives the
algebraic odd-prime map; the order-two formulas are separate.
Neither result tracks the analytic image of the mark. The
`ShimuraOrbits` counts permute analytic orbit terms under modular
matrices; they do not give a Galois permutation over `K`.

A route to the comparison is the finite coset sum for the
Weierstrass function on a superlattice. Compare its Laurent
coefficients to identify the Vélu coefficients, and its value at
the marked point to identify the quotient coordinate; include the
order-two kernel and the normalization of the good-prime branch.
Even with the existing formulas, this is several linked
algebraic and analytic lemmas (several hundred lines), beyond
identifying a single extra polynomial relation.

Once the comparison exists, the family of pairs is finite and
Galois stable: the marking is fixed up to sign and the test
`C ∩ ⟨P⟩ = 0` is invariant. Take a normal closure if
needed, sum the Abel–Jacobi images of the generic points, and
descend the invariant sum over `K`. The finite Galois descent
lemma does this through the stalk evaluation, whose image lies
in the fixed field. The proper-target extension lemma then
gives a morphism from `X`. For specialization of this sum,
extend the finite-germ valuation and compare the generic lifts
using separatedness; the specializations of the branches must
be the specified `AtTau` points. The pointwise sum then follows
because the group law is a morphism. No Picard functor or symmetric
power is required. The specialization of the prime trace
still needs to be proved from the marked quotient comparison.
