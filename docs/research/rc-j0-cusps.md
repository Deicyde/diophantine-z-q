# Based cusp classes on the modular curve

**Scoped stop.** The comparison on the support of the quadratic sum needs only the source chart at cusp `0`. Lean proves that all the weighted fractions represent that same cusp and that the analytic limits also agree on its orbit. It gives the based Jacobian and its class map from the curve and point data, proves the Albanese factorization on schemes and on field-valued points, and transports the limit at `0` through the rational morphism. The concrete cusp on `X0Branch.model N` and its source limit, the group-scheme realization of the given Weierstrass target with its compatible uniformization, and the identification with the manuscript's independently chosen quotient remain open. The at-scale construction still uses rationality of the scale.

## Source, classes and the limit

Use `E = Ecurve ℓ`, `N = newformLevel E`, `X = Over.mk (X0Branch.model N).overQ`, `m = d.natAbs`, and `T = ℂ ⧸ Lat.lattice.toAddSubgroup`. The underlying curve is already integral and proper, and the proof of relative dimension one in `RC/X0Branch/Overlap.lean` is private but uses the same normalization as `X0Model.Rational`. The rational section to use is `X0Model.cusp N`, the special point of `formalQArc N` in `RC/X0Model/Specialization.lean`. Its triangle is `X0Model.cusp_overQ`. A rational section of a connected scheme over a field gives geometric connectedness by the tree's `geometricallyConnected_of_connectedSpace_of_section`. This is independent of cusp specialization under any map. With the relative dimension one proof, `pointedSourceCurve` and `basedJacobian CloudW07JacobiansCurves.JacobianExists_proof X b` are the adapter terms for the actual source; the module spelling out this application on the concrete model was not built in this run. The checked adapter takes the existing theorem `JacobianExists`, not an additional Jacobian postulate. The tree implements it with `Curve.abelJacobiMap` on the represented degree-zero Picard functor. In particular `Curve.abelJacobiMap_classifies_pullback` identifies its value by restriction of the family `abelJacobiClass`, the inverse diagonal ideal tensored with the constant-section ideal.

The points represented by `0` and `∞` on the classical modular curve are rational over ℚ. A rational number representing a cusp does not imply that the corresponding point is defined over ℚ: the other classes in the Elkik API should be taken over `AlgebraicClosure ℚ`, then transported by a fixed embedding into ℂ for the comparison. The usual finer residue field for a reduced fraction with denominator `v` is the cyclotomic field of order `gcd(g, N/g)`, where `g = gcd(v,N)` (UNVERIFIED in this run; no field-of-definition theorem for those points is used). The weights are `jacobiSym (a : ℤ) m`. A nonzero weight forces `a.Coprime m`, and the existing `GenusPartner` input implies `m.Coprime N` by `coprime_of_squareAt`, restricting the primes dividing `2*N` to those dividing `N`. Choose integers `u,v` with `u*(a*N) + v*m = 1`; the matrix with rows `(v,a)` and `(-u*N,m)` sends `0` to `a/m`. So all the weighted classes are the same class `[c(0)] − [c(∞)]` once the geometric cusp assignment is in place. This does not reduce the all-cusp domain of the existing printed torsion proposition or its finiteness reduction.

The exact source limit used by the new comparison is below. Write `x τ = Over.homMk ((X0Branch.model N).complexPoint τ).val ((X0Branch.model N).complexPoint τ).property` and write `c₀ℂ` for the complex extension of the **rational cusp at zero**, as a point over ℚ. The limit at infinity uses the same statement with `τ` in place of `ModularGroup.S • τ` and the complex extension of `X0Model.cusp N` in place of `c₀ℂ`.

```lean
Tendsto (fun τ : UpperHalfPlane => complexLift (x (ModularGroup.S • τ)))
  UpperHalfPlane.atImInfty (𝓝 (complexLift c₀ℂ))
```

This is an input about the source scheme alone; it mentions neither `E` nor `f`, a Jacobian map, or a period. It is sufficient together with the interior comparison from `ParamJoin` and the compatible target uniformization. A route to the source point at zero is to exchange the two j-functions on the normalization using the rational Fricke involution and transport the formal q-arc at infinity. At `S • τ`, the functions `j` and `j_N` are `j(τ)` and `j(τ/N)`, so this is the width-`N` branch. The comparison must identify the branch on the normalization: the pair of values `(∞,∞)` in the plane model does not identify it. Proving the limit from convergent evaluations of regular functions on a neighborhood of that branch is sufficient; a full compactified uniformization theorem is unnecessary here. At infinity one can alternatively prove that the projective at-scale map is based by specializing its Laurent coordinates along `formalQArc N`, then using the identity point of the group-scheme target. The inherited `projective_identity_iff_lattice` compares interior points only.

## Exact remaining inputs

No new structure or hypothesis proposition is defined by this patch. The new theorems use existing data and predicates. `IsJacobian` is the universal property of the source and does not pick a modular quotient, target lattice or cusp limit. A based morphism is required to send just one rational point to the group identity, which says nothing about its other cusp values. The point assignment `c` is data on the source scheme. The target evaluation assumptions concern only the chosen uniformization. A source `Tendsto` assumption contains no image under a morphism; conversely `hAJ`, a collection of target equalities, does not imply that source convergence. Interior comparison, level invariance and the analytic limits are inherited from the parametrization lane and do not identify any algebraic cusp. Thus none of these inputs is a rephrasing of `hAJ`.

## The independent map and fixed-scale rationality

`abelJacobi_comp_isBased` proves that an independently specified group-scheme map gives a based rational map by composition with Abel–Jacobi. Given the manuscript's actual `q_f : J₀(N) → A_f` and `α : A_f → E` over ℚ, this construction uses no scale. The factorization in the other direction, starting with the at-scale map, does not construct either `q_f` or `α`, or establish that their composite is the at-scale factor. Milne's typed statement in this tree supplies the analytic input only. The required independent input is the specific quotient and isogeny of tex 1781–1787 (ELLIOT input, geometric realization not built), and on the fixed model the exact tail comparison is still
```lean
∃ (π : X.left ⟶ Proj E.toProjective.grading)
  (hQ : π ≫ projectiveOverQ E = X.hom) (T₀ : ℝ),
  ∀ τ : UpperHalfPlane, T₀ < τ.im →
    projectiveValue (((X0Branch.model N).complexPoint τ).comp π hQ).val = phiAn E f Lat κ τ
```
with `π` the composite just described. This is tex 1785–1787 and 1825–1828, and the missing identification of its differential on the given chart is substantial. The conditional result in `fc04c16c8` can extract rationality from that tail match. A limit at the endpoint, even at both endpoints, does not give the tail match, and applying the current at-scale theorem to obtain the map would reuse `hr`.
