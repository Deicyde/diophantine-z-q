# The dyadic Kato input to Appendix E

**Known source gap at the base.** The application in tex 1884–1892 and
7206–7241 cites Kato 2004, Theorem 13.4(1)(2), at 2. Issue #168 identifies
a gap in that proof route: the cited Perrin-Riou result assumes odd
coefficient prime, the Rubin route needs irreducibility over the cyclotomic
tower, and Kato 1999 §0.6(ii) asks for an element fixing ℚᵃᵇ. The argument
at tex 7228–7231 only supplies an element fixing the dyadic roots of unity.
This is a gap in the cited implication; it does not show that (K15) is
false. The frozen implication takes Theorem 5.3 as a hypothesis.

**Result: conditional reroute.** The new path from the Kato 1999 draft to
(K15) uses the enlarged index set and the evaluation ideal of the original
retained class. The Appendix E adapters give the existing output types
without the old dyadic bound. The exact 0.8 statement and the maps
comparing its full-open cohomology with the retained cohomology must be
checked before integration. The enlarged domain and descent follow the
Version 11 revision record for the dyadic reroute introduced in Version 10.

**Lean verification.** All eight new modules pass `lake env lean`. The
[audit](../../scratch/rc-kato-p2-reroute/axioms.txt) covers all 36 new
theorems and finds only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
It checks that (K15) has the old conclusion's exact type and that the
replacement entry points do not depend on that old declaration or on the
legacy bundles, even through theorem proofs. No new module imports
`ZInQKernel.OneProof.Main`.

## Extended family and comparison

For each constituent, use `d = |h|` (the full odd conductor) and
`D = d·2^a` (the primitive discriminant's absolute value). Keep one
symbol, one lattice comparison and the two (K5) smoothers for the family. The
constituent input uses the actual rationalized Tate representation twisted
by a quadratic character, with a separate identity equating that character
to the finite weight on the full conductor.
`TwistTransfer` describes the class formed by tensoring at level `D·m`
and corestricting. The arithmetic construction is needed only for positive
`m` prime to `c·d_s`; outside that domain the total Lean data carries no
arithmetic meaning. The old `KatoXi` range embeds in this larger
positive/smoother-coprime family, but generally does not consist of
multiples of `N_K = 2Nd`. Its intersection with those multiples is a
subfamily of the Kodai domain. Conversely, the Kodai domain can have new
odd base divisors that `KatoXi` excludes.

On `N_K ∣ m ∣ m'`, the support of `D` and the fixed support already lie
in `prime(m)`. Therefore the difference of the two supports of the
untwisted classes is exactly `prime(m') ∖ prime(m)`. The quadratic
semilinear transfer changes the coefficient of `X` to `−a_q χ(q)`;
the quadratic coefficient stays `q`. Thus the norm is evaluated at
`q⁻¹ σ_q⁻¹` for arithmetic Frobenius. At a repeated prime
there is no factor. If `q` is new it cannot divide `2Nd`; no bad-prime
polynomial is invoked. The finite (E.1) comparison can still be made
with the same untwisted norm on the two primitive levels.

For `n ≥ 1`, use the actual corestriction from `N_K·2^n` to `2^n`.
Transitivity reduces the comparison with `y_(2^n)` to the norm on the
untwisted classes from `D·N_K·2^n` to `D·2^n`. Every prime of the
upper level is already in the lower omitted support. This norm has
multiplier 1, so the §0.7 class is the image of the retained class
under restriction of the open. Equality uses jointly injective tower
projections. No degree scalar occurs, even when odd primes of `d`
divide `N`.

At an away-two height-one prime, a commuting restriction square and
injectivity of the localized degree-two open restriction give an
injection on the localization kernels. The localized degree-one
dual extension condition gives
`J_retained,𝔭 ⊆ J_full,𝔭`;
hence the latter quotient length is at most the former. This is the
direction required for (K15). The reverse inclusion comes from pulling
full-open evaluations back along restriction and is insufficient by
itself. An integral isomorphism of the degree-one modules is not used.

The two support conventions are substantive. At finite coefficients,
(K7) supplies the retained `j_*` class by vanishing of inertia
restrictions. It must map to the full-open class obtained by the same
tensor and trace after inverting `N_K`. The rational comparison uses
(K16) at the odd primes of `N_K`: its degree-one differential is
injective at the tested prime. Thus the predicted cohomology comparison
has unchanged rational H¹ and injectivity on H². Identifying the actual
comparison and controlling the integral torsion kernel remain GLUE.

## Known issues

- The base issue affects the justification of the p = 2 divisibility,
  not the statement of the frozen implication. The old cyclotomic
  fixed-space proof does not supply a witness over ℚᵃᵇ.
- The exact 1999 hypotheses and controlled module still need the page
  check. One must not identify full H² and the `j_*` kernel by notation.
  If the print imposes further local conditions, `KatoSupportComparison`
  must use the actual target with those conditions before the swap is sound.
- `RetainedZeta` does not construct the zeta elements. The support
  maps from the actual cohomology, the objects in explicit reciprocity,
  and the integral `j_*` comparison remain to be supplied. No residual
  irreducibility or theorem at primes over 2 is used.
- The odd filter is retained in `D` and in the finite quadratic weight.
  Removing an odd prime just because it divides the fixed level would
  change the representation.
