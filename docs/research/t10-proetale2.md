# T10: degree-one pro-étale comparison, continuation

**Scoped stop: `t10TateDuality` remains GLUE; count delta 0.** The degree-one reductions from the row into the compatible finite pro-étale groups are injective. The surjectivity argument now reduces to one epimorphism in the category of coefficient sheaves. On the finite side the coefficients are constant, the Ext fibres are actual locally trivial sheaves, and their local models are represented by split covers; descent of those local models and the comparison with big étale Ext are still open. The geometric multiplication square is proved, but the semilinear square for the same Milne family is not.

The only extra condition in the degree-one comparison above is `Epi (Limits.Pi.map (fun n => (injectivePresentation (residueSheaf X ℓ n)).g))`. This is a statement about local lifting of sections of a product of sheaf morphisms, independent of either site cohomology group or of the Tate module. It supplies one step of the Lean dictionary at tex 10954–10972. It would follow from countable exact products on the small pro-étale site; the proof uses only the particular product. The results on an individual lift use an ordinary short exact sequence of abelian sheaves, which is available from the predecessor's injective presentation. The split-cover lemmas use the finiteness of the set of sheets and a chosen local lift; neither assumption supplies a global representative. No new proposed literature statement is introduced.

## Remaining work

1. **Finite cohomology on the two sites.** Descend the split models of the local lift sheaf across a weakly étale covering; the output needed is a finite étale cover representing that sheaf with its action. The formal descent step needs effectivity (existence of a descended algebra or scheme), not just descent of the property of being finite étale. Mathlib has property descent and comonadicity for modules; the fork's `ZInQKernel/Contrib/CloudR2NOS/Descent/Assembly.lean` has a scheme descent construction with a projective presentation and line data. Neither directly turns the split models of an arbitrary lift torsor into a global finite étale object. Then finish the reverse construction from torsors to classes, and identify the big étale Ext computed from the sheafified free representable with the same torsors. The comparison must intertwine quotient and scalar maps on coefficients and pullback by the scheme isomorphism. The proved local representations give the objects to descend. This is still library-scale work (SPECULATIVE: hundreds of lines at least; plausibly thousands including the big-site and functoriality dictionaries).
2. **Surjectivity onto compatible classes.** For the proved route, show the product epimorphism above. In geometric terms, find a common pro-étale cover for countably many local lifts at a test object; a route uses affine refinements and a sequential affine limit whose map to the test object is again a covering map. The existing affine limit API does not by itself supply that weakly étale covering result or exact countable products on this site. Alternatively, it suffices to show the compatible families lie in the image of `productH1Coordinates`. The algebra of the limit and injectivity are already proved. Finiteness of the finite H¹ groups would give Mittag-Leffler for their own inverse system (the criterion is formalized in Mathlib, with Stacks tag 0594), but by itself supplies no common pro-étale local lift. In degree one the kernel calculation uses the difference operator on **H⁰**, where the maps are already onto.
3. **The same Milne family.** The draft supplies naturality in homomorphisms over `K`. The desired square uses pullback by `1 × Spec(σ)`, a map over a generally nonidentity automorphism of the geometric base. The geometric square proved here would be used by a proof identifying the specific classical isomorphism with the cover construction. The draft neither specifies that construction nor gives naturality for changing the base field. A checked source that supplies the same-family compatibility would be the shortest route; constructing and identifying the family in Lean would add substantial work. Once both cohomological squares are proved, `compose_transport_squares` proves the equivariance of the composite.

## Known issues

- The 2008 citation and the 1986 print draft have the scope issue noted above. The pro-étale comparison is separate from either Milne citation. Bhatt–Scholze, *The pro-étale topology for schemes*, is the relevant published comparison; the reported §5.6 locator is UNVERIFIED.
- The manuscript needs rational duality in characteristic zero. The Lean row uses integral coefficients and arbitrary fields at an invertible prime. A source stated using the separable closure needs either a comparison to the algebraic closure in positive characteristic or an application over the algebraically closed geometric base.
- This work does not change the bundle, the draft, or the caller. The base remains at 4 published and 18 GLUE open H rows under the strict count. There is no `#discharges` result.
