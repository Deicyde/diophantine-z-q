## 4. B1 (Berger's `B_max[log [π̄]] = B_max[log [p̃]]`): not formalized, not needed

**Finding (VERIFIED by reading p. 11).** Berger writes `log[π̄]` as the limit of the series for
`log(π̄^(0)) + log([π̄]/π̄^(0))`. Here `π̄^(0) = θ([π̄]) ∈ 𝓞_ℂ_p`, and `ℂ_p` is not a subring of `B_dR^+`: only
`ℚ̄_p` is, by Hensel. So the formula is literal only when `x^(0) ∈ ℚ̄_p^×`. For `p̃` it is literal and gives
`log p + log([p̃]/p)`, which is the tree's `logPTilde` plus `log p`. For `π̄` it is not. A faithful Lean definition
of `log[π̄]` needs Prop. 2.23's `p`-adic logarithm, a series converging in `B_max^+`. The tree has no
`p`-adically complete `A_max` and no `p`-adic limits in `B_dR^+`.

**Algebraic half (VERIFIED).** `bmax_adjoin_eq_bst`: for `ℓ ∈ B_dR` and `c ∈ ℚ_p` with
`(p − 1) ℓ − p (c + log [p̃]) ∈ B_max^+`, we have `B_max[ℓ] = B_st^{tree}`.

**Analytic half (on-the-spot, not in Lean).**

1. Let `π̄_1 = ε^{1/p} − 1`, so `π̄ = π̄_1^p` in characteristic `p`.
2. `v(π̄_1^{p−1}) = 1 = v(p̃)`, so `u_1 = π̄_1^{p−1}/p̃` is a unit of `𝓞_ℂ_p♭`.
3. For `p` odd, `u_1 ≡ −1 (mod m)`. Indeed `π̄_1^♯ ≡ ζ_p − 1 (mod p)` (coefficient 0), and `p/(ζ_p − 1) ∈ m`.
   So `u_1^♯ ≡ (ζ_p − 1)^{p−1}/p (mod m)`. Also `(ζ_p − 1)^{p−1}/p ≡ 1/((p−1)!) ≡ −1`, from
   `Π_{a<p} (1 − ζ^a) = p` and `(1 − ζ^a)/(1 − ζ) ≡ a`.
4. Hence `(−u_1)^{p^N} ≡ 1 (mod p̃)` for large `N`. Prop. 2.23's series gives
   `log[(−u_1)^{p^N}] ∈ B_max^+`. In Lean terms this would be `[x] = (1 + pa)(1 + ξb)`, with `log(1 + pa) ∈ A_inf`
   `p`-adically and `log(1 + ξb) ∈ A_max` as in `logTeich_mem_amax`.
5. With `log[−1] = 0` (Berger: `log = 0` on `k`) and multiplicativity, `log[π̄] = p log[π̄_1]`, and
   `(p − 1) log[π̄_1] = (log p + log[p̃]) + p^{−N} log[(−u_1)^{p^N}]`.
6. So `(p − 1) log[π̄] − p (log p + log[p̃]) ∈ B_max^+`, and `bmax_adjoin_eq_bst` gives B1 (for odd `p`; `p = 2`
   needs `ε^{1/4}`).

This is Berger's own Prop. 2.23 argument applied to `π̄`. What is on-the-spot is reading the §1.2 formula as the
multiplicative extension, and the cyclotomic congruence in step 3.

**Why B1 is not needed.** Neither new leaf mentions Berger's ring. B1 would make the *old* `BergerPrintedVT` (over
`B_max[log [p̃]]`) literally Berger 2002's statement, and with Berger's p. 11 remark it would give the comparison of
§5. It remains a possible second line of support, not a step of the route.

* *Ring*: `B_st = B_cris[log [p̃]]` as II.3.3 prints it (the image of `B_cris[Y]` under `Y ↦ log [p̃]`,
  `log_p(p) = 0`). `B_cris = B_cris^+[1/t]` and `B_cris^+` = the printed set (`bcrisPlusOf_eq_set`).
* *Choices*: every generator `ε₀` of `ℤ_p(1)` (the survey's `ε`). Every generator `w` of `ker θ`: the survey's `ω`
  is one (II.2.2), and `A_cris` does not depend on it (`acrisOf_eq`). The tree's `p̃` (survey: any `p̃` with
  `p̃^(0) = p`).
* *One reading (on-the-spot)*: "`x_n → 0` in `B̃^+`" is read `p`-adically, with `x_n ∈ p^{−j} A_inf`, as bst-construct
  reads `A_max`. If the print means the weak topology (II.3.5 calls it "the p-adic topology and the topology of
  `Ẽ^+`"), it gives the same set. Sketch: a weakly null sequence lies in `p^{−j} A_inf` with
  `x_n ∈ p^{k_n} A_inf + [p̃]^{N_n} A_inf`, where `k_n, N_n → ∞`. Expanding
  `[p̃]^N = (p − ξ)^N = Σ_j C(N,j) p^{N−j} (−ξ)^j` gives
  `[p̃]^N ξ^n/n! = Σ_j C(N,j) (−1)^j p^{N−j} ((n+j)!/n!) ξ^{n+j}/(n+j)!`. Here
  `v_p(p^{N−j} (n+j)!/n!) ≥ N − j + ⌊j/p⌋ ≥ N/p − 1`, so the rearranged coefficients tend to `0` `p`-adically.
* *Not stronger than the print*: same hypotheses; the conclusion is the print's, over the print's ring.

## 8. Lean notes
