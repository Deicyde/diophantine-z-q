# Torsion for the stack-free integral model of $Y_0(N)$

## Result and scoped stop

Lean proves that the abscissa polynomial is separable at every invertible level, that the abscissa algebra has unit discriminant, and that the affine nonidentity torsion algebra is finite free and étale of rank $n^2-1$. Both algebras commute with arbitrary scalar extension. The affine algebra specializes to the universal short Weierstrass locus over $\mathbb Z_{(\ell_0)}$ at every level prime to $\ell_0$. These statements include even levels, with the two-torsion branch included once, and odd levels in characteristic two.

**Scoped stop.** The next input for the level moduli problem is addition on the finite étale torsion scheme over the universal short Weierstrass locus. The pinned point-group APIs apply over fields. The scheme operations, the exact-order and basis loci, the cyclic quotient and the universal level algebra $S$ remain to be formalized.

## Moduli interface for assembly

**ON THE SPOT (c).** Once addition on the finite étale torsion scheme is implemented, take the complement in $E[N]$ of the kernels of $[N/q]$ for the prime divisors $q$ of $N$. Each kernel is clopen by pullback of the identity section. Their complement cuts out exactly the geometric order-$N$ points, and $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$ acts freely by $P\mapsto[a]P$. For prime $N$, this complement is the nonidentity torsion chart.

For a base algebra $A'$, define $\operatorname{Cyc}_N(A')$ as the finite étale subgroup schemes $C\subset E_{A'}[N]$ which admit an étale-local group isomorphism $(\mathbb Z/N)_{A'}\simeq C$. A local generator determines the subgroup. Its changes of generator are precisely the unit classes, including when the generator torsor of a subgroup over a connected base is nontrivial. Over a splitting cover the generator orbits are a disjoint union of copies of the base. They inherit finite étale descent data, and the same action on the split subgroup sets identifies the descended quotient with $\operatorname{Cyc}_N$. This gives the quotient argument for prime and composite $N$ without inverting $\varphi(N)$. Mathlib's `Algebra.IsFiniteSplit.exists_tensorProduct_of_etale` gives a finite faithfully flat étale splitting cover in constant rank. The vendored `CategoryTheory.Over.DescentDatum.isEffective_of_isFinite` supports the affine descent. These APIs remain to be wired to the level functor.

For unit prime $p$, remove $P=0$ and the equalities $Q=[a]P$ for $a\in\mathbb F_p$ from $E[p]^2$. The diagonals of finite étale schemes are clopen. The count $p^2$ and $[p]=0$ give dimension two over $\mathbb F_p$ in each geometric fibre. The complement therefore parametrizes ordered bases, and change of basis acts freely and transitively on each geometric fibre. The vendored `AddCommGroup.nonempty_zmod_prod_addEquiv_torsionBy_of_card_torsionBy_eq_sq` was inspected. Its divisor-by-divisor hypothesis would also give the geometric group structure at composite level. The prime basis argument uses only the count at $p$.

Take $R=\mathbb Z_{(\ell_0)}$ with $\ell_0\ge5$ and $A_U=R[a_4,a_6][1/\Delta]$. Excluding 3 along with the other finite exclusions at 8365–8368 ensures the lower bound. Dirichlet's theorem supplies auxiliary primes $p\equiv2\bmod\ell_0$. Choose one with $p\ge3$ and $p\nmid N\ell_0$. Then $\ell_0\nmid|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|=p(p-1)^2(p+1)$. The assembly input is

$$
S=\mathcal O(\operatorname{Cyc}_N)\otimes_{A_U}\mathcal O(\operatorname{Basis}_p).
$$

This is finite étale once the factors are constructed. $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ acts on the ordered basis. An isomorphism of Weierstrass models transports the subgroup and basis. In short form use $x\mapsto u^2x,\ y\mapsto u^3y,\ a_4\mapsto u^4a_4,\ a_6\mapsto u^6a_6$. The coaction gives the integer grading, with $\Delta^{-1}$ of degree $-12$. The action on functions is pullback, with the inverse for a left action, and the two actions commute.

The parallel Reynolds lane supplies finite-group averaging when the group order is a unit, projection to degree zero, base change of both, and the geometric points of the quotient as orbits. Assembly uses these with $S$ to form $\operatorname{Spec}((S^G)_0)$.
