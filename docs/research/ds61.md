# Diamond–Shurman 9.6.5 from Deligne–Serre Théorème 6.1

The open hypothesis `OpenResearchLeaves65.dsThm965Printed` (`DSCoeffExt.DSThm965Printed`) states the λ-adic
representation of a normalized eigenform of weight k ≥ 2 over the completion `K_{f,λ}` of its coefficient field,
with the characteristic polynomial `x² − a_p x + χ(p)p^{k−1}` of arithmetic Frobenius. Its recorded source for
this field, Diamond–Shurman Theorem 9.6.5, is flagged unread here (kinds.tsv records an AI reading of 9.6.5 under
`afCharpolyDS965`). A statement check (AI-READ, 2026-11-18; recorded as
`docs/one-proof/statement-checks/dsThm965Printed-sweep-20261118.md` on the statement-check branch) found the statement to be a
consequence of Deligne–Serre, *Formes modulaires de poids 1*, Ann. Sci. ÉNS (4) 7 (1974), Théorème 6.1, p. 520
(Frobenius convention on p. 513), which the project had not used.

`ZInQKernel/Contrib/OneProof/DeligneSerreThm61.lean` adds

* `DSCoeffExt.DeligneSerreThm61`: Théorème 6.1 as printed — a nonzero form `f` of type `(k, χ)` on `Γ₀(N)` (Deligne–Serre's Théorème 6.1 is the theorem for `k ≥ 2`; weight one is Théorème 4.1)
  (here a cusp form, a narrower input), `k ≥ 2`, an eigenvector of the `T_p` for `p ∤ N` with eigenvalues `a_p`;
  a number field `K ⊂ ℂ` containing the `a_p` and the `χ(p)`; a finite place `λ` of `K` of residue
  characteristic `ℓ`. Then there is a continuous `ρ_λ : G_ℚ → GL₂(K_λ)`, trivial on the inertia groups above
  every `p ∤ Nℓ`, with `Tr ρ_λ(σ) = a_p` and `det ρ_λ(σ) = χ(p)p^{k−1}` for every arithmetic Frobenius `σ` at
  `p`. Semisimplicity is omitted, so the conclusion is weaker than the print;

and proves

* `DSCoeffExt.dsThm965Printed_of_deligneSerre61 : DeligneSerreThm61 → DSThm965Printed`.

The proof takes `K = K_f`: a normalized eigenform is nonzero, of type `(k, χ)`, and an eigenvector of every `T_p`
with eigenvalue `a_p(f)`; the existing Hecke relation `coeff_sq_relation` gives `χ(p) ∈ K_f` for `p ∤ N`. The
characteristic polynomial of a 2×2 matrix is `X² − tr · X + det`, which gives the left disjunct.
