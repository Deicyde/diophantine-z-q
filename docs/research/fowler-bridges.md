# Fowler's Proposition 2·7: the order dictionary

Lean proves `fowlerProp27_of_print : FowlerProp27Print → FowlerProp27`. The only input is Proposition 2·7 with
Fowler's **proper ideal class number**. The trace discriminant, finiteness and proper ideal bridges all compile.
The separate correspondence with binary quadratic form classes (Cox, Theorem 7.7) remains open under the scoped
stop rule; Fowler defines his class number by ideals, so the application does not need that correspondence.

## Printed statement

Fowler, *Some uniform effective results on André–Oort for sums of powers in ℂⁿ*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos.
Soc. 180 (2026), 607–641, Proposition 2·7, p. 615 (proof pp. 615–616), uses a negative integer Δ congruent to
0 or 1 modulo 4 and a positive integer n. If the order class number h(Δ) is at most n, then
√|Δ| ≤ 2n² exp(21000n²). The supplied [human reading of the publisher PDF](https://github.com/Deicyde/diophantine-z-q/issues/14#issuecomment-6973387139)
confirms that there is no exceptional-field exclusion, including field discriminants −3 and −4. Page 612 explains
Δ as the discriminant of the primitive equation for the CM period and writes it as f²D for the field discriminant D.
Page 613 defines the classes and h(Δ) through the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant Δ.

`FowlerProp27Print` keeps Δ, the congruence, positivity of n, the bound on h(Δ), and the inequality explicit.
It quantifies over an order in a totally complex degree-two number field whose trace discriminant equals Δ. This
expresses the order instance without picking a canonical order or requiring an order uniqueness theorem. Its h(Δ)
is the cardinal of the quotient of proper fractional ideals (multiplier ring equal to the order) by nonzero
principal scaling. It includes no hypothesis about the project's Picard group, and no assumption of maximality.

The trace discriminant multiplies by the square of the lattice index. Its sign follows from the single complex
place. The mod-four computation uses a basis `(1, θ)` and writes `θ² = A + Bθ`: the trace determinant is
`B² + 4A`. Class group finiteness comes from the vendored surjection from a finite ray class group, with
`ClassGroup.equivPic` providing the equivalence to the module-theoretic Picard group. This rules out the
infinite-type convention of `Nat.card`.

For Cox (1989), Proposition 7.4, scale a basis of the proper fractional ideal to `(1, τ)`. A noninteger order element
preserves this lattice and supplies a quadratic equation for τ, which can be divided by its content. With primitive
coefficients `(a,b,c)`, the elements `a` and `−b−aτ` multiply both lattice generators into the multiplier ring.
They therefore belong to the inverse ideal. The product contains the three integers `a,b,c`, so Bézout gives
`1` and proves invertibility. The converse is already in the vendored multiplier-ring library. The quotient
comparison respects multiplication by the principal fractional ideal generated by a nonzero element of the field.

## Scoped stop: Cox's form classes

The remaining statement from Cox (1989), Theorem 7.7, is named `CoxFormClassEquivalence` in
`FowlerBridgesForms.lean`: for a degree-two totally complex number field and an order O, there is an equivalence
between `ProperIdealClasses O` and `ProperFormClasses O.discr`. The equivalence expresses the cardinality
assertion together with finiteness; no group law on forms is required. The carrier on the right uses integral
triples with gcd 1, a positive leading coefficient, and negative discriminant, modulo change of variables by
`SL₂(ℤ)`. This is a follow-up statement, not an added leaf.

The existing period lattice computation gives the primitive equation for one already oriented lattice and
identifies its discriminant. It does not establish the correspondence on quotients. A proof of the latter still
needs a form-to-ideal constructor (from the upper-half-plane root in the chosen embedding), an ideal-to-form
constructor with orientation and uniqueness of primitive coefficients, and the agreement of oriented change of
basis with `SL₂(ℤ)` equivalence and principal scaling in both directions. The two constructions then induce
inverse quotient maps. SPECULATIVE scope: several independent lemmas and a few hundred lines of Lean,
rather than a new use of a counting lemma. Once available, `card_pic_eq_card_properFormClasses_of` supplies the form count for the
singular modulus degree route; the ideal count is already the relevant dictionary for Fowler.
