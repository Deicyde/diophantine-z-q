# Rationality of the fixed scale via the period subgroup

**Scoped stop at the independent modular realization.** The period comparison reduces rationality of the original `κ` to an independent rational reference scale for the same normalized form and period lattice. The existing open `ModularToWeakWeil` statement suffices to supply that reference, with no new print. The two scales need not coincide. The represented `J0 N` supplies a geometric route to this reference scale: an independent based composite with the analytic form of the normalized integral at any nonzero complex scale is enough. The quotient and its analytic identification are still open. The five consumer statements retain their original `κ` and do not ask that it be rational.

The inherited Milne audit flags a citation scope error at tex 1784 and 1788: the cited pair covers the map and Jacobian quotient to the elliptic curve; the normalized newform is addressed elsewhere in the notes, and the pair does not state the displayed Hecke equality. This is a locator issue, not a counterexample to that equality. See **Known issues** below.

## Fixed data and route

Write `E := Ecurve ℓ` and `N := newformLevel E` for the application. Tex 1785–1787 chooses `α : A_f → E` and makes the based map `π = α q_f ∘ aj₀`; tex 1825–1828 fixes the periods for that choice. The manuscript does not name a scalar `κ` in those lines. In the Lean model it is the coefficient of the based primitive of `2πi f dτ` in the Weierstrass uniformization. `IsLatticeOf E Lat` prescribes the Weierstrass invariants of the lattice. `IsParamScale N f Lat κ` requires only `κ ≠ 0` and containment of the scaled periods in the lattice. Multiplying by any nonzero integer preserves that condition. A particular based map determines its scalar, but neither these Lean conditions nor the manuscript's choice of an isogeny specifies a canonical one.

The leanest remaining input, with `f` and `Lat` fixed and with the existing non-CM multiplier condition, is this instance of the existing `ModularWeil.NewformPeriodsRational`:

```lean
∃ r : ℚ, IsParamScale N f Lat (r : ℂ)
```

The reference scale need only scale the periods of the given form into the same lattice. It need not equal any designated complex scale. As a free-standing input this does not assert rationality of a given admissible `κ`; for example, with a zero form every nonzero complex scale is admissible. Under the nonzero and non-CM conditions in this patch, the new theorem supplies the missing implication. The reverse implication under `IsParamScale N f Lat κ` is immediate. No new hypothesis structure, named Prop or print is introduced.

The comparison has two parts (on the spot, provenance (c)).

1. Scaled periods of a nonzero weight-two cusp form contain a nonzero integer multiple of the target lattice. In integer coordinates on the lattice, two vectors with nonzero determinant give such a multiple by the adjugate. If there is no such pair, multiplication by a nonzero complex constant sends all periods to integers: use one nonzero integral coordinate on the line of periods, or any nonzero constant when they are all zero. The exponential of the resulting primitive then descends in weight zero. Its translates have finite limits at the cusps by the weight-two Eichler integral transformation and the decay of the translated cusp form. Weight-zero constancy makes the exponential constant. Its derivative and the identity `q d/dq F = f` force `f = 0`, a contradiction. For a form matched to `E`, the coefficient at 1 of its Dirichlet series is 1.
2. If `d · Lat.lattice` lies in the subgroup generated by the `r`-scaled periods, then `dκ/r` multiplies the whole lattice into itself, since each generator goes to the corresponding `κ`-scaled period after multiplication by `κ/r`. The existing `IsNonCMLattice Lat` gives `dκ/r = n` for an integer `n`. The returned witness is `nr/d`. This proves the claim at the designated scale without identifying the two maps or changing any period used by a consumer.

An existing `ModularToWeakWeil` provider is enough: the prescribed nonzero-level form already witnesses `IsModular E`. The form chosen by the provider has the same positive coefficients and hence equals the prescribed form as a function. Apply the proved isogeny period transport to the *identity* rational isogeny of `E`: its invariant differential scalar is 1, so the provider's lattice is contained in the chosen one. This gives the pointwise reference and, using the existing trivial case at level zero, all of `NewformPeriodsRational`. Together with the existing opposite reduction, the two open interfaces have the same strength here. No equality of chosen basis vectors is required.

The manuscript's independent supplier of the reference scale is the composite in tex 1785–1787. On the represented objects, the exact sufficient geometric statement at the already fixed `E, N, f, Lat` is:

```lean
∃ (φ : (J0 N).X ⟶ projectiveTarget E) (b : ℂ), b ≠ 0 ∧
  ∀ τ : ℍ, projectiveValue (interior N τ ≫ aj0 N ≫ φ).left = phiAn E f Lat b τ
```

Here `φ` is meant to be the composite `α q_f`, a morphism over ℚ. The reduction only uses that underlying morphism: no group homomorphism law, Hecke identity or surjectivity field is needed. The comparison is the integrated normalized pullback differential with basepoint infinity. It mentions neither `κ` nor rationality of `b`. Chart invariance and injectivity of the period uniformization give `IsParamScale` for `b`. The width-one comparison in `KappaRational.rational_of_projective_values_on_tail` proves `b ∈ ℚ` from the two Laurent coordinates on the rational model. Thus no separate rationality theorem about the differential is required once the independent map and its analytic identification are supplied. A cusp-tail comparison is sufficient for the Laurent part; the whole-interior statement is used here to get invariance without another analytic continuation lemma.

## Known issues
