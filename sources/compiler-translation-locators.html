    Input C

    Section 1, lines 102-104: input C of Lemma 1.2 (lem:assembly), "the actual five-input natural polynomial and its rational translation have the exact computation and substitution semantics of Section 10, including the power template".     Section 3.1 natural predicate

    Section 3.1, lines 362-371: raw addition has the narrow contract on canonical codes; every later use supplies canonical input codes; multiplication imposes membership of its three roots and is sound on arbitrary rational tuples; in the natural predicate four initially untyped tuples enter multiplication calls, so are typed before their squares are added; Lagrange's theorem then gives exactly \(\iota(\mathbb N)\); this uses multiplication completeness.

    Section 4 height constants

Line 968: \(S_{\rm ht}=\{p:p\le324\}\).

    Section 10 opening

    Section 10 (sec:compiler), lines 5745-5789: the signed encoding \(\operatorname{enc}(n)=2n\) for \(n\ge0\) and \(-2n-1\) for \(n<0\) (lines 5746-5749); the input order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\) and the early rejections \(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(a,l\ge1\), \(j=na\) (lines 5750-5751); \(z=-x/y\), \(z([l]P_0)=u_l+v_ls\), \(\epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|\), \(r(a,j)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(z([j]P_0)/z([a]P_0))\) (lines 5752-5757); \(A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}=H_6(S_{\rm ht})\) and the two strict final tests (lines 5758-5763); the reference program S21 and the ideal IR S22 with its all-input argument (lines 5766-5775); the integer form \(4\epsilon<4^{-B}\iff 4^B\le\lfloor(V-1)/(4U)\rfloor\) and the saturation \(\sigma(x)=\min(x,L+1)\) (lines 5777-5788).

    Section 10.1 Cantor wrapper and acceptance

    Section 10.1, lines 5790-5815: the saved precision polynomial has \(1\,305\,811\) natural variables, the first five inputs (lines 5791-5792); S23 specializes a universal natural halting polynomial at the code of a wrapper that inverts four Cantor pairs (lines 5796-5798); the pairing equations \(2t_i=(c_i+t_{i+1})(c_i+t_{i+1}+1)+2t_{i+1}\) (\(0\le i<4\)), \(t_4=c_4\), and the new polynomial as the sum of the squared old polynomial and the four squared differences (lines 5799-5804); acceptance of a fixed increment/decrement/branch program means reaching the unique final halt with all registers zero, with a finite cleanup stage (lines 5807-5809).

    Section 10.3 program numbering

    Section 10.3, lines 5977-5982: the universal numbering \(e+1=2^w(2D+1)\); \(w=0\) halts; otherwise the four base-\(2^w\) digits at positions \(4p,\dots,4p+3\) give opcode, register and two targets of state \(p\); opcode one increments, opcode two decrements or takes its zero branch, every other opcode halts; missing digits are zero, so decoding is total.

    Section 10.3 interpreter

    Section 10.3, lines 5984-5997: the tape code \(0\) or \(\operatorname{cons}(h,t)=2^h(2t+1)\); decoding by repeated halving, the tail smaller than the code; register update by saving the reversed prefix; absent entries are zero; the reserved jump register stays zero; copies restore their source; aliased addition snapshots its operand; division maintains \(x=qy+r\) and subtracts only when \(r\ge y\), with divisors at least two; one interpreter loop executes exactly one decoded instruction with finite work; a simulated halt enters cleanup; an infinite simulation never does; the all-zero halt is equivalent to virtual halting.

    Section 10.3 precision IR

    Section 10.3, lines 5999-6016: normalized signed pairs and pairs over \(1,\sqrt2\); norm zero implies zero (a primitive solution of \(a^2=2b^2\) would have both entries even); every division guarded by that test; the Jacobian formulas satisfy the curve equation and the affine chord and tangent formulas after multiplying denominators; equal abscissas split into doubling and opposite points; zero ordinate doubles to the identity; the fixed projective point is \(P_0\); these identities are independent of the generator theorem; the scalar loop halves, the precision loop divides by four; capped arithmetic computes \(\min(x,L+1)\) with zero bases, unit bases and zero exponents separate; the ideal IR terminates and agrees with the predicate on all five natural inputs.

    Section 10.3 lowering

    Section 10.3, lines 6018-6025: each primitive lowering uses fresh auxiliary registers, snapshots overlapping sources and preserves the live-register map at the end of each finite block; saturating decrement gives natural subtraction; repeated addition gives multiplication; variable divisions are guarded; induction on completed loop iterations handles loops; acceptance enters cleanup, rejection a permanent self-loop; the primitive machine halts exactly on acceptance.

    Section 10.3 unpairing wrapper
