    Line ranges refer to diophantine_zq.tex in this repository (the 11/5 manuscript, SHA-256 cb1148cd6c7769d2af3f1636b60ff0ada4b40431978d1e98d0c339274b3d792b). The saved specification and its generators are project-authored artifacts; they are listed last and serve as cross-checks of the construction, not as inputs to the proof.

    Lemma 1.2

    lem:assembly, Section 1 (sec:main), lines 93-121: the six inputs G, M, H, PC, C, B (lines 93-108), the statement "Assume G, M, H, PC, C, B. Then \(P_*\) satisfies Theorem 1.1" (lines 110-113) and its three-sentence proof (lines 114-121): Section 11 gives the exact ordinary-value relation with one shared shift and disjoint private witness blocks, the literal substitutions of B identify the existential polynomial with \(P_*\), and Section 12 gives the bounds.

    Theorem 1.1 proof

    Section 1, lines 60-76 (thm:target) and 123-140 (proof of Theorem 1.1): the assignment of G to Section 3 and Proposition 3.1, H to Theorem 4.1, PC to Sections 5 and 7-9, M to Section 6, C to Section 10 and B to Sections 2, 12 and Appendix C.

    Section 2.1 literal operations

    Section 2.1, lines 166-201: calls with private witness blocks, sums of squares and products over \(\mathbb Q\), inactive product blocks, rational constants by global equations (\(8g_0=1641\), \(4g_1=-15465\), \(32g_2=87483\)), inverses as equations \(uv=1\), positivity and absolute value by four squares.

    Section 3 codes

    sec:model, Section 3, lines 254-266: \(K_0\), \(d=75-53s\), \(E\), \(P_0\), the codes of points and \(\iota(n)\), the code of \([n]P_0\).

    Section 3.1 natural codes

    Section 3.1, lines 350-371: membership in \(2E(K_0)\), the raw addition contract \(\operatorname{Add}_{\rm raw}(\iota(a),\iota(b),R)\iff R=\iota(a+b)\), and (lines 366-371) the natural predicate: four untyped tuples are typed by multiplication calls before their squares are added, and Lagrange's theorem gives exactly \(\iota(\mathbb N)\), using multiplication completeness.

    Section 4 height constants

    sec:height, Section 4, lines 377-403: \(\mathcal C\), \(F(u)\), \(M_S(u)\) and the constants \(F_0,D,G_0,E_0,Q_R,B_{\rm cm},C_{\rm cm},H_6(S;R)\), with \(H_6(S)=H_6(S;\max(D,\max S,10^{12}))\); Theorem 4.1 (thm:height), lines 404-409. Lines 968-971: the final value argument uses \(S_{\rm ht}=\{p:p\le324\}\).

    Lemma 5.1

    lem:pole, Section 5, lines 973-1000: the pole polynomial (lines 974-987) and the one-sided obstruction: if \(B\ne0\) and \(\Phi(B)\), then \(v_p(B)\) is not negative and odd for any \(p\notin S_0=\{2,3,5,7,13\}\).

    Theorem 5.2

    hyp:PC, Section 5, lines 1001-1003: pole completeness.

    Section 6 formal conventions

    Section 6 (sec:mult), lines 1131-1150: the conventions \(z=-x/y\), \(w=1/x\) with \(w(O)=0\), \(v(w)=2v(z)\), the formal logarithm and \(v(z([n]Q))=v(z(Q))+v(n)\) in the formal neighbourhood, citing Silverman IV.5.5, IV.6.3-6.4 and VII.2.1-2.2.

    Section 10 precision predicate

    sec:compiler, Section 10, lines 5746-5789: the signed encoding \(\operatorname{enc}(n)=2n\) \((n\ge0)\), \(-2n-1\) \((n<0)\); the input order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\); the early rejections unless \(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(a,l\ge1\), \(j=na\); \(z=-x/y\), \(z([l]P_0)=u_l+v_ls\), \(\epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|\), \(r(a,j)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}_{K_0/\mathbb Q}(z([j]P_0)/z([a]P_0))\); the constant \(A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}=H_6(S_{\rm ht})\) and the final tests \(|r(a,j)-n|<\epsilon_l\), \(4\epsilon_l<4^{-A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}|\kappa F(n)|^{24}}\) (lines 5758-5763).

    Section 10.4 translation

    Section 10.4, lines 6052-6093: the typing order (membership, raw addition on canonical inputs, multiplication, then natural codes), the signed-encoding and exceptional branches starting their addition chains at the natural code, the literal origin or the fixed signed generator, and the generator equations outside internal products.

    Section 11 opening

    sec:bridge, Section 11, lines 6094-6101: the ordinary-value deduction from the exact index model, the compiled precision relation, the height theorem and PC; codes are capital letters, indices lower case; \(t\) is any rational number.

    Section 11.1 approximation

    Section 11.1, lines 6102-6129: the relation \(\operatorname{Close}(N,\mathcal K,t)\) (choose \(A,L,J\), link the five signed codes to precision inputs in the order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\), impose \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\)), \(B(n,\kappa)=H_6(S_{\rm ht})|\kappa F(n)|^{24}\), the soundness (eq:close-sound, line 6112) and completeness (eq:close-complete, line 6114) of Close, and their proofs (lines 6116-6129): \(|t-n|<2\epsilon_l<4^{-B}/2<e^{-B}\); positive multiples of \(P_0\) approach the identity in \(E(\mathbf R)\times E(\mathbf R)\); the derivative of \([n]\) at the identity is \(n\) (Silverman IV.2.3(a)).

    Section 11.2 six local tests

    Section 11.2, lines 6130-6150: the points \(H,U,V_0=H-\mathcal K,V_1=U-\iota(\kappa n)\), the guards \(\kappa\ne0\), \(H,V_0\ne O\), \(w(Q)=1/x(Q)\), the six tests \(D_1,\dots,D_6\), the quantities \(\eta,q,R_0,a_0,R,C_j,W,\Theta,B_{\rm pole}\) and the relation \(\operatorname{Test}(N,\mathcal K,t)\).

    Lemma 11.1

    lem:test, lines 6151-6201: if \(\operatorname{Test}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),t)\) holds, then \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\mid\kappa F(n)\); the proof uses Lemma 5.1, the formal groups at the places above \(p\) and Silverman IV.5.5 and IV.6.4.

    Lemma 11.2

    lem:test-complete, lines 6202-6235: for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\mathcal C\) there is \(\kappa\ne0\) with \(\operatorname{Test}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),n)\); the proof uses Silverman VII.6.3, the choices \(H=[2\kappa]P_0\), \(U=[2\kappa n]P_0\), \(V_0=[\kappa]P_0\), \(V_1=[\kappa n]P_0\), a rational \(q\) close to \(1/\sqrt{R_0}\) and Theorem 5.2.

    Section 11.3 exact ordinary value

    Section 11.3, lines 6236-6271: the relation \(\operatorname{Val}(N,t)\), the height bound \(h(t)\le B(n,\kappa)\) in an accepting main branch, the separation \(|t-n|\ge1/b\ge e^{-h(t)}\), completeness with disjoint private blocks, and the identification of the polynomial (S01, S16) with the saved root \(P_*\).

    Section 12 size

    sec:size, Section 12, lines 6272-6442: the cutoff (Proposition 12.1, prop:cutoff) and the quantitative syntax bounds for \(P_*\), which complete the bounds of Theorem 1.1.

    Section 13 scope

    Section 13, lines 6443-6465: the table of inputs of Theorem 1.1, listing "Ordinary value: shared-shift Close and Test, height and rational separation, Section 11".
