    Line ranges refer to diophantine_zq.tex in this repository (the 11/5 manuscript, SHA-256 cb1148cd6c7769d2af3f1636b60ff0ada4b40431978d1e98d0c339274b3d792b).     Input B

    Section 1 (sec:main), lines 105-107: the input B of Lemma 1.2 (lem:assembly). The templates, fixed constructors and private witness substitutions specify one expression \(P_*\), to which the quantitative syntax calculation applies. Lines 135-137 (proof of Theorem 1.1) assign B to Sections 2 and 12 and Appendix C.

    Section 2 opening

    Section 2 (sec:syntax), lines 141-164: the specification has 23 library templates and seven recipes; the root recipe is integers, whose only parameter is the rational variable at index zero; every other variable is existentially quantified over \(\mathbb Q\). Provenance normalization of the saved file (three diagnostic fields).

    Section 2.1 literal operations

    Section 2.1, lines 166-188: scalar nodes, calls with private consecutive witness blocks, shared nodes, recipes as sums of squares or products; the equivalences \(\sum f_i^2=0\iff\bigwedge f_i=0\) and \(\prod f_i=0\iff\bigvee f_i=0\) over \(\mathbb Q\) (lines 172-176); private blocks moved to the front and arbitrary values in inactive product blocks (lines 177-180); rational constants \(p/q\) by a fresh \(c\) and the global equation \(qc=p\), with \(8g_0=1641\), \(4g_1=-15465\), \(32g_2=87483\) (lines 180-187); inverses as equations \(uv=1\) (lines 187-188).

    Section 2.1 positivity and absolute value

    Section 2.1, lines 190-201: \(s>0\iff\exists z_1,\dots,z_4\in\mathbb Q,\ s\sum z_i^2=1\), with the four-square proof sketch (lines 195-197), and \(a=|x|\iff\exists z_1,\dots,z_4,\ a=\sum z_i^2,\ a^2=x^2\) (lines 197-201).

    Section 2.2 fixed constructors

    Section 2.2, lines 203-208: the three constructor node types fixed_integer, psi_component and finite_guard_product.

    Section 2.2 division polynomials

    Section 2.2, lines 210-236: the normalized division polynomials \(f_n\) on \(y^2=x^3+Ax\), \(\psi_n=f_n\) (\(n\) odd), \(\psi_n=2yf_n\) (\(n\) even), the bases \(f_0,\dots,f_4\), the even and odd recurrences, \(\psi_{-n}=-\psi_n\); the citation of Sutherland §§5.5-5.6 (Lemmas 5.20, 5.24, Theorem 5.21); \(x([n]P)=\phi_n/\psi_n^2\), \(y([n]P)=\omega_n/\psi_n^3\), \(\omega_n=(\psi_{n+2}\psi_{n-1}^2-\psi_{n-2}\psi_{n+1}^2)/(4y)\), \(\psi_{2n}=2\psi_n\omega_n\); the fixed-scalar ordinate equation \(2y([n]P)\psi_n(P)^4=\psi_{2n}(P)\) and the nonvanishing of \(\psi_n(P)\) on the torsion-free carrier (lines 233-236).

    Section 2.2 catalogue and formal multiple

    Section 2.2, lines 238-252: the prime catalogue (first 22 primes above 324 in each unit class modulo \(W=4\prod_{p\le324}p\)), \(N_s=(\prod_{p\le324}p)H_{\rm cat}\), the least formal multiple \(M_0\) (least \(m\ge1\) with \(N_s^4\mid a,b\) and \(\gcd(c,N_s)=1\) for \(1/x([m]P_0)=(a+bs)/c\) normalized), and the remark that the guard is a finite integer polynomial whose arithmetic zero set is a separate issue.

    Section 3 point codes

    Section 3 (sec:model), lines 254-266: \(K_0=\mathbb Q(s)\), \(s^2=2\), \(d=75-53s\), \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\), the point \(P_0\), the codes \((1,0,0,0,0)\) and \((0,x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1)\), and \(\iota(n)\), the code of \([n]P_0\).

    Section 3.1 membership and natural codes

    Section 3.1, lines 350-374: the membership equations, the raw-addition contract, and the natural predicate (four multiplication calls and three additions, Lagrange's theorem).

    Section 6 multiplication template

    Section 6 (sec:mult), lines 1123-1224: the multiplication template with 35,134 witnesses, the formal logarithm \(L\), the twelve tests of a test group, the slope predicate, and the height certificate.

    Section 10 translation

    Section 10.4, lines 6052-6093: the typing order of the templates, the power-value recipe (three signed code inputs, seventy-nine private natural witnesses, root equality \([17751579,15]\)), the precision parent (54,056 explicit variables, 15,845 private blocks of seventy-nine variables), and the remark that equality is a difference, conjunction a sum of squares and disjunction a product, including the globally conjoined fixed equations.

    Section 12.2 syntax bounds

    Section 12.2, lines 6362-6441: the quantitative syntax calculation; the counts 173 scalar constant nodes and 50 finite constructors (lines 6372-6379); the root recipe declares \(292\,890\,231\,904\) witnesses (lines 6438-6439).

    Appendix C opening

    Appendix C (app:literal), lines 6666-6682: the root integers; variable zero is the input and variables \(1,\dots,292890231904\) its witnesses; point references \(r\ge0\), \(-1\), \(-2\), \(-3\); the three generator constraints conjoined by every model recipe.

    Appendix C.1 scalar nodes

    Appendix C.1, lines 6683-6721: node semantics in stored topological order; calls (substitute the parameter polynomials for variables \(0,\dots,p-1\) and \(X_{w+j}\) for variable \(p+j\)); fixed_integer and its operators (literals, references, addition, multiplication, powers, maximum, factorial, catalogue product and maximum, least formal multiple); psi_component with pair multiplication and \(A=(-11243,7950)\); the fixed point codes \(x_n\), \(y_n\); finite_guard_product over \([-T,T]^3\) with \(ab\ne c\) and the factor \(\sum_{j=1}^{15}(DX_j-(D/B_j)A_j)^2\).

    Appendix C.2 recipes and counts

    Appendix C.2, lines 6722-6782: constraints of a model-evaluation recipe; composition recipes (sum of squares, product); parameters [var,i]; disjoint private blocks; the table of the 23 templates (parameters, witnesses, root node) and the table of the seven recipes (parameters, witnesses, total variables); the five scalar-constant templates and the byte-for-byte retention of large program literals.
