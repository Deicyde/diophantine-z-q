    The complete 2-descent map

    The map \(P\mapsto(x-e_1,x-e_2)\) modulo squares on a curve \(y^2=(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3)\) with full rational 2-torsion, with the stated values at the 2-torsion points, is a homomorphism with kernel \(2E(K)\): J. H. Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, 2nd ed., Springer (2009), Proposition X.1.4, as cited by docs/research/pc-audit.md RK8, which records its use for \(C_d\). The proposition number was not rechecked against the book.

    Halving

    docs/research/cr3-map-literature.md §8, N2, Lean cost (i): the Kummer map \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\to(\mathbb Q^*/\mathbb Q^{*2})^2\) is a homomorphism with kernel \(2C_d(\mathbb Q)\), by the lemma that \(x\), \(x\pm d\) squares make \((x,y)\) halvable (classical, elementary). The explicit half, with \(x'=x+r_1r_2+r_1r_3+r_2r_3\) where \(r_i^2=x-e_i\), is the standard formula for curves with full 2-torsion.

    RK9

    docs/research/pc-audit.md §3, record RK9 (and §2.2): if \(\dim\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)=3\) and \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\), then \(C_d(\mathbb Q)/2C_d(\mathbb Q)\), which injects into \(\mathrm{Sel}_2\) and has dimension \(2+\operatorname{rank}\ge3\), equals \(\mathrm{Sel}_2\), so every Selmer class comes from a rational point, which can be taken of infinite order. No pairing and no finiteness of \(Ш\) are needed. The count uses that \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is finitely generated.

    Two-torsion of the congruent number curves

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §1.3, item "No prescribed class": \(C_n(\mathbb Q)[2]=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\) (standard), so \(\dim C_n(\mathbb Q)/2C_n(\mathbb Q)=2+\mathrm{rank}\).

    Mordell-Weil for the congruent number curves

    KERNEL_PLAN.md, section "What can and cannot be formalized", the paragraph beginning "These hypotheses all conclude": \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is finitely generated for every \(d\ne0\), from the two-isogeny descent and the descent theorem with heights, so that a point of infinite order and \(\#\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)=8\) give every Selmer class. docs/research/cr3-map-literature.md §8, N2, Lean cost (iii): a height descent for \(C_d\) over \(\mathbb Q\) of the kind already done for the generator curve. docs/research/pc-audit.md record RK9: the counting argument that uses finite generation.

    The two curves of a slot choice

    docs/research/rv-cr3-dl.md §1.1: pole completeness applies the rank hypothesis at exactly two curves, \(C_{d_1}\) and \(C_{d_1|\theta|}=C_{d_2}\) of a slot choice. Quantifying over every triple \(\beta,\theta,p_0\) that satisfies the inputs makes the hypothesis stronger than needed. The existential slot choice makes it weaker, which is legitimate because pole solubility holds for any slot choice. The form over admissible inputs quantifies only over admissible \(B\). §1.2: the inputs can hold (\(\beta=-7\), \(\theta=1\), \(p_0=7\), where a slot choice gives \(d_1=7q_0\) for a prime \(q_0\equiv3\pmod8\)), slot data exist whenever they hold, and no slot choice is degenerate (\(d_1\ge21\), and both 2-Selmer groups have order \(8\)). docs/research/cr3-map-needs.md §1 (L4) and §2 (W3): the proof of pole completeness applies CR₃ only at these two curves, and W3 is the drop-in narrowing of CR₃ that keeps the construction of the descent.     The infinitely-many-points forms

    A project-authored restatement, not taken from the manuscript or from any published source: KERNEL_PLAN.md, the paragraph beginning "These hypotheses all conclude", records that the four positive-rank hypotheses CR₃^Rk, CR₃^TYZ,Rk, CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk and CR₃^DL,Rk are restated with the conclusion that \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions, so that the hypotheses can be read without the group law, and that each form is equivalent to its positive-rank form through Mordell–Weil for \(C_d\) (see Mordell-Weil for the congruent number curves above). For squarefree \(d>0\) this conclusion is the classical statement that \(d\) is a congruent number; that reading is standard background and no proof here uses it.

    The L-series of an elliptic curve

    For a Weierstrass equation \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q\), \(L(E,s)\) is the Euler product over the primes \(p\) of \(f_p(p^{-s})^{-1}\), where \(f_p\) is the local polynomial of a minimal model over \(\mathbb Q_p\), with Silverman's factors: \(1-a_pT+pT^2\) with \(a_p=p+1-\#\tilde E(\mathbb F_p)\) for good reduction, \(1-T\) for split and \(1+T\) for nonsplit multiplicative reduction, and \(1\) for additive reduction. docs/research/wk-cr3.md §1.2 (item "The L-function") and §3 (paragraph "The L-series definition": for odd squarefree \(d\) the model \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has \(\Delta=64d^6\) and \(c_4=48d^2\), is minimal at every prime, has good reduction off \(2d\) and additive reduction at \(2\) and at \(p\mid d\); PARI confirms conductor \(32d^2\), root number \(-1\), minimality and the local data on 20 squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) below 4000). docs/research/rv-lit.md §2 (the recipe checked by reading for every residue characteristic, including split and nonsplit multiplicative reduction at \(2\) and \(3\), and, for a transcription of that recipe (the defined series itself cannot be computed), against PARI's coefficients up to \(n=300\) on 29 curves, non-minimal models among them, with no mismatch). docs/research/wk-cr3.md §3 (residual risk) records that no proved statement linked the defined series to point counts, and that a mismatch would make the literature statements false, not vacuous; for \(C_d\) with odd squarefree \(d\) the link is now proved (see the L-series of the congruent number curves below), and for other curves it is not. The normalization is arithmetic, with centre \(s=1\); the series converges absolutely for \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\) by Hasse's bound (STANDARD; wk-cr3 §3, rv-lit §1.1).

    Entire continuation

    docs/research/wk-cr3.md §1.2 (item "Analytic continuation"): the order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is taken of an entire function that agrees with the series on \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\), and such a function is unique. docs/research/rv-lit.md §1.1: the half-plane is \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\), not \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), because on \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\) the series converges absolutely (Hasse), while on \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) it need not converge and no entire function need agree with it; an entire continuation exists for every elliptic curve over \(\mathbb Q\), since \(E\) is modular (Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor) and the L-function of a weight-2 newform is entire (Hecke), and for \(C_d\) by Deuring and Hecke (STANDARD), so the statements that quantify over all entire continuations are not vacuous; and since a continuation is not identically zero, its order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is finite.

    Minimal equations over a discrete valuation ring

    Over a discrete valuation ring with valuation \(v\): a change of variables \(x=u^2x'+r\), \(y=u^3y'+su^2x'+t\) multiplies \(\Delta\) by \(u^{-12}\) and \(c_4\) by \(u^{-4}\); an equation with coefficients in the ring and \(v(\Delta)<12\) is minimal; two minimal equations differ by a change of variables with \(u\) a unit and \(r,s,t\) in the ring; and for a minimal equation the reduction is good if \(v(\Delta)=0\), multiplicative if \(v(\Delta)>0\) and \(v(c_4)=0\), and additive if \(v(\Delta)>0\) and \(v(c_4)>0\). J. H. Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, 2nd ed., Springer (2009), §III.1 (changes of variables, Table 3.1), §VII.1 (minimal equations: the criterion \(v(\Delta)<12\) and the uniqueness up to a change of variables with \(u\) a unit) and Proposition VII.5.1 (the reduction types). docs/research/rv-lit.md §2 (an AI model review) cites Proposition VII.5.1 and checks by reading that the local polynomial used for \(L(E,s)\) takes the minimal equation over the completion, with these reduction types and Silverman's factors; docs/research/wk-cr3.md (an AI-authored project note) §1.2 (item "The L-function") records the same check. The section numbers other than VII.5.1 are not cited by any project note and were not checked against the book. That the local polynomial at a place of good reduction, or of additive reduction with nonzero discriminant, can be read off any minimal equation, not only a chosen one, is proved here from minimality: for nonzero discriminant, a change of variables between two minimal equations has \(v(u)=0\) (the unit half of Silverman's uniqueness statement). A change of variables, and an isomorphism of fields applied to the coefficients, preserve the number of points over a field (STANDARD).

    The L-series of the congruent number curves

    For odd squarefree \(d\), the equation \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has \(\Delta=64d^6=(2d)^6\) and \(c_4=48d^2\); it is minimal at every prime, since \(p^{12}\nmid(2d)^6\); it has good reduction at the primes \(p\nmid2d\) and additive reduction at \(2\) and at the primes \(p\mid d\). So the local factor of \(L(C_d,s)\) is \(1-a_pT+pT^2\) with \(a_p=p+1-\#C_d(\mathbb F_p)=p-\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2:y^2=x^3-d^2x\}\) at the primes \(p\nmid2d\), and \(1\) at the primes \(p\mid2d\), where the coefficient \(a_p\) is \(0\); the coefficients are multiplicative, and \(L(C_d,s)\) is the Dirichlet series of the Euler product over the primes \(p\nmid2d\). The formula \(a_p=p-\#\{(x,y)\}\) holds only at the primes \(p\nmid2d\); the minimality and the additive reduction are stated for odd squarefree \(d\), while good reduction at \(p\nmid2d\) holds for every \(d\). The completion of \(\mathbb Q\) at \(p\) is \(\mathbb Q_p\), with valuation ring \(\mathbb Z_p\), uniformizer \(p\) and residue field \(\mathbb F_p\) (STANDARD).

    docs/research/wk-cr3.md (an AI-authored project note) §4.2 ("Medium investment"): the plan of this computation, namely that the model is minimal at every prime with good reduction for \(p\nmid2d\) and additive reduction for \(p\mid2d\), and that the coefficient at \(p\) is \(p-\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2: y^2=x^3-d^2x\}\) for \(p\nmid2d\) and \(0\) for \(p\mid2d\), to remove the risk that the definition of \(L(E,s)\) used here differs from the textbook one. Its third item (\(a_p=0\) for \(p\equiv3\pmod4\)) and its optional Hasse's bound for \(C_d\) are not proved here. The checks of the same facts by reading and by PARI (wk-cr3 §3, rv-lit §2) are recorded under the L-series of an elliptic curve; the residual risk named in wk-cr3 §3, that no lemma connects the definition to point counts, is what this computation removes, for odd squarefree \(d\).

    N. Koblitz, Introduction to Elliptic Curves and Modular Forms, 2nd ed., Graduate Texts in Mathematics 97, Springer (1993), Chapter II: the zeta function of \(y^2=x^3-n^2x\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) and its Hasse–Weil L-function. docs/research/cr3-trust.md item D1 cites Chapter II of this book for the conductor \(32d^2\) of \(C_d\) (odd squarefree \(d\)), as an unverified locator; no project note cites it for the zeta function or the point counts of \(C_d\), and the chapter was not checked here.

    Hecke and Deuring

    \(C_d\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb Z[i]\), so \(L(C_d,s)\) is the L-function of a Hecke character of \(\mathbb Q(i)\) (Deuring; Hecke), which is entire and satisfies a functional equation (STANDARD; locators not checked; the primary-source locator audit docs/research/locators.md, AI agents with web access, 2026-11-12, did not read an original source for it: its row for the functional equation is UNVERIFIED as a complete original-supplier audit). docs/research/wk-cr3.md §1.2 (item "Analytic continuation") and §4.3 (proving this here would need Hecke L-functions of \(\mathbb Q(i)\) and Deuring's identification of \(L(C_d,s)\); out of reach); docs/research/rv-lit.md §1.1 (existence of the continuation for \(C_d\)) and the row for Hecke's continuation in the table of §1.2 (true, STANDARD).

    The functional equation and the root number

    With \(\Lambda(s)=N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(C_d,s)\), the functional equation is \(\Lambda(2-s)=w\Lambda(s)\), with conductor \(N=32d^2\) for odd squarefree \(d\) and root number \(w=-1\) for \(d\equiv5,6,7\pmod8\): Deuring and Hecke for the functional equation, and B. J. Birch and N. M. Stephens, Topology 5 (1966), for the sign, cited as docs/research/cr3-trust.md B9 cites it (title and pages not checked here). The conductor \(32d^2\) for odd squarefree \(d\) is standard (docs/research/cr3-trust.md §1, D1, cites Koblitz, GTM 97, Ch. II; locator not checked); it is checked with PARI for 20 squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) below 4000 (docs/research/wk-cr3.md §3) and for the first 30 such \(d\), from \(5\) to \(285\) (docs/research/rv-lit.md §3.2). The statement used here is restricted to \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\), on which neither \(s\) nor \(2-s\) is a pole of \(\Gamma\), and is used only at \(s=1\).

    Reading A of Tian-Yuan-Zhang

    Theorem and proposition numbers are those of arXiv:1411.4728v1 (Theorem 3.5 is label m3), the version the project's notes read (docs/research/nt-tyz.md §1.1 and the numbering warning of §3). The primary-source locator audit docs/research/locators.md (AI agents with web access, 2026-11-12; not a human check; rows "TYZRow5b / reading A: arXiv" and "TYZ journal comparison") read the author-hosted typeset journal version (Asian J. Math. 21 (2017), no. 4, 721–774): there Theorem 3.5(2) is on p. 740 and the proof of Proposition 3.4 on pp. 751–753; the arXiv Theorem 1.2 is Theorem 1.4 (pp. 723–724), which keeps the restriction that every factor exceed \(1\), while the journal's Theorem 1.2 is a different result; and neither version's Theorem 3.5(2) settles the partition convention. The journal's proof was not assessed on Proposition 3.4. For \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) the second sum of Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5(2) has terms of two types, \((7,3)\) and \((5,1)\), and the \((5,1)\) terms admit three readings. Reading A admits \(d_1=1\), so the blocks other than the one \(\equiv5\) form any, possibly empty, partition into blocks \(\equiv1\pmod8\); the sum is then \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5a}(n)\), and the theorem says that if \(2^{-\rho(n)}\mathcal L(n)\) is even then \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5a}(n)\) and \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(n)\) are both even (their sums, with \(g(d)=\#2\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d}))\); the vault's \(T^{\rm Cl}\)). The printed Theorem 3.5(2) permits reading A, and their proof of Proposition 3.4 needs it; the printed Theorem 1.2 (journal Theorem 1.4), whose last sentence selects the other readings, is false as written in both versions (for \(n=26565\) and \(33117\), which have rank \(3\) and so \(\mathcal L(n)=0\), the second sum under those readings is odd; nt-tyz §1.4) and is not cited (docs/research/nt-tyz.md §1.3–1.4). Only the case \(\mathcal L(n)=0\), that is, order of vanishing at least two, is needed. The printed hypothesis is a condition on their genus point \(P(n)\), followed by an "i.e." that restates it as "\(2^{-\rho(n)}\mathcal L(n)\) even" and is not proved in the paper; when \(\mathcal L(n)=0\) the condition on \(P(n)\) follows from the first assertion of their Theorem 3.5 and their Lemma 3.18, so the "i.e." is not needed (nt-tyz §2.2, fact F6, and §4.1). Proposition N proves reading A in this form (its first sentence) from unambiguous statements of Tian–Yuan–Zhang (nt-tyz §4); it is unpublished, written for this project, with two model reviews (rv-nt-tyz, rv-prop-n) and no human review. The only printed statement of reading A found is A. Smith, arXiv:1603.08479, Theorem 2.1, with the two sums as separate rows; it is unrefereed.
