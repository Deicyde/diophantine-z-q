    Locators for the row-5b chapter

    The row-5b determinant is not in the manuscript. Its mathematics is written in the project-authored research note docs/research/tyz-5b.md of this repository, a specification written for this project and checked by an automated referee, not a published source. The published works it uses, and the published theorems that the hypotheses CR₃^5b and CR₃^TYZ rest on, are cited at the end.

    Notation

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md, Notation: the bits \(a_{ij}=(p_j/p_i)_+\), \(y_i=(-1/p_i)_+\), \(z_i=(2/p_i)_+\), Smith's matrix \(L\) (Smith's \(A\)), the matrices \(M_1\) and \(M_{5b}\), and the genus sum \(T_{5b}(n)\) of row 5b of Smith's Table 2.

    Lemma 2.1

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §2.1: the forest lemma, \(\det L[U\setminus F,U\setminus W]\) as a sum over the forests rooted at \(F\) with one element of \(W\) in every tree, and its proof (expansion of the diagonal, cancellation of cycles, the unique compatible bijection of a forest). The section states that all determinants are taken over \(\mathbb F_2\), where they equal permanents.

    Lemma 2.2

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §2.1, Consequences and Lemma 2.2: \(\kappa_\rho(B)\) counts spanning arborescences; the column sums \(c_v=y_v(1+y(B))\); equal cofactors when \(y(B)=1\); \(\det L_B[B\setminus j,B\setminus i]=\kappa_j(B)\); and the \(\kappa\)-difference \(\kappa_i+\kappa_j=\sum_\varphi\mathrm{wt}(\varphi)c(T_i(\varphi))\).

    Proposition 2.3

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §2.2: Rédei's formulas \(g(B)=\kappa(B)\) if \(y(B)=1\) and \(g(B)=\sum_\rho z_\rho\kappa_\rho(B)\) if \(y(B)=0\) (rows \(n\bmod4=3,1\) of Smith's Table 1), and the consequences (2.1) and (2.2). The row-5b chapter takes the right-hand sides as the definition of Rédei's \(g\). Their derivation from genus theory is followed, by a different route, in the Rédei chapter.

    Propositions 3.3 and 3.4

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §3.1: the forest forms of \(\det M_1\) and \(\det M_{5b}\) and their partition forms. The formal proofs replace Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 and the involution expansion by a rank-one update and the matrix \(P(c,d)=\begin{pmatrix}D_d&L^{\mathsf T}\\L&D_c\end{pmatrix}\).

    Proposition 3.5

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §3.2: for \(y(S)=0\), the sums of \(\prod g(B)\) over the partitions into class-1 blocks and into class-3 blocks agree (row 1 of Smith's Table 2), by comparing forest sums and counting perfect matchings of trees.

    Theorem 3.6

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §3.3: Theorem 3.6, \(T_{5b}(n)=\det M_{5b}\) for \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) (Smith, arXiv:1603.08479, Theorem 2.2, row 5b of Table 2). The note checks it on random abstract data and on PARI class groups (§6), and docs/research/rv-cr5b-sec3.md §4 checks \(T_{5b}=\det M_{5b}\) against PARI on 30 values of \(n\).

    Lemma 4.1

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §4.1: for odd squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) the Selmer system of docs/research/slot-k.md §2.1 for the class \(c=5\), the torsion classes \((-1,-n)\) and \((n,2)\), the subspace \(H\) (\(b_1=0\), \(a_0=\lambda\cdot u\)) and the isomorphism \(H\cong\ker M_1\), \((a,b)\mapsto(u+v,u)\). The Lean proof works first for any configuration of product class \(5\) and then transfers to \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_n)\) through Proposition 0.

    Lemma 4.2

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §4.2 (recalled from docs/research/pc-audit.md, rule RK6): the determinant of a symmetric corank-one matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) bordered by a vector. The note proves it with the adjugate; the Lean proof argues with kernels instead (module docstring of RouteK/Rank5b/Bordered.lean).

    Theorem 4.3

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §4.2: the rule of row 5b, \(\det M_{5b}=\sum_{p\mid a}y_p\) for a non-torsion Selmer class on a Selmer-minimal curve.

    Corollary 4.4

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §4.2: a first coordinate \(\beta\) with \(|\beta|_{\rm odd}\equiv3\pmod4\) forces \(\det M_{5b}=1\). The Lean statement takes the 2-adic shape of CR₃^Sel, \(|a|\equiv7\pmod8\).

    Row 5b of Smith's table

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §§2–3: the forest lemma, Rédei's formulas in terms of the cofactors \(\kappa_\rho(B)\), and Theorem 3.6, \(T_{5b}(n)=\det M_{5b}\) for \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) (Smith's Theorem 2.2, row 5b, reproved). Theorem 3.6 of the row-5b chapter follows these sections with one change of route: both sides are written as the same forest sum, the determinant through a rank-one update of the matrix \(P(z,y)\) and the genus sum through a marked form glued along the partition. So the proof does not pass through Proposition 3.5 or the partition form of Proposition 3.4, which are proved separately. Rédei's formulas are proved from genus theory in the Rédei chapter.

    The hypothesis in Tian-Yuan-Zhang form

    KERNEL_PLAN.md, the paragraph beginning "That step is done": the hypothesis CR₃^TYZ (for squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) with \(\#\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_n)=8\) and \(T_{5b}(n)\) odd, every Selmer class comes from a rational point) and its trust statement, which names Tian–Yuan–Zhang through Proposition N, Rédei's formula and Gross–Zagier. docs/research/rv-cr5b-sec3.md: a referee report on CR₃^TYZ, covering its definitions (\(\kappa\), \(g\), \(T_{5b}\) and the edge cases, §1), its trust statement (§2) and the proof chain to CR₃^5b (§3), with a numeric red team against PARI on 30 values of \(n\) (§4). The class-number form CR₃^TYZ_Cl is located in the locators of the Rédei chapter.

    Proposition N

    docs/research/nt-tyz.md §4: Proposition N. For squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\), if \(T_{5b}(n)=\sum_{d_0\equiv7,\,d_1\equiv3}g(d_0)g(d_1)\mathcal L_1(n/d_0d_1)\) is odd, then \(\mathcal L(n)\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(C_n,s)=1\) (the note writes \(E_n\) for \(C_n\)). It is proved from Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5 (first assertion), Theorem 3.3 (the \(\varepsilon\)-classes of the recursion), Proposition 3.2(1), Lemmas 3.16, 3.18 and 3.21, Lemma 2.8, Proposition 2.3 and the recursion of their §3.1. §4.5 gives the recommended citation; their Theorem 1.2 must not be cited in its literal form. Proposition N is not refereed; it has two model reviews, docs/research/rv-nt-tyz.md and docs/research/rv-prop-n.md (the second written without the source text of Tian–Yuan–Zhang; verdict: it holds as stated, relative to the inputs as quoted, with presentational corrections recorded at the end of docs/research/nt-tyz.md).

    Validation

    docs/research/tyz-5b.md §6: random checks of the lemmas on abstract data and on actual curves, and the slot curves; §7 records an independent automated referee run.

    Tian-Yuan-Zhang

    Y. Tian, X. Yuan, S.-W. Zhang, Genus periods, genus points and congruent number problem, Asian J. Math. 21 (2017), no. 4, 721–774; arXiv:1411.4728. Theorem 3.5(2) and its proof in §3.3; docs/research/tyz-5b.md §1.2 locates the form used (if \(T_{5b}(n)\) is odd, then \(2^{-\rho(n)}\mathcal L(n)\) is odd) in that proof; the form used holds as completed by Proposition N (see above), not by Proposition 3.4 as written (docs/research/nt-tyz.md §2.3).

    Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin

    The manuscript's Lemma 5.5 (lem:forward-rank): analytic rank one implies rank one, by Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin. The justification of the hypotheses here does not use it: Gross–Zagier over an auxiliary field (below) gives rank at least one, which is all that is needed (docs/research/cr3-trust.md §1, D4).

    Gross-Zagier over an auxiliary field

    B. Gross and D. Zagier, Heegner points and derivatives of L-series, Invent. Math. 84 (1986), 225–320, applied with an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field \(K\) that satisfies the Heegner hypothesis and has \(L(C_n^K,1)\ne0\) (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein). docs/research/cr3-trust.md §1, D2 names the suppliers: Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, Waldspurger, or Friedberg–Hoffstein, not Murty–Murty (the derivative case); their theorem numbers, and whether the level \(32n^2\) is allowed, are still to be checked. The curve \(C_n\) is modular because it has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Only "analytic rank one implies rank at least one" is used; Kolyvagin's theorem is not needed (docs/research/pc-audit.md RK7).

    Smith

    A. Smith, The congruent numbers have positive natural density, arXiv:1603.08479, Table 2 and Theorem 2.2. Only the matrix of row 5b is used; its identity with the genus sum is reproved in the note.

    Matrix-tree theorem

    S. Chaiken, A combinatorial proof of the all minors matrix tree theorem, SIAM J. Algebraic Discrete Methods 3 (1982), no. 3, 319–329, cited by docs/research/tyz-5b.md §2.1 (which gives only the journal and year). Lemma 2.1 is its case over \(\mathbb F_2\); the title, issue and pages were not checked against the journal here.
