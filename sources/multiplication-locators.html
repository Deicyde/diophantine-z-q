    Locators for the multiplication chapter

    Line ranges refer to diophantine_zq.tex in this repository (the 11/5 manuscript, SHA-256 cb1148cd6c7769d2af3f1636b60ff0ada4b40431978d1e98d0c339274b3d792b). TeX labels are given where the item has one. The JavaScript generators under construction/ are project-authored artifacts; they are listed last and serve as cross-checks of the shapes and constants, not as inputs to the proof.

    Multiplication input M

    Section 1 (sec:main), lines 96-97: the input M of Lemma 1.2: the actual guarded multiplication template has rational witnesses exactly for triples with indices \(a,b,ab\). Lines 129-131 (proof of Theorem 1.1) assign M to Section 6, on all rational input codes, including the finite false-triple guard.

    Assembly lemma

    lem:assembly, Lemma 1.2, lines 110-121: assuming G, M, H, PC, C and B, the specified polynomial satisfies Theorem 1.1.

    Least formal multiple

    Section 2.2, lines 238-252: the fixed prime catalogue, \(N_s=(\prod_{p\le324}p)H_{\rm cat}\), the definition of \(M_0\) as the least \(m\ge1\) with \(N_s^4\mid a,b\) and \(\gcd(c,N_s)=1\) for \(1/x([m]P_0)=(a+bs)/c\), and the remark that bounding \(T\) does not by itself exclude false triples.

    Membership and addition

    Section 3.1, lines 351-374: membership in \(2E(K_0)\) is an all-input statement; multiplication first imposes membership of its three roots and is sound on arbitrary rational tuples.

    Contact prime sets

    End of Section 4, lines 967-971: \(S_{\rm ht}=\{p:p\le324\}\) and \(S_{\rm mul}=S_{\rm ht}\cup\mathcal H\cup\operatorname{supp}(c_*)\), with \(\mathcal H\) the fixed catalogue; a common numerical \(R\) may bound both sets.

    Pole predicate

    sec:pole, Section 5, lines 973-987: \(S_0=\{2,3,5,7,13\}\), the eleven witnesses, the multiplier \(m\), \(A=mB\), \(H=A(A-B^2)\), \(T_1\), \(T_2\), \(Q_{\rm pole}\), \(\Phi(B)\); for \(B\ne0\), \(m\) is a signed divisor of \(2730\) and the equations give points \((A,r_1)\in E_e\) and \((Az^2,zr_2)\in E_{eH}\) with \(eHz\ne0\), where \(E_\ell:y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+3\ell)\); total degree \(75\). The blueprint uses the Route K polynomial instead, with the factors \(A+e\) and \(Az^2+eH\) and the curves \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell)\); see the Route K locators.

    One-sided obstruction

    lem:pole, Lemma 5.1, lines 988-991, proof lines 992-1000: if \(B\ne0\) and \(\Phi(B)\), then \(v_p(B)\) is not negative and odd for any \(p\notin S_0\).

    Pole completeness

    hyp:PC, Theorem 5.2, lines 1001-1004. Used here only as the hypothesis of the completeness direction; for the Route K polynomial it is proved from the certified rank hypothesis CR₃.

    Section 6 opening

    sec:mult, Section 6, lines 1123-1129: the template forces membership of its three inputs inside the height certificate; accepted untyped inputs have unique indices; the template has \(35{,}134\) rational witnesses (construction S03, specification S13).

    Local conventions

    Lines 1131-1149: \(z=-x/y\), \(w=1/x\) with \(w(O)=0\), \(w=z^2\) times an integral unit; the formal logarithm converges on \(v(z)>0\) and is an isometry on \(v(z)>e/(p-1)\) (Silverman IV.5.5, IV.6.3-6.4); the relative error estimate \((r-1)v(z)-e\,v_p(r)>0\); \(\log([n]Q)=n\log(Q)\) and \(v(z([n]Q))=v(z(Q))+v(n)\); identification with the reduction kernel of a minimal model (Silverman VII.2.1-2.2).

    Logarithm series

    Lines 1150-1161: the invariant differential \(dx/(2y)=dz/\sqrt{1+4d^2z^4}\), the series \(\log(z)=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom{2j}{j}d^{2j}z^{4j+1}/(4j+1)\), and the estimate: at a contact prime \(p>324\) with \(v(z)\ge1\), \(v(p)=1\), every omitted term relative to \(z\) has valuation at least \(r-1-v_p(r)\ge28\) for \(r\ge29\), so \(L(z)/16575=\log(z)(1+\epsilon_z)\) with \(v(\epsilon_z)\ge28\).

    Twelve tests

    Lines 1162-1176: \(H=[h]P_0\), \(U_i=[z_i]P_0\), \(V_0=H-[c_*]P_0\), \(V_i=U_i-[c_*n_i]P_0\), \(c_*=4M_0\); the truncated logarithm \(L\); the twelve tests (four inverse abscissas, four conjugate counterparts, three differences of \(L\)-ratios, one product test); identity inverse abscissas are zero and all denominators are guarded.

    Slope predicate

    Lines 1177-1192: for a slope \(t\notin\mathcal C\cup\{1\}\), \(R_0=\eta F(t)/(t-1)^8\), \(\eta^2=1\), \(a=1-R_0q^2\ne0\), \(R=\ell_0^2a^2R_0\); the normalized values \(C_{gj}=D_{gj}/(R_0^{k_j}(t-1)^{k_j+1})\) with \(k_j=1\) (\(j\le8\)) and \(25\) (\(j\ge9\)); six groups, \(W_g=\prod_j(1+RC_{gj}^2)\), \(\Theta=\operatorname{Tr}\sum_gW_g^{-1}\), both nonzero, and \(\Phi(R/\Theta^2)\); the pole input is nonzero.

    Local congruence

    Lines 1194-1213: at a positive odd contact prime outside \(S_{\rm mul}\), the factor valuations, the trace and Lemma 5.1 give one common group and place with the first eight tests of valuation at least one and the last four at least \(25\); the formal group, the unit among \(h,h-c_*\), the full-log and truncated ratios, and \(p^{25}\mid n_1n_2-n_3\).

    Height certificate

    Lines 1215-1223: the abscissas \(x_{i0}+x_{i1}s\) (zero at the identity), \(b_ix_{ij}=a_{ij}\) with \(b_i\ne0\), each of the nine quantities a sum of three slopes, the slope predicate on all \(27\) slopes, membership of the three inputs; the multiplication predicate conjoins it with the scalar graphs and the inverse of the guard.

    Finite-box deduction

    prop:box, Proposition 6.1, lines 1225-1229, proof lines 1230-1246: every false triple accepted by the unguarded certificate has \(\max_i|n_i|<T=(48A_{\rm ht})^{13}\); the constants \(12A_{\rm ht}\), \(B_0=4\log3+\tfrac32\log2+2\log(1+\sqrt2)+\tfrac13\log676<10\) and \(2\widehat h(P_0)>1\).

    Finite guard

    Lines 1248-1259: the guard factors \(g_{abc}\) over false triples in \([-T,T]^3\); a false accepted triple makes its own factor zero; at a true triple every factor is positive by injectivity of codes; the sound direction uses the generator and the height theorem but not pole completeness.

    Prime pattern

    prop:fixed-prime-pattern, Proposition 6.2, lines 1267-1297 (statement); its proof, lines 1299-1704, is replaced in this project by the hypothesis 22P.

    Choice of shifts

    Lines 1705-1723: the nine integral homogeneous quantities of a true triple; at most six odd-contact primes per slope; \(h\equiv0\pmod{\operatorname{lcm}(M_0,d_1)}\), \(h\equiv c_*\pmod{d_2}\), compatibility via \(\gcd(d_1,d_2)=4\); a solution larger than \(c_*\); one private group per contact prime; \(U_i=[n_i]H\).

    Fixed local clearing

    Lines 1723-1730: \(S_b=S_{\rm ht}\cup\mathcal H\), \(B_p\), \(C_p=14B_p\), \(D_p=51C_p\), \(|v_p(R_0)|,|v_p(t-1)|\le C_p\), \(v_p(D_{gj})\ge0\) at \(S_b\) from the small formal subgroup of \(M_0\), \(v_p(C_{gj})\ge-D_p\), \(v_p(\ell_0)=E_p=52C_p+v_p(12)+4\).

    Pole input of a completed slope

    Lines 1732-1748: weak approximation for \(q\); \(\eta\) with \(R_0>0\); at \(S_b\), \(v_p(RC_{gj}^2)\ge2E_p-C_p-2D_p>v_p(12)\) and \(v_p(R/\Theta^2)\ge103C_p+8\ge4\); at a selected odd-contact prime \(v_p(R)=1\) and the trace residues; even valuation elsewhere; positivity in both real embeddings and \(R/\Theta^2\to0\) as \(a\to0\).

    Conclusion of the multiplication proof

    Lines 1748-1754: the hypotheses of PC hold, negative and zero indices and coincident points are handled by the group-law branches, and M holds, with completeness for every integer triple \((n_1,n_2,n_1n_2)\) and soundness on arbitrary rational input codes.

    Fixed constants

    Section 12 (sec:size), lines 6272-6283: \(H_{\rm cat}\), \(H_{\max}\), \(N_s\), \(M_0\), \(c_*=4M_0\), \(R=\max(D,H_{\max},c_*,10^{12})\), \(A_{\rm ht}=2\cdot10^{13}\max(1,C_{\rm cm},Q_R^{17})\), \(T=(48A_{\rm ht})^{13}\), and \(\ell_0=12P_s^{2916}H_{\rm cat}^{732}\).

    Least formal multiple exists

    Proof of prop:cutoff, Proposition 12.1, lines 6310-6349: the search defining \(M_0\) succeeds by \(N_s^4m_0\), so \(1\le M_0\le N_s^{10}\).

    Generator height

    Proof of prop:generator, lines 303-325: the duplication bounds on \(C\), the comparison \(h_x(T)\le2\widehat h(T)+\log392/3\), and \(x(2R)=(57-28s)/8\) of height \(\log41\).

    Silverman

    J. H. Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, 2nd ed., cited at lines 1132-1149: IV.1 (the parameters \(z\) and \(w\)), IV.5.5 (the formal logarithm and its coefficients), IV.6.3-6.4 (the logarithm is an isomorphism on \(v(z)>e/(p-1)\)), VII.2.1-2.2 (the reduction kernel and the formal group).

    Multiplication certificate generator

    construction/multiplication-certificate.js (project-authored): the definitions of raw_z, raw_inverse_x, log25, pole, test_group (twelve tests, precisions \(1,\dots,1,25,25,25,25\)), slope (normalizations \(R_0(t-1)^2\) and \(R_0^{25}(t-1)^{26}\), six groups, the trace \(2\sum_g(\omega_g)_0\)) and height_certificate.

    Fixed constant prescription

    construction/fixed-certificate-constants.js (project-authored): the prescription of \(P_0\), \(W\), the catalogue, \(N_s\), \(M\), \(c_*\), \(\ell\), \(D\), \(G_0\), \(E_0\), \(R\), \(Q\), \(B_{\rm cm}\), \(C_{\rm cm}\), \(A_{\rm height}\) and \(T\), never evaluated.

    Six-contact finite checks

    construction/verify-six-contact.js (project-authored): among other finite identities, the clearing inequalities \(2E_p-C_p-2D_p>v_p(12)\) and \(2E_p-C_p-2v_p(12)\ge4\) for \(B_p\in\{1,4\}\) and \(v_p(12)\in\{0,1,2\}\).
