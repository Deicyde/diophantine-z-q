    Locators for the natural compiler chapter

    Line ranges refer to diophantine_zq.tex in this repository (the 11/5 manuscript, SHA-256 cb1148cd6c7769d2af3f1636b60ff0ada4b40431978d1e98d0c339274b3d792b). TeX labels are given where the item has one. Section 10 has the label sec:compiler; its subsections have none. The construction notes and generators in construction/ are project-authored specifications; they are listed after the manuscript items and serve as cross-checks, not as inputs to the proof. The sources S21, S22, S23 and the universal interpreter source named in Appendix B are not part of this repository.

    Section 1 input C

    Section 1 (sec:main), lines 102-104: input \(\mathsf C\) of Lemma 1.2 (lem:assembly): the actual five-input natural polynomial and its rational translation have the exact computation and substitution semantics of Section 10, including the power template. Lines 130-132 assign \(\mathsf C\) to Section 10.

    Section 4 height constants

    Section 4 (sec:height), lines 377-399: \(\mathcal C=\{0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60\}\), \(F(u)=u(u+15)(u+20)(u+24)(u+30)(u+60)\), the constants \(F_0,D,G_0,E_0,Q_R,B_{\rm cm},C_{\rm cm}\), \(H_6(S;R)\) for \(R\ge\max(D,\max S,10^{12})\), and the convention \(H_6(S)=H_6(S;\max(D,\max S,10^{12}))\).

    Section 4 height prime set

    Section 4, lines 968-971: \(S_{\rm ht}=\{p:p\le324\}\) is the prime set of the final value argument.

    Section 10 signed encoding

    Section 10 (sec:compiler), lines 5745-5750: \(\operatorname{enc}(n)=2n\) for \(n\ge0\) and \(-2n-1\) for \(n<0\); the five natural inputs decode in the order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\).

    Section 10 decision predicate

    Section 10, lines 5751-5764: rejection unless \(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(a,l\ge1\), \(j=na\); \(z=-x/y\) on nonzero multiples of \(P_0\); \(z([l]P_0)=u_l+v_ls\), \(\epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|\), \(r(a,j)=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(z([j]P_0)/z([a]P_0))\); \(A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}=H_6(S_{\rm ht})\); the strict final tests \(|r(a,j)-n|<\epsilon_l\) and \(4\epsilon_l<4^{-A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}|\kappa F(n)|^{24}}\).

    Section 10 reference program and IR

    Section 10, lines 5766-5775: the reference program S21 (exact quadratic-field arithmetic, capped integer operations), the ideal register IR S22 computing the same predicate on every five-natural tuple, the all-input argument (normalized signed arithmetic, origin and zero-ordinate cases, equal-abscissa addition, early rejections), nine polynomial identities for the projective formulas, termination of the halving, division-by-four and factorial loops.

    Section 10 threshold

    Section 10, lines 5777-5781: for \(\epsilon=U/V>0\) and \(B\ge0\), \(4\epsilon<4^{-B}\iff4^B\le\lfloor(V-1)/(4U)\rfloor\).

    Section 10 saturation

    Section 10, lines 5782-5788: \(\sigma(x)=\min(x,L+1)\) for \(L>0\) commutes with saturated addition and multiplication; powers and factorials with saturation; capping exponents for bases at least two; bases zero and one and exponent zero separately.

    Section 10.1 saved polynomial

    Section 10.1, lines 5790-5795: the saved precision polynomial has \(1\,305\,811\) natural variables, the first five being inputs; its SHA-256.

    Section 10.1 wrapper specialization

    Section 10.1, lines 5796-5805: S23 specializes a universal natural halting polynomial at the code of a wrapper that inverts four Cantor pairs and then runs the precision machine; the pairing equations \(2t_i=(c_i+t_{i+1})(c_i+t_{i+1}+1)+2t_{i+1}\), \(t_4=c_4\); unique unpairing; the new polynomial is the square of the old one plus the four squared differences.

    Section 10.1 packed histories

    Section 10.1, lines 5807-5824: acceptance (unique final halt, all registers zero, cleanup), \(c>0\), \(K=2^c\), \(B=4K>m\), \(T=B^q\), \(F_q=1+B+\dots+B^q\), \(D=(K-1)F_q\), inputs below \(K\), masking, \(R_j\) masked by \(D\) with \(R_j<T\), \(S_i\) masked by \(F_q\), \(\sum_iS_i=F_q\), \(S_{\rm halt}=T\), and their digit consequences.

    Section 10.1 nonzero and branch masks

    Section 10.1, lines 5826-5841: \(KN_j=(R_j+D)\,\mathrm{AND}\,(KF_q)\) with the digit argument \(r+K-1<2K<B\); \(V_i=S_i\,\mathrm{AND}\,N_j\), \(S_i=V_i+W_i\); the indicators \(A_j,C_j,I_i\) with zero-one digits.

    Section 10.1 transition equations

    Section 10.1, lines 5841-5853: \(R_j+BC_j=\mathrm{initial}_j+BR_j+BA_j\), \(S_i=\mathbf 1_{i=0}+BI_i\); coefficients at most \(K<B\); uniqueness of base-\(B\) digits; the extra final slot; the case \(q=0\); completeness for every finite accepting run.

    Section 10.2 alpha recurrence

    Section 10.2, lines 5855-5878: the AFP sources (Exp_Matrices.alpha_equations, alpha_equivalence, Exponential_Relation.exp_repr, exp_dioph); \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\); growth for \(b\ge3\); the matrix \(M\) and \(M^n\); determinant one, coprimality, the quadratic identity; parameter congruence; \(\alpha_2(n)=n\).

    Section 10.2 Pell classification

    Section 10.2, lines 5880-5885: every natural solution of \(x^2+y^2=1+bxy\), \(b>2\), is an unordered adjacent pair, by descent.

    Section 10.2 divisibility

    Section 10.2, lines 5887-5896: \(U_k\mid U_m\iff k\mid m\) for \(k>0\), and equation (C.1): \(U_k^2\mid U_m\iff kU_k\mid m\) for \(m>0\), including \(k=0\).

    Section 10.2 period and reflection

    Section 10.2, lines 5898-5905: \(v=U_{m+1}-U_{m-1}=\operatorname{tr}(M^m)\), \(M^{2m}\equiv-I\), \(U_n\equiv(-1)^l\varepsilon U_j\pmod v\) for \(n=2lm+\varepsilon j\), and \(v>2U_m\) for \(b\ge4\).

    Section 10.2 alpha formula

    Section 10.2, lines 5907-5922: the fifteen conditions (C.2) and the replacement of strict inequality, divisibility and congruence by equations with fresh natural witnesses.

    Section 10.2 alpha soundness

    Section 10.2, lines 5924-5932: from (C.2) to \(X=U_N\).

    Section 10.2 alpha completeness

    Section 10.2, lines 5934-5943: witnesses for \(X=\alpha_b(N)\), including \(N=X=0\).

    Section 10.2 power formula

    Section 10.2, lines 5945-5960: the power formula (C.3) with its two special branches and the proof that it expresses \(P=Q^R\).

    Section 10.2 binomial and bits

    Section 10.2, lines 5962-5975: the binomial digit equation, Lucas's criterion, disjointness and the bitwise AND gadget.

    Section 10.3 numbering

    Section 10.3, lines 5977-5982: the numbering \(e+1=2^w(2D+1)\) and its total decoding.

    Section 10.3 interpreter

    Section 10.3, lines 5984-5997: tape lists \(\operatorname{cons}(h,t)=2^h(2t+1)\), register update, reserved jump register, copies, aliased addition, division, one loop per decoded instruction, cleanup, and equivalence of the all-zero halt with virtual halting.

    Section 10.3 precision IR

    Section 10.3, lines 5999-6016: signed pairs, field pairs, norm zero, guarded divisions, Jacobian formulas and branches, the fixed projective point, the loops and capped arithmetic; the IR terminates and agrees with the predicate on all five natural inputs.

    Section 10.3 lowering

    Section 10.3, lines 6018-6025: primitive lowering with fresh auxiliary registers, saturating decrement, repeated addition, guarded division, loops; acceptance enters cleanup, rejection a permanent self-loop.

    Section 10.3 unpairing and wrapper

    Section 10.3, lines 6027-6034: unpairing by the remainder loop, four repetitions into registers zero to four, fresh cleared scratch, relocation of the precision machine.

    Section 10.3 specialization

    Section 10.3, lines 6036-6050: the specialization (program input fixed to the wrapper code, old packed input to variable five, \(i\mapsto i+7\) for \(i\ge2\) and block starts, pairing variables five to eight), equation (C.4), and the absence of height, restricted-converse or rational-model premises.

    Section 10.4 power blocks

    Section 10.4, lines 6078-6086: the precision parent has \(54056\) explicit variables and \(15845\) disjoint private blocks of \(79\) variables; no parent variable node accesses a private coordinate.

    Section 12 literal constants

    Compiler specification

    Project-authored: construction/integer-compiler-spec.txt, Sections 1-6: circuit and formula conventions (equality as a difference, conjunction as a sum of squares, disjunction as a product, strict inequality, divisibility and congruence gadgets), the alpha and power macros, binomial and bit macros, register-machine semantics, packed histories with the emitted equations \(BF+1=F+BT\) and \(D+F=KF\), soundness and completeness.

    Halting correctness proof

    Project-authored: construction/integer-halting-correctness-proof.txt (S24), Sections 1-6: transcription of the AFP alpha and power systems, binomial and bit macros, the packed-machine theorem for any fixed program, Boolean compilation and hierarchical substitution of the power template with blocks of \(79\) witnesses, correctness of the fixed interpreter (\(1{,}399\) states, \(204\) registers), and the binding to the saved polynomial (\(1{,}305{,}804\) variables, \(15{,}845\) calls, one \(474\)-node power template).

    Compiler source

    Project-authored: construction/integer-diophantine-compiler.js: the classes and functions Builder (methods lt, divides, congruent, alpha, power, binomial, odd, orthogonal, mask, bitAnd, polynomial), validateProgram, compileRegisterMachine and runMachine; construction/verify-integer-compiler.js holds its finite tests.

    Saved precision polynomial

    Project-authored: construction/precision-natural-polynomial.json (cross-check only). Its structure, read with a JSON parser: \(54056\) explicit variables (inputs code_0..code_4 at \(0..4\), tuple_0..tuple_3 at \(5..8\)), \(293620\) nodes (\(10\) constants, \(54056\) variable nodes, \(125327\) additions, \(98382\) multiplications, \(15845\) calls), \(15845\) witness blocks of length \(79\), one power template with \(3\) parameters, \(79\) witnesses and \(474\) nodes; node \(0\) is the program literal (\(3{,}181{,}317\) decimal digits); the first \(293568\) nodes are the universal halting circuit with variable \(0\) replaced by that literal, variable \(1\) by variable \(5\) and every other variable and block start shifted by \(7\); the last \(52\) nodes form the four pairing equations and the final sum of squares. Decoding the literal with the numbering gives \(w=18\) and a primitive program of \(146{,}779\) encoded states plus the implicit final halt, using \(23{,}633\) registers. The universal interpreter program (\(1{,}399\) instructions: \(1{,}022\) decrements, \(376\) increments, the final halt; \(204\) registers) is recovered from the variable names and state equations of the universal part, and recompiling it with compileRegisterMachine in hierarchical mode reproduces those \(293568\) nodes exactly.
