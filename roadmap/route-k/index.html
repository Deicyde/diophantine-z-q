    Pole completeness from certified rank (Route K)

    The manuscript's pole polynomial puts two points on curves \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+3\ell)\), and the manuscript derives pole completeness from its restricted converse theorem, whose proof uses Kato's Euler system, Waldspurger's formula, Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein and Nekovář's Selmer complexes. Route K changes the polynomial to the congruent number curves \(C_\ell:y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell)\); the elementary direction, Lemma 5.1, is unchanged (the one-sided pole obstruction). This chapter proves pole completeness for the new polynomial from one narrow hypothesis on congruent number curves, the certified rank hypothesis CR₃, by an explicit descent: for every admissible input \(B\) it produces two curves \(C_n\) and \(C_{n\theta}\) whose 2-Selmer groups, written as an \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear system in Legendre symbols, have dimension \(3\) and contain the class \(\beta\). CR₃ turns these certificates into rational points, and two such points give a zero of the pole polynomial.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\); it depends only on \(d^2\).

    Configurations and the Selmer system

    An odd squarefree \(d>0\) is recorded by its primes, their classes modulo \(8\) and their Legendre symbols; Proposition 0 writes the 2-Selmer group of \(C_d\) as the solutions of a linear system in these data. The system makes sense for any configuration, realized by a number or not.

    For an odd class \(c\) modulo \(8\) put \(\lambda(c)=[c\equiv3\pmod4]\) and \(\mu(c)=[c\equiv3,5\pmod8]\), as elements of \(\mathbb F_2\).

    A configuration \(\mathcal C\) on a finite set \(S\) of symbols (the "primes" of \(\mathcal C\)) consists of a class \(c_x\in\{1,3,5,7\}\subset\mathbb Z/8\) for every \(x\in S\) and a bit \([y/x]\in\mathbb F_2\) for every pair \(x\ne y\) of elements of \(S\), subject to reciprocity: $\([x/y]+[y/x]=\lambda_x\lambda_y\qquad(x\ne y),\)$ where \(\lambda_x=\lambda(c_x)\) and \(\mu_x=\mu(c_x)\). Bits \([x/x]\) on the diagonal play no role. The product class of \(\mathcal C\) is \(c(\mathcal C)=\prod_{x\in S}c_x\) modulo \(8\); it is again odd. A symbol \(r\) with \(c_r=1\) is a glue prime; for it \(\lambda_r=\mu_r=0\), so reciprocity makes its bits symmetric, \([r/x]=[x/r]\).

    The model is an odd squarefree \(d>0\): its primes with their classes modulo \(8\) and their Legendre symbols form a configuration, by quadratic reciprocity (the configuration of an odd number). Configurations that no number realizes occur only as intermediate steps of the descent.

    Let \(\mathcal C\) be a configuration on \(S\). The Selmer system \(\Sigma(\mathcal C)\) has the unknowns \(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1\in\mathbb F_2\) and \(u,v\in\mathbb F_2^S\). Write \((Ku)_x=\sum_{y\ne x}[y/x](u_y+u_x)\) (Monsky's matrix) and \(\lambda\cdot u=\sum_x\lambda_xu_x\), and likewise \(\mu\cdot u\), \(\lambda\cdot v\). The equations are $\(\begin{aligned} (\mathrm{E1}_x)\quad&(Ku)_x+\lambda_xv_x=a_0\lambda_x+a_1\mu_x&&(x\in S),\\ (\mathrm{E2}_x)\quad&\mu_xu_x+(Kv)_x+\lambda_xv_x=b_0\lambda_x+b_1\mu_x&&(x\in S),\\ (\mathrm R)\quad&a_0=b_0, \end{aligned}\)$ and the two rows (D) for the product class \(c=c(\mathcal C)\):

    \(c\) rows (D)
    \(1\) \(a_1=0\), \(\mu\cdot u=0\), \(\lambda\cdot v=\lambda\cdot u\)
    \(3\) \(a_1=0\), \(b_0=\lambda\cdot v\), \(b_1=\mu\cdot u\)
    \(5\) \(a_1=0\), \(b_1=\mu\cdot u\), \(\lambda\cdot v=\lambda\cdot u\)
    \(7\) \(a_1=0\), \(\mu\cdot u=0\), \(b_0=\lambda\cdot v\)

    Its solutions form a finite set (an \(\mathbb F_2\)-subspace); "dimension \(3\)" means exactly \(8\) solutions. The first coordinate of a solution is \((a_0,a_1,u)\), standing for the class \(a=(-1)^{a_0}2^{a_1}\prod_xx^{u_x}\); the solution stands for the pair of classes \((a,b)\) with \(b=(-1)^{b_0}2^{b_1}\prod_xx^{v_x}\).

    Every solution has \(a_1=0\), and the system depends only on the classes and the off-diagonal bits of \(\mathcal C\).

    For the configuration of an odd squarefree \(d>0\), Proposition 0 (descent-alt §4.1) identifies the solutions of \(\Sigma(\mathcal C_d)\), through \((a,b)\), with the 2-Selmer group of \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\): the rows E1, E2 are the local conditions at the primes of \(d\), (R) the real place and (D) the place \(2\). Proposition 0 is formalized in its own chapter (Proposition 0); the descent of this chapter does not use it, and it connects the system to rational points only through the certified rank hypothesis and its Selmer form.

Then a tuple \((a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1,u,v)\) solves \(\Sigma(\mathcal C)\) if and only if \(u_r=u_y\), \(v_r=v_y\) and its restriction to \(S\setminus\{r\}\) solves \(\Sigma(\mathcal C\setminus r)\).     The same holds for a family: if \(S=S_0\sqcup R\) where every \(r\in R\) is a glue prime with a partner \(y(r)\in S_0\) and \([r/z]=0\) for every \(z\notin\{r,y(r)\}\) (other elements of \(R\) included), then restriction to \(S_0\) is a bijection from the solutions of \(\Sigma(\mathcal C)\) onto those of \(\Sigma(\mathcal C|_{S_0})\), inverted by \(u_r=u_{y(r)}\), \(v_r=v_{y(r)}\).

    Proof sketch. Since \(c_r=1\), \(\lambda_r=\mu_r=0\) and \([z/r]=[r/z]\), so \(\mathrm{E1}_r\) reads \([y/r](u_y+u_r)=u_y+u_r=0\) and \(\mathrm{E2}_r\) reads \(v_y+v_r=0\).
