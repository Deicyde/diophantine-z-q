    The height hypothesis in its \(j\)-form

    The proof of the six-contact estimate passes through the Hauptmodul value $\(J(u)=-57600\,\frac{F(u)^2}{G(u)^6},\qquad F(u)=u(u+15)(u+20)(u+24)(u+30)(u+60),\quad G(u)=7u^2+300u+3600.\)$ For \(j=J(u)\), the manuscript bounds \(h(j)\) through the projective height \(h([z_m])\) of the even theta constants of an abelian surface \(A\) and the Faltings height \(h_F(A)\). Combining three of its bounds, \(h(j)\le40h([z_m])+22\), \(h([z_m])\le10^4\cdot2^{24}\max(1,h_F(A))\) and \(\max(1,h_F(A))\le\max(1,C_{\rm cm},Q_R^{17})M_S(u)^{24}\), gives, for \(S\) and \(R\) as in the six-contact estimate, $\(h(J(u))\le(40\cdot10^4\cdot2^{24}+22)\max(1,C_{\rm cm},Q_R^{17})\,M_S(u)^{24}.\)$ The manuscript does not display this combined bound; it returns from \(J(u)\) to \(u\) by the rational-root theorem. This section proves the return as a different inequality, \(12h(u)\le h(J(u))+450\) for \(u\notin\mathcal C\), from two explicit polynomial identities that bound the cancellation in \(J(u)\); it replaces the manuscript's rational-root step.

    The \(j\)-form of the height hypothesis is the bound on \(h(J(u))\) above at \(R=D\), stated for every \(S\). It is not proved here and it is not a published theorem. It is what the argument of the six-contact estimate gives before its last step, in the form of the trust statement for H: the manuscript's steps, with the integral model and the CM case replaced, the finiteness of a twist proved by an argument from another version of the manuscript, the theta expression for \(j\) given other published sources together with computations, and several steps checked by no referee. That argument supports the \(j\)-form at \(S=S_{\rm mul}\), the set the main theorems use. At \(S=S_{\rm mul}\) the \(j\)-form implies the minimal height hypothesis (the \(j\)-form implies the minimal height hypothesis).

    Notation (\(\mathcal C\), \(M_S\), \(D\), \(E_0\), \(Q_R\), \(C_{\rm cm}\)) is as in the six-contact height estimate, and \(h(a/b)=\log\max(|a|,b)\) for a reduced fraction \(a/b\) with \(b>0\).

    The \(j\)-form

    DefinitionThe \(j\)-form of the height hypothesis#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.JHeight.heightX ZInQ.JHeight.JHeightBound

    Let \(\mathcal C\), \(D\), \(E_0\), \(Q_R=R^{RE_0}\) and \(C_{\rm cm}\) be as in the six-contact height estimate, for a reduced fraction \(a/b\) with \(b>0\) let \(h(a/b)=\log\max(|a|,b)\), and let \(J\) be the Hauptmodul value. For real \(R\) put $\(X(R)=\max\bigl(1,C_{\rm cm},Q_R^{17}\bigr).\)$ For a finite set \(S\) of natural numbers and \(u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C\), \(M_S(u)\) is the product of the primes \(p\notin S\) for which \(v_p(u-c)\) is positive and odd for some \(c\in\mathcal C\) (as in the six-contact estimate, now for any such \(S\)).

    For a finite set \(S\) of natural numbers, the \(j\)-form of the height hypothesis at \(S\) is the assertion that for every \(u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C\), $\(h(J(u))\le(40\cdot10^4\cdot2^{24}+22)\,X(D)\,M_S(u)^{24}.\)$

    The main theorems use it at \(S=S_{\rm mul}\).

    The constant comes from combining three bounds of the manuscript's proof of the six-contact estimate, for \(j=J(u)\), the projective height \(h([z_m])\) of the even theta constants of an abelian surface \(A\) and its Faltings height \(h_F(A)\): \(h(j)\le40h([z_m])+22\), \(h([z_m])\le10^4\cdot2^{24}\max(1,h_F(A))\) and \(\max(1,h_F(A))\le X(R)\,M_S(u)^{24}\), using \(\max(1,h_F(A))\ge1\); the \(j\)-form takes this at \(R=D\). The manuscript does not display the combination. It proves the bound on \(\max(1,h_F(A))\) only for \(S\) containing every prime at most \(324\) and \(R\ge\max(D,\max S,10^{12})\). So the combination gives the \(j\)-form at \(R=D\) only for such \(S\) with \(\max S\le D\), among them \(S=S_{\rm mul}\) (every prime of \(S_{\rm mul}\) is at most \(D\)), the only set the main theorems use.

    The \(j\)-form is not a published theorem; it rests on the steps of the trust statement for H before the last one, some of which replace or re-source the manuscript's steps and some of which no referee has checked. At \(S=S_{\rm mul}\) it implies the minimal height hypothesis (proved here). Here it is a hypothesis of two variants of the main theorem (the main theorem from the \(j\)-form and the L-series statements).

    Back from \(J(u)\) to \(u\)

    Two polynomial identities express \(3a^7\) and \(2^{10}3^85^6\,b^7\) as combinations of the binary forms \(F_h(a,b)\) and \(G_h(a,b)\). They bound the common factor of the numerator and denominator of \(J(a/b)\), and they bound the larger of \(57600F_h(a,b)^2\) and \(G_h(a,b)^6\) below in terms of \(m=\max(|a|,|b|)\): \(m^{12}\le10^{48}\max(57600F_h(a,b)^2,G_h(a,b)^6)\) when \(m>0\). Together these give the height of \(u\) in terms of the height of \(J(u)\).

    TheoremThe cancellation in \(J(u)\) is bounded#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.JHeight.cert_x ZInQ.JHeight.cert_y ZInQ.JHeight.C0 ZInQ.JHeight.dvd_C0

    Let \(F_h\) and \(G_h\) be the binary forms of the Hauptmodul value. For all integers \(a,b\), $\(3a^7=(-200a-4800b)F_h(a,b)+(29a^5+3700a^4b+164400a^3b^2+2880000a^2b^3+17280000ab^4)\,G_h(a,b),\)$ $\(104976000000\,b^7=(-1001a-37650b)F_h(a,b)+(143a^5+20557a^4b+1026600a^3b^2+20691900a^2b^3+133110000ab^4+29160000b^5)\,G_h(a,b),\)$ where \(104976000000=2^{10}3^85^6\). Put $\(C_0=(57600\cdot3^7)(57600\cdot104976000000^7)=57600^2\cdot3^7\cdot(2^{10}3^85^6)^7.\)$ If \(a,b\) are coprime integers and an integer \(c\) divides both \(57600F_h(a,b)^2\) and \(G_h(a,b)^6\), then \(c\mid C_0\).

    Proof sketch. The two identities are polynomial identities, checked by expanding. For all integers \(x,y\), $\(57600(x+y)^7=57600x^2\,(x^5+7x^4y+21x^3y^2+35x^2y^3+35xy^4+21y^5)+y^6\cdot57600(7x+y).\)$ With \(x=AF_h(a,b)\) and \(y=BG_h(a,b)\), the first term is a multiple of \(57600F_h(a,b)^2\) and the second a multiple of \(G_h(a,b)^6\), so \(c\) divides \(57600(AF_h(a,b)+BG_h(a,b))^7\) for all integers \(A,B\). Raising the two identities to the seventh power gives \(c\mid57600\cdot3^7a^{49}\) and \(c\mid57600\cdot104976000000^7b^{49}\). Since \(a,b\) are coprime, so are \(a^{49},b^{49}\), and there are integers \(s,t\) with \(sa^{49}+tb^{49}=1\). Then $\(C_0=(57600\cdot104976000000^7s)(57600\cdot3^7a^{49})+(57600\cdot3^7t)(57600\cdot104976000000^7b^{49}),\)$ a combination of two multiples of \(c\), so \(c\mid C_0\).

    ZInQ.JHeight.abs_monomial_le ZInQ.JHeight.lower_bound

    Let \(F_h\) and \(G_h\) be the binary forms of the Hauptmodul value. If \(a,b,m\) are integers with \(|a|\le m\) and \(|b|\le m\), then \(|a^ib^j|\le m^{i+j}\) for all natural numbers \(i,j\). Let \(a,b,m\) be integers with \(|a|\le m\), \(|b|\le m\), \(m>0\), and \(m=|a|\) or \(m=|b|\). Then $\(m^{12}\le10^{48}\max\bigl(57600F_h(a,b)^2,\ G_h(a,b)^6\bigr).\)$

    Proof sketch. Suppose not. Then \(57600\cdot10^{48}F_h^2<m^{12}\) and \(10^{48}G_h^6<m^{12}\), so taking square and sixth roots, \(240\cdot10^{24}|F_h|<m^6\) and \(10^8|G_h|<m^2\); hence \(240\cdot10^{24}\,m|F_h|<m^7\) and \(10^8\,m^5|G_h|<m^7\). Since \(|a^ib^j|\le m^{i+j}\), each cofactor in the two identities of the cancellation bound is at most the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients times the matching power of \(m\).

    If \(m=|a|\), the first identity and the triangle inequality give $\(3m^7\le5000\,m|F_h|+20328129\,m^5|G_h|<\Bigl(\frac{5000}{240\cdot10^{24}}+\frac{20328129}{10^8}\Bigr)m^7<3m^7,\)$ a contradiction. If \(m=|b|\), the second identity gives $\(104976000000\,m^7\le38651\,m|F_h|+184009200\,m^5|G_h|<\Bigl(\frac{38651}{240\cdot10^{24}}+\frac{184009200}{10^8}\Bigr)m^7,\)$ again a contradiction.

    ZInQ.JHeight.twelve_mul_ratHeight_le

    Let \(\mathcal C\) be as in the six-contact height estimate, for a reduced fraction \(a/b\) with \(b>0\) let \(h(a/b)=\log\max(|a|,b)\), and let \(J\) be the Hauptmodul value. For every \(u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C\), $\(12\,h(u)\le h(J(u))+450.\)$

    This replaces the manuscript's rational-root bound \(h(u)<h(J(u))+74\), a different inequality.

    Proof sketch. Write \(u=a/b\) in lowest terms with \(b>0\), and \(F_h=F_h(a,b)\), \(G_h=G_h(a,b)\). Then \(F(u)=F_h/b^6\) and \(G(u)=G_h/b^2\), and \(G_h>0\) since \(7G_h=(7a+150b)^2+2700b^2\). So \(J(u)=n/d\) with \(n=-57600F_h^2\) and \(d=G_h^6>0\). Writing \(J(u)\) in lowest terms as \(\nu/\delta\) with \(\delta>0\), there is an integer \(c\) with \(n=c\nu\) and \(d=c\delta\); \(c>0\) because \(d,\delta>0\). Then \(c\) divides both \(57600F_h^2\) and \(G_h^6\), so \(c\mid C_0\) by the cancellation bound, and \(c\le C_0\). Hence $\(\max(57600F_h^2,\,G_h^6)=c\,H,\qquad H=\max(|\nu|,\delta)\ge1.\)$ Put \(m=\max(|a|,b)>0\). The lower bound gives $\(m^{12}\le10^{48}cH\le10^{48}C_0H\le10^{139}H.\)$ Since \(h(u)=\log m\) and \(h(J(u))=\log H\), taking logarithms gives \(12h(u)\le139\log10+h(J(u))\). Finally \(e>2.7\) and \(2.7^3>10\) give \(\log10<3\), so \(139\log10<417\le450\).
