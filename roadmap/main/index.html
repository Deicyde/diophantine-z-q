    The main theorem and its hypotheses

    Throughout, \(K_0=\mathbb Q(s)\) with \(s^2=2\), \(d=75-53s\), and \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(K_0\). For a reduced fraction \(a/b\) with \(b>0\), \(h(a/b)=\log\max(|a|,b)\). In the rank hypotheses and the articles that state them, \(d\) instead denotes a positive integer and \(C_d\) the congruent number curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb Q\); the row-5b chapter writes \(n\) and \(C_n\) for these.

    The target is the manuscript's Theorem 1.1, in the exact form frozen by the upstream project.

    TheoremThe main theorem#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.exists_bounded_integer_polynomial_defining_integers_of_sixContactMin_tyzThm35ReadingA_heckeContinuation_functionalEquation5_grossZagier

    Assume the minimal height hypothesis and four statements about L-series of elliptic curves: the Gross–Zagier statement, the functional equation of \(L(C_d,s)\) on the strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\), Hecke's continuation of \(L(C_d,s)\), and Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5(2) in reading A. Then the target statement holds: some integer polynomial in \(292{,}890{,}231{,}905\) variables, of degree below \(2\uparrow\uparrow7\) and with coefficients below \(2\uparrow\uparrow8\), has rational zero projection to its first variable exactly \(\mathbb Z\).

    Here \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb Q\), for a natural number \(d\). For a Weierstrass curve \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q\), \(L(E,s)\) is its L-series, the Dirichlet series whose coefficients are those of the Euler product \(\prod_p f_p(p^{-s})^{-1}\), where \(f_p\) is the local polynomial of a minimal model of \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\); its value at \(s\) is the sum of the series where it converges absolutely, and \(0\) elsewhere. An entire continuation of \(L(E,s)\) is a function \(F:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\), complex differentiable everywhere, with \(F(s)=L(E,s)\) whenever \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\); two entire continuations of the same \(L(E,s)\) are equal (entire continuations). The order of vanishing of an entire function at a point is its order there as an analytic function, a value in \(\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\). \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5a}(d)\) and \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)\) in \(\mathbb Z/2\) are the first genus sum and the genus sum of class numbers of row 5b. The five hypotheses are:

    • the minimal height hypothesis: for every \(u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal C\), \(h(u)\le H_6(D)\,M_{S_{\rm mul}}(u)^{24}\), with the notation of the minimal height hypothesis;
    • the Gross–Zagier statement: for every Weierstrass curve \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q\) with nonzero discriminant and every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(E,s)\) whose order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is exactly \(1\), \(E(\mathbb Q)\) has a point of infinite order;
    • the functional equation on the strip: for every squarefree natural number \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\) and every complex \(s\) with \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\), $\(\Lambda(2-s)=-\Lambda(s),\qquad \Lambda(s)=(32d^2)^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s)\,F(s),\)$ where \((32d^2)^{s/2}=e^{(s/2)\log(32d^2)}\) and \((2\pi)^{-s}=e^{-s\log2\pi}\) (conductor \(32d^2\), root number \(-1\));
    • Hecke's continuation: for every natural number \(d\ne0\), \(L(C_d,s)\) has an entire continuation;
    • Theorem 3.5(2) in reading A: for every squarefree natural number \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) and every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\) whose order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is at least \(2\), \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5a}(d)=0\) and \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)=0\) in \(\mathbb Z/2\).

    None of the five is proved here. The Gross–Zagier statement is a textbook theorem: Gross–Zagier (1986) with an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein 1990, or Friedberg–Hoffstein 1995) and modularity (Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor 2001); Kolyvagin's conclusion that the rank is exactly one is not used. The functional equation and Hecke's continuation are textbook theorems: \(C_d\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb Z[i]\), so \(L(C_d,s)\) is the L-function of a Hecke character of \(\mathbb Q(i)\) (Hecke, Deuring), which is entire, with conductor \(32d^2\) for odd squarefree \(d\) and root number \(-1\) for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) (Birch–Stephens). The functional equation is stated only on the strip, where \(\Gamma(s)\) and \(\Gamma(2-s)\) are both finite, and it is used only at \(s=1\). No original source of Hecke's continuation was read, and the original source of the conductor and the functional equation has not been read in full. The minimal height hypothesis is the one instance of the manuscript's six-contact height estimate (its Theorem 4.1) that the proof uses; the proof of that estimate combines refereed theorems and exact computations with new arguments, the manuscript's and this project's, reviewed only by AI models, and it is not a published theorem.

    Tian–Yuan–Zhang. The last hypothesis is a consequence of Theorem 3.5(2) of Tian, Yuan and Zhang (arXiv:1411.4728v1; Asian J. Math. 21 (2017)) for \(n=d\equiv5\pmod8\), under one reading of their genus sums, reading A: the decompositions of \(n\) are the set partitions of its primes, each counted once, with a trivial factor allowed (reading A). It is needed only when their invariant \(\mathcal L(n)\) is \(0\): they define \(\mathcal L(n)\) from \(L(C_n,1)\) or \(L'(C_n,1)\) and set it to \(0\) exactly when the order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is at least \(2\). Their printed hypothesis is a condition on their genus point \(P(n)\), followed by an "i.e." that restates it as the evenness of \(2^{-\rho(n)}\mathcal L(n)\) and is not proved in the paper; when \(\mathcal L(n)=0\) the condition on \(P(n)\) follows from the first assertion of their Theorem 3.5 and their Lemma 3.18, so the "i.e." is not needed.     The target also holds under other hypotheses. Hecke's continuation and reading A imply Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b (proof below), so the form of the main theorem from the \(j\)-form and the L-series statements with the minimal height hypothesis, row 5b, the functional equation and the Gross–Zagier statement assumes less. The four L-series statements imply the certified rank hypothesis CR₃, so the main theorem from two hypotheses, under the minimal height hypothesis and CR₃, assumes less again; CR₃ is not a statement of the literature. The main theorem from three hypotheses assumes the minimal height hypothesis, CR₃ and the manuscript's twenty-two-prime pattern.

    Proof sketch. Let \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) be squarefree with \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)=1\). Then \(d\ne0\), so Hecke's continuation gives an entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\). If its order of vanishing at \(s=1\) were at least \(2\), reading A would give \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)=0\); so the order is at most \(1\). This is Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b (row 5b from reading A and Hecke's continuation), and the main theorem from the \(j\)-form and the L-series statements, in its form with the minimal height hypothesis, row 5b, the functional equation and the Gross–Zagier statement, gives the target.

    That form is proved as follows. Taking \(s=1\), which lies in the strip, the functional equation gives \(\Lambda(1)=-\Lambda(1)\), so \(\Lambda(1)=0\); since \(\Gamma(1)=1\) and the two powers are nonzero, every entire continuation of \(L(C_d,s)\) vanishes at \(s=1\) (vanishing at the centre). With row 5b the order of vanishing is exactly \(1\) (analytic rank one). The discriminant of \(C_d\) is \(64d^6\ne0\), so the Gross–Zagier statement gives a point of infinite order on \(C_d\). This is the positive-rank class-number hypothesis CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk (its derivation), and the form of the main theorem from the positive-rank forms with the minimal height hypothesis and CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk applies. Its proof passes to CR₃: when \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8\), a point of infinite order puts a rational point in every 2-Selmer class (RK9, with Mordell–Weil for \(C_d\)), so CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk gives CR₃^TYZ_Cl; Rédei's formula turns it into CR₃^TYZ; Theorem 3.6 gives CR₃^5b; Corollary 4.4 gives CR₃^Sel; Proposition 0 gives CR₃. Finally the main theorem from two hypotheses applies to the minimal height hypothesis and CR₃: route AP gives the completeness of the multiplication template, and CR₃ gives pole completeness.
