    Proposition D: the density of good slope primes

    Route AP replaces the twenty-two-prime pattern by long arithmetic progressions of good slope primes. Szemerédi's theorem in the primes provides them once the good slope primes are known to have positive relative upper density in the primes; this chapter proves that density statement, Proposition D of the route AP note in its weak form, following the project's research note on it. No complex multiplication enters: the description of the good slope primes uses elementary character sums only, the main term uses Dirichlet's theorem in residue classes, and the one deep-ish step, a uniform bound for the primes removed at a medium prime \(\ell\), uses a two-dimensional Selberg sieve.

    Notation: \(G\) is the set of good slope primes, \(\chi\) the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_p\), \(\varphi_p(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x^3+ax)\), \(\pi(N)\) the number of primes at most \(N\), and for a set \(S\) of natural numbers \(\theta_S(N)=\sum_{n\le N,\,n\in S}\log n\) and \(\Phi_S(x)=\sum_{n\in S}(\log n)/n^x\).

    Character sums and the description of good primes

    The point counts of the two reductions of \(E\) at a split prime are expressed through \(\varphi_p\), and Jacobsthal's identity writes \(p=c_p^2+e_p^2\) with \(c_p=\varphi_p(1)/2\). For the primes in the class \(p\equiv1\pmod8\), \((p/7)=-1\), being a good slope prime becomes \(\gcd(c_p,p+1)=1\).

    Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(\chi\) the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_p\), and for \(a\in\mathbb F_p\) put \(\varphi_p(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x^3+ax)\). Let \(\#E_a(\mathbb F_p)\) be the number of solutions of \(y^2=x^3+ax\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) plus one (the point at infinity). Then:

    1. \(\#E_a(\mathbb F_p)=p+1+\varphi_p(a)\);
    2. \(\varphi_p(k^2a)=\chi(k)\,\varphi_p(a)\) for \(k\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), and \(\varphi_p(0)=0\);

    Proof sketch. (1) The equation \(y^2=c\) has \(1+\chi(c)\) solutions; sum over \(x\) with \(c=x^3+ax\). (2) Substitute \(x=kX\): \((kX)^3+k^2a\,kX=k^3(X^3+aX)\) and \(\chi(k^3)=\chi(k)\); and \(\varphi_p(0)=\sum\chi(x)^3=\sum\chi(x)=0\).     Let \(p\) be an odd prime. Every \(\varphi_p(a)\) is even, and \(\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}\varphi_p(a)^2=p(p-1)(1+\chi(-1))\). If \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(r\in\mathbb F_p\) is not a square, then $\(\varphi_p(1)^2+\varphi_p(r)^2=4p .\)$

    Proof sketch.     TheoremFour divides the point count of \(y^2=x^3-D^2x\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Density.four_dvd_pointCount ZInQ.Density.four_dvd_pointCount_of_mod_four

    Let \(p\) be an odd prime and \(D\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). Then \(4\) divides the number of points, the point at infinity included, of \(y^2=x^3-D^2x\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).

    The research note states this for \(p\equiv1\pmod4\); the proof needs only that \(p\) is odd.

    Proof sketch. Put \(g(x)=x(x-D)(x+D)\), with three distinct roots. Counting row by row, the point count is \(1+3+2m\) with \(m=\#X\), \(X=\{x\in\mathbb F_p^\times: g(x)\text{ a nonzero square}\}\). The involution \(\tau(x)=-D^2/x\) of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) satisfies \(g(\tau x)=(D^2/x^2)^2g(x)\), so \(\tau(X)=X\), and an involution has \(\#X\equiv\#\mathrm{Fix}(\tau|_X)\pmod 2\). The fixed points are the solutions of \(x^2=-D^2\): none for \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), and \(\pm iD\) for \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), which lie in \(X\) together or not at all because \(g\) is odd and \(\chi(-1)=1\). So \(m\) is even and \(4\mid4+2m\).

    The residue set and the main term

    The congruence conditions modulo \(8\), \(7\) and the small primes \(\ell\equiv1\pmod4\) define a set \(C_L\) of primes of Dirichlet density \(\delta_L\ge3/(8(L-1))\); removing the primes \(T_L\) bad at some \(\ell>L\) leaves good slope primes only.
