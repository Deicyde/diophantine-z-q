    Ambiguous classes and genus characters

    This section is the genus theory of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\), in the notation of the chapter: \(\mathcal O\), \(\sigma\), \(N\), \(\mathrm{Cl}\), the set \(T_n\) with \(t=|T_n|\), the prime ideals \(\mathfrak p_p\) and the residue bits \([a/q]\). The main results assume \(n\) odd and squarefree; each article states its own hypotheses, and some auxiliary facts hold for every \(n\ge1\).

    • For \(n\) odd and squarefree, the ambiguous class map \(\psi:\mathbb F_2^{T_n}\to\mathrm{Cl}\) sends \(e\) to \(\prod_p[\mathfrak p_p]^{e_p}\). By Hilbert 90 for ideals its image is \(\mathrm{Cl}[2]\), and its kernel contains the exponent vector \(e_n\) of \((\sqrt{-n})\), so \(|\mathrm{Cl}[2]|\le2^{t-1}\) for \(n>1\).
    • For each prime \(q\mid n\), the genus character \(\chi_q\) sends the class of a nonzero ideal \(I\) with \(q\nmid N(I)\) to \([N(I)/q]\). It is well defined because norms of elements are squares modulo \(q\), and on the ambiguous classes it is given by the Rédei matrix.
    • Through the slot lemma, Dirichlet's theorem shows that the genus characters take every family of values \((\varepsilon_q)_{q\mid n}\), subject only to \(\sum_q\varepsilon_q=0\) when \(n\equiv3\pmod4\).
