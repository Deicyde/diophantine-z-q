    Rédei's formula for the 4-rank of class groups

    For a set \(B\) of primes of an odd number \(d\), the genus sum \(T_{5b}(d)\) of the row-5b chapter uses Rédei's \(g_d(B)\), a determinant in the residue bits of the primes. Tian, Yuan and Zhang state their criterion for analytic rank one with the genus class numbers \(g(d_B)=|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d_B}))^2|\) instead, where \(d_B=\prod_{p\in B}p\). This chapter proves Rédei's formula, \(g(d_B)\equiv g_d(B)\pmod2\) for every block of a genus sum. So the certified rank hypothesis stated with Tian, Yuan and Zhang's own class numbers is equivalent to the one stated with Rédei's \(g\).

    The proof is genus theory in the ring of integers of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\), \(n\) odd and squarefree, with Dirichlet's theorem on primes in progressions in place of the Hasse–Minkowski theorem:

    • the classes of the ramified primes fill \(\mathrm{Cl}[2]\);
    • the genus characters \(\chi_q([\mathfrak a])=[N\mathfrak a/q]\), one for each prime \(q\mid n\), together take at least \(2^{t-1}\) values;
    • counting then gives the principal genus theorem \(\mathrm{Cl}^2=\bigcap_q\ker\chi_q\), and Rédei's theorem: \(g(n)\) is odd if and only if the kernel of the Rédei matrix is spanned by the exponent vector of \((\sqrt{-n})\);
    • linear algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\) turns that kernel condition into \(g_d(B)=1\).

    Throughout, \(n\ge1\) is an integer and \(K=\mathbb Q(\omega)\) with \(\omega^2=-n\). We write \(\mathcal O\) for its ring of integers, \(\sigma\) for its nontrivial automorphism and \(\mathrm{Cl}\) for the class group of \(\mathcal O\). \(N\) is the absolute norm of elements and ideals, and \([a/p]\in\mathbb F_2\) is the residue bit. The set \(T_n\) of ramified primes, \(t=|T_n|\), and the prime ideals \(\mathfrak p_p\) are defined in the ramified primes.

    On notation: the letter \(n\) is reserved for the field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\), while \(d\) is the odd number whose primes carry the bits \(a_{pq}\), \(y_p\), \(z_p\), and \(g_d(B)\) is Rédei's \(g\) of a set \(B\) of its primes. The row-5b chapter writes \(n\) and \(C_n\) for that number; here, as in the Selmer chapter, the congruent number curve is \(C_d\).

    For a natural number \(n\) let \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\) be the quadratic algebra \(\mathbb Q[\omega]/(\omega^2+n)\). For \(n\ge1\) the number \(-n\) is not a square in \(\mathbb Q\), so \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\) is a field, and a number field. For \(n\ge1\) let \(\mathrm{Cl}\) be the class group of the ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\). The genus class number of \(n\) is $\(g(n)=\bigl|\mathrm{Cl}^2\bigr|,\)$ the order of the subgroup of squares \(\mathrm{Cl}^2=\{c^2:c\in\mathrm{Cl}\}\) of the class group.

    This is the number \(g(n)=\#2\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n}))\) of Tian, Yuan and Zhang, who write the class group additively; it is the order of the subgroup of squares, not of the 2-torsion. A finite commutative group has a subgroup of squares of odd order exactly when that subgroup has no element of order \(2\) (when the subgroup of squares has odd order), so \(g(n)\) is odd exactly when no element of order \(2\) of \(\mathrm{Cl}\) is a square, that is, when \(\mathrm{Cl}[2]\cap\mathrm{Cl}^2=1\).

    DefinitionThe statement of Rédei's formula#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank5b.RedeiFormula

    Rédei's formula is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be odd and squarefree, and let \(B\) be a nonempty set of primes of \(d\) whose product \(d_B=\prod_{p\in B}p\) satisfies \(d_B\equiv7\), \(3\) or \(1\pmod8\). Then $\(g(d_B)\equiv g_d(B)\pmod 2,\)$ where \(g(d_B)\) is the genus class number of \(d_B\), the order of the subgroup of squares of the class group of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d_B})\), and \(g_d(B)\in\mathbb F_2\) is Rédei's \(g\) of \(B\) for the bits \(a_{pq}\), \(y_p\), \(z_p\) of the primes of \(d\): $\(g_d(B)=\sum_{\rho\in B}y_\rho\,\kappa_\rho(B)\ \text{ if }y(B)=1,\qquad g_d(B)=\sum_{\rho\in B}z_\rho\,\kappa_\rho(B)\ \text{ if }y(B)=0.\)$

    The blocks allowed include every block that occurs in the genus sum \(T_{5b}(d)\): the blocks of a set partition are nonempty, and the partitions counted by the genus sum have only blocks with \(d_B\equiv7\), \(3\) or \(1\pmod8\). Blocks with \(d_B\equiv5\pmod8\) never occur. The empty set is excluded because \(g(1)=1\) (the class group of \(\mathbb Q(i)\) is classically trivial), while Rédei's \(g\) of the empty set is \(0\).

    TheoremRédei's formula#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Redei.redeiFormula

    Rédei's formula holds. Let \(d\) be odd and squarefree, and let \(B\) be a nonempty set of primes of \(d\) with \(d_B=\prod_{p\in B}p\equiv7\), \(3\) or \(1\pmod8\). Then $\(g(d_B)\equiv g_d(B)\pmod 2:\)$ the parity of the order of the subgroup of squares of the class group of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d_B})\) is Rédei's \(g\) of \(B\), computed from the residue bits of the primes of \(d\).

    Proof sketch. Rédei's theorem on the 4-rank proves the 4-rank statement: for odd squarefree \(n>1\), \(g(n)\) is odd if and only if the kernel of the Rédei matrix of \(n\) is \(\{0,e_n\}\). From the 4-rank statement, \(g(d_B)\equiv g_d(B)\pmod2\) follows for every odd \(d\) and every nonempty set \(B\) of primes of \(d\). The number \(d_B\) is odd, squarefree and greater than \(1\), and its primes are the elements of \(B\). The Rédei matrix of \(d_B\) is the Laplacian \(L_B\) of the bits of \(d\), bordered by the column \(z_B\) exactly when \(y(B)=0\). Its kernel is \(\{0,e_{d_B}\}\) if and only if the kernel of \((L_B\mid z_B)\) is trivial, and that holds if and only if \(g_d(B)=1\). The argument uses neither the squarefreeness of \(d\) nor the congruence of \(d_B\) modulo \(8\).

    The Rédei matrix

    DefinitionThe Rédei matrix#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Redei.redeiEntry

    Let \(n\) be a natural number and \(T_n\) its set of ramified primes: the primes of \(n\), together with \(2\) unless \(n\equiv3\pmod4\). The Rédei matrix \(R\) of \(n\) is the matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) with rows indexed by the primes \(q\mid n\) and columns by \(T_n\), with entries $\(R_{q2}=[2/q],\qquad R_{qq}=\Bigl[\frac{n/q}{q}\Bigr]\quad(q\ne2),\qquad R_{qp}=[p/q]\quad(p\ne q,\ p\ne2),\)$ where \([a/q]\in\mathbb F_2\) is the residue bit. A vector \(e\in\mathbb F_2^{T_n}\) is killed by \(R\) when \(\sum_{p\in T_n}R_{qp}e_p=0\) for every prime \(q\mid n\).

    For odd squarefree \(n\), \(R\) is the matrix of the genus characters on the ambiguous classes: \(\chi_q(\psi(e))=\sum_{p\in T_n}R_{qp}e_p\) (the genus characters on the ambiguous classes).

    Consequences for the certified rank hypothesis

    By Rédei's formula, the genus sum of class numbers equals the genus sum of Rédei bits, so the class-number form CR₃^TYZ_Cl of the certified rank hypothesis is equivalent to CR₃^TYZ.

    For a natural number \(d\) and a set \(B\) of primes of \(d\), the product \(d_B=\prod_{p\in B}p\) is a positive integer, so its genus class number \(g(d_B)\) is defined.     DefinitionThe certified rank hypothesis with class numbers#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank5b.CertifiedRankTYZCl

    The hypothesis CR₃^TYZ_Cl is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be squarefree with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\). If \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8\) and the genus sum of class numbers \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)\) is \(1\), then for every \((a,b)\in\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) the 2-covering \(D_{a,b}\) of \(C_d\) has a rational point; that is, every 2-Selmer class of \(C_d\) comes from a rational point.

    CR₃^TYZ_Cl is not proved here; it is a hypothesis, stated in Tian, Yuan and Zhang's own terms, with their genus class numbers \(g(d_B)\), so that no Rédei formula enters its justification. It rests on the following:

    • Tian–Yuan–Zhang, Theorem 3.5 and the argument of the proof of their Theorem 3.5(2) (§3.3), as completed in nt-tyz §4 (Proposition N): for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), if the genus sum \(\sum_{d_0\equiv7,\,d_1\equiv3\,(8)}g(d_0)g(d_1)\mathcal L_1(d/d_0d_1)\), which is \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)\) modulo \(2\), is odd, then \(L(C_d,s)\) vanishes to order exactly one at \(s=1\). Here \(\mathcal L_1(m)\) is the sum, over the set partitions of the primes of \(m\) into blocks \(B\) with \(d_B\equiv1\pmod8\), of \(\prod_B g(d_B)\).
    • Gross–Zagier, over an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis with \(L(C_d^K,1)\ne0\): analytic rank one gives \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\).
    • The step from positive rank to every Selmer class: when \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8\) and \(C_d\) has a rational point of infinite order, every 2-Selmer class of \(C_d\) comes from a rational point. This step is proved (positive rank and a Selmer group of order eight give every Selmer class), with Mordell–Weil for the congruent number curves.

    By Rédei's formula, CR₃^TYZ_Cl is equivalent to the hypothesis CR₃^TYZ stated with Rédei's \(g\) (CR₃ with class numbers is CR₃^TYZ).
