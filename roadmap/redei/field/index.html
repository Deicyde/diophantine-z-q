    The field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\) and its primes

    As in the rest of the chapter, \(n\ge1\) is an integer and \(K=\mathbb Q(\omega)\) with \(\omega^2=-n\); \(\mathcal O\) is its ring of integers, \(\sigma\) its nontrivial automorphism and \(N\) the norm of elements and of ideals. This section collects what the genus theory of the next section needs about \(K\):

    • its conjugation and norms;
    • the rational integers in \(\mathcal O\);
    • the splitting of a rational prime into split, inert and ramified primes;
    • the set \(T_n\) of primes of the discriminant and the prime ideals \(\mathfrak p_p\) over them, with \((\omega)=\prod_{p\mid n}\mathfrak p_p\);
    • the identity \(I\cdot\sigma(I)=(N(I))\) for ideals \(I\) of \(\mathcal O\).

    The hypotheses vary from fact to fact: the field, its primes and \(I\cdot\sigma(I)=(N(I))\) need only \(n\ge1\); that the primes of \(n\) ramify needs \(n\) squarefree; the description of \(\mathfrak p_p\) for every \(p\in T_n\) and of \((\omega)\) needs \(n\) odd and squarefree. Ramification is decided by norms of ideals and residue fields, so no integral basis of \(\mathcal O\) is needed.

    Let \(n\ge1\) and let \(K=\mathbb Q(\omega)=\mathbb Q[\omega]/(\omega^2+n)\), so that \(\omega^2=-n\). Then \(K\) has exactly two automorphisms over \(\mathbb Q\), and a nontrivial one exists. Every nontrivial automorphism \(\sigma\) satisfies \(\sigma(\omega)=-\omega\) and is the conjugation $\(\sigma(a+b\omega)=a-b\omega\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb Q).\)$ Hence the nontrivial automorphism \(\sigma\) is unique, \(\sigma\circ\sigma=1\), and every automorphism of \(K\) is \(1\) or \(\sigma\). The automorphisms of \(K\) act on its ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) compatibly with the inclusion \(\mathcal O\subset K\).

    Proof sketch. \(K/\mathbb Q\) is Galois of degree \(2\), so it has two automorphisms, and one of them is not the identity. Let \(\sigma\ne1\). The image \(z=\sigma(\omega)\) is again a root of \(X^2+n\), so it has trace \(0\) and norm \(n\); writing \(z=a+b\omega\), this gives \(a=0\) and \(b^2=1\). If \(b=1\), then \(\sigma\) fixes the generator \(\omega\), so \(\sigma=1\). Hence \(b=-1\), that is, \(\sigma(\omega)=-\omega\), and writing \(x=a+b\omega\) gives \(\sigma(x)=a-b\omega\). So any two nontrivial automorphisms agree, and applying the conjugation twice gives the identity.

    Primes

    A prime of \(\mathcal O\) over \(p\) is split, inert or ramified according to its norm and to whether \(\sigma\) fixes it. The ramified ones lie over the primes of \(T_n\), and for odd squarefree \(n\) every prime of \(T_n\) ramifies.

    Let \(n\ge1\), \(\mathcal O\) the ring of integers of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\) and \(\sigma\) the nontrivial automorphism. An ideal \(P\) of \(\mathcal O\) lies over \(p\) if \(P\cap\mathbb Z=p\mathbb Z\). Let \(p\) be a prime and \(P\) a maximal ideal of \(\mathcal O\) lying over \(p\). Then one of the following holds:

    • split: \(\sigma P\ne P\), \(P\cdot\sigma P=p\mathcal O\) and \(N(P)=p\);
    • inert: \(\sigma P=P\), \(P=p\mathcal O\) and \(N(P)=p^2\);
    • ramified: \(\sigma P=P\), \(P^2=p\mathcal O\) and \(N(P)=p\).

    Along the way, with \(\tau\) any automorphism of \(K\) acting on the ideals of \(\mathcal O\):

    • \(N(m\mathcal O)=m^2\) for every natural number \(m\), and \(m\ne0\) in \(\mathcal O\) for \(m\ne0\);
    • an ideal \(P\) over \(p\) contains \(p\), so \(p\mathcal O\subseteq P\) and \(p\mathcal O\subseteq\tau P\); an integer lies in \(P\) if and only if \(p\) divides it; and for \(p\) prime, \(P\ne0\);
    • \(p\mathbb Z\) is a prime ideal of \(\mathbb Z\), a prime ideal of \(\mathcal O\) containing \(p\) lies over \(p\), and every nonzero prime ideal of \(\mathcal O\) lies over some prime;
    • \(\tau\) fixes every integer and every ideal \(m\mathcal O\), and sends nonzero ideals to nonzero ideals and maximal ideals to maximal ideals;
    • a maximal ideal \(P\) with \(P^k\subseteq\tau P\) for some \(k\) satisfies \(\tau P=P\);
    • if \(I\) divides \(J\) and \(N(I)=N(J)\ne0\), then \(I=J\).

    Proof sketch. Since \(p\mathcal O\subseteq P\), write \(p\mathcal O=PM\). Then \(N(P)N(M)=N(p\mathcal O)=p^2\), and \(N(P)\ne1\) because \(P\ne\mathcal O\).

    • If \(N(P)=p^2\), then \(N(M)=1\), so \(M=\mathcal O\) and \(P=p\mathcal O\). As \(p\mathcal O\subseteq\sigma P\), the maximal ideal \(P\) is contained in \(\sigma P\), so \(\sigma P=P\): inert.
    • If \(N(P)=p\), then \(N(M)=p\) is prime, so \(M\) is a prime ideal; it contains \(p\), so it lies over \(p\). The automorphisms act transitively on the primes over \(p\), so \(M=\tau P\) with \(\tau=1\) or \(\tau=\sigma\).
    • If \(M=P\), then \(P^2=p\mathcal O\subseteq\sigma P\), so \(\sigma P=P\): ramified.
    • If \(M=\sigma P\), the case \(\sigma P=P\) is ramified and the case \(\sigma P\ne P\) is split.

    Only norms of ideals and the transitivity of the Galois action are used, not ramification indices.

    Let \(n\) be odd and squarefree and \(p\in T_n\). Then \(\mathfrak p_p\) is maximal, \(\mathfrak p_p^2=p\mathcal O\) and \(N(\mathfrak p_p)=p\). Moreover \(\mathfrak p_p\) is the only prime ideal of \(\mathcal O\) lying over \(p\), and every automorphism of \(K\) fixes \(\mathfrak p_p\).

    These rest on the following, for \(n\ge1\):

    • if \(n\) is squarefree and \(p\) is a prime dividing \(n\), every maximal ideal \(P\) over \(p\) has \(P^2=p\mathcal O\);
    • if \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), every maximal ideal \(P\) over \(2\) has \(P^2=2\mathcal O\);
    • if \(P\) is a maximal ideal over a prime \(p\) with \(P^2\ne p\mathcal O\), then for \(x\in\mathcal O\), \(x^2\in p\mathcal O\) implies \(x\in p\mathcal O\).

    Proof sketch. For the last item, \(P\) is split or inert. If it is split, \(x\) lies in the prime ideals \(P\) and \(\sigma P\), which both contain \(p\mathcal O\); they are distinct maximal ideals, so coprime, and \(x\in P\cap\sigma P=P\cdot\sigma P=p\mathcal O\). If it is inert, \(p\mathcal O=P\) is prime.

    • For \(p\mid n\): \(\omega^2=-n\in p\mathcal O\). If \(P^2\ne p\mathcal O\), then \(\omega=py\), and taking norms gives \(n=p^2N(y)\), contradicting squarefreeness.

So \(\mathfrak p_p^2=p\mathcal O\), and then \(N(\mathfrak p_p)=p\). A prime ideal \(Q\) over \(p\) contains \(p\mathcal O=\mathfrak p_p^2\), so it contains the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak p_p\) and equals it. For an automorphism \(\tau\), \(\mathfrak p_p^2=p\mathcal O\subseteq\tau\mathfrak p_p\), so \(\tau\mathfrak p_p=\mathfrak p_p\).

    Let \(n\ge1\), \(\mathcal O\) the ring of integers of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})\) and \(\sigma\) the nontrivial automorphism. Every ideal \(I\) of \(\mathcal O\) satisfies $\(I\cdot\sigma(I)=\bigl(N(I)\bigr),\)$ the principal ideal generated by the natural number \(N(I)\). Consequently \(N(\tau I)=N(I)\) for every automorphism \(\tau\) of \(K\) and every ideal \(I\).

    Proof sketch. Induction on the factorization of \(I\) into prime ideals. For \(I=0\) both sides are \(0\), and for \(I=\mathcal O\) both are \(\mathcal O\). For a nonzero prime \(P\), lying over a prime \(p\), the trichotomy gives \(P\cdot\sigma P=p\mathcal O=(N(P))\) when \(P\) is split or ramified, and \(P\cdot\sigma P=P^2=p^2\mathcal O=(N(P))\) when it is inert. Both sides are multiplicative in \(I\). For the norms, the case \(\tau=1\) is clear, and \(N(I)=0\) means \(I=0=\tau I\). Otherwise \(\tau=\sigma\) and \(N(I)N(\sigma I)=N(N(I)\mathcal O)=N(I)^2\), and \(N(I)\ne0\) can be cancelled.
