    Formulas, gadgets and witness scope

    Templates are generated from formulas: equality is a difference, conjunction a sum of squares, disjunction a product, and rational constants are fresh variables tied by global equations. The node encoding of a constructed template denotes the compiled formula, and every construction keeps its private blocks apart.

    Formulas. A formula over \(\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in\mathbb N]\) is built from atoms \(a\doteq b\) (\(a,b\) polynomials), \(\langle f\rangle\) (\(f\) a polynomial) and call atoms \(\langle\tau[g;w]\rangle\) (\(\tau\) an existential polynomial with \(p\) parameters and \(m\) witnesses, \(g\) a \(p\)-tuple of polynomials, \(w\in\mathbb N\) the witness start) by the connectives \(\bigwedge(\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_k)\) and \(\bigvee(\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_k)\), \(k\ge0\). A formula holds at \(x:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\) when its atoms are read as \(a(x)=b(x)\), \(f(x)=0\) and \(\tau[g;w](x)=0\) and the connectives as conjunction and disjunction (the empty conjunction is true, the empty disjunction false). Its compilation \(\kappa\) is $\(\kappa(a\doteq b)=a-b,\quad \kappa(\langle f\rangle)=f,\quad \kappa(\langle\tau[g;w]\rangle)=\tau[g;w],\quad \kappa\Bigl(\bigwedge_i\varphi_i\Bigr)=\sum_i\kappa(\varphi_i)^2,\quad \kappa\Bigl(\bigvee_i\varphi_i\Bigr)=\prod_i\kappa(\varphi_i),\)$ with empty sum \(0\) and empty product \(1\). A conjunction nested inside a conjunction is squared twice; no flattening takes place. The call atom \(\langle\tau[g;w]\rangle\) owns the block \([w,w+m)\).

    Template constructions. A construction of a template with \(p\) parameters keeps a counter \(c\), initially \(p\), and a list of global equations, initially empty; the parameters are \(X_0,\dots,X_{p-1}\). It may:

    1. take a fresh variable \(X_c\) and increase \(c\) by one;
    2. call an existential polynomial \(\tau\) with \(m\) witnesses on parameter polynomials \(g\): this takes the block \(X_c,\dots,X_{c+m-1}\), increases \(c\) by \(m\) and yields the call atom \(\langle\tau[g;c]\rangle\) (a block of length zero takes no variable);
    3. request a rational constant \(u/q\) (the pair \((u,q)\) normalized only by the sign of \(q\)): an integer constant (\(q=1\)) is the polynomial \(u\); otherwise the first request takes a fresh variable \(X_c\) and records the global equation \(q\,X_c\doteq u\), and later requests of the same normalized pair return the same variable.

    If the construction ends with formula \(\varphi\), recorded global equations \(E_1,\dots,E_r\) (in order of creation) and counter \(v\), the template has \(p\) parameters, \(v-p\) witnesses and polynomial $\(\kappa\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge(E_1,\dots,E_r,\varphi)\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^r\kappa(E_i)^2+\kappa(\varphi)^2 .\)$ In particular a template without rational constants has polynomial \(\kappa(\varphi)^2\).

    Gadgets. \(\mathrm{NZ}(a)\) takes a fresh \(w\) and is the atom \(a\cdot w\doteq1\). \(\mathrm{POS}(a)\) takes fresh \(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\) and is the atom \(a\,(z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2+z_4^2)\doteq1\).

    This is a definition. Every later template article describes its construction by listing its variables in allocation order and its formula; the order of the constructor's steps is the order in which the allocations occur.

    ZInQ.Syntax.Build.Allocates ZInQ.Syntax.Build.Allocates.bind ZInQ.Syntax.Build.template_construction

    Call a construction allocating if, run from any counter and list of global equations, it never decreases the counter and only appends global equations \(q\,X_c\doteq u\) whose variables \(c\) are newly allocated (at least the initial and below the final counter), in increasing order. Every fresh variable, call, rational constant and gadget is allocating, and so is every construction composed of them.

    Let an allocating construction with \(p\) parameters end with formula \(\varphi\), global equations \(E_1,\dots,E_r\) and counter \(v\), and let \(\tau\) be its template. Then \(\tau\) has parameter count \(p\) and variable count \(v\), $\([\![\tau]\!]=\kappa\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge(E_1,\dots,E_r,\varphi)\bigr),\)$ the variables \(c_1<\dots<c_r\) of \(E_1,\dots,E_r\) lie in \([p,v)\), and if every variable of \(\kappa(\varphi)\) has index below \(v\), so has every variable of \([\![\tau]\!]\).

    Proof sketch. The polynomial and the counts are the definition of the template. Allocation is closed under sequencing: the counter of the second step starts where the first ended, so the equations appended by the first step lie below every equation appended by the second. A fresh variable or call only advances the counter; a rational constant either returns a constant or a recorded variable, or records the equation for the variable \(X_c\) it allocates. Run from \((p,[\,])\) this gives the ranges of the constant variables, and the polynomial of \(E_i\) has the single variable \(c_i<v\).

    Call an existential polynomial \(\tau\) with \(p\) parameters and \(v\) variables in scope if \([\![\tau]\!]=\kappa(\varphi)\) for a formula \(\varphi\) such that

    1. every call atom of \(\varphi\) with callee witness count \(m\) and witness start \(w\) satisfies \(p\le w\) and \(w+m\le v\), and its callee has all its variables below its variable count;
    2. the blocks \([w,w+m)\) of the call atoms are pairwise disjoint;
    3. no variable of a block occurs in a parameter polynomial of any call atom or in any atom \(a\doteq b\) or \(\langle f\rangle\) of \(\varphi\);
    4. every variable occurring in \(\kappa(\varphi)\) has index below \(v\).

    Then \(\mathrm{Rel}_\tau(a)\) holds exactly when \(\varphi^\ast\) holds at some assignment extending \(a\), and every variable of \([\![\tau]\!]\) has index below \(v\).

    Every template of the library is in scope (with \(\varphi=\bigwedge(E_1,\dots,E_r,\varphi_0)\) for its construction). A composition recipe is in scope when its call arguments lie below a bound \(B\ge p\), its calls start at or above \(B\), and its call blocks are pairwise disjoint; the five composition recipes of the specification satisfy this with \(B=51\) (close), \(11\) (main), \(6\) (value_union, value, integers): their blocks are allocated one after another above the parameters and fresh ranges. The model-evaluation translation of a well-formed natural circuit is in scope. Hence every recipe of the specification is in scope.

    Proof sketch. For a template, the list of call blocks of its construction is computed from the counter and checked to be separated and above \(p\); the parameter polynomials and atoms are checked to use only variables below \(v\) outside the blocks; constant variables are allocated by the counter. A construction may use a call atom twice, so this check is made per template. For a composition recipe the arguments are parameters or fresh-range variables below \(B\) and the blocks lie at or above \(B\). For the translation every reference, generator variable and block is allocated by its counter. The relational form is the private-witness lemma.

    Let \(\tau\) be a template or recipe with \(p\) parameters, \(v\) variables and \(m=v-p\) witnesses, all of whose variables have index below \(v\). Let \(g_0,\dots,g_{p-1}\) be polynomials, \(w\in\mathbb N\) and \(x:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\). Then $\(\tau[g;w]\,(x)=[\![\tau]\!]\,(g_0(x),\dots,g_{p-1}(x),x_w,x_{w+1},\dots,x_{w+m-1}).\)$ Consequently, if none of the variables \(X_w,\dots,X_{w+m-1}\) occurs in any \(g_i\), then for every assignment \(x\) of the remaining variables $\(\bigl(\exists\,y_0,\dots,y_{m-1}\in\mathbb Q:\ \tau[g;w]\,(x[X_{w+j}\mapsto y_j])=0\bigr) \iff\mathrm{Rel}_\tau(g_0(x),\dots,g_{p-1}(x)).\)$

    Proof sketch. Evaluation commutes with the substitution homomorphism: the value of \(\tau[g;w]\) at \(x\) is the value of \([\![\tau]\!]\) at the assignment \(i\mapsto g_i(x)\) for \(i<p\) and \(i\mapsto x_{w+i-p}\) for \(i\ge p\), and only \(i<v\) matters. If the block variables do not occur in the \(g_i\), changing \(x\) on the block leaves every \(g_i(x)\) unchanged, and the block values range over all of \(\mathbb Q^m\); this is the existential statement.

    ZInQ.Syntax.Formula.exists_blocks_iff

    Let \(\varphi\) be a formula whose atoms of the form \(\langle\cdot\rangle\) are calls \(\langle\tau_i[g_i;w_i]\rangle\), \(i=1,\dots,k\), where \(\tau_i\) has \(p_i\) parameters, \(v_i\) variables, \(m_i=v_i-p_i\) witnesses and all its variables below \(v_i\). Put \(B_i=\{w_i,\dots,w_i+m_i-1\}\) and assume:

    • the blocks \(B_1,\dots,B_k\) are pairwise disjoint;
    • no variable of any \(B_i\) occurs in any parameter polynomial \(g_j\) or in any atom \(a\doteq b\) of \(\varphi\).

    Let \(\varphi^\ast(x)\) be the statement obtained from \(\varphi\) by replacing each call atom by \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\tau_i}(g_i(x))\) and each atom \(a\doteq b\) by \(a(x)=b(x)\). Then for every \(x:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\), $\(\bigl(\exists\,y:\textstyle\bigcup_iB_i\to\mathbb Q,\ \ \kappa(\varphi)(x[y])=0\bigr)\iff\varphi^\ast(x),\)$ where \(x[y]\) agrees with \(y\) on \(\bigcup_iB_i\) and with \(x\) elsewhere; both sides depend only on \(x\) outside the blocks. In particular a conjunction of calls with disjoint blocks has a rational zero exactly when every callee relation holds, a product of calls has one exactly when some callee relation holds, and the block of an inactive factor may be given arbitrary values (for instance all zero). Variables occurring in no atom and no block may likewise take arbitrary values.

    Proof sketch. By the compilation theorem the left side says that \(\varphi\) holds at \(x[y]\) for some \(y\). Induct on \(\varphi\), proving the statement for every \(x\). For an atom \(a\doteq b\) the value does not depend on \(y\). For a call atom this is the evaluation of a call. For a conjunction \(\bigwedge\varphi_j\) the blocks used by different conjuncts are disjoint, so witnesses \(y_j\) for the conjuncts glue to one \(y\) that restricts to each \(y_j\) on its own blocks; conversely a common \(y\) restricts to witnesses for each conjunct. For a disjunction, a witness for the true disjunct extends by arbitrary values (the rational domain is nonempty) to the blocks of the other disjuncts. Fixed equations inside an active callee are part of that callee's relation and are not discarded.
