    The finite specification of the polynomial

    The target polynomial \(P_*\) is defined by construction. It is a finite composition of existential polynomial gadgets: a template is a polynomial in parameter and witness variables, a call substitutes parameter polynomials and moves the callee's witnesses into a fresh private block of the caller, a sum of squares expresses conjunction over \(\mathbb Q\), and a product expresses disjunction. A few astronomically large integers and finite polynomials enter through three constructors whose values are never expanded. This chapter defines the expression language and its semantics, proves the semantic lemmas that make the gadgets behave as intended, defines every template, recipe, constant and constructor of the manuscript's specification exactly, and defines \(P_*\) with its witness accounting. Whether each template expresses its intended relation is proved in the chapters that use it; the inventory at the end says where.

    The expression language

    Scalar nodes, templates, calls with witness blocks and recipes denote integer polynomials.

    DefinitionSemantics of templates and recipes#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.Template.denote ZInQ.Syntax.Recipe.denote ZInQ.Syntax.ExPoly.Rel

    Polynomials live in \(\mathbb Z[X_i : i\in\mathbb N]\) and every variable ranges over \(\mathbb Q\). A rational assignment is a map \(x:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\), and \(f(x)\) is the value of \(f\) at it.

    Scalar nodes. A node list \(n_0,\dots,n_{k-1}\) is a finite list in which every node refers only to earlier nodes. A node is one of:

    • \(\mathrm{const}\ c\) with \(c\in\mathbb Z\);
    • \(\mathrm{var}\ i\) with \(i\in\mathbb N\) a local variable;
    • \(\mathrm{add}\ j\ j'\) or \(\mathrm{mul}\ j\ j'\) with \(j,j'\) earlier nodes;
    • \(\mathrm{call}\ \tau\ (a_0,\dots,a_{p-1})\ w\): a call of an earlier template \(\tau\) with \(p\) parameters, parameter nodes \(a_i\) and witness start \(w\in\mathbb N\);
    • \(\mathrm{fixed}\ e\) with \(e\) a fixed integer expression;
    • \(\mathrm{psi}\ e\ (a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)\ c\) with \(e\) a fixed integer expression and \(c\in\{0,1\}\);
    • \(\mathrm{guard}\ e\ (a_1,\dots,a_{15})\) with \(e\) a fixed integer expression and fifteen coordinate nodes in point-major order.

    Templates. A template \(\tau=(p,v,n_0,\dots,n_{k-1},\mathrm{root})\) has parameter count \(p\le v\) and variable count \(v\); its local variables \(0,\dots,p-1\) are parameters and \(p,\dots,v-1\) are its \(m=v-p\) witnesses. Node values are polynomials: \([\![\mathrm{const}\ c]\!]=c\), \([\![\mathrm{var}\ i]\!]=X_i\), \([\![\mathrm{add}\ j\ j']\!]=[\![n_j]\!]+[\![n_{j'}]\!]\), \([\![\mathrm{mul}\ j\ j']\!]=[\![n_j]\!][\![n_{j'}]\!]\), \([\![\mathrm{fixed}\ e]\!]\) is the constant \(\mathrm{val}(e)\), \([\![\mathrm{psi}\ e\ a\ c]\!]=\Psi_{\mathrm{val}(e),c}([\![n_{a_0}]\!],\dots,[\![n_{a_3}]\!])\), \([\![\mathrm{guard}\ e\ a]\!]=G_{\mathrm{val}(e)}([\![n_{a_1}]\!],\dots,[\![n_{a_{15}}]\!])\) and \([\![\mathrm{call}\ \tau'\ a\ w]\!]=\tau'[[\![n_{a_0}]\!],\dots,[\![n_{a_{p'-1}}]\!];w]\). The polynomial of \(\tau\) is \([\![\tau]\!]=[\![n_{\mathrm{root}}]\!]\).

    Calls. For a template or recipe \(\tau\) with \(p\) parameters, polynomials \(g_0,\dots,g_{p-1}\) and \(w\in\mathbb N\), the call \(\tau[g_0,\dots,g_{p-1};w]\) is the image of \([\![\tau]\!]\) under the ring homomorphism with \(X_i\mapsto g_i\) for \(i<p\) and \(X_i\mapsto X_{w+i-p}\) for \(i\ge p\). Thus witness \(p+j\) of the callee becomes the caller's variable \(X_{w+j}\).

    Relations. For \(a\in\mathbb Q^p\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_\tau(a)\) holds when there are \(y_0,\dots,y_{m-1}\in\mathbb Q\) with \([\![\tau]\!]\,(a_0,\dots,a_{p-1},y_0,\dots,y_{m-1})=0\) (variables of index at least \(v\) set to \(0\)).

    Recipes. A recipe \(\rho\) has parameter count \(p\), variable count \(v\) and a list of constraint polynomials \(c_1,\dots,c_k\); its polynomial is \([\![\rho]\!]=\sum_i c_i^2\) for a sum-of-squares recipe or a model-evaluation recipe and \([\![\rho]\!]=\prod_i c_i\) for a product recipe (empty sum \(0\), empty product \(1\)). Calls and relations of recipes are as for templates.

    • A composition recipe lists calls \((\kappa_i,(j_{i,0},\dots,j_{i,p_i-1}),w_i)\), where \(\kappa_i\) is a template or an earlier recipe with \(p_i\) parameters; its constraints are \(c_i=\kappa_i[X_{j_{i,0}},\dots,X_{j_{i,p_i-1}};w_i]\). It also records ranges of fresh variables, which add no constraint.
    • A model-evaluation recipe records generator indices \(g_0,g_1,g_2\), source variable rows \((r,s)\) with a point reference \(r\) and a natural start \(s\) or none, constant definitions \((\sigma,r,s)\) with a scalar template \(\sigma\), operations \((0,a,b,o,s)\), \((1,a,b,o,s)\) and \((2,a,b,c,o,s)\), and a root equality \((r,r')\), together with operation templates \(\nu\) (natural), \(\alpha\) (raw addition), \(\mu\) (multiplication) and possibly a power recipe \(\pi\). Writing \(\mathrm{ref}(r)\) for the five polynomials of a point reference and juxtaposing references for the concatenated coordinate lists, its constraints are, in this order: the three generator constraints \(8X_{g_0}-1641\), \(4X_{g_1}+15465\), \(32X_{g_2}-87483\); \(\nu[\mathrm{ref}(r);s]\) for each row with a natural start \(s\); \(\sigma[\mathrm{ref}(-2)\,\mathrm{ref}(r);s]\) for each constant definition; \(\alpha[\mathrm{ref}(a)\,\mathrm{ref}(b)\,\mathrm{ref}(o);s]\), \(\mu[\mathrm{ref}(a)\,\mathrm{ref}(b)\,\mathrm{ref}(o);s]\) and \(\pi[\mathrm{ref}(a)\,\mathrm{ref}(b)\,\mathrm{ref}(c)\,\mathrm{ref}(o);s]\) for the operations of kinds \(0\), \(1\) and \(2\); and the five differences \(\mathrm{ref}(r)_i-\mathrm{ref}(r')_i\).

    The order of summands does not change the polynomial; the order listed follows the specification's description of model recipes. Source node numbers, source-node point lists, names, metadata and provenance strings carry no constraints.

    Over \(\mathbb Q\) a sum of squares vanishes exactly when every term does, and a product when some factor does. Inverses are equations \(uv=1\), and pairs of rationals represent the arithmetic of \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) faithfully.

    TheoremSums of squares and products over the rationals#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.Formula.ev_compile_eq_zero_iff ZInQ.Syntax.ev_sum_sq_eq_zero_iff ZInQ.Syntax.ev_prod_eq_zero_iff

    For every formula \(\varphi\) and every \(x:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q\), $\(\kappa(\varphi)(x)=0\iff\varphi\text{ holds at }x .\)$ In particular, for polynomials \(f_1,\dots,f_k\), \(\sum_i f_i(x)^2=0\iff f_1(x)=\dots=f_k(x)=0\) and \(\prod_i f_i(x)=0\iff f_i(x)=0\) for some \(i\).

    Proof sketch. Induction on \(\varphi\). For an atom, \(\kappa(a\doteq b)(x)=a(x)-b(x)\). For a conjunction, each square is a nonnegative rational, so a finite sum of squares vanishes exactly when every square vanishes, and \(t^2=0\iff t=0\); the empty sum is \(0\) and the empty conjunction is true. For a disjunction, \(\mathbb Q\) has no zero divisors, so a finite product vanishes exactly when a factor does; the empty product is \(1\ne0\) and the empty disjunction is false.

    ZInQ.Syntax.exists_mul_eq_one_iff ZInQ.Syntax.exists_mul_pairNorm_eq_one_iff

    Inverses are expressed by equations \(uv=1\), never by partial division.

    1. For \(a\in\mathbb Q\): \(a\ne0\iff\exists w\in\mathbb Q,\ aw=1\), and then \(w=1/a\).
    2. For \(a_0,a_1\in\mathbb Q\) and \(s=\sqrt2\): \(a_0+a_1s\ne0\iff a_0^2-2a_1^2\ne0\iff \exists w\in\mathbb Q,\ (a_0^2-2a_1^2)\,w=1\).

    Consequently the gadget \(\mathrm{NZ}(a)\) is satisfiable by a choice of its fresh variable exactly when \(a\ne0\), and \(\mathrm{NZ}(a_0^2-2a_1^2)\) exactly when the pair \((a_0,a_1)\) is not \((0,0)\).

    Proof sketch. Part 1 is the field structure of \(\mathbb Q\). For part 2, if \(a_0^2=2a_1^2\) with \(a_1\ne0\) then \((a_0/a_1)^2=2\), contradicting the irrationality of \(\sqrt2\); so \(a_0^2-2a_1^2=0\) forces \(a_1=0\) and then \(a_0=0\). Conversely \((0,0)\) has norm \(0\). Then apply part 1 to the norm. Since \(1,s\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), \(a_0+a_1s=0\) exactly when \(a_0=a_1=0\).

    The constructor psi_component inserts one coordinate of a normalized division polynomial. The normalized recurrences agree with the classical division polynomials; the classical multiplication-by-\(n\) formulas, stated as a proposition, give the fixed-scalar ordinate equation \(2y([n]P)\psi_n(P)^4=\psi_{2n}(P)\), and with it the meaning of the fixed-scalar templates on \(2E(K_0)\).

    DefinitionNormalized division polynomials and their components#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.normDiv ZInQ.Syntax.divPsi ZInQ.Syntax.psiComponent

    Let \(R\) be a commutative ring and \(A\in R\). The normalized division polynomials \(f_n\in R[x]\), \(n\ge0\), of \(y^2=x^3+Ax\) are defined by $\(f_0=0,\quad f_1=f_2=1,\quad f_3=3x^4+6Ax^2-A^2,\quad f_4=2(x^6+5Ax^4-5A^2x^2-A^3),\)$ and, for larger indices, $\(f_{2m}=f_m\bigl(f_{m+2}f_{m-1}^2-f_{m-2}f_{m+1}^2\bigr)\qquad(m\ge3),\)$ $\(f_{2m+1}=\begin{cases}D_0L-R_m,& m\text{ even},\\ L-D_0R_m,& m\text{ odd},\end{cases}\qquad(m\ge2),\)$ where \(L=f_{m+2}f_m^3\), \(R_m=f_{m-1}f_{m+1}^3\) and \(D_0=16(x^3+Ax)^2\). The recursion terminates because every index on the right is smaller than the index on the left for \(n\ge5\). The division polynomials \(\psi_n\in R[x,y]\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), are $\(\psi_n=f_n\ (n\ge0\text{ odd}),\qquad\psi_n=2y\,f_n\ (n\ge0\text{ even}),\qquad\psi_{-n}=-\psi_n .\)$

    Components. Take \(R=\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1][s]/(s^2-2)\), whose elements are pairs over \(\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1]\) with the pair product \((a,b)(c,d)=(ac+2bd,ad+bc)\), and \(A=-11243+7950s\). For \(n\in\mathbb Z\) and \(c\in\{0,1\}\), the component polynomial \(\Psi_{n,c}\in\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1]\) is the coefficient of \(s^c\) in \(\psi_n(x_0+x_1s,\ y_0+y_1s)\). The constructor \(\mathrm{psi}\ e\ (a_0,\dots,a_3)\ c\) denotes \(\Psi_{\mathrm{val}(e),c}\) evaluated at the polynomials of its four argument nodes.

    The index of every occurring component is a fixed integer expression (\(n\), \(n+1\), \(n-1\) or \(2n\) for a fixed index \(n\) of a scalar template); the polynomials \(\Psi_{n,c}\) for the astronomical index \(c_*\) are never expanded, and every statement about them is proved from the recurrences.

    ZInQ.DivisionPolynomialMultiplication

    This article states, as a proposition, the classical formulas for multiplication by \(n\) on \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(K_0\) (so \(A=-d^2=-11243+7950s\)). The proposition asserts: for every affine point \(P=(x,y)\in E(K_0)\) and every integer \(n\ne0\),

    1. \([n]P=O\) if and only if \(\psi_n(x,y)=0\);
    2. if \([n]P\ne O\), then $\(x([n]P)=x-\frac{\psi_{n+1}(x,y)\,\psi_{n-1}(x,y)}{\psi_n(x,y)^2},\qquad y([n]P)=\frac{\psi_{2n}(x,y)}{2\,\psi_n(x,y)^4}.\)$

    Equivalently \(x([n]P)=\phi_n/\psi_n^2\) and \(y([n]P)=\omega_n/\psi_n^3\) with \(\phi_n=x\psi_n^2-\psi_{n+1}\psi_{n-1}\), \(\omega_n=(\psi_{n+2}\psi_{n-1}^2-\psi_{n-2}\psi_{n+1}^2)/(4y)\) and \(\psi_{2n}=2\psi_n\omega_n\); the second formula is the fixed-scalar ordinate equation \(2y([n]P)\psi_n(P)^4=\psi_{2n}(P)\).

It enters the proof only through the fixed-scalar templates. This article fixes the statement as a proposition; the elliptic-model chapter proves it as a background theorem, and the fixed-scalar semantics uses that theorem. Its formal proof is a substantial project independent of this manuscript.
