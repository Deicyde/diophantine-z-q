    Fixed constants and the guard product

    A fixed integer expression is built from literals, references, arithmetic, powers, maxima, factorials, the prime catalogue and the least formal multiple. Its value is a constant, never a quantified witness, and the astronomical values are never computed.

    DefinitionThe prime catalogue#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.cataloguePrime ZInQ.Syntax.catalogueProduct ZInQ.Syntax.catalogueMax

    Let \(W\ge1\), \(k\ge0\) and \(B\ge0\) be integers. For each residue \(r\) with \(1\le r<W\) and \(\gcd(r,W)=1\), let \(p_{r,1}<p_{r,2}<\dots<p_{r,k}\) be the first \(k\) primes \(p\) with \(p>B\) and \(p\equiv r\pmod W\). Define $\(\mathrm{catalogue\_product}(W,k,B)=\prod_{r}\prod_{j=1}^kp_{r,j},\qquad \mathrm{catalogue\_max}(W,k,B)=\max_{r,j}p_{r,j},\)$ with the maximum of an empty family equal to \(0\). The fixed catalogue of the manuscript is \(W=4\prod_{p\le324}p\), \(k=22\), \(B=324\): the first twenty-two primes above \(324\) in every unit residue class modulo \(W\). Its product is \(H_{\rm cat}\) and its maximum \(H_{\max}\).

    The primes \(p_{r,j}\) exist by Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions. The definition takes \(p_{r,j}\) to be the \(j\)-th element, in increasing order, of the set of primes \(p>B\) with \(p\equiv r\pmod W\); Dirichlet's theorem shows this set is infinite, so the elements listed are the intended primes. Neither \(H_{\rm cat}\) nor \(H_{\max}\) is evaluated; the catalogue bound hypothesis controls \(H_{\max}\).

    DefinitionThe least formal multiple#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.normalizedRep ZInQ.Syntax.xi ZInQ.Syntax.FormalCondition ZInQ.Syntax.leastFormalMultiple

    Every \(u\in K_0\) has a unique normalized representation \(u=(a+bs)/c\) with \(a,b,c\in\mathbb Z\), \(c>0\) and \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\) (for \(u=0\) this is \((0,0,1)\)). For a point \(Q\) of \(E(K_0)\) put \(\xi(Q)=1/x(Q)\) if \(Q\ne O\) and \(x(Q)\ne0\), and \(\xi(Q)=0\) otherwise.

    For an integer \(N\ge1\), the least formal multiple \(\mathrm{LFM}(N)\) is the least \(m\ge1\) such that the normalized representation \((a,b,c)\) of \(\xi([m]P_0)\) satisfies $\(N^4\mid a,\qquad N^4\mid b,\qquad \gcd(c,N)=1 .\)$ If no such \(m\) exists, \(\mathrm{LFM}(N)=0\); this convention only makes the definition total, and the existence of a successful index for the fixed \(N_s\) is proved with the cutoff in Section 12. The fixed constant \(M_0=\mathrm{LFM}(N_s)\) is never evaluated.

    Since \(P_0\) has infinite order, \([m]P_0\) is never \(O\) or a point of order two for \(m\ge1\), so the conventions for \(\xi\) at those points are never used for \(P_0\); they match the specification's rule that the identity has inverse abscissa zero.

    DefinitionFixed integer expressions#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.FixedExpr ZInQ.Syntax.FixedExpr.val

    A fixed integer expression is built from

    • integer literals;
    • references to earlier named fixed constants;
    • \(\mathrm{add}(e_1,e_2)\), \(\mathrm{mul}(e_1,e_2)\), \(\mathrm{pow}(e_1,e_2)\);
    • \(\max(e_1,\dots,e_k)\) with \(k\ge1\);
    • \(\mathrm{factorial}(e)\);
    • \(\mathrm{catalogue\_product}(e_W,e_k,e_B)\) and \(\mathrm{catalogue\_max}(e_W,e_k,e_B)\);
    • \(\mathrm{least\_formal\_multiple}(e_N)\).

    Its value \(\mathrm{val}(e)\in\mathbb Z\) is defined recursively: literals are themselves, a reference is the value of the named constant, \(\mathrm{add}\) and \(\mathrm{mul}\) are \(+\) and \(\times\), \(\mathrm{pow}(e_1,e_2)=\mathrm{val}(e_1)^{\mathrm{val}(e_2)}\) (the specification uses only nonnegative exponents; to make the definition total a negative exponent is replaced by \(0\), so the power is then \(1\)), \(\max\) is the maximum, \(\mathrm{factorial}(e)=\mathrm{val}(e)!\) (with \(n!=1\) for \(n\le0\)), the catalogue operators are the catalogue product and maximum of the prime catalogue with modulus \(\mathrm{val}(e_W)\), count \(\mathrm{val}(e_k)\) and floor \(\mathrm{val}(e_B)\), and \(\mathrm{least\_formal\_multiple}(e_N)\) is the least formal multiple of \(\mathrm{val}(e_N)\). Negative arguments of the last three operators do not occur; they are read through the natural-number truncation.

    These expressions produce constants, not quantified witnesses, and none of the astronomical ones is ever evaluated: every later statement about them is proved symbolically from these defining equations.

    DefinitionThe finite false-triple product#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.guardPoly ZInQ.Syntax.guardPoly_eval_eq_zero_iff

    For an integer \(T\ge0\), a triple \((a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z^3\) is a false triple in the box if \(|a|,|b|,|c|\le T\) and \(ab\ne c\). For such a triple write the fifteen coordinates of \((\iota(a),\iota(b),\iota(c))\), in point-major order, as reduced fractions \(A_j/B_j\) with \(B_j>0\) (\(j=1,\dots,15\)), and put \(D_{abc}=\prod_{j=1}^{15}B_j\). The guard polynomial is $\(G_T(X_1,\dots,X_{15})=\prod_{\substack{(a,b,c)\in[-T,T]^3\\ ab\ne c}}\ \sum_{j=1}^{15}\Bigl(D_{abc}X_j-\frac{D_{abc}}{B_j}A_j\Bigr)^2\ \in\mathbb Z[X_1,\dots,X_{15}],\)$ the product over the finite set of false triples (empty product \(1\)). Each factor has integer coefficients because \(B_j\mid D_{abc}\). The constructor \(\mathrm{guard}\ e\ (a_1,\dots,a_{15})\) denotes \(G_{\mathrm{val}(e)}\) at the polynomials of its argument nodes; the specification uses only \(e=T\).

    The polynomial is never expanded: its arithmetic zero set is described by the guard semantics, and its size by the size chapter.

    The specification describes the coordinates of \(\iota(n)\), \(n>0\), through division polynomials at \(P_0\), namely \(x_n=x(P_0)-\psi_{n+1}(P_0)\psi_{n-1}(P_0)/\psi_n(P_0)^2\) and \(y_n=\psi_{2n}(P_0)/(2\psi_n(P_0)^4)\), with \(\iota(-n)\) obtained by negating the ordinate. These are the coordinates of \([n]P_0\) by the multiplication-by-\(n\) formulas, because \(P_0\) has infinite order; the definition here uses \(\iota\) directly.
