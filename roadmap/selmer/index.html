    Proposition 0: the explicit system is the 2-Selmer group

    The certified rank hypothesis CR₃ of the main theorem is stated on the explicit linear system \(\Sigma(\mathcal C_d)\) of the Route K chapter, built from classes modulo \(8\) and Legendre symbols. Proposition 0 says that for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) the solutions of this system are, in coordinates, the 2-Selmer group of the congruent number curve \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\), defined by the local solubility of its 2-coverings. This chapter proves it, and so replaces CR₃ by the narrower hypothesis CR₃^Sel, stated on the Selmer group itself: when \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has dimension \(3\) and contains a class with \(|a|\equiv7\pmod8\), every Selmer class comes from a rational point.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is \(y^2=x(x-d)(x+d)\), and \(u\equiv v\) means that \(u/v\) is a nonzero square.

    Let \(K\) be a field with \(2\ne0\) and \(d\ne0\) in \(K\).     Local solubility

    At each place the soluble classes are computed: by sign at the real place, by Hensel's lemma and square classes at the finite places. At odd primes not dividing \(d\) every unit pair is soluble, through a residue witness over \(\mathbb F_p\) found with Jacobsthal's bound on cubic character sums (and a kernel check for \(p<29\)); at odd primes dividing \(d\) only the four torsion classes are soluble; at \(2\), for odd \(d\), the torsion classes and their products with \((1,5)\).
