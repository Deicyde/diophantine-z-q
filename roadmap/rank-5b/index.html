    The row-5b determinant

    The certified rank hypothesis CR₃ rests, through Proposition 0, on the Selmer form CR₃^Sel: for squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) with \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_n)|=8\) and a Selmer class with \(|a|\equiv7\pmod8\), every 2-Selmer class of \(C_n\) comes from a rational point. The route to CR₃^Sel used here goes through Tian, Yuan and Zhang (as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed), whose criterion for analytic rank one is a genus sum \(T_{5b}(n)\) of 4-ranks of class groups; row 5b of Smith's Table 2 writes that sum as the determinant of an explicit matrix \(M_{5b}\) in Legendre symbols. This chapter proves the elementary step between the Selmer group and that determinant (tyz-5b §4), and the identity of the determinant with the genus sum (tyz-5b §§2–3). By the first, on a Selmer-minimal curve, \(\det M_{5b}\) is the parity of the number of primes \(\equiv3\pmod4\) dividing the first coordinate of a non-torsion Selmer class. So CR₃^Sel follows from CR₃^5b, the same hypothesis with the 2-adic condition replaced by \(\det M_{5b}=1\); CR₃^5b is a hypothesis, not proved here. By the second, \(T_{5b}(n)=\det M_{5b}\) for squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\), with Rédei's \(g\) in Legendre symbols (Smith's row 5b, proved below by forest expansions of both sides), so CR₃^5b follows from CR₃^TYZ, the same hypothesis with the genus sum \(T_{5b}(n)\) in place of the determinant. By Rédei's formula the genus sum is the same with the class numbers of Tian, Yuan and Zhang; that form of the hypothesis is in the Rédei chapter.

    Throughout, \(C_n\) is the congruent number curve \(y^2=x^3-n^2x\), and the Selmer system and the configuration of \(n\) are those of the Route K chapter; the Selmer group is that of the Proposition 0 chapter. The Rédei chapter and the positive-rank chapter, outside its Mordell–Weil section, write \(d\) and \(C_d\) for the same number and curve.

    Let \(n>0\) be odd and squarefree with prime set \(P\). For \(p\ne q\) in \(P\) put \(a_{pq}=(q/p)_+\in\mathbb F_2\), which is \(0\) if \(q\) is a square modulo \(p\) and \(1\) otherwise, and put \(y_p=(-1/p)_+=[p\equiv3\pmod4]\) and \(z_p=(2/p)_+=[p\equiv\pm3\pmod8]\). Smith's matrix \(L\) (Smith's \(A\)) is the \(P\times P\) matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(L_{pq}=a_{pq}\) for \(p\ne q\) and \(L_{pp}=\sum_{q\ne p}a_{pq}\), so its rows sum to zero. With \(D_z\) the diagonal matrix of the \(z_p\), define the \((P\sqcup P)\)-square matrix \(M_1\) and its bordering \(M_{5b}\): $\(M_1=\begin{pmatrix}L+L^{\mathsf T}&L^{\mathsf T}\\ L&D_z\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{5b}=\begin{pmatrix}L+L^{\mathsf T}&L^{\mathsf T}&0\\ L&D_z&y\\ 0&y^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},\)$ that is, \(M_{5b}\) is \(M_1\) bordered by the column \((0,y)\), the row \((0,y)^{\mathsf T}\) and a zero corner. The same matrices are defined for any configuration, with \([q/p]\), \(\lambda\), \(\mu\) in place of \(a_{pq}\), \(y\), \(z\), and for \(n\) they are those of the configuration of \(n\): \(a_{pq}=[q/p]\), \(y=\lambda\), \(z=\mu\).

    \(M_1\) is symmetric, and by quadratic reciprocity \(a_{pq}+a_{qp}=y_py_q\) for \(p\ne q\), so \(L+L^{\mathsf T}\) has entries \(y_py_q\) off the diagonal and \(0\) on it.

    These are the matrices of row 5b of Smith's Table 2; row 5b says that for \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) the determinant of \(M_{5b}\) is the genus sum \(T_{5b}(n)\) of Tian, Yuan and Zhang (Theorem 3.6 for Rédei's \(g\) in Legendre symbols, and with their class numbers through the agreement of the two genus sums, by Rédei's formula).

    Let \(\mathcal C\) be a configuration on \(S\) of product class \(c(\mathcal C)=5\), and let \(M_1\) be its matrix. For \(e,f\in\mathbb F_2\) let \(\tau_{e,f}\) be the tuple with \(a_0=b_0=e\), \(a_1=0\), \(b_1=f\), \(u=f\mathbf 1\), \(v=e\mathbf 1\) (for a number \(n\), the class of \((-1,-n)^e(n,2)^f\)). Let \(H\) be the set of solutions of \(\Sigma(\mathcal C)\) with \(b_1=0\) and \(a_0=\lambda\cdot u\). Then:

    1. adding \(\tau_{e,f}\) coordinatewise maps solutions to solutions, and every solution is uniquely \(h+\tau_{e,f}\) with \(h\in H\), namely with \((e,f)=(a_0+\lambda\cdot u,\,b_1)\);
    2. the map \(h\mapsto(u+v,\,u)\) is a bijection from \(H\) onto \(\ker M_1\), with inverse \((s,t)\mapsto(u,v)=(t,s+t)\), \(a_0=b_0=\lambda\cdot t\), \(a_1=b_1=0\);
    3. consequently \(\Sigma(\mathcal C)\) has exactly \(4\,|\ker M_1|\) solutions.
