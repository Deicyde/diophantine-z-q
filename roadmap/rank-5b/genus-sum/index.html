    Row 5b of Smith's table

    Row 5b of Smith's Table 2 says that for squarefree \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) the determinant of \(M_{5b}\) is the genus sum \(T_{5b}(n)\) of Tian, Yuan and Zhang (Theorem 3.6). Here Rédei's \(g\) is defined from the bits through the cofactors \(\kappa_\rho(B)\) of the Laplacian of a block, so both sides depend only on the bits \(a_{pq}\), \(y_p\), \(z_p\) of the primes of \(n\). That this \(g\) is the parity of a class number is Rédei's formula; it is not needed in this section.

    Most statements are about bits \(a_{ij}\), \(y_i\), \(z_i\) on an abstract finite set \(V\); for the primes of \(n\), \(V\) is the set of primes, and the odd number is still written \(n\), as in the rest of the chapter. The proof writes both sides of Theorem 3.6 as the same sum over the forests on \(V\). The determinant goes through a rank-one update and the matrix \(P(c,d)\) of the forests section. The genus sum goes block by block, through Lemma 2.2 and the cofactor forms (2.1) and (2.2) of \(g\), glued along the partition. Only reciprocity \(a_{pq}+a_{qp}=y_py_q\) and \(y(V)=0\) are used, and \(y(V)=0\) holds since \(n\) is squarefree and \(n\equiv1\pmod4\). The partition forms of Propositions 3.3–3.5 are proved as well, but the proof of Theorem 3.6 does not pass through them.

    Cofactors as forest counts. For \(\rho\in V\), \(\kappa_\rho(B)=\operatorname{per}L_B[B\setminus\{\rho\},B\setminus\{\rho\}]\). For \(\rho\in B\), \(\kappa_\rho(B)=\sum_f\mathrm{wt}(f)\), the sum over the forests \(f\) on \(B\) rooted at \(\{\rho\}\), that is, the spanning arborescences of \(B\) with root \(\rho\).

    Under reciprocity, the column sums are \(c_v=y_v\,(1+y(B))\) for every \(v\in B\). Moreover:

    1. Under reciprocity, if \(y(B)=1\), then \(\kappa_i(B)=\kappa_j(B)\) for all \(i,j\in B\).
    2. For \(i,j\in B\), \(\operatorname{per}L_B[B\setminus\{i\},B\setminus\{j\}]=\kappa_i(B)\).
    3. (The \(\kappa\)-difference.) For \(i\ne j\) in \(B\), $\(\kappa_i(B)+\kappa_j(B)=\sum_\varphi\mathrm{wt}(\varphi)\sum_{v\in T_i(\varphi)}c_v,\)$ the sum over the forests \(\varphi\) on \(B\) rooted at \(\{i,j\}\).

    Along the way: for \(i\ne j\) in \(B\), \(v\in B\) with \(v\ne j\) and a forest \(\varphi\) on \(B\) rooted at \(\{i,j\}\), the set \(\{j,v\}\) is a transversal of \(\varphi\) if and only if \(v\) lies in the tree of \(i\).

    Proof sketch. Forest counts. The principal minor is a permanent over \(\mathbb F_2\). For \(\rho\in B\), apply the forest lemma with \(U=B\) and \(F=W=\{\rho\}\): a forest rooted at \(\{\rho\}\) has the single tree \(B\), so \(\{\rho\}\) is always a transversal. Column sums. The diagonal entry of column \(v\) is \(\sum_{u\in B,u\ne v}a_{vu}\), so \(c_v=\sum_{u\in B,u\ne v}(a_{vu}+a_{uv})=\sum_{u\in B,u\ne v}y_vy_u=y_v(y(B)+y_v)=y_v(1+y(B))\), using \(y_v^2=y_v\). (2) The forest lemma with \(F=\{i\}\) and \(W=\{j\}\); again \(j\) lies in the single tree. (3) Put \(R=B\setminus\{j\}\), so that \(\kappa_j(B)=\operatorname{per}L_B[R,R]\). Replace row \(i\) by the sum of all rows of \(R\). By linearity in that row the minor changes by the minors with row \(i\) replaced by another row of \(R\), and these vanish because they have two equal rows. The sum of the rows of \(R\) is \(c+(L_B)_{j,\cdot}\), so by linearity \(\kappa_j(B)\) is the minor with row \(i\) replaced by \((L_B)_{j,\cdot}\) plus the minor with row \(i\) replaced by \(c\). Expanding the first along that row and comparing with the expansion of (2) along row \(j\) gives \(\kappa_i(B)\). Expanding the second along the row \(c\) gives \(\sum_{v\in R}c_v\operatorname{per}L_B[B\setminus\{i,j\},B\setminus\{j,v\}]\). By the forest lemma with \(F=\{i,j\}\) and \(W=\{j,v\}\), and the transversal criterion above, each of these minors is the sum of \(\mathrm{wt}(\varphi)\) over the forests \(\varphi\) rooted at \(\{i,j\}\) with \(v\) in the tree of \(i\). Exchanging the sums, and noting that \(j\) is never in the tree of \(i\), gives the right side of (3). (1) If \(y(B)=1\), every \(c_v\) with \(v\in B\) vanishes by the column sums, and (3) gives \(\kappa_i(B)=\kappa_j(B)\).
