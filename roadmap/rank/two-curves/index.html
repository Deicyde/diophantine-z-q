    The rank hypothesis on the two curves of a slot choice

    Pole completeness uses the rank hypothesis at two curves only. For an admissible input \(B\), a multiplier \(m\), a prime \(p_0\mid\beta\) and slot data for \(\beta,\theta,p_0\), these are \(C_{d_1}\) and \(C_{d_1|\theta|}=C_{d_2}\), with \(d_1=|\beta|\,q_0\prod r_p\prod r_\ell\) as in the realization. Both carry Selmer certificates for \(\beta\), so through Proposition 0 their 2-Selmer groups have order \(8\) and contain a class with first coordinate \(\beta\). This section states the positive-rank hypothesis for exactly these curves, CR₃^DL,Rk: for every triple \(\beta,\theta,p_0\) satisfying the hypotheses of the realization, some slot choice gives two curves with rational points of infinite order. CR₃^DL,Rk follows from the positive-rank forms CR₃^Rk and CR₃^TYZ,Rk of the positive-rank chapter, and it implies pole completeness: on a certified curve, a point of infinite order makes every Selmer class come from a rational point, and this gives the points with abscissas in \(\beta\mathbb Q^{*2}\) that the pole polynomial needs. The same holds for CR₃^DL,Adm,Rk, which asks only that for every admissible \(B\) some multiplier, prime \(p_0\) and slot choice give two curves with points of infinite order.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\), as in the rest of the positive-rank chapter.

    Certificates and the 2-Selmer group

    Through Proposition 0, a Selmer certificate for \((d,a)\) says that \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has order \(8\) and contains a class with first coordinate \(a\).

    TheoremThe 2-Selmer group of a certified curve#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.sel_of_selmerCertified

    Let \(d\) be a squarefree natural number with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), and let \(a\) be a squarefree integer dividing \(d\) such that \((d,a)\) carries a Selmer certificate: the system \(\Sigma(\mathcal C_d)\) has exactly \(8\) solutions, and one of them has first coordinate the class of \(a\). Then \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8\), and \((a,b)\in\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) for some integer \(b\).

    Proof sketch. By Proposition 0, unknowns \(\sigma\) solve \(\Sigma(\mathcal C_d)\) if and only if \((a(\sigma),b(\sigma))\in\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\), and every element of \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has this form. So \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) is the image of the solution set under \(\sigma\mapsto(a(\sigma),b(\sigma))\). This map is injective for odd \(d\), so \(|\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)|=8\). Let \(\sigma\) be the solution whose first coordinate is the class of \(a\), that is \(a_0=[a<0]\), \(a_1=0\) and \(u_p=[p\mid a]\). Since \(a\) is a squarefree divisor of \(d\), \(a(\sigma)=(-1)^{[a<0]}\prod_{p\mid a}p=a\), so \((a,b(\sigma))\in\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\). These are steps 1 and 2 of the proof of CR₃ from its Selmer form, at a single certificate.

    The hypothesis

    The slot data are existential, so the curves of any one slot choice suffice. Both forms depend on the slot construction: a change to the slot conditions changes what they assert. CR₃^DL,Rk implies CR₃^DL,Adm,Rk, since the inputs that an admissible number produces satisfy the hypotheses of CR₃^DL,Rk.

    DefinitionThe positive-rank hypothesis on the two curves of a slot choice#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankDLRk

    The hypothesis CR₃^DL,Rk is the following assertion. Let \(\beta,\theta\) be integers and \(p_0\) a natural number such that:

    • \(\beta\) is odd and squarefree with \(|\beta|\equiv7\pmod8\), and \(p_0\) is a prime dividing \(\beta\);
    • \(\theta>0\) is squarefree with \(\theta\equiv1\pmod8\);
    • \(\beta\) and \(\theta\) are coprime;
    • \([\beta/\ell]=0\) for every prime \(\ell\mid\theta\), and \([\theta/p]=0\) for every prime \(p\mid\beta\).

    Then there are slot data \(q_0\), \(r_p\) (\(p\in P(\beta)\setminus\{p_0\}\)) and \(r_\ell\) (\(\ell\in P(\theta)\)) for \(\beta,\theta,p_0\), satisfying the slot conditions, such that, with $\(d_1=|\beta|\,q_0\prod_{p}r_p\prod_{\ell}r_\ell ,\)$ both \(C_{d_1}(\mathbb Q)\) and \(C_{d_1|\theta|}(\mathbb Q)\) contain a point of infinite order. Here \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\).

    The hypotheses on \(\beta,\theta,p_0\) are those of the realization, which the multiplier lemma and (θ1)–(θ4) give for an admissible input. By the realization, \(d_1\) and \(d_1|\theta|=d_1\theta=d_2\) are squarefree and \(\equiv5\pmod8\), \(\beta\) divides both, and both \((d_1,\beta)\) and \((d_2,\beta)\) carry Selmer certificates. So these are the two curves at which the proof of pole completeness applies the rank hypothesis, and both have 2-Selmer groups of order \(8\). The slot data are existential: points of infinite order on the two curves of any one slot choice suffice. The assertion depends on the slot conditions, so a change to the slot construction changes what it asserts.

    CR₃^DL,Rk is not proved here; it is a hypothesis. It follows from CR₃^Rk and from CR₃^TYZ,Rk, so it rests on the same theorems: Tian–Yuan–Zhang as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed, and Gross–Zagier with an auxiliary field.
