    The rank hypothesis as positive rank

    The forms CR₃^Sel, CR₃^5b, CR₃^TYZ and CR₃^TYZ_Cl of the certified rank hypothesis met so far conclude that every 2-Selmer class of \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) comes from a rational point, and CR₃ itself that one given class does. The theorems behind them (Tian–Yuan–Zhang as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed, and Gross–Zagier with an auxiliary field; Kriz's 2-converse with the corank lemma is an alternative that is not needed) give a rank statement (\(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\)), not a statement about Selmer classes. This chapter proves the step between the two, RK9, by the complete 2-descent. The descent map \(\delta_d\) has kernel \(2C_d(\mathbb Q)\) and image in \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\), and \(C_d(\mathbb Q)[2]\) has order \(4\). So when \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is finitely generated, a point of infinite order gives \(|C_d(\mathbb Q)/2C_d(\mathbb Q)|\ge8\), and when \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has order \(8\) the image is all of it. So CR₃^Sel, CR₃^TYZ and CR₃^TYZ_Cl have positive-rank forms that ask only for a point of infinite order on \(C_d\). With Mordell–Weil for every \(C_d\), proved in its own section, the positive-rank forms imply the original ones, and the positive-rank form of CR₃^Sel implies CR₃. A section derives CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk from three statements about L-series of elliptic curves. The proof of pole completeness applies the hypothesis at only two curves, those of a slot choice, and a further section states the positive-rank hypothesis for exactly those curves. A last section restates the positive-rank hypotheses with the conclusion that the equation \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions, which mentions no group law.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(E_{-d^2}\), \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) is the Selmer group of the Proposition 0 chapter, and \(u\equiv v\) means that \(u/v\) is a nonzero square. The Mordell–Weil section writes \(C_n\) for a positive integer \(n\), as the row-5b chapter does; elsewhere in this chapter the letter is \(d\).

    The complete 2-descent

    The Kummer maps at the roots \(0\) and \(d\) of \(x^3-d^2x\) make up the descent map \(\delta_d\). Over \(\mathbb Q\) its kernel is \(2C_d(\mathbb Q)\), its image lies in the 2-Selmer group, and RK9 follows by counting.

    RK9 needs \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) to be finitely generated. The two-isogeny descent and the descent theorem with the naive height prove it for every \(d\ne0\).

    The hypotheses in positive-rank form

    Three positive-rank hypotheses: CR₃^Rk keeps the premises of CR₃^Sel, CR₃^TYZ,Rk asks, for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod 8\), only for an odd genus sum of Rédei's bits, and CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk for an odd genus sum of class numbers, as in Tian, Yuan and Zhang's own statement. CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk implies CR₃^TYZ,Rk by Rédei's formula, and CR₃^TYZ,Rk implies CR₃^Rk by Corollary 4.4 and Theorem 3.6.

    DefinitionThe certified rank hypothesis in positive-rank form#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankRk

    The hypothesis CR₃^Rk is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be a squarefree positive integer with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\). Suppose that the 2-Selmer group \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has exactly \(8\) elements and contains a pair \((a,b)\) with \(|a|\equiv7\pmod8\). Then \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) has a point of infinite order.

    The premises are those of CR₃^Sel; only the conclusion changes, from "every Selmer class comes from a rational point" to \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\), which is what the theorems below conclude. It rests on:

    • for this 2-adic shape, Tian–Yuan–Zhang (Theorem 3.5 and the argument of the proof of Theorem 3.5(2), as completed in nt-tyz §4 by Proposition N, which is not refereed), which gives that \(L(C_d,s)\) vanishes to order exactly one at \(s=1\), and Gross–Zagier over an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein), which gives \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\). Their hypothesis, an odd genus sum of class numbers, follows from the premises of CR₃^Rk by Corollary 4.4, Theorem 3.6 and Rédei's formula;
    • as an alternative that is not needed, Kriz's 2-converse theorem (Theorem 10.13, a preprint whose publication status was not checked) for \(C_d\) at \(p=2\), whose standing assumption holds since \(d\ge21\) (by Proposition 0, as in CR₃^Sel), together with the corank lemma (the Cassels–Tate pairing on \(Ш/Ш_{\rm div}\) is nondegenerate and alternating, so \(\dim(Ш/Ш_{\rm div})[2]\) is even, with no finiteness of \(Ш\) assumed), giving \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)=1\).

    Neither rank theorem is proved here, so CR₃^Rk is a hypothesis. With Mordell–Weil for \(C_d\) it implies CR₃^Sel, by RK9.

    DefinitionThe class-number hypothesis in positive-rank form#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankTYZClRk

    The hypothesis CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be a squarefree positive integer with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) whose genus sum of class numbers $\(T^{\mathrm{Cl}}_{5b}(d)=\sum_\pi\prod_{B\in\pi}\#\bigl(2\,\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d_B}))\bigr)\pmod 2\)$ is \(1\). Here \(\pi\) runs over the set partitions of the primes of \(d\) with exactly one block with \(d_B\equiv7\), exactly one with \(d_B\equiv3\), and all other blocks with \(d_B\equiv1\pmod8\); \(d_B\) is the product of the primes in \(B\), and \(2\,\mathrm{Cl}(K)\) is the subgroup of squares of the class group of the ring of integers of \(K\). Then \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) has a point of infinite order.

    This is Tian–Yuan–Zhang with their own genus class numbers (Theorem 3.5, first assertion, with Theorem 3.3, Proposition 3.2, Lemmas 2.8, 3.16, 3.18, 3.21 and Proposition 2.3), as completed by Proposition N (nt-tyz §4; an unpublished argument written for this project, with two automated reviews and no human review): if \(T^{\mathrm{Cl}}_{5b}(d)\) is odd then their invariant \(\mathcal L(d)\) is nonzero, and since the root number is \(-1\), \(L(C_d,s)\) vanishes to order exactly one at \(s=1\); followed by Gross–Zagier over an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein), which gives \(\operatorname{rank}C_d(\mathbb Q)\ge1\). No Rédei formula and no condition on \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) enter. It follows from three statements about \(L(C_d,s)\) and \(L(E,s)\): Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b, which rests on the unrefereed Proposition N, the vanishing of \(L(C_d,s)\) at \(s=1\), and the Gross–Zagier statement (Row 5b, vanishing at the centre and Gross–Zagier give CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk). The vanishing in turn follows from the textbook functional equation, with root number \(-1\), on the strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\) (the functional equation gives vanishing at the centre). Those statements are themselves hypotheses, so CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk is not proved here.
