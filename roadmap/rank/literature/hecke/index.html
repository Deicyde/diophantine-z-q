    Hecke's continuation and the functional equation, proved

    This section proves two of the literature statements of the chapter: Hecke's continuation of \(L(C_d,s)\) for every natural number \(d\ne0\), and the functional equation \(\Lambda_{32d^2}(2-s)=-\Lambda_{32d^2}(s)\) on the strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\) for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\). Both are proved with no hypothesis. The main classical input is Gauss's count of the points of \(y^2=x^3-x\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), which is proved here too, by a quartic Jacobi sum; the extension to every \(d\) also uses minimal equations at \(2\) and changes of variables, which are proved as well.

    The route is Hecke's. Classically \(C_d\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb Z[i]\) (this is not proved or used here); what is proved is that, for squarefree \(d\equiv1\pmod4\), its coefficients are those of the character \(\psi_d(\alpha)=\left(\frac{N(\alpha)}d\right)u(\alpha)\,\alpha\) of \(\mathbb Q(i)\), where \(u(\alpha)\) is the primary unit of \(\alpha\); this is Deuring's identity, \(c_d(n)=\lambda_d(n)\) with \(\lambda_d(n)=\frac14\sum_{N(\alpha)=n}\psi_d(\alpha)\). It is proved by comparing Euler products: the Hecke coefficients are multiplicative and satisfy the Hecke relation at prime powers, by unique factorization in \(\mathbb Z[i]\), and at the primes the two sides agree by direct counts at \(2\), at \(p\mid d\) and at \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), and by Gauss's count at \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) after a quadratic twist. Deuring's identity makes \(L(C_d,s)\) a periodic twist \(\sum_\alpha c(\alpha\bmod4d)\,\alpha\,N(\alpha)^{-s}\) of the lattice series of \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Each such series is a finite combination of Mellin transforms of theta kernels of shifted lattices, which are entire, so \(L(C_d,s)\) has an entire continuation. The transformation law of the theta kernels, together with a finite Fourier identity for \(c\) proved with quadratic Gauss sums, gives the functional equation, with the factor \((32d^2)^{s/2}\) and sign \(\left(\frac2d\right)=-1\).

    Deuring's identity is proved for squarefree \(d\equiv1\pmod4\), which covers the functional equation. For Hecke's continuation at every \(d\ne0\), the computation of \(L(C_d,s)\) is extended to even squarefree \(d\), the coefficients of \(C_d\) are written as a quadratic twist of those of \(C_1\), and a square factor of \(d\) is removed by a change of variables, under which the L-series does not change.

    The section also records the consequence for the class-number hypothesis: with the functional equation and the case \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) of Hecke's continuation proved, CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk follows from two hypotheses that remain unproved, Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5(2) in reading A and the Gross–Zagier statement. Reading A is one reading of their theorem that no refereed text found states (reading A).

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\); \(c_d(n)\) are the coefficients of the standard Euler product; a pair \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\) is also the Gaussian integer \(a+bi\), with norm \(N(a,b)=a^2+b^2\); and \(\left(\frac an\right)\) is the Jacobi symbol as in the Hecke character \(\psi_d\).

    (3) Deuring's identity holds for each such \(d\) (from the Euler product (3)); apply (2).
