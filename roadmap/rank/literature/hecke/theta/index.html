    Theta series of \(\mathbb Z[i]\) and Hecke's continuation

    This subsection carries out Hecke's method for the Gaussian integers, with no reference to elliptic curves. For a shift \((a,b)\) of the lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\), the theta kernel \(\theta_{a,b}(x)=\sum_{(m,n)}((m+a)+i(n+b))e^{-\pi((m+a)^2+(n+b)^2)x}\) is a product of one-variable theta functions, and Poisson summation in one variable gives the transformation law \(\theta_{a,b}(1/x)=x^2\theta^*_{a,b}(x)\) with a dual kernel \(\theta^*_{a,b}\). Both kernels decay faster than every power of \(x\), so their Mellin transforms \(\Lambda_{a,b}\), \(\Lambda^*_{a,b}\) are entire and satisfy \(\Lambda_{a,b}(2-s)=\Lambda^*_{a,b}(s)\).

    On \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\) the Mellin transform is \(\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\) times the lattice Dirichlet series \(\sum((m+a)+i(n+b))((m+a)^2+(n+b)^2)^{-s}\), which therefore has an entire continuation. Splitting \(\mathbb Z^2\) into residue classes modulo \(M\) gives the same for every series \(T_c(s)=\sum_\alpha c(\alpha\bmod M)\,\alpha\,N(\alpha)^{-s}\) over the Gaussian integers, and grouping its terms by the norm turns it into an ordinary Dirichlet series. When the finite Fourier transform of \(c\) is supported on \((1+i)\mathbb Z[i]\) and is a multiple of \(c\) there, the completed function \(\Lambda_c\) satisfies a functional equation under \(s\mapsto2-s\).

    Throughout, a point \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z^2\) is also the Gaussian integer \(\alpha_1+\alpha_2i\), with norm \(N(\alpha)=\alpha_1^2+\alpha_2^2\), and an infinite sum over \(\mathbb Z^2\) is read as \(0\) where the family is not summable.

    For \(a\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) and real \(x>0\) the four one-variable kernels are $\(E_a(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi(n+a)^2x},\qquad O_a(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(n+a)\,e^{-\pi(n+a)^2x},\)$ $\(C_a(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\cos(2\pi an)\,e^{-\pi n^2x},\qquad S_a(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}n\sin(2\pi an)\,e^{-\pi n^2x};\)$ each depends only on \(a\) modulo \(1\). They are real functions defined for every real \(x\); only \(x>0\) is used, where they are these sums. For \(a,b\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) the theta kernel of the shifted lattice \((a,b)+\mathbb Z^2\) and its dual kernel are the complex functions $\(\theta_{a,b}(x)=O_a(x)E_b(x)+i\,E_a(x)O_b(x),\qquad \theta^*_{a,b}(x)=S_a(x)C_b(x)+i\,C_a(x)S_b(x).\)$

    1. For \(x>0\): \(O_a(1/x)=x^{3/2}S_a(x)\) and \(E_a(1/x)=x^{1/2}C_a(x)\).
    2. (Transformation law.) For \(x>0\): \(\theta_{a,b}(1/x)=x^2\,\theta^*_{a,b}(x)\).
    3. If \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) and real numbers \(c\) and \(p>0\) satisfy \(f(x)-c=O(e^{-px})\) as \(x\to\infty\), then \(f(x)=O(1)\) as \(x\to\infty\). In particular \(E_a\) and \(C_a\) are \(O(1)\) as \(x\to\infty\).
    4. If \(u,v:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\), \(u(x)=O(e^{-px})\) for some \(p>0\) and \(v(x)=O(1)\) as \(x\to\infty\), then \(u(x)v(x)=O(x^r)\) as \(x\to\infty\) for every real \(r\).
    5. For every real \(r\), \(\theta_{a,b}(x)=O(x^r)\) and \(\theta^*_{a,b}(x)=O(x^r)\) as \(x\to\infty\).
    6. \(\theta_{a,b}\) and \(\theta^*_{a,b}\) are continuous on \((0,\infty)\).

    Proof sketch. (1) These are the one-variable transformation laws \(O_a(x)=x^{-3/2}S_a(1/x)\) and \(E_a(x)=x^{-1/2}C_a(1/x)\) (Poisson summation), read at \(1/x\).

    (2) Substitute (1) in the definition of \(\theta_{a,b}(1/x)\) and use \(x^2=x^{3/2}x^{1/2}\).

    (3) Write \(f=(f-c)+c\); \(e^{-px}\le1\) for \(x\ge0\), so both summands are \(O(1)\). The kernels \(E_a\) and \(C_a\) differ from a constant by \(O(e^{-px})\) for some \(p>0\) (standard), so (3) applies.

    (4) \(u(x)v(x)=O(e^{-px})\), and \(e^{-px}=o(x^r)\) for every \(r\).

    (5) \(O_a\) and \(S_a\) are \(O(e^{-px})\) for some \(p>0\) (standard), and \(E_b\), \(C_b\) are \(O(1)\) by (3). Apply (4) to each of the two products in \(\theta_{a,b}\) and in \(\theta^*_{a,b}\), and add.

    (6) The four kernels are continuous on \((0,\infty)\).

    For \(a,b\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) let \(\theta_{a,b}\) and \(\theta^*_{a,b}\) be the theta kernels. The completed lattice function and its dual are the Mellin transforms $\(\Lambda_{a,b}(s)=\int_0^\infty\theta_{a,b}(x)\,x^{s-1}\,dx,\qquad \Lambda^*_{a,b}(s)=\int_0^\infty\theta^*_{a,b}(x)\,x^{s-1}\,dx\qquad(s\in\mathbb C),\)$ each integral read as \(0\) where the integrand is not integrable.

    1. The pair \((\theta_{a,b},\theta^*_{a,b})\) is a functional-equation pair of weight \(2\) and sign \(1\) with constant terms \(0\): both functions are locally integrable on \((0,\infty)\), \(\theta_{a,b}(1/x)=x^2\theta^*_{a,b}(x)\) for \(x>0\), and both are \(O(x^r)\) as \(x\to\infty\) for every real \(r\).
    2. For every \(s\in\mathbb C\) the integral defining \(\Lambda_{a,b}(s)\) converges absolutely.
    3. \(\Lambda_{a,b}\) and \(\Lambda^*_{a,b}\) are entire.
    4. For every \(s\in\mathbb C\), \(\Lambda_{a,b}(2-s)=\Lambda^*_{a,b}(s)\).

    Proof sketch. (1) The transformation law and the growth bounds are theta kernels (2) and (5); continuity on \((0,\infty)\), theta kernels (6), gives local integrability.

    (2)–(4) This is the general theorem on such pairs (Hecke's argument): for a pair \((f,g)\) with \(f(1/x)=\varepsilon x^kg(x)\), both decaying faster than every power of \(x\) at infinity and with constant terms \(0\), the Mellin integrals of \(f\) and \(g\) converge for every \(s\), define entire functions, and satisfy \(\Lambda_f(k-s)=\varepsilon\Lambda_g(s)\). One splits the integral at \(x=1\) and moves the part over \((0,1)\) to \((1,\infty)\) with the transformation law. Here \(k=2\) and \(\varepsilon=1\).

    For real \(a,b\) and \((m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2\) put $\(z_{a,b}(m,n)=(m+a)+i(n+b),\qquad N_{a,b}(m,n)=(m+a)^2+(n+b)^2,\qquad e_{a,b}(m,n)=e^{2\pi i(am+bn)}.\)$ For \(N\ge0\) real and \(s\in\mathbb C\), \(N^s\) is the complex power (\(e^{s\log N}\) for \(N>0\), and \(0^s=0\) for \(s\ne0\)). The lattice Dirichlet series is $\(L_{a,b}(s)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2}\frac{z_{a,b}(m,n)}{N_{a,b}(m,n)^s},\)$ read as \(0\) where the family is not summable. The lattice \(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Zi\subset\mathbb C\) has the basis \(1,i\). Let \(\theta_{a,b},\theta^*_{a,b}\) be the theta kernels and \(\Lambda_{a,b},\Lambda^*_{a,b}\) the completed lattice functions, with \(a,b\) taken modulo \(1\).

    1. \(N_{a,b}(m,n)=|z_{a,b}(m,n)|^2\ge0\), and \(|e^{2\pi ix}|=1\) for real \(x\); so \(|e_{a,b}(m,n)|=1\).
    2. For \(t>0\), \(\displaystyle\theta_{a,b}(t)=\sum_{(m,n)}z_{a,b}(m,n)\,e^{-\pi N_{a,b}(m,n)t}\) and \(\displaystyle\theta^*_{a,b}(t)=\sum_{(m,n)}-i\,(m+in)\,e_{a,b}(m,n)\,e^{-\pi(m^2+n^2)t}\), both families summable.
    3. For real \(r<-2\), \(\sum_{(m,n)}|z_{a,b}(m,n)|^r<\infty\) (a term with \(z_{a,b}(m,n)=0\) is \(0\)).
    4. For \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\): the family \(z_{a,b}/N_{a,b}^s\) is absolutely summable, and $\(\Lambda_{a,b}(s)=\sum_{(m,n)}\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\frac{z_{a,b}(m,n)}{N_{a,b}(m,n)^s},\qquad \Lambda^*_{a,b}(s)=\sum_{(m,n)}\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\frac{-i\,(m+in)\,e_{a,b}(m,n)}{(m^2+n^2)^s}.\)$
    5. (Hecke.) There is an entire function \(F\) with \(F(s)=L_{a,b}(s)\) for every \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Direct computation.

    (2) \(O_a(t)\) and \(E_b(t)\) are absolutely convergent sums over \(\mathbb Z\), so their product is the sum over \(\mathbb Z^2\) of the products of terms, and \(e^{-\pi N_{a,b}(m,n)t}=e^{-\pi(m+a)^2t}e^{-\pi(n+b)^2t}\); likewise for \(E_a(t)O_b(t)\), and the two combine to \(z_{a,b}(m,n)\). For the dual kernel use the expansions \(S_a(t)=\sum_n-i\,n\,e^{2\pi ian}e^{-\pi n^2t}\) and \(C_a(t)=\sum_ne^{2\pi ian}e^{-\pi n^2t}\) (the odd parts cancel under \(n\mapsto-n\)) and multiply in the same way.

    (3) The standard summability of \(\sum_{\omega}|\omega-w|^r\) over a lattice \(\omega\) of rank \(2\) in \(\mathbb C\), for \(r<-2\), applied to \(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Zi\) and \(w=-(a+bi)\).

    (4) A function given for \(t>0\) by \(\sum_kc_ke^{-\pi\lambda_kt}\) with \(\lambda_k\ge0\) (and \(c_k=0\) when \(\lambda_k=0\)) has Mellin transform \(\sum_k\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)c_k\lambda_k^{-s}\) whenever \(\sum_k|c_k|\lambda_k^{-\operatorname{Re}s}<\infty\) and \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\). Apply it to (2): with \(\sigma=\operatorname{Re}s\), \(|z_{a,b}|N_{a,b}^{-\sigma}=|z_{a,b}|^{1-2\sigma}\) and \(1-2\sigma<-2\), so (3) gives the hypothesis; for the dual, \(|(m+in)e_{a,b}(m,n)|=|m+in|\) by (1). Absolute summability of \(z_{a,b}/N_{a,b}^s\) follows from the first identity after dividing by \(\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\ne0\).

    (5) Take \(F(s)=\pi^s\Gamma(s)^{-1}\Lambda_{a,b}(s)\). It is entire, since \(\pi^s\) and \(1/\Gamma\) are entire and \(\Lambda_{a,b}\) is entire (completed lattice functions (3)). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\), \(\Gamma(s)\ne0\) and (4) gives \(F(s)=L_{a,b}(s)\).
