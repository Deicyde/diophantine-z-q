    The L-series of \(C_d\) for every \(d\ne0\)

    Hecke's continuation is stated for every \(d\ne0\), while the computation of \(L(C_d,s)\) covers odd squarefree \(d\). This subsection removes both restrictions.

    For even squarefree \(d=2e\), the discriminant \((2d)^6\) of \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has \(2\)-adic valuation \(12\), so the discriminant criterion does not decide minimality at \(2\). Instead \(c_4=48d^2=192e^2\) has \(2\)-adic valuation \(6\), while every integral equation over \(\mathbb Z_2\) has \(c_4\) a unit or divisible by \(16\); so no change of variables with \(v(u)>0\) keeps the equation integral, and the equation is minimal. With that, the computation of \(L(C_d,s)\) holds for every squarefree \(d\).

    For non-squarefree \(d=m^2d'\), the scaling \((x,y)\mapsto(x/m^2,y/m^3)\) carries \(C_{m^2d'}\) to \(C_{d'}\). The local polynomial at each place does not depend on the equation chosen in the isomorphism class when \(j\) is integral, which excludes multiplicative reduction; for \(C_d\), \(c_4^3=1728\Delta\). So \(L(C_{m^2d'},s)=L(C_{d'},s)\).

    For squarefree \(d\), the substitution \(x\mapsto dx\) makes the coefficients of \(C_d\) those of \(C_1\) twisted by the Jacobi symbol \(\left(\frac dn\right)\). With Deuring's identity for \(C_1\), this writes \(L(C_d,s)\) as a periodic twist of the lattice series of \(\mathbb Z[i]\) modulo \(4d\), which has an entire continuation.

    Throughout, notation is that of the computation of \(L(C_d,s)\).

    1. (Minimality from \(c_4\).) Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) and fraction field \(K\), and \(W\) a Weierstrass equation with coefficients in \(R\).     2. For a finite place \(v\) of \(\mathbb Q\) and \(x\in\mathcal O_v\): \(x\in\mathfrak m_v\) exactly when \(p_v\) divides \(x\) in \(\mathcal O_v\). Moreover \(2\ne0\) in \(\mathcal O_v\).
    3. If \(p_v=2\), then every Weierstrass equation \(W\) over \(\mathcal O_v\) has \(c_4(W)=16X\) for some \(X\in\mathcal O_v\), or \(c_4(W)\notin\mathfrak m_v\).
    4. If \(p_v=2\) and \(e\) is an odd natural number, then \(C_{2e,v}\) is minimal over \(\mathcal O_v\).

    Proof sketch. (1) \(W\) is integral. Let \(C\) be a change of variables, with scaling factor \(u\), such that \(C\cdot W\) is integral.     (2) \(\mathfrak m_v\) is generated by \(p_v\); \(\mathcal O_v\) has characteristic \(0\).

    (3) If \(a_1\in\mathfrak m_v\), write \(a_1=2t\); then \(c_4=b_2^2-24b_4=16\bigl((t^2+a_2)^2-3(a_4+ta_3)\bigr)\). Otherwise, if \(c_4\in\mathfrak m_v\), the identity \(a_1^4=c_4-2(4a_1^2a_2+8a_2^2-24a_4-12a_1a_3)\) puts \(a_1^4\), hence \(a_1\), in \(\mathfrak m_v\).

    (4) \(C_{2e,v}\) comes from the model over \(\mathcal O_v\), with \(c_4=48(2e)^2=16\cdot12e^2\). Suppose \(c_4=r^4c_4(W')\) with \(r=2s\in\mathfrak m_v\) and \(W'\) integral, so \(12e^2=s^4c_4(W')\). Both contradict \(3e^2\) odd. Apply (1).

    Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\) and valuation \(v\), and \(W\) a Weierstrass equation over \(K\). Changes of variables \(C\) (with scaling factor \(u\)), minimal equations, reduction types and local polynomials are as in minimal Weierstrass equations and the local polynomial; the local polynomial of \(W\) is computed from the minimal equation chosen once and for all for \(W\). Say that \(j\) is integral for \(W\) if \(3v(c_4(W))\ge v(\Delta(W))\).

    1. If \(j\) is integral for \(W\), then for every change of variables \(C\), the equation \(C\cdot W\) does not have multiplicative reduction, and \(j\) is integral for \(C\cdot W\).
    2. If \(j\) is integral for \(W\), the local polynomial of \(W\) is \(1-(q+1-\#\tilde W(\kappa))T+qT^2\) if the chosen minimal equation has good reduction, where \(q\) is the number of elements of the residue field \(\kappa\) and \(\tilde W(\kappa)\) is the set of points of the reduction of that equation (the number of elements of an infinite set read as \(0\)), and \(1\) otherwise.
    3. If \(\Delta(W)\ne0\) and \(j\) is integral for \(W\), then \(C\cdot W\) and \(W\) have the same local polynomial for every change of variables \(C\).

    Proof sketch. (1) \(c_4(C\cdot W)=u^{-4}c_4(W)\) and \(\Delta(C\cdot W)=u^{-12}\Delta(W)\), so integrality of \(j\) is preserved. Multiplicative reduction of \(C\cdot W\) would mean \(v(\Delta(C\cdot W))>0\) and \(v(c_4(C\cdot W))=0\), whence \(v(\Delta(C\cdot W))\le3v(c_4(C\cdot W))=0\), a contradiction.

    (2) By (1) the chosen minimal equation is not multiplicative, so of the four cases defining the local polynomial only good reduction and the remaining case occur.

    (3) Both local polynomials are given by (2). The chosen minimal equations of \(W\) and of \(C\cdot W\) differ by a change of variables \(D\) whose scaling factor has \(v=0\) (minimal Weierstrass equations), so their discriminants have the same valuation and one has good reduction exactly when the other does. In the good case their reductions differ by a change of variables over \(\kappa\), which preserves the number of points (point counts).

    For natural numbers \(d,p,k\) let \(e_{d,p}(k)\) be the coefficient of \(T^k\) in \(P_{d,p}(T)^{-1}\), where \(P_{d,p}\) is the Euler polynomial; \(c_d(n)\) are the coefficients of the standard Euler product, and \(\left(\frac an\right)\) is the Jacobi symbol as in the Hecke character.

    1. For every integer \(a\) and coprime natural numbers \(x,y\), \(\left(\frac a{xy}\right)=\left(\frac ax\right)\left(\frac ay\right)\); and for \(n\ne0\), \(\left(\frac an\right)=\prod_{p^k\parallel n}\left(\frac ap\right)^k\).
    2. \(e_{d,p}(0)=1\); reading \(e_{d,p}(k)\) in \(\mathbb C\) gives the coefficient of \(T^k\) in the inverse of \(P_{d,p}\) with complex coefficients; and if \(p\mid2d\), then \(e_{d,p}(k)=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).
    3. If \(p\) is a prime with \(p\nmid2d\), then there is an integer \(b\) with \(P_{d,p}=1-\left(\frac dp\right)bT+pT^2\) and \(P_{1,p}=1-bT+pT^2\) (over \(\mathbb C\)), and \(\left(\frac dp\right)^2=1\).
    4. For every natural number \(d\), prime \(p\) and \(k\ge0\), \(e_{d,p}(k)=\left(\frac dp\right)^ke_{1,p}(k)\).
    5. For every natural number \(d\) and every \(n\ge1\), \(c_d(n)=\left(\frac dn\right)c_1(n)\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Multiplicativity of the Jacobi symbol in its denominator (trivial when \(x\) or \(y\) is \(0\), as coprimality then forces the other to be \(1\)), and induction over the factorization.
