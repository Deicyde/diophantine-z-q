    Deuring's identity for \(C_d\)

    Deuring's identity says that the L-series of \(C_d\) has the same coefficients as the Hecke L-series of \(\psi_d\): \(c_d(n)=\lambda_d(n)\) for every \(n\ge1\), where \(c_d(n)\) are the coefficients of the standard Euler product of \(C_d\), which are those of \(L(C_d,s)\) for odd squarefree \(d\). Both sides are Euler products with quadratic local factors, so for odd squarefree \(d\) it suffices to compare them at the primes.

    At \(2\) and at the primes dividing \(d\) both coefficients vanish. At a prime \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) the involution \(x\mapsto-x\) shows that \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has exactly \(p\) affine solutions over \(\mathbb F_p\), so \(c_d(p)=0\), while no Gaussian integer has norm \(p\). At a prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) the substitution \(x\mapsto dx\) and quadratic reciprocity reduce the comparison, for squarefree \(d\equiv1\pmod4\), to the single curve \(y^2=x^3-x\), where it is Gauss's point count.

    Throughout, \(c_d(n)\) are the coefficients of the standard Euler product and \(\lambda_d(n)\) the Hecke coefficients.

    Let \(c_d(n)\) be the coefficients of the standard Euler product, \(\lambda_d(n)\) the Hecke coefficients, and for a prime \(p\) let \(\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)\) be the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_p\) (with value \(0\) at \(0\)).

    1. If \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) is prime and \(d\) is a natural number, then \(\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)=0\).
    2. Under the same hypotheses, \(\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2:y^2=x^3-d^2x\}=p\).
    3. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree, \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) is prime and \(p\nmid d\), then \(c_d(p)=0\).
    4. Under the hypotheses of (3), \(c_d(p)=\lambda_d(p)\).
    5. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree and \(p\) is a prime dividing \(d\), then \(c_d(p)=\lambda_d(p)\).
    6. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree, then \(c_d(2)=\lambda_d(2)\).

    Proof sketch. (1) The substitution \(x\mapsto-x\) multiplies each term by \(\left(\frac{-1}p\right)=-1\), so the sum equals its negative.

    (2) A value \(x\) contributes \(1+\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)\) solutions \(y\); sum over \(x\) and use (1).

    (3) \(p\nmid2d\), so \(c_d(p)\) is \(p\) minus the number of affine solutions (deuring identity and the coefficients at primes), which is \(0\) by (2).

    (4) \(A_p\) is empty since \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) (norm fibres at primes (1)), so \(\lambda_d(p)=0\); and \(c_d(p)=0\) by (3).

    (5) \(c_d(p)=0\) since \(p\mid2d\) (the coefficients at primes). For \(\alpha\in A_p\), \(\left(\frac{N(\alpha)}d\right)=\left(\frac pd\right)=0\) since \(p\) and \(d\) have the common factor \(p\); so \(\lambda_d(p)=0\).

    (6) \(c_d(2)=0\) since \(2\mid2d\). For \(\alpha=(a,b)\in A_2\), \(a^2+b^2\equiv2\pmod4\), and the table of the primary unit gives \(u(\alpha)=0\) for these residues; so \(\lambda_d(2)=0\).

    Let \(c_d(n)\) be the coefficients of the standard Euler product with Euler polynomials \(P_{d,p}\), and \(\lambda_d(n)\) the Hecke coefficients. Gauss's theorem at the split primes holds for \(d\) if \(c_d(p)=\lambda_d(p)\) for every prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) with \(p\nmid d\).

    1. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree and \(p\) is a prime with \(p\nmid2d\), then \(c_d(p)=p+1-N_d(p)\), where \(N_d(p)\) is the number of points of the model \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\), the point at infinity included.
    2. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree, the Hecke coefficients have the Euler product for \(d\), and \(c_d(p)=\lambda_d(p)\) for every prime \(p\), then Deuring's identity holds for \(d\).
    3. If \(d\) is odd and squarefree, the Hecke coefficients have the Euler product for \(d\), and Gauss's theorem at the split primes holds for \(d\), then Deuring's identity holds for \(d\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Deuring's identity for \(C_d\) identifies \(c_d(p)\) with the Dirichlet coefficient \(a_p(C_d)\), which the coefficients at primes computes for \(p\nmid2d\).

    (2) Both sides of Deuring's identity at \(n\ge1\) are products over \(p^k\parallel n\), of the coefficients of \(T^k\) in \(P_{d,p}(T)^{-1}\) and in \(H_{d,p}(T)^{-1}\) respectively. The Hecke Euler polynomial \(H_{d,p}\) is \(P_{d,p}\) with its coefficients read in \(\mathbb C\): both are \(1\) at \(p\mid2d\), and at \(p\nmid2d\) the middle coefficient \(-\lambda_d(p)=-c_d(p)=-(p+1-N_d(p))\) by the hypothesis and (1). Reading the coefficients in \(\mathbb C\) commutes with inversion (quadratic power series (2)).

    (3) The hypothesis of (2) at every prime \(p\): if \(p\mid d\), by the small primes (5); if \(p=2\), by the small primes (6); if \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(p\nmid d\), by Gauss's theorem at the split primes; if \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) and \(p\nmid d\), by the small primes (4).

    For a prime \(p\), \(\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)\) is the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_p\) (with value \(0\) at \(0\)). Gauss's theorem at the split primes for \(d\) is as in Deuring's identity from the Euler product, and Gauss's point count is the statement about \(y^2=x^3-x\) named there.

    1. If \(p\ne2\) is prime and \(d\) a natural number, then \(\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2:y^2=x^3-d^2x\}=p+\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)\).
    2. If \(p\) is prime and \(p\nmid d\), then \(\sum_x\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)=\left(\frac dp\right)\sum_x\left(\frac{x^3-x}p\right)\).
    3. If \(d\equiv1\pmod4\) is squarefree and Gauss's theorem at the split primes holds for \(d=1\), then it holds for \(d\).
    4. Gauss's point count implies Gauss's theorem at the split primes for \(d=1\).

    Proof sketch. (1) A value \(x\) contributes \(1+\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)\) solutions \(y\).

    (2) Substitute \(x=dt\): \((dt)^3-d^2(dt)=d^2\cdot d(t^3-t)\), and \(\left(\frac{d^2}p\right)=1\).

    (3) Let \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) be prime with \(p\nmid d\); then \(p\nmid2d\) and \(p\nmid2\). By Deuring's identity for \(C_d\), the coefficients at primes and (1), \(c_d(p)=-\sum_x\left(\frac{x^3-d^2x}p\right)\) and \(c_1(p)=-\sum_x\left(\frac{x^3-x}p\right)\), so \(c_d(p)=\left(\frac dp\right)c_1(p)\) by (2). Since \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(d\) is odd, quadratic reciprocity gives \(\left(\frac dp\right)=\left(\frac pd\right)\). On the other side \(\lambda_d(p)=\left(\frac pd\right)\lambda_1(p)\) (Hecke coefficients (2)), and \(c_1(p)=\lambda_1(p)\) by the hypothesis.

    (4) For a prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), \(c_1(p)\) is \(p\) minus the number of solutions of \(y^2=x^3-x\) (the coefficients at primes), and \(\lambda_1(p)=\frac14\sum_{\alpha\in A_p}u(\alpha)\alpha\) since \(\left(\frac{\cdot}1\right)=1\); Gauss's point count says these are equal.
