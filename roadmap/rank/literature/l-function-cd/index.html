    The L-series of the congruent number curves, computed

    Most literature statements of this section are about the L-series \(L(C_d,s)\), defined by the recipe of entire continuations: an Euler product over the finite places of \(\mathbb Q\) of local polynomials, each read off a minimal equation of \(C_d\) over the completion, where the minimal equation is one chosen once and for all. This subsection computes that recipe for odd squarefree \(d\), which includes every squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\). Nothing here is assumed; every statement is proved.

    Two general facts come first. Over a discrete valuation ring, an integral equation whose discriminant is not divisible by the twelfth power of the maximal ideal is minimal, and two minimal equations with nonzero discriminant differ by a change of variables with \(u\) a unit, which reduces to a change of variables over the residue field. So the local polynomial at a place of good reduction, or of additive reduction with nonzero discriminant, can be computed from any minimal equation, not only from the chosen one.

    For \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) the discriminant is \((2d)^6\) and \(c_4=48d^2\). For odd squarefree \(d\) no prime has its twelfth power dividing \((2d)^6\), so the equation is minimal at every prime; the reduction is good at the primes \(p\nmid2d\) and additive at the primes \(p\mid2d\). The local polynomial is therefore \(1-a_pT+pT^2\) with \(a_p=p+1-\#C_d(\mathbb F_p)\) (the point at infinity included) at \(p\nmid2d\), and \(1\) at \(p\mid2d\). Since only one place lies over each prime, the Dirichlet coefficient of \(L(C_d,s)\) at \(p^k\) is the coefficient of \(T^k\) in the inverse of that polynomial. At a prime \(p\nmid2d\) the coefficient is \(p\) minus the number of affine solutions of \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb F_p\); at a prime \(p\mid2d\) it is \(0\). The coefficients are multiplicative, so \(L(C_d,s)\) is the Dirichlet series whose coefficients are those of the formal Euler product \(\prod_{p\nmid2d}(1-a_pp^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\).

    The computation proves none of the literature statements of this section (the functional equation, Gross–Zagier, row 5b, reading A and Hecke's continuation): it shows only that \(L(C_d,s)\), for the squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) that the functional equation, row 5b and reading A concern, is the standard L-series. It says nothing about \(L(C_d,s)\) for even or non-squarefree \(d\), which Hecke's continuation also covers, nor about \(L(E,s)\) for the other elliptic curves in Gross–Zagier. Neither the continuation of \(L(C_d,s)\), nor Hasse's bound, nor the conductor is proved here.

    Throughout, \(d\) is a natural number, \(p\) a prime, and \(v\) a finite place of \(\mathbb Q\) with prime \(p_v\), completion \(\mathbb Q_v\), valuation ring \(\mathcal O_v\), maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m_v\) and residue field \(\kappa_v\). The number of elements of an infinite set is taken to be \(0\); only finite residue fields occur.

    Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), residue field \(\kappa\) and fraction field \(K\), and let \(v:K\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}\) be its normalized valuation, so that \(v(x)\ge0\) exactly when \(x\in R\). For a field \(F\), a change of variables \(C=(u,r,s,t)\) over \(F\) (\(u\in F^\times\), \(r,s,t\in F\)) takes a Weierstrass equation \(W\) over \(F\) to the equation \(C\cdot W\) obtained by the substitution \(x=u^2x'+r\), \(y=u^3y'+su^2x'+t\); then \(\Delta(C\cdot W)=u^{-12}\Delta(W)\) and \(c_4(C\cdot W)=u^{-4}c_4(W)\). Below \(F=K\) unless stated. The equation \(W\) is integral if its coefficients lie in \(R\), and minimal if it is integral and \(v(\Delta(C\cdot W))\ge v(\Delta(W))\) for every change of variables \(C\) over \(K\) with \(C\cdot W\) integral. The reduction \(\tilde W\) of a minimal \(W\) is the equation over \(\kappa\) obtained by reducing its coefficients modulo \(\mathfrak m\).

    1. If \(W\) is a Weierstrass equation with coefficients in \(R\) and \(\Delta(W)\notin\mathfrak m^{12}\), then \(W\), as an equation over \(K\), is minimal.
    2. Let \(W\) be a Weierstrass equation over \(K\) with \(\Delta(W)\ne0\) and \(C=(u,r,s,t)\) a change of variables over \(K\). If \(W\) and \(C\cdot W\) are both minimal, then \(v(u)=0\).
    3. Let \(W\) be a Weierstrass equation over \(K\) and \(C=(u,r,s,t)\) a change of variables over \(K\) such that \(W\) and \(C\cdot W\) are both minimal and \(v(u)=0\). Then there is a change of variables \(C'\) over \(\kappa\) with \(\widetilde{C\cdot W}=C'\cdot\tilde W\).

    Proof sketch. (1) \(W\) is integral. Let \(C\cdot W\) be integral. If \(v(u)\le0\), then \(v(\Delta(C\cdot W))=v(\Delta(W))-12v(u)\ge v(\Delta(W))\). If \(v(u)>0\), then \(u\in\mathfrak m\) and \(\delta=\Delta(C\cdot W)\in R\), so \(\Delta(W)=u^{12}\delta\in\mathfrak m^{12}\), which is excluded.

    (2) Minimality of \(W\) applied to \(C\), and of \(C\cdot W\) applied to \(C^{-1}\), gives \(v(\Delta(W))=v(\Delta(C\cdot W))=v(\Delta(W))-12v(u)\). Since \(\Delta(W)\ne0\) its valuation is finite, so \(12v(u)=0\) and \(v(u)=0\).

    (3) \(u\) is a unit of \(R\). Between two integral equations, a change of variables whose \(u\) is a unit of \(R\) has \(r,s,t\in R\) as well (a standard fact), so it is a change of variables over \(R\) between the two equations with coefficients in \(R\). Reducing \(u,r,s,t\) modulo \(\mathfrak m\) gives \(C'\), and reduction commutes with the substitution.

    1. For \(x,y\in F\), \((x,y)\) is a solution of \(C\cdot E\) if and only if \((u^2x+r,\ u^3y+su^2x+t)\) is a solution of \(E\).
    2. The map \((x,y)\mapsto(u^2x+r,\ u^3y+su^2x+t)\) is a bijection from the solutions of \(C\cdot E\) in \(F^2\) to those of \(E\), with inverse \((x,y)\mapsto\bigl((x-r)/u^2,\ (y-s(x-r)-t)/u^3\bigr)\).
    3. If \(\Delta(E)\ne0\), the points of \(E\) over \(F\) are in bijection with the solutions of \(E\) in \(F^2\) together with one extra element (the point at infinity): every solution is nonsingular.
    4. If \(\Delta(E)\ne0\), then \(C\cdot E\) and \(E\) have the same number of points over \(F\).
    5. If \(\Delta(E)\ne0\) and \(f:F\to F'\) is an isomorphism of fields, then the equation \(E^f\) over \(F'\) obtained by applying \(f\) to the coefficients has the same number of points over \(F'\) as \(E\) has over \(F\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Substitute the coefficients of \(C\cdot E\) and clear the powers of \(u\ne0\); the two equations differ by the factor \(u^6\). (4) By (3) for \(E\) and for \(C\cdot E\) (whose discriminant \(u^{-12}\Delta(E)\) is nonzero), and the bijection (2). (5) \(f\times f\) maps the solutions of \(E\) bijectively to those of \(E^f\), and (3) adds the point at infinity on both sides.

    Let \(R\), \(\mathfrak m\), \(\kappa\), \(K\) and \(v\) be as in minimal Weierstrass equations, and let \(q\) be the number of elements of \(\kappa\) (\(0\) if \(\kappa\) is infinite). A minimal equation \(W\) has good reduction if \(v(\Delta(W))=0\), multiplicative reduction if \(v(\Delta(W))>0\) and \(v(c_4(W))=0\) (split or nonsplit according as the slopes of the tangents at the node lie in \(\kappa\) or not), and additive reduction if \(v(\Delta(W))>0\) and \(v(c_4(W))>0\).

    Every Weierstrass equation \(W\) over \(K\) has a minimal equation of the form \(C_0\cdot W\); fix one, \(W^{\min}\), chosen once and for all. The local polynomial of \(W\) is $$f_W(T)=\begin{cases}1-aT+qT^2,\quad a=q+1-#\tilde W^{\min}(\kappa), & \text{if \(W^{\min}\) has good reduction},\ 1-T & \text{if \(W^{\min}\) has split multiplicative reduction},\ 1+T & \text{if \(W^{\min}\) has nonsplit multiplicative reduction},\ 1 & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$ where \(\#\tilde W^{\min}(\kappa)\) is the number of points of the reduction over \(\kappa\), the point at infinity included.

    Now let \(W\) be a Weierstrass equation over \(K\) that is itself minimal.

    1. If \(\Delta(W)\ne0\), then \(v(\Delta(W^{\min}))=v(\Delta(W))\) and \(v(c_4(W^{\min}))=v(c_4(W))\).
    2. If \(\Delta(W)\ne0\) and \(W\) has additive reduction, then \(f_W(T)=1\).
    3. If \(W\) has good reduction, then \(\#\tilde W^{\min}(\kappa)=\#\tilde W(\kappa)\) and $\(f_W(T)=1-\bigl(q+1-\#\tilde W(\kappa)\bigr)T+qT^2 .\)$

    So at a place of good reduction, or of additive reduction with nonzero discriminant, the local polynomial can be read off any minimal equation, not only the chosen one.

    Proof sketch. (1) \(W^{\min}=C_0\cdot W\) with \(W\) and \(C_0\cdot W\) both minimal, so by minimal Weierstrass equations (2) the \(u\) of \(C_0\) has \(v(u)=0\); since \(\Delta\) and \(c_4\) are multiplied by \(u^{-12}\) and \(u^{-4}\), their valuations do not change. (2) By (1), \(v(\Delta(W^{\min}))>0\) and \(v(c_4(W^{\min}))>0\), so \(W^{\min}\) has neither good nor multiplicative (split or nonsplit) reduction, and \(f_W=1\). (3) Good reduction gives \(\Delta(W)\ne0\), so by (1) \(W^{\min}\) has good reduction as well. By minimal Weierstrass equations (2) and (3) there is a change of variables \(C'\) over \(\kappa\) with \(\tilde W^{\min}=C'\cdot\tilde W\). The reduction \(\tilde W\) has nonzero discriminant, since \(W\) has good reduction, so by point counts (4) the two reductions have the same number of points, and the first case of the definition gives the formula.

    A finite place \(v\) of \(\mathbb Q\) is a nonzero prime ideal of the ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q\). Write \(p_v\) for the positive generator of \(v\), \(\mathbb Q_v\) for the completion of \(\mathbb Q\) at \(v\), \(\mathcal O_v\) for its valuation ring, \(\mathfrak m_v\) for the maximal ideal of \(\mathcal O_v\) and \(\kappa_v=\mathcal O_v/\mathfrak m_v\) for the residue field.

    1. \(p_v\) is a prime number. Every prime \(p\) is \(p_v\) for some finite place \(v\), and distinct finite places have distinct primes.
    2. For every natural number \(b\), only finitely many finite places \(v\) have \(\#\kappa_v\le b\).
    3. There is a ring isomorphism \(\mathcal O_v\cong\mathbb Z_{p_v}\) onto the \(p_v\)-adic integers.
    4. For an integer \(n\): \(n\) is a unit of \(\mathcal O_v\) if and only if \(p_v\nmid n\); and for every \(k\ge0\), \(n\in\mathfrak m_v^k\) if and only if \(p_v^k\mid n\). For a natural number \(n\), \(n\in\mathfrak m_v\) if and only if \(p_v\mid n\).
    5. \(p_v\) is irreducible in \(\mathcal O_v\) and generates \(\mathfrak m_v\).
    6. There is a ring isomorphism \(\kappa_v\cong\mathbb Z/p_v\mathbb Z\), and \(\#\kappa_v=p_v\).

    Proof sketch. (1) The finite places correspond bijectively to the primes by \(v\mapsto p_v\). (3) is the standard identification of the completion of \(\mathbb Q\) at \(p\) with \(\mathbb Q_p\), restricted to the valuation rings. Transport (4)–(6) along it: in \(\mathbb Z_p\) an integer is a unit exactly when its \(p\)-adic absolute value is \(1\), that is when \(p\nmid n\); \(p\) is irreducible, so it is a uniformizer and \(\mathfrak m^k=(p^k)\); an integer lies in \(p^k\mathbb Z_p\) exactly when \(p^k\) divides it; and \(\mathbb Z_p/p\mathbb Z_p\cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\), which has \(p\) elements. (2) By (6) \(\#\kappa_v=p_v\), and by (1) only finitely many places have \(p_v\le b\).
