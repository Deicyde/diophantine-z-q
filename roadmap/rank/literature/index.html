    CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk from three literature statements

    The positive-rank class-number hypothesis CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk follows from three statements about the L-series \(L(C_d,s)\) of the curves \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) and the L-series \(L(E,s)\) of elliptic curves \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Two of them are textbook theorems: the functional equation of \(L(C_d,s)\) with root number \(-1\), stated on the strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\) for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), and the positive-rank half of Gross–Zagier for every elliptic curve over \(\mathbb Q\). The third is Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b: when the genus sum of class numbers \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)\) is odd, the order of vanishing of \(L(C_d,s)\) at \(s=1\) is at most one. Row 5b follows from their Theorem 3.5(2) in reading A, which no refereed text found by a literature search carried out by AI agents states explicitly (AI agents compared the journal version of their paper with the arXiv version; Tian's expository articles were not read). The bound on the order follows from reading A, which Proposition N, an unpublished argument reviewed only by AI models and never by a human, derives from their unambiguous statements; the existence of the continuation is Hecke's.

    The order of vanishing is read off an entire continuation of the L-series from the half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\), and such a continuation is unique. The functional equation gives vanishing at the centre; with row 5b this gives an entire continuation with a simple zero at \(s=1\), and Gross–Zagier then gives a point of infinite order on \(C_d\). Row 5b in turn follows from Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5(2) in reading A (both genus sums are even when the order of vanishing is at least two) together with Hecke's continuation of \(L(C_d,s)\).

    The L-series in these statements is defined from local polynomials of minimal equations over the completions of \(\mathbb Q\). A subsection computes \(L(C_d,s)\) for odd squarefree \(d\), which includes every squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\): the equation \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) is minimal at every prime, the local factor at a prime \(p\nmid2d\) is \(1-a_pT+pT^2\) with \(a_p=p+1-\#C_d(\mathbb F_p)\) and the factor at \(p\mid2d\) is \(1\), so \(L(C_d,s)\) is the Dirichlet series of the formal standard Euler product. That computation is proved; it shows that the L-series in the functional equation, row 5b and reading A, for squarefree \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), is the standard one, and proves none of the literature statements. It says nothing about \(L(C_d,s)\) for even or non-squarefree \(d\), which Hecke's continuation also covers, nor about the other curves in Gross–Zagier.

    None of the literature statements is proved here; the reductions between them are.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb Q\) for a natural number \(d\), and the order of vanishing of an entire function at a point is its order as an analytic function, a value in \(\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\).

    Entire continuations

    DefinitionEntire continuations of the L-series of an elliptic curve#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Literature.IsLContinuation ZInQ.Literature.IsLContinuation.eq

    Let \(E\) be a Weierstrass equation over \(\mathbb Q\). For each prime \(p\) let \(f_p(T)\) be the local polynomial of a minimal model of \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\): \(1-a_pT+pT^2\) with \(a_p=p+1-\#\tilde E(\mathbb F_p)\) (the points of the reduction, the point at infinity included) if the reduction is good, \(1-T\) if it is split multiplicative, \(1+T\) if it is nonsplit multiplicative, and \(1\) if it is additive. The L-series of \(E\) is the Dirichlet series $\(L(E,s)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nn^{-s},\)$ whose coefficients \(a_n\) are those of the formal Euler product \(\prod_pf_p(p^{-s})^{-1}\); its value at \(s\) is the sum of the series where the series converges absolutely, and \(0\) elsewhere. The recipe applies to every Weierstrass equation over \(\mathbb Q\); the case that matters is an elliptic curve, where the discriminant is nonzero.

    A function \(F:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) is an entire continuation of \(L(E,s)\) if \(F\) is complex differentiable on all of \(\mathbb C\) and \(F(s)=L(E,s)\) for every complex \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\).

    Two entire continuations of the same \(L(E,s)\) are equal everywhere.

    When \(E\) is an elliptic curve, the series converges absolutely on the half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\), by Hasse's bound on \(a_p\) (a standard fact, not proved here), so there \(L(E,s)\) is the sum of the series. By the uniqueness, a statement about the order of vanishing of an entire continuation at \(s=1\) does not depend on which continuation is chosen.

    Proof sketch (of the uniqueness). Let \(F\) and \(G\) be entire continuations of \(L(E,s)\). The half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2\) is open and contains \(2\), so \(F\) and \(G\) agree on a neighbourhood of \(2\). Both are complex differentiable on \(\mathbb C\), hence analytic on the connected set \(\mathbb C\), and the identity theorem gives \(F=G\).

    ZInQ.Literature.TYZRow5b

    Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b is the following assertion. For every natural number \(d\) with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), \(d\) squarefree and genus sum of class numbers \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)=1\) in \(\mathbb Z/2\), there is an entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\) whose order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is at most \(1\).

    The assertion includes the existence of the continuation. It is Tian–Yuan–Zhang's Theorem 3.5(2) for \(n\equiv5\pmod8\) in reading A (the reading in which \(d_1=1\) is admitted), in contrapositive form and for the row \((7,3)\) only, with Hecke's continuation for the existence. No refereed text found by the project's literature search, which AI agents carried out, prints this form (their journal version was compared with the arXiv version by AI agents, and Tian's expository articles were not read); the only printed statement found is Smith's, which is unrefereed. The bound on the order follows from Proposition N, an unpublished argument from unambiguous statements of Tian, Yuan and Zhang that has not been refereed or checked by a human reader. The existence of the continuation is Hecke's, which Proposition N does not supply. It is not proved here except from two other hypotheses, reading A and Hecke's continuation.

    DefinitionThe functional equation of \(L(C_d,s)\) on the strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Literature.completedL ZInQ.Literature.FunctionalEquation5

    For a natural number \(N\), a function \(F:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) and a complex number \(s\), the completed function is $\(\Lambda_N(s)=N^{s/2}\,(2\pi)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s)\,F(s),\)$ where \(\Gamma\) is the gamma function, given the value \(0\) at its poles \(0,-1,-2,\dots\); \((2\pi)^{-s}=e^{-s\log2\pi}\); \(N^{s/2}=e^{(s/2)\log N}\) if \(N>0\); and \(0^{s/2}\) is \(0\) for \(s\ne0\) and \(1\) for \(s=0\). Below, only \(N=32d^2\) with \(d>0\) is used, and only at points \(s\) and \(2-s\) with \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\).

    The functional equation on the critical strip is the following assertion. For every natural number \(d\) with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) and \(d\) squarefree, every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\), and every complex number \(s\) with \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\), $\(\Lambda_{32d^2}(2-s)=-\Lambda_{32d^2}(s).\)$

    For every \(s\) in the strip, neither \(s\) nor \(2-s\) is a pole of \(\Gamma\), so there \(\Gamma(s)\) and \(\Gamma(2-s)\) are the true values. The textbook functional equation of \(L(C_d,s)\), which is the L-function of a Hecke character of \(\mathbb Q(i)\) since \(C_d\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb Z[i]\) (Hecke, Deuring), has conductor \(32d^2\) for odd squarefree \(d\) and root number \(-1\) for \(d\equiv5,6,7\pmod8\) (Birch–Stephens); restricted to the strip it gives this statement. The root number \(-1\) for these \(d\) is also printed by Tian, Yuan and Zhang, but no original source of the conductor and of the functional equation has been read in full. Only \(s=1\) is used. The identity is not asserted off the strip; with the value \(0\) at the poles of \(\Gamma\) it would fail at \(s=0\), where it would say \(L(C_d,2)=0\). It is a hypothesis, not proved here.

    DefinitionGross–Zagier: a simple zero gives a point of infinite order#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Literature.GrossZagier

    The Gross–Zagier statement is the following assertion. Let \(E\) be a Weierstrass equation over \(\mathbb Q\) with nonzero discriminant, that is, an elliptic curve over \(\mathbb Q\) (the equation need not be minimal), and let \(F\) be an entire continuation of \(L(E,s)\). If the order of vanishing of \(F\) at \(s=1\) is exactly \(1\), then \(E(\mathbb Q)\) has a point of infinite order.

    This is the positive-rank half of the theorem of Gross and Zagier (1986), which gives a Heegner point of infinite order over an imaginary quadratic field \(K\) when \(L'(E/K,1)\ne0\). It is applied with an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field \(K\) that satisfies the Heegner hypothesis and has \(L(E^K,1)\ne0\) (Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein 1990, through Waldspurger's formula, or Friedberg–Hoffstein 1995), and with the modularity of \(E\) (Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor 2001; Shimura 1971 for curves with complex multiplication). Since the order of vanishing of \(L(E,s)\) at \(s=1\) is odd, the root number of \(E\) is \(-1\), so the Heegner point lies in \(E(\mathbb Q)\) up to torsion and gives a point of infinite order there. Kolyvagin's conclusion that the rank is exactly one is not used. The exact theorem numbers, and the level and discriminant hypotheses of these results, have not been checked here. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.

    Vanishing at the centre and analytic rank one

    Root number \(-1\) makes the completed L-function vanish at the centre \(s=1\); with row 5b this makes the order of vanishing exactly one.

    DefinitionVanishing at the centre for \(d\equiv5\pmod8\)#✓fully proved

    Vanishing at the centre is the following assertion. For every natural number \(d\) with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) and \(d\) squarefree, and every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\), $\(F(1)=0.\)$

    This is what root number \(-1\) gives at the centre of the functional equation: there \(\Lambda(1)=-\Lambda(1)\), and \(\Gamma(1)\ne0\). The functional equation gives vanishing at the centre derives it from the functional equation on the critical strip. As stated here it is a hypothesis, not proved here.

    TheoremThe functional equation gives vanishing at the centre#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Literature.rootNumberVanishing_of_functionalEquation

    The functional equation of \(L(C_d,s)\) on the critical strip implies vanishing at the centre: if for every squarefree natural number \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), every entire continuation \(F\) of \(L(C_d,s)\) and every \(s\) with \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<2\) one has \(\Lambda_{32d^2}(2-s)=-\Lambda_{32d^2}(s)\), then \(F(1)=0\) for every such \(d\) and \(F\).

    Proof sketch. Let \(d\) and \(F\) be given and take \(s=1\), which lies in the strip. Since \(2-1=1\), the functional equation reads \(\Lambda_{32d^2}(1)=-\Lambda_{32d^2}(1)\), so \(\Lambda_{32d^2}(1)=0\). Since \(\Gamma(1)=1\), this says \((32d^2)^{1/2}(2\pi)^{-1}F(1)=0\). As \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), \(d\ne0\), so \(32d^2\ne0\); also \(2\pi\ne0\). A nonzero base raised to a nonzero exponent is nonzero, so both powers are nonzero and \(F(1)=0\).

    TheoremRow 5b, vanishing at the centre and Gross–Zagier give CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk#✓fully proved

    Tian–Yuan–Zhang's row 5b, vanishing at the centre and the Gross–Zagier statement together imply the class-number hypothesis in positive-rank form CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk: for every natural number \(d\) with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), \(d\) squarefree and \(T^{\rm Cl}_{5b}(d)=1\), the curve \(C_d\) has a rational point of infinite order.

    ZInQ.Literature.TYZThm35ReadingA

    This is a consequence of Theorem 3.5(2) of Tian, Yuan and Zhang for \(n=d\equiv5\pmod8\), under one reading of their genus sums, reading A: the decompositions of \(n\) are the set partitions of its primes, each counted once, with a trivial factor \(d_1=1\) allowed. Only the case \(\mathcal L(n)=0\) of their theorem is needed: they define the invariant \(\mathcal L(n)\) from \(L(C_n,1)\) or \(L'(C_n,1)\) and set it to \(0\) exactly when the order of vanishing at \(s=1\) is at least \(2\).
