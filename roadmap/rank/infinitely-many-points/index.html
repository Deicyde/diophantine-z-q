    The rank hypotheses as infinitely many rational points

    The positive-rank hypotheses CR₃^Rk, CR₃^TYZ,Rk, CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk and CR₃^DL,Rk conclude that a curve \(C_d:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has a rational point of infinite order, so their statements involve the group law on \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\). This section restates each of them with the conclusion that the equation \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions, a statement that mentions no group law. For \(d\ne0\) the two conclusions are equivalent: a point of infinite order has infinitely many distinct multiples, and conversely, by Mordell–Weil, a finitely generated group of points all of finite order is finite. Hence each new form is equivalent to its positive-rank form, with the same premises, and the main theorem holds under the minimal height hypothesis and any of them.

    Throughout, \(C_d\) is the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\), as in the rest of the positive-rank chapter.

    Infinitely many points and infinite order

    DefinitionInfinitely many rational solutions of \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.InfinitelyManyPoints

    For a natural number \(d\), the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational points when the set $\(\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2 : y^2=x^3-d^2x\}\)$ of its affine rational solutions is infinite.

    The statement involves only rational solutions of one equation: no group law, no point at infinity and no condition on \(d\).

    Remark (classical meaning). For a squarefree positive integer \(d\), the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) having infinitely many rational points is the classical statement that \(d\) is a congruent number. This is a remark about what the condition means classically; it is not proved here, and no article uses it.

    def InfinitelyManyPoints (d : ℕ) : Prop :=
  Set.Infinite {p : ℚ × ℚ | p.2 ^ 2 = p.1 ^ 3 - (d : ℚ) ^ 2 * p.1}

    TheoremA point of infinite order exists iff there are infinitely many rational points#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.exists_infinite_order_iff_infinitelyManyPoints ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.infinitelyManyPoints_iff_infinite_points ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.infPtsMap_bijective ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.infPtsMap ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.infPts_nonsingular

    Let \(d\ne0\) be a natural number and \(C_d\) the curve \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Then \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) contains a point of infinite order if and only if \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions \((x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2\).

    Along the way, for \(d\ne0\):

    • every rational solution \((x,y)\) of \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) is a nonsingular point of \(C_d\);
    • the map that sends one extra element to the point at infinity \(O\) and each rational solution \((x,y)\) to the affine point \((x,y)\) is a bijection from the set of rational solutions with one extra element adjoined onto \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\);
    • hence \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions if and only if \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is infinite.

    Proof sketch. The discriminant of \(C_d\) is a nonzero multiple of \(d^6\), hence nonzero, so every solution of the equation is a nonsingular point. The points of \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) are \(O\) and the affine points, which are exactly the rational solutions; so the map above is a bijection, and the solution set is infinite exactly when \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is. If \(P\) has infinite order, the multiples \(nP\) (\(n\ge0\)) are pairwise distinct, so \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is infinite. Conversely, suppose every point has finite order. By Mordell–Weil, \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is finitely generated, and a finitely generated abelian group in which every element has finite order is finite; so \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) is finite, and there are only finitely many solutions.

    The hypotheses

    The four hypotheses keep the premises of CR₃^Rk, CR₃^TYZ,Rk, CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk and CR₃^DL,Rk.

    DefinitionThe certified rank hypothesis in infinitely-many-points form#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankInf

    The hypothesis CR₃^Inf is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be a squarefree positive integer with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\). Suppose that the 2-Selmer group \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) has exactly \(8\) elements and contains a pair \((a,b)\) with \(|a|\equiv7\pmod8\). Then \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions \((x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2\).

    The premises are those of CR₃^Rk; only the conclusion changes, from "a point of infinite order on \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\)" to "infinitely many rational solutions", which mentions no group law. The two hypotheses are equivalent, so CR₃^Inf rests on the same theorems: Tian–Yuan–Zhang as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed, and Gross–Zagier with an auxiliary field. None of them is proved here, so CR₃^Inf is a hypothesis.

    Remark (classical meaning). For the squarefree \(d\) here, infinitely many rational solutions is the classical statement that \(d\) is a congruent number. This is not part of the hypothesis and is not used.

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankTYZInf

    The hypothesis CR₃^TYZ,Inf is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be a squarefree positive integer with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) whose genus sum of row 5b, built from Rédei's bits, is odd: \(T_{5b}(d)=1\). Then \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions \((x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2\).

    DefinitionThe class-number hypothesis in infinitely-many-points form#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankTYZClInf

    The hypothesis CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Inf is the following assertion. Let \(d\) be a squarefree positive integer with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\) whose genus sum of class numbers $\(T^{\mathrm{Cl}}_{5b}(d)=\sum_\pi\prod_{B\in\pi}\#\bigl(2\,\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d_B}))\bigr)\pmod 2\)$ is \(1\). Here \(\pi\) runs over the set partitions of the primes of \(d\) with exactly one block with \(d_B\equiv7\), exactly one with \(d_B\equiv3\), and all other blocks with \(d_B\equiv1\pmod8\); \(d_B\) is the product of the primes in \(B\), and \(2\,\mathrm{Cl}(K)\) is the subgroup of squares of the class group of the ring of integers of \(K\). Then \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions \((x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2\).

    The premises are those of CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk; only the conclusion changes, from a point of infinite order to infinitely many rational solutions, which mentions no group law. The two hypotheses are equivalent, so CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Inf rests on Tian–Yuan–Zhang with their own genus class numbers, as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed, followed by Gross–Zagier with an auxiliary field. No Rédei formula and no condition on \(\mathrm{Sel}_2(C_d)\) enter. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.

    Remark (classical meaning). For the squarefree \(d\) here, infinitely many rational solutions is the classical statement that \(d\) is a congruent number. This is not part of the hypothesis and is not used.

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.CertifiedRankDLInf

    The hypothesis CR₃^DL,Inf is the following assertion. Let \(\beta,\theta\) be integers and \(p_0\) a natural number such that:

    • \(\beta\) is odd and squarefree with \(|\beta|\equiv7\pmod8\), and \(p_0\) is a prime dividing \(\beta\);
    • \(\theta>0\) is squarefree with \(\theta\equiv1\pmod8\);
    • \(\beta\) and \(\theta\) are coprime;
    • \([\beta/\ell]=0\) for every prime \(\ell\mid\theta\), and \([\theta/p]=0\) for every prime \(p\mid\beta\).

    Then there are slot data \(q_0\), \(r_p\) (\(p\in P(\beta)\setminus\{p_0\}\)) and \(r_\ell\) (\(\ell\in P(\theta)\)) for \(\beta,\theta,p_0\), satisfying the slot conditions, such that, with $\(d_1=|\beta|\,q_0\prod_{p}r_p\prod_{\ell}r_\ell ,\)$ both \(y^2=x^3-d_1^2x\) and \(y^2=x^3-(d_1|\theta|)^2x\) have infinitely many rational solutions \((x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2\).

    The premises are those of CR₃^DL,Rk; only the conclusion changes, from points of infinite order on the two curves to infinitely many rational solutions of their equations, which mentions no group law. The assertion still depends on the slot conditions, so a change to the slot construction changes what it asserts. The two hypotheses are equivalent. Since CR₃^DL,Rk follows from CR₃^Rk and from CR₃^TYZ,Rk, the equivalences make CR₃^DL,Inf follow from CR₃^Inf and from CR₃^TYZ,Inf, so it rests on the same theorems: Tian–Yuan–Zhang as completed by Proposition N, which is not refereed, and Gross–Zagier with an auxiliary field. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.

    Equivalence with the positive-rank forms

    Each equivalence holds pointwise, since every curve in these hypotheses has \(d\equiv5\pmod8\), so \(d\ne0\). The main theorem in each of the four forms is the main theorem from the infinitely-many-points forms.

    TheoremThe infinitely-many-points forms are equivalent to the positive-rank forms#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.certifiedRankInf_iff_certifiedRankRk ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.certifiedRankTYZInf_iff_certifiedRankTYZRk ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.certifiedRankTYZClInf_iff_certifiedRankTYZClRk ZInQ.RouteK.Rank.certifiedRankDLInf_iff_certifiedRankDLRk

    The hypotheses CR₃^Inf and CR₃^Rk are equivalent. Likewise CR₃^TYZ,Inf is equivalent to CR₃^TYZ,Rk, CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Inf to CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Rk, and CR₃^DL,Inf to CR₃^DL,Rk.

    Proof sketch. In each pair the two hypotheses have the same premises and differ only in the conclusion, so it suffices to compare the conclusions for each fixed value of the data. For CR₃^Inf, CR₃^TYZ,Inf and CR₃^TYZ_Cl,Inf, fix \(d\) with \(d\equiv5\pmod8\); then \(d\ne0\), and by the equivalence of infinite order and infinitely many points, \(C_d(\mathbb Q)\) has a point of infinite order exactly when \(y^2=x^3-d^2x\) has infinitely many rational solutions. For CR₃^DL,Inf, fix \(\beta,\theta,p_0\) satisfying the premises and a choice of slot data. By the realization, \(d_1\equiv5\pmod8\), and \(d_1|\theta|\equiv5\pmod8\) as well (as in CR₃^DL,Rk from CR₃^Rk), so both are nonzero and the same equivalence applies to each of the two curves.
