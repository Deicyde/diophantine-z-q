    The elliptic model of integer indices

    The polynomial never manipulates an integer index directly. It represents the index \(n\) by the five rational coordinates of the point \([n]P_0\) on an elliptic curve over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), and this chapter proves that the representation is faithful: the canonical codes form exactly the infinite cyclic group \(2E(K_0)=\mathbb ZP_0\) (input \(\mathsf G\) of the assembly lemma), the membership polynomial recognizes exactly those codes among arbitrary rational inputs, and raw addition computes the group law on canonical codes. Beyond the classical background (two-isogeny descent, reduction modulo primes, heights and division polynomials), the one input beyond the manuscript's elementary arguments is the Mordell-Weil theorem for \(E/K_0\), which is proved from the descent index with Mathlib's height theory, so it is not a hypothesis; every finite check is isolated as its own statement.

    Point codes

    Let \(K_0=\mathbb Q(s)\) with \(s^2=2\), \(d=75-53s\), \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) and \(P_0=\bigl(\frac{1641-1160s}{8},\frac{-123720+87483s}{32}\bigr)\). A point is coded by five rationals: \((1,0,0,0,0)\) for the identity and \((0,x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1)\) for an affine point with \(x=x_0+x_1s\), \(y=y_0+y_1s\); \(\iota(n)\) is the code of \([n]P_0\). The integer index and the coordinates play distinct roles throughout.

    The generator certificate

    By rank one, \(R=(1+s,2s)\) on \(C:y^2=x^3-(3-s)^2x\) is \([m]S+T\) with \(S\) a generator modulo torsion and \(m\) odd. If \(|m|\ge3\), the generator has \(x\)-height below \(\log9\), and the finite search shows that every such nontorsion point is \(\pm(R+T')\) with \(T'\) two-torsion, so has the same canonical height as \(R\), contradicting \(\widehat h(R)=m^2\widehat h(S)>0\). Hence \(2C(K_0)\) is generated by \(2R\), and the change of variables sends \(2R\) to \(P_0\).

    TheoremThe generator certificate#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.generator_certificate ZInQ.carrierD_eq_zmultiples ZInQ.indexEquivCarrier ZInQ.iota_injective

    The point \(P_0\) has infinite order and $\(2E(K_0)=\mathbb ZP_0 .\)$ Consequently \(n\mapsto[n]P_0\) is an isomorphism \(\mathbb Z\to2E(K_0)=D\), the carrier \(D\) is torsion-free, and \(n\mapsto\iota(n)\) is injective.

    This is Proposition 3.1. The manuscript assumes the Mordell–Weil theorem to pass from the descent index and the torsion bound to rank one; here Mordell–Weil is itself proved from the descent, so the statement has no hypothesis. That \(P_0\) has infinite order already follows from the torsion theorem.

    Proof sketch. Work on \(C\) through the isomorphism \(\theta\). By the rank-one theorem, \(C(K_0)=\mathbb ZS\oplus C(K_0)[2]\) with \(S\) of infinite order; write \(R=[m]S+T\) with \(T\in C(K_0)[2]\). Then \(m\) is odd, in particular \(m\ne0\). Suppose \(|m|\ge3\). Then \(h_x(S)<\log9\), so by the search box \(x(S)\) lies in the candidate set of the finite search, and \(f(x(S))=y(S)^2\) is a square; hence \(x(S)\) is one of the seven survivors. The survivors \(0,\pm\delta\) are abscissas of two-torsion points, excluded because \(S\) has infinite order. The other four are the abscissas of \(R+T'\) for \(T'\in C(K_0)[2]\), so \(S=\pm(R+T')\) for some \(T'\) (equal abscissas force equal or opposite points), and the basic properties give \(\widehat h(S)=\widehat h(R)\). But \(\widehat h(R)=\widehat h([m]S)=m^2\widehat h(S)=m^2\widehat h(R)\) with \(m^2\ge9\) and \(\widehat h(R)>0\), a contradiction. So \(m=\pm1\), \(C(K_0)=\mathbb ZR\oplus C(K_0)[2]\) and \(2C(K_0)=\mathbb Z\,[2]R\), with \([2]R\) of infinite order. Applying \(\theta^{-1}\), which sends \([2]R\) to \(P_0\) by the certificate for \(R\), gives \(2E(K_0)=\mathbb ZP_0\) with \(P_0\) of infinite order. Injectivity of \(\iota\) follows from injectivity of codes.

    Membership and raw addition

    Membership in \(2E(K_0)\), apart from the identity, is expressed by choosing a preimage with nonzero ordinate and its tangent slope. It is an all-input statement, and with the generator certificate it shows that accepted inputs are exactly the codes \(\iota(n)\), each with a unique index. The template itself is defined in the specification chapter (the membership template).

    TheoremPolynomial membership in the doubled group#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.rel_member_iff

    For every \(p\in\mathbb Q^5\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{member}}(p)\iff p=\operatorname{code}(Q)\ \text{for some }Q\in2E(K_0),\)$ where \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{member}}(p)\) means that the polynomial of the membership template vanishes at \(p\) and some rational values of its seven witnesses \(X=(X_0,X_1)\), \(Y=(Y_0,Y_1)\), \(H=(H_0,H_1)\), \(u\). This is an all-input statement: \(p\) is an arbitrary rational \(5\)-tuple, not assumed to be a code.

    Explicitly, apart from the identity branch \(p=(1,0,0,0,0)\), the template asks for \(X,Y,H\in K_0\) and \(u\in\mathbb Q\) with $\(p_0=0,\quad Y^2=X^3-d^2X,\quad u\operatorname N(Y)=1,\quad 2YH=3X^2-d^2,\quad x=H^2-2X,\quad y=H(X-x)-Y,\)$ where \(x=p_1+p_2s\), \(y=p_3+p_4s\), each field equation being two rational equations (the template writes the last as \(y=H(X-(H^2-2X))-Y\), equivalent given the abscissa equation).

    Proof sketch. The template polynomial is the square of the compilation of its formula, so it vanishes exactly when one branch holds. If the identity branch holds, \(p=\operatorname{code}(O)\) and \(O\in2E(K_0)\). If the doubling branch holds, \((X,Y)\in E(K_0)\), \(Y\ne0\) because \(u\operatorname N(Y)=1\), \(H=(3X^2-d^2)/(2Y)\) is the tangent slope, and \((x,y)\) is given by the doubling formula, so \(p=\operatorname{code}([2](X,Y))\). Conversely let \(Q=[2]T\). If \(Q=O\), the identity branch holds with arbitrary witnesses. Otherwise \(T=(X,Y)\) is affine with \(Y\ne0\), since a point with \(Y=0\) has \([2]T=O\): every nonidentity double has a preimage with nonzero ordinate. Take \(H\) the tangent slope and \(u=\operatorname N(Y)^{-1}\); the doubling branch holds with \(p=\operatorname{code}(Q)\).

    TheoremRaw addition on canonical codes#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.rel_rawAdd_iff

    For all \(P,Q\in E(K_0)\) and every \(r\in\mathbb Q^5\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{raw\_add}}\bigl(\operatorname{code}(P),\operatorname{code}(Q),r\bigr)\iff r=\operatorname{code}(P+Q),\)$ where \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{raw\_add}}(p,q,r)\) means that the polynomial of the raw addition template vanishes at \((p,q,r)\) and some rational values of its six witnesses (two slope pairs \(m_g,m_d\) and two inverse witnesses \(u_g,u_d\)). No assumption beyond \(P,Q\in E(K_0)\) is needed; two-torsion inputs are included.

    Write \(x,y\) for the abscissa and ordinate encoded by \(p\), \(x',y'\) for those of \(q\), and \(x_r,y_r\) for those of \(r\). The five branches of the template are: (1) \(p=O\) and \(q=r\); (2) \(p_0=0\), \(q=O\) and \(p=r\); (3) \(p_0=q_0=0\), \(x=x'\), \(y=-y'\) and \(r=O\); (4) \(p_0=q_0=0\), \(u_g\operatorname N(x'-x)=1\), \((x'-x)m_g=y'-y\), \(r_0=0\), \(x_r=m_g^2-x-x'\), \(y_r=m_g(x-x_r)-y\); (5) \(p_0=q_0=0\), \(x=x'\), \(y=y'\), \(u_d\operatorname N(y)=1\), \(2ym_d=3x^2-d^2\), \(r_0=0\), \(x_r=m_d^2-x-x'\), \(y_r=m_d(x-x_r)-y\). Here \(O=(1,0,0,0,0)\).

    Proof sketch. The template polynomial is the square of the compilation of its formula, so it vanishes exactly when one branch holds, the witnesses of the other branches being arbitrary. Cases. \(P=O\): branches 2-5 need \(p_0=0\), and branch 1 holds exactly when \(r=\operatorname{code}(Q)=\operatorname{code}(P+Q)\). \(P\) affine, \(Q=O\): branch 1 needs \(p_0=1\), branches 3-5 need \(q_0=0\), and branch 2 holds exactly when \(r=\operatorname{code}(P)\). Both affine: if \(x\ne x'\), branches 3 and 5 fail, branch 4 is satisfiable (\(\operatorname N(x'-x)\ne0\)), it forces \(m_g\) to be the chord slope, and then \(r\) is the code of the chord sum. If \(x=x'\) then \(y'=\pm y\) by the curve equation, and branch 4 fails. If \(y=-y'\), branch 3 holds with \(r=\operatorname{code}(O)=\operatorname{code}(P+Q)\), and branch 5 would need \(y=y'\) and \(\operatorname N(y)\ne0\), i.e. \(y=0\) and \(y\ne0\). If \(y=y'\ne0\), branch 3 fails and branch 5 forces the tangent slope and \(r=\operatorname{code}([2]P)\). In each case the forced \(r\) is the code given by the group law of \(E\), and conversely that \(r\) with the forced witnesses satisfies the branch.
