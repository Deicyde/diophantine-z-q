    Heights and the finite search

    The \(x\)-height on \(C\) changes under doubling by a factor four up to the explicit constants \(\log392\) and \(\log676\), coming from the duplication forms, their archimedean sizes and their common divisor at \((s)\) and \((\delta)\).

    DefinitionThe \(x\)-height on \(C(K_0)\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.hK0 ZInQ.hx

    For \(x\in K_0\) let \(h(x)\) be the absolute logarithmic Weil height, $\(h(x)=\frac1{[K_0:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{v}n_v\log\max(1,|x|_v) =\frac12\sum_v n_v\log\max(1,|x|_v),\)$ the sum over the places \(v\) of \(K_0\) (the two real places and the finite places), with \(|\cdot|_v\) extending the usual absolute value of \(\mathbb Q\) and local degrees \(n_v\).     LemmaThe height of a fraction in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.hK0_div ZInQ.hK0_div_of_isCoprime ZInQ.hK0_ratCast

    Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z[s]\) with \(b\ne0\), and let \(g\) be a greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\) in \(\mathbb Z[s]\). Then $\(h(a/b)=\frac12\Bigl(\log\max(|\sigma_+a|,|\sigma_+b|)+\log\max(|\sigma_-a|,|\sigma_-b|) -\log|\operatorname N(g)|\Bigr).\)$ In particular, for coprime \(a,b\), \(\exp(2h(a/b))=\max(|\sigma_+a|,|\sigma_+b|)\cdot\max(|\sigma_-a|,|\sigma_-b|)\); for a rational \(p/q\) in lowest terms with \(q>0\), \(h(p/q)=\log\max(|p|,q)\); and \(h\ge0\).

    Proof sketch. Write \(a=ga'\), \(b=gb'\) with \(a',b'\) coprime; the right side is unchanged because \(\prod_\sigma|\sigma g|=|\operatorname N(g)|\). For coprime \(a',b'\) and \(x=a'/b'\), \(\sum_v n_v\log\max(1,|x|_v)=\sum_v n_v\log\max(|a'|_v,|b'|_v)-\sum_vn_v\log|b'|_v\); the last sum vanishes by the product formula, and at every finite place \(\max(|a'|_v,|b'|_v)=1\) by coprimality, leaving the two real places. Integers \(p,q\) coprime in \(\mathbb Z\) are coprime in \(\mathbb Z[s]\), and then both real places give \(\max(|p|,q)\).

    LemmaArchimedean bounds for the duplication forms#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.duplication_archimedean

    Let \(\Delta\) be a real number with \(1\le\Delta\le5\), let \(a,b\in\mathbb R\), \(M=\max(|a|,|b|)\), \(F=(a^2+\Delta^2b^2)^2\) and \(G=4ab(a^2-\Delta^2b^2)\). Then $\(M^4\le\max(|F|,|G|)\le676\,M^4 .\)$ Since \(\sigma_\pm(\delta)=3\mp\sqrt2\in[1,5]\), at either real embedding of \(K_0\) the values of the duplication forms satisfy \(\max(|\sigma a|,|\sigma b|)^4\le\max(|\sigma F_2(a,b)|,|\sigma G_2(a,b)|)\le676\max(|\sigma a|,|\sigma b|)^4\).

    Proof sketch. \(a^2+\Delta^2b^2\ge a^2+b^2\ge M^2\) gives the lower bound from \(F\) alone. \(a^2+\Delta^2b^2\le26M^2\) gives \(F\le676M^4\), and \(|G|\le4|a||b|(a^2+\Delta^2b^2)\le104M^4\). The constant \(676=26^2\).

      max |dupF Δ a b| |dupG Δ a b| ≤ 676 * max |a| |b| ^ 4

    LemmaThe common divisor of the duplication forms#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.duplication_common_divisor

    Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z[s]\) be coprime and let \(g\) be a greatest common divisor of \(F_2(a,b)\) and \(G_2(a,b)\) (assume they are not both zero). Then \(v_\pi(g)=0\) for every prime \(\pi\) associate to neither \(s\) nor \(\delta\), \(v_s(g)\le6\) and \(v_\delta(g)\le4\). Consequently $\(|\operatorname N(g)|\le2^6\cdot7^4=392^2 .\)$

    Proof sketch. It suffices to bound \(v_\pi(F_2(a,b))\) whenever \(\pi\) divides both forms. Primes \(\pi\nmid2\delta\). If \(\pi\mid a\) then \(\pi\mid a^2+\delta^2b^2\) forces \(\pi\mid b\); similarly \(\pi\mid b\) forces \(\pi\mid a\); and \(\pi\mid a^2-\delta^2b^2\) together with \(\pi\mid a^2+\delta^2b^2\) gives \(\pi\mid2a^2\). All contradict coprimality. At \(\delta\). If \(\delta\nmid a\), \(F_2\equiv a^4\not\equiv0\). If \(\delta\mid a\), then \(\delta\nmid b\) and with \(a=\delta a'\), \(F_2=\delta^4(a'^2+b^2)^2\); since \(-1\) is not a square modulo \(\delta\) (residue field \(\mathbb F_7\)), \(\delta\nmid a'^2+b^2\), so \(v_\delta(F_2)=4\). At \(s\). If exactly one of \(a,b\) is divisible by \(s\), \(a^2+\delta^2b^2\) is prime to \(s\) and \(v_s(F_2)=0\). If neither is, the dyadic valuation computation gives \(v_s(a^2+\delta^2b^2)\in\{2,3\}\), so \(v_s(F_2)\le6\). Finally \(|\operatorname N(s)|=2\), \(|\operatorname N(\delta)|=7\) and \(2^6\cdot7^4=153664=392^2\).

    TheoremHeight bounds for doubling on \(C\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.hx_two_smul_bounds

    For every \(T\in C(K_0)\), $\(4h_x(T)-\log392\ \le\ h_x([2]T)\ \le\ 4h_x(T)+\log676 .\)$

    Proof sketch. If \(T=O\) both sides are trivial. If \(T=(x,0)\), then \([2]T=O\) and \(x\in\{0,\pm\delta\}\) with \(h(0)=0\) and \(h(\pm\delta)=\tfrac12\log7\) (since \(\operatorname N(\delta)=7\) and both embeddings of \(\delta\) exceed \(1\)); then \(4h_x(T)-\log392\le2\log7-\log392<0\) and the upper bound is trivial.     The canonical height

    The canonical height is the limit of \(4^{-m}h_x([2^m]T)\); it differs from \(h_x\) by bounded amounts and is a quadratic form.

    DefinitionThe canonical height on \(C(K_0)\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.canonHeight

    For \(T\in C(K_0)\) the canonical height \(\widehat h(T)\) is defined by $\(2\,\widehat h(T)=\lim_{m\to\infty}4^{-m}\,h_x\bigl([2^m]T\bigr).\)$ Formally, \(\widehat h(T)\) is half the limit of the real sequence \(4^{-m}h_x([2^m]T)\); that the sequence converges for every \(T\) is proved, from the duplication height bounds, with the basic properties of \(\widehat h\). The factor \(2\) is the manuscript's normalization, under which \(2\widehat h\) is comparable with \(h_x\).

    noncomputable def canonHeight (T : curveC.toAffine.Point) : ℝ :=
  (limUnder atTop fun m : ℕ => hx ((2 ^ m) • T) / 4 ^ m) / 2
    def curveC : WeierstrassCurve K0 := shortCurve (-delta ^ 2)

    TheoremThe canonical height is quadratic#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.canonHeight_parallelogram ZInQ.canonHeight_zsmul

    For all \(P,Q\in C(K_0)\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z\), $\(\widehat h(P+Q)+\widehat h(P-Q)=2\widehat h(P)+2\widehat h(Q),\qquad \widehat h([m]P)=m^2\,\widehat h(P).\)$

    Proof sketch. (Silverman, Theorem VIII.9.3; Néron-Tate.) The naive height satisfies the approximate parallelogram law: there is a constant \(c\) with \(|h_x(P+Q)+h_x(P-Q)-2h_x(P)-2h_x(Q)|\le c\) for all \(P,Q\) (Silverman, Theorem VIII.6.2). Apply it to \([2^n]P,[2^n]Q\), divide by \(4^n\) and let \(n\to\infty\): since \([2^n](P\pm Q)=[2^n]P\pm[2^n]Q\), the error \(c/4^n\) tends to \(0\) and the exact parallelogram law follows. Taking \(Q=P\) gives \(\widehat h(O)=0\) and the case \(m=2\); induction on \(m\ge1\) with \(\widehat h((m+1)P)=2\widehat h(mP)+2\widehat h(P)-\widehat h((m-1)P)\) gives \(m^2\widehat h(P)\), and \(\widehat h(-P)=\widehat h(P)\) gives negative \(m\). The constant \(c\) is not needed explicitly.
