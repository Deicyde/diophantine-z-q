    The field, the curve and division polynomials

    Everything takes place over \(K_0=\mathbb Q(s)\), \(s^2=2\), written in the basis \(1,s\), so that each field equation is two rational equations. A field element is nonzero exactly when its norm is, which is how one rational witness expresses every nonvanishing condition; the pair arithmetic and the inverse witnesses are set up in the specification chapter (pair arithmetic, inverse witnesses).

    DefinitionThe field \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.K0 ZInQ.sqrt2 ZInQ.epsilon ZInQ.delta ZInQ.Zs

    Let \(K_0=\mathbb Q(s)\) with \(s^2=2\). Every element of \(K_0\) is uniquely \(u+vs\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb Q\), its components; addition is componentwise and $\((a+bs)(c+es)=(ac+2be)+(ae+bc)s .\)$ The conjugate of \(z=u+vs\) is \(\bar z=u-vs\), the norm is \(\operatorname N(z)=z\bar z=u^2-2v^2\in\mathbb Q\) and the trace is \(\operatorname{Tr}(z)=2u\). The two real embeddings are \(\sigma_+(u+vs)=u+v\sqrt2\) and \(\sigma_-(u+vs)=u-v\sqrt2\); \(\operatorname N(z)=\sigma_+(z)\sigma_-(z)\). An element is totally positive if both embeddings are positive, and its sign vector is the pair of signs of \(\sigma_+(z),\sigma_-(z)\).

    The subring \(\mathbb Z[s]=\{u+vs:u,v\in\mathbb Z\}\) has fraction field \(K_0\). Two fixed elements recur throughout the chapter: $\(\epsilon=1+s,\qquad \delta=3-s .\)$

    The field is \(\mathbb Q\) adjoined a square root of \(2\); \(2\) is not a rational square, so \(K_0\) is a field of degree two over \(\mathbb Q\).

    Put \(d=75-53s\in\mathbb Z[s]\), so that $\(d^2=11243-7950s,\qquad A:=-d^2=-11243+7950s,\qquad \operatorname N(d)=75^2-2\cdot53^2=7 .\)$ The curve \(E\) over \(K_0\) is the short Weierstrass curve $\(E:\ y^2=x^3-d^2x=x^3+Ax .\)$ Its discriminant \(-64A^3=64d^6\) is nonzero, so \(E\) is an elliptic curve.     Division polynomials

    Fixed multiples \([n]Q\), for fixed integers \(n\) that may have millions of digits, are expressed by the normalized division polynomials of the specification (their definition). The specification chapter states their classical multiplication formulas as a proposition (the multiplication-by-n formulas) and derives from it the semantics of the fixed-scalar templates (fixed-scalar semantics). Here the proposition is proved as a background theorem, and it gives the fixed point codes \(\iota(n)\). The recursion is only ever used symbolically. The proof goes through Jacobian coordinates: the triples \((\phi_n,\omega_n,\psi_n)\) satisfy exact doubling and adding identities, first at the generic point of the curve, where no division polynomial vanishes, and an induction on \(n\) shows that these triples represent the multiples \([n]P\).

    TheoremDivision polynomials compute multiples#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.mulByN_formulas

    The multiplication-by-\(n\) formulas hold for \(E\) over \(K_0\): for every affine point \(P=(x,y)\in E(K_0)\) and every integer \(n\ne0\),

    1. \([n]P=O\) if and only if \(\psi_n(P)=0\);
    2. if \([n]P\ne O\), then $\(x([n]P)=x-\frac{\psi_{n+1}(P)\,\psi_{n-1}(P)}{\psi_n(P)^2},\qquad y([n]P)=\frac{\psi_{2n}(P)}{2\,\psi_n(P)^4},\)$

    where \(\psi_k\) are the normalized division polynomials of \(y^2=x^3+Ax\) with \(A=-d^2=-11243+7950s\). More generally the same holds for \(y^2=x^3+Ax\) over any field of characteristic zero with \(A\ne0\).

    Proof sketch. This is the classical theorem (Sutherland, Lecture 5, Lemmas 5.20 and 5.24 and Theorem 5.21; Silverman, Exercise 3.7). A route through Jacobian coordinates avoids divisors and handles the identity uniformly. Represent points of \(E\) by nonzero triples \((X,Y,Z)\) with \(Y^2=X^3+AXZ^4\), up to the weighted scaling \(u\cdot(X,Y,Z)=(u^2X,u^3Y,uZ)\) with \(u\ne0\): the triple represents the affine point \((X/Z^2,Y/Z^3)\) if \(Z\ne0\) and \(O\) if \(Z=0\). The Jacobian doubling formula sends a representative of \(Q\) to a representative of \([2]Q\), for every \(Q\), and the Jacobian addition formula sends representatives of two distinct points \(Q_1\ne Q_2\) to a representative of \(Q_1+Q_2\) (including the cases where one of them is \(O\) or \(Q_1=-Q_2\)).

    Fix an affine \(P=(x,y)\), put \(\phi_n=x\psi_n^2-\psi_{n+1}\psi_{n-1}\) and \(\omega_n=\psi_{2n}/(2\psi_n)\) (a polynomial in \(x,y\)), and write \(J_n(P)=(\phi_n(P),\omega_n(P),\psi_n(P))\). The doubling and adding identities \(\mathrm{dbl}(J_k)=J_{2k}\) and \(\mathrm{add}(J_{k+1},J_k)=(-1)\cdot J_{2k+1}\) (\(k\ge1\)) hold at every point of the curve: at the generic point of \(y^2=x^3+Ax\), in the function field, no \(\psi_n\) with \(n\ne0\) vanishes, so there the abscissas of the multiples can be computed by strong induction from the recurrences of \(\psi\) (the duplication formula for \(n=2m\), the chord through \([m+1]P\) and \([m]P\) for \(n=2m+1\)), which gives the identities in the coordinate ring; evaluating at \(P\) gives them at \(P\). Claim: for every \(n\ge1\), \(J_n(P)\) is a representative of \([n]P\) (in particular a nonzero triple). Strong induction on \(n\):

    • \(n=1\): \(\phi_1=x\), \(\omega_1=y\), \(\psi_1=1\).
    • \(n=2k\), \(k\ge1\): \(J_k(P)\) represents \([k]P\), so its double represents \([2k]P\), and the double of \(J_k\) is \(J_{2k}\).
    • \(n=2k+1\), \(k\ge1\): \(J_{k+1}(P)\) and \(J_k(P)\) represent \([k+1]P\) and \([k]P\), which are distinct because their difference is \(P\ne O\); so their sum \(\mathrm{add}(J_{k+1},J_k)(P)=(-1)\cdot J_{2k+1}(P)\) represents \([2k+1]P\), and so does \(J_{2k+1}(P)\).

    Reading off the third coordinate gives part 1 for \(n\ge1\). If \(\psi_n(P)\ne0\), then \([n]P=(\phi_n/\psi_n^2,\ \omega_n/\psi_n^3)\) evaluated at \(P\), and \(\phi_n/\psi_n^2=x-\psi_{n+1}\psi_{n-1}/\psi_n^2\), \(\omega_n/\psi_n^3=2\psi_n\omega_n/(2\psi_n^4)=\psi_{2n}/(2\psi_n^4)\): part 2. Negative \(n\) follow from \(\psi_{-n}=-\psi_n\) (so \(\psi_{-n\pm1}=-\psi_{n\mp1}\) and \(\psi_{-2n}=-\psi_{2n}\)) and \([-n]P=-[n]P=(x([n]P),-y([n]P))\).

    Instance\(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) is a principal ideal domain#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Zs.isPrincipalIdealRing ZInQ.Zs.uniqueFactorizationMonoid

    The ring \(\mathbb Z[s]\) is a principal ideal domain (the ring of integers of \(K_0\) has class number one). Consequently it is a unique factorization domain with fraction field \(K_0\): every \(x\in K_0^\times\) is \(x=u\prod_\pi\pi^{v_\pi(x)}\) with \(u\in\mathbb Z[s]^\times\), \(\pi\) running over a set of representatives of the prime elements up to units, and integers \(v_\pi(x)\), almost all zero, uniquely determined by \(x\). The valuations satisfy \(v_\pi(xy)=v_\pi(x)+v_\pi(y)\) and \(v_\pi(x+y)\ge\min(v_\pi(x),v_\pi(y))\), with equality when the two valuations differ.

    Proof sketch. The manuscript's route: \(K_0\) has discriminant \(8\) and the Minkowski bound is \(\frac{2!}{2^2}\sqrt8=\sqrt2<2\), so every ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm \(1\), i.e. the unit ideal. An elementary route better suited to formalization: \(\mathbb Z[s]\) is Euclidean for \(|\operatorname N|\). Given \(a,b\in\mathbb Z[s]\), \(b\ne0\), write \(a/b=u+vs\) and let \(q=\lfloor u\rceil+\lfloor v\rceil s\) with nearest integers; then \(|\operatorname N(a/b-q)|\le\max(\tfrac14,\tfrac24)<1\), so \(a=qb+\rho\) with \(|\operatorname N(\rho)|<|\operatorname N(b)|\). Unique factorization and the valuation rules follow from the principal ideal property in the usual way.
