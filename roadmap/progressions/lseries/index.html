    Character sums and \(L\)-values near \(s=1\)

    Throughout, \(q\ge1\), \(\chi\) is a Dirichlet character modulo \(q\), \(\chi_0\) the principal one, and \(M=\log q+3\). For \(\chi\ne\chi_0\) the \(L\)-function \(L(s,\chi)\) is entire and equals \(\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)n^{-s}\) for \(\mathrm{Re}\,s>1\). Every bound below is explicit, and the value at \(s=1\) is always reached as a limit from the right, never through a conditionally convergent series.

    9 fully proved

    Summation by parts

    A nonprincipal character sums to zero over every period, so its partial sums are bounded by \(q\); summation by parts against \(n^{-\sigma}\) and \(\log n\cdot n^{-\sigma}\) bounds the tails.

    Let \(q\ge1\) and let \(\chi\) be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo \(q\). For every real \(x\), $\(\Bigl|\sum_{1\le n\le x}\chi(n)\Bigr|\le q .\)$ More generally \(\bigl|\sum_{a\le n<b}\chi(n)\bigr|\le q\) and \(\bigl|\sum_{a<n\le b}\chi(n)\bigr|\le q\) for all integers \(a,b\ge0\) (the sums are empty when \(b\le a\)). The sum of \(\chi\) over any \(q\) consecutive integers \(a\le n<a+q\) is \(0\).

    Proof sketch. The values of \(\chi\) on any \(q\) consecutive integers run once through its values on \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), whose sum is \(0\) because \(\chi\) is nonprincipal. Removing complete blocks of \(q\) consecutive integers from the interval leaves fewer than \(q\) terms, each of absolute value at most \(1\).

    Let \(q\ge1\) and let \(\chi\) be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo \(q\). For all integers \(N,K\ge0\) and real \(\sigma\):

    1. if \(\sigma\ge0\), then \(\bigl|\sum_{N<n\le K}\chi(n)n^{-\sigma}\bigr|\le q\,(N+1)^{-\sigma}\); if \(\sigma\ge1\) and \(N\ge1\), this is at most \(2q/N\);
    2. if \(\sigma\ge1\) and \(N\ge2\), then \(\bigl|\sum_{N<n\le K}\chi(n)\log n\cdot n^{-\sigma}\bigr|\le q\log(N+1)\,(N+1)^{-\sigma}\); if moreover \(N\ge3\), this is at most \(2q\log N/N\).

    For real \(\sigma>1\) the same bounds hold for the infinite tails: with \(L(\sigma,\chi)\) the value of the \(L\)-function and \(N\ge0\), $\(\Bigl|L(\sigma,\chi)-\sum_{1\le n\le N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\Bigr|\le q\,(N+1)^{-\sigma},\)$ which is at most \(2q/N\) for \(N\ge1\); and for \(N\ge2\), $\(\Bigl|\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\chi(n)\log n}{n^{\sigma}} -\sum_{1\le n\le N}\frac{\chi(n)\log n}{n^{\sigma}}\Bigr|\le q\log(N+1)\,(N+1)^{-\sigma},\)$ which is at most \(2q\log N/N\) for \(N\ge3\).

    Proof sketch. Summation by parts: if every partial sum \(\sum_{N<n\le m}c_n\) has absolute value at most \(B\) and \(f\ge0\) is nonincreasing on \(n\ge N+1\), then \(\bigl|\sum_{N<n\le K}c_nf(n)\bigr|\le B\,f(N+1)\). Take \(c_n=\chi(n)\), so \(B=q\) by the bound on character sums, and \(f(n)=n^{-\sigma}\), respectively \(f(n)=\log n\cdot n^{-\sigma}\), which is nonincreasing for \(n\ge3\) because \(\log t/t\) decreases on \([e,\infty)\) and \(t^{1-\sigma}\) is nonincreasing. Then \(q(N+1)^{-\sigma}\le q/(N+1)\le2q/N\), and \(q\log(N+1)(N+1)^{-\sigma}\le q\log(N+1)/(N+1)\le q\log N/N\le2q\log N/N\) for \(N\ge3\). For \(\sigma>1\) both series converge absolutely (the first to \(L(\sigma,\chi)\)), and a bound on every finite tail \(\sum_{N<n\le K}\) passes to the limit \(K\to\infty\).

    Bounds to the right of \(1\)

    For real \(\sigma\ge1\), \(L(\sigma,\chi)\) is at most \(M\); for \(\sigma>1\) its derivative is at most \(M^2\), so \(L(\sigma,\chi)\) moves by at most \((\sigma-1)M^2\); the partial sums of \(\chi(n)/n\) approach \(L(1,\chi)\) at rate \(2q/x\) and those of \(\chi(n)\log n/n\) stay below \(M^2\).
