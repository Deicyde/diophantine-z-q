    Real characters

    A real nonprincipal character \(\chi\) agrees, on primes not dividing \(2q\), with the Legendre symbol \((d/\cdot)\) of a squarefree \(d\ne1\) with \(|d|\) dividing \(q\). Through the factorization of the Dedekind zeta function of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\), the value \(L(1,\chi)\) is the residue \(\kappa_K\) times finitely many local factors, each at least \((p-1)/(p+1)\); the residue bound of the quadratic fields section then gives \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge1/(40q^{3/2})\).

    ZInQ.Progressions.exists_legendre_of_real_char ZInQ.Progressions.exists_legendre_of_real_char_dvd

    Let \(q\ge1\) and let \(\chi\) be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo \(q\) with \(\chi^2=\chi_0\). Then there is a squarefree integer \(d\ne1\) such that \(|d|\) divides \(q\), \(8\) divides \(q\) if \(d\) is even, and $\(\chi(p)=\Bigl(\frac dp\Bigr)\qquad\text{for every prime }p\nmid2q .\)$ In particular \(|d|\le q\) and every prime factor of \(d\) divides \(q\).

    Proof sketch. A real character is \(1\) at the square of every unit. Hensel lifting shows which units are squares: modulo \(p^k\) with \(p\) odd, the squares of units are the residues that are squares modulo \(p\); modulo \(2^k\), every \(n\equiv1\pmod8\) is a square. So modulo \(p^k\), \(p\) odd, \(\chi\) is \(1\) at the quadratic residues and constant at the nonresidues, hence \(\chi(\ell)=(p^*/\ell)\) for primes \(\ell\nmid2p\) or \(\chi\) is trivial, with \(p^*=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p\) (by reciprocity, \((\ell/p)=(p^*/\ell)\)). Modulo \(2^k\), \(\chi(\ell)\) depends only on \(\ell\bmod8\) and is fixed by \(\chi(3),\chi(5)\in\{\pm1\}\); the four cases give \(d=1,-1,2,-2\), and \(\chi(5)=-1\) forces \(k\ge3\). For coprime moduli, split \(\chi\) by the Chinese remainder theorem; the integers \(d\) of the two components multiply, by multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol. Induction over the factorization of \(q\) gives a squarefree \(d\) with \(|d|\mid q\), \(2\mid d\Rightarrow8\mid q\) and \(\chi(p)=(d/p)\) for primes \(p\nmid2q\). If \(d=1\), then \(\chi(p)=1\) for every prime \(p\nmid2q\); since every unit class modulo \(q\) contains such primes (Dirichlet's theorem, without bounds), \(\chi\) would be principal. So \(d\ne1\).

    theorem exists_legendre_of_real_char {q : ℕ} (hq : 1 ≤ q) (χ : DirichletCharacter ℂ q)
    (hχ1 : χ ≠ 1) (hχ2 : χ ^ 2 = 1) :
    ∃ d : ℤ, Squarefree d ∧ d ≠ 1 ∧ d.natAbs ≤ q ∧
      (∀ p : ℕ, p.Prime → p ≠ 2 → (p : ℤ) ∣ d → p ∣ q) ∧ (2 ∣ d → 8 ∣ q) ∧
      ∀ (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime], ¬ p ∣ 2 * q → χ p = legendreSym p d

    TheoremA lower bound for \(L(1,\chi)\) at real characters#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Progressions.norm_LFunction_one_ge_of_sq_eq_one ZInQ.Progressions.log_golden_div_le_norm_LFunction_one

    Let \(q\ge1\) and let \(\chi\) be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo \(q\) with \(\chi^2=\chi_0\). Then $\(|L(1,\chi)|\ge\frac1{40\,q^{3/2}} .\)$

    Proof sketch. Take \(d\) from the classification of real characters: \(d\ne1\) is squarefree, \(|d|\) divides \(q\), and \(\chi(p)=(d/p)\) for every prime \(p\nmid2q\). Then \(d\) is not the square of a rational number; let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\), with \(\alpha=\sqrt d\in\mathcal O_K\) and \(\varepsilon_p=a_K(p)-1\in\{-1,0,1\}\). Since \(|d|\) divides \(q\), no prime \(p\nmid2q\) divides \(d\), so the factorization of \(\zeta_K\) applies: for real \(s>1\), $\(\zeta_K(s)=\zeta(s)\,L(s,\chi)\prod_{p\mid2q}\frac{1-\chi(p)p^{-s}}{1-\varepsilon_pp^{-s}},\)$ where \(L(s,\chi)\), the sum of the Dirichlet series, is the value of the \(L\)-function. Multiply by \(s-1\) and let \(s\to1^+\): \((s-1)\zeta_K(s)\to\kappa_K\) by the class number formula, \((s-1)\zeta(s)\to1\), \(L(s,\chi)\to L(1,\chi)\) by continuity, and the finite product is continuous at \(s=1\) (its denominators do not vanish since \(|\varepsilon_p|\le1<p\)). Hence $\(\kappa_K=L(1,\chi)\prod_{p\mid2q}\frac{1-\chi(p)/p}{1-\varepsilon_p/p}.\)$ Each factor has absolute value at most \((1+1/p)/(1-1/p)=(p+1)/(p-1)\), and \(\prod_{p\mid2q}(p+1)/(p-1)\le3\prod_{p\mid q,\ p\text{ odd}}p\le3q\), since \((p+1)/(p-1)\le p\) for \(p\ge3\) and the factor at \(p=2\) is \(3\). So \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge\kappa_K/(3q)\). The residue bound with \(|d|\le q\) gives \(\kappa_K\ge\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2/(6\sqrt q)\), and \(\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2>0.48>18/40\), so \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge1/(40q^{3/2})\).

    (hχ2 : χ ^ 2 = 1) (hχ1 : χ ≠ 1) :
    1 / (40 * (q * √q)) ≤ ‖DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ 1‖
