    Counting primes in a residue class

    The count follows the classical proof of Dirichlet's theorem with explicit constants. Mertens' first theorem controls \(\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)/n\) to within \(3\), and the principal character inherits this up to the primes dividing \(q\).

    Let \(\Lambda\) be the von Mangoldt function and \(\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\). For every real \(x\ge0\), \(\psi(x)\le3x\). For every real \(x\ge1\), $\(\log x-2\le\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}\le\log x+3,\qquad\text{so}\qquad \Bigl|\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}-\log x\Bigr|\le3 .\)$

    Proof sketch. Chebyshev's bound \(\psi(x)\le\log4\cdot x+2\sqrt x\log x\) for \(x\ge1\), with \(\log x\le2\sqrt x/e\) (from \(\log t\le t-1\) at \(t=\sqrt x/e\)) and \(4/e\le1.5\), gives \(\psi(x)\le(\log4+1.5)x\le3x\); for \(x<2\), \(\psi(x)=0\). Let \(N=\lfloor x\rfloor\ge1\). Since \(\log n=\sum_{d\mid n}\Lambda(d)\), $\(\log N!=\sum_{d\le N}\Lambda(d)\Bigl\lfloor\frac Nd\Bigr\rfloor =N\sum_{d\le N}\frac{\Lambda(d)}d-\sum_{d\le N}\Lambda(d)\Bigl\{\frac Nd\Bigr\},\)$ and the last sum lies in \([0,\psi(N)]\). With \(N\log N-N\le\log N!\le N\log N\) and \(\psi(N)\le3N\), this gives \(\log N-1\le\sum_{d\le N}\Lambda(d)/d\le\log N+3\). Finally \(\log N\le\log x\le\log N+1\), since \(x/N<2\).

    ZInQ.Progressions.sum_nonprime_vonMangoldt_div_le_two ZInQ.Progressions.sum_Ioc_nonprime_vonMangoldt_div_le ZInQ.Progressions.sum_vonMangoldt_mul_le_sum_primePow ZInQ.Progressions.sum_Icc_log_div_mul_pred_le'

    For every integer \(N\ge0\), $\(\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\ n\text{ not prime}}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}\le2 ,\)$ that is, the prime powers \(p^k\) with \(k\ge2\) contribute at most \(2\) to \(\sum_{n\le N}\Lambda(n)/n\). Moreover, for all integers \(Y\ge1\) and \(M\ge0\), with \(s=\lfloor\sqrt Y\rfloor\), $\(\sum_{\substack{Y<n\le M\\ n\text{ not prime}}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n} \le\frac{\log s+2+2\log4}{s}.\)$

    Proof sketch. A sum of \(\Lambda(n)h(n)\) with \(h\ge0\) is at most the double sum of \(\log p\cdot h(p^k)\) over primes \(p\) and exponents \(k\) that cover its prime powers. The telescoping bound \(\sum_{s<n\le N}\log n/(n(n-1))\le(\log s+2)/s-(\log N+2)/N\) for \(1\le s\le N\) holds term by term. For the first bound, the sum is at most \(\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}\log p/p^k\le\sum_p\log p/(p(p-1))\le\sum_{m\ge2}\log m/(m(m-1))\le2\), by the telescoping bound at \(s=1\). For the second, a prime \(p\le s\) contributes at most \(2\log p/Y\), since its powers above \(Y\) form a geometric series with ratio \(1/p\le1/2\) starting below \(1/Y\); these primes give at most \(2\theta(s)/Y\le2\log4\cdot s/Y\le2\log4/s\). A prime \(p>s\) contributes at most \(\log p/(p(p-1))\), and these primes give at most \(\sum_{n>s}\log n/(n(n-1))\le(\log s+2)/s\).
