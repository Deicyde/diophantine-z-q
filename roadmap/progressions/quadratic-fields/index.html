    Quadratic fields

    For an integer \(d\) that is not the square of a rational number (for instance a squarefree \(d\ne1\)), the field \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\) is a number field of degree \(2\). The residue of its Dedekind zeta function at \(s=1\) is given by the class number formula, and this section bounds each ingredient of that formula so as to bound the residue from below by \(\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2/(6\sqrt{|d|})\).

    For an integer \(d\), let \(K_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)=\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-d)\) be the \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra with basis \(1,\sqrt d\) and \((\sqrt d)^2=d\). If \(d\) is not the square of a rational number, then \(K_d\) is a number field of degree \([K_d:\mathbb Q]=2\), and \(\sqrt d\) is an algebraic integer of \(K_d\) with square \(d\). Every squarefree integer \(d\ne1\) satisfies this hypothesis.

    Proof sketch. If \(d\) is not a rational square, \(X^2-d\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), so the algebra is a field; it has characteristic \(0\) and dimension \(2\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(\sqrt d\) is a root of the monic integer polynomial \(X^2-d\). A squarefree \(d\ne1\) is not the square of a rational number (\(d\ne0\), and \(d=a^2/b^2\) in lowest terms forces \(b=1\) and \(d=a^2\), so \(d=1\)).

    Let \(d\) be an integer that is not the square of a rational number, and let \(D_K\) be the discriminant of the ring of integers of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\). Then $\(|D_K|\le4|d| .\)$

    Proof sketch. The trace of \(x+y\sqrt d\) is \(2x\), so the basis \(1,\sqrt d\) has discriminant \(\det\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2d\end{pmatrix}=4d\). Since \(\sqrt d\) is integral, the matrix expressing \(1,\sqrt d\) in an integral basis of \(\mathcal O_K\) has integer entries, so \(4d=m^2D_K\) with \(m\) its determinant, a nonzero integer. Hence \(|D_K|\le4|d|\). The ring of integers is never computed. (In fact, for squarefree \(d\), \(D_K=d\) if \(d\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(4d\) otherwise.)

    theorem Qsqrt.abs_discr_le : |discr (Qsqrt d)| ≤ 4 * |d|

    ∃ m : ℤ, 4 * d = m ^ 2 * discr (Qsqrt d)

    Let \(d\) be an integer that is not the square of a rational number and \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\), with \(r_1\) real and \(r_2\) complex places. If \(d<0\), then \(r_1=0\) and \(r_2=1\), and \(K\) is totally complex; if \(d>0\), then \(r_1=2\) and \(r_2=0\), and \(K\) is totally real.

    Proof sketch. Every embedding of \(K\) into \(\mathbb C\) sends \(x+y\sqrt d\) to \(x+y\beta\) with \(\beta^2=d\). The embeddings are real exactly when \(d>0\); when \(d<0\) none is real, and the two complex conjugate embeddings form one complex place. In both cases \(r_1+2r_2=2\).

    Let \(K\) be a number field of degree \(2\), for example \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\) with \(d\) an integer that is not the square of a rational number. The group of roots of unity of \(K\) has order \(w_K\le6\).

    Proof sketch. The roots of unity of \(K\) form a finite cyclic group, so \(K\) contains a primitive root of unity \(\zeta\) of order \(w_K\). Then \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\subseteq K\) has degree \(\varphi(w_K)\le2\). By induction over prime powers and coprime products, \(\varphi(n)\le2\) forces \(n\mid12\), and then \(n\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), so \(w_K\le6\).

    Let \(d>0\) be an integer that is not the square of a rational number and \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\). The regulator of \(K\) satisfies $\(R_K\ge\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.\)$

    Proof sketch. By the signature, the unit rank is \(1\), and \(R_K=|\log|\sigma(\varepsilon_0)||\) for a fundamental unit \(\varepsilon_0\) and a real embedding \(\sigma\), with \(|\sigma(\varepsilon_0)|\ne1\) because \(R_K>0\). For a unit \(u\), the trace \(t=u+u'\) and the norm \(n=uu'\) are rational integers, with \(n=\pm1\), so \(y=\sigma(u)\) satisfies \(y^2-ty+n=0\). Suppose \(|y|>1\). If \(n=-1\), then \(|t|=|y-1/y|>0\), so \(|t|\ge1\); since \(t\mapsto t-1/t\) is increasing and equals \(1\) at \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(|y|\ge\phi\). If \(n=1\), then \(|t|=|y+1/y|>2\), so \(|t|\ge3\) and \(|y|\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2=\phi^2>\phi\). If \(|y|<1\), the same argument applies to \(1/y\). In all cases \(|\log|y||\ge\log\phi\).

    Let \(d\) be an integer that is not the square of a rational number, \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\), and let $\(\kappa_K=\frac{2^{r_1}(2\pi)^{r_2}h_KR_K}{w_K\sqrt{|D_K|}}\)$ be the residue at \(s=1\) of the Dedekind zeta function \(\zeta_K(s)\) (the class number formula), where \(h_K\) is the class number, \(R_K\) the regulator, \(w_K\) the number of roots of unity and \(D_K\) the discriminant. Then $\(\kappa_K\ge\frac{\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}}{6\sqrt{|d|}} .\)$

    Proof sketch. Note \(0<\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2<1\). Use \(h_K\ge1\), \(w_K\le6\) and \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\le2\sqrt{|d|}\), so the denominator is at most \(12\sqrt{|d|}\). If \(d<0\), then \(r_1=0\), \(r_2=1\) and \(R_K=1\), so the numerator is at least \(2\pi>2\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2\) and \(\kappa_K\ge\pi/(6\sqrt{|d|})\). If \(d>0\), then \(r_1=2\), \(r_2=0\) and \(R_K\ge\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2\), so the numerator is at least \(4\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2\) and \(\kappa_K\ge\log\frac{1+\sqrt5}2/(3\sqrt d)\). Both are at least the claimed bound.

    theorem dedekindZeta_residue_ge :
    Real.log ((1 + √5) / 2) / (6 * √|(d : ℝ)|) ≤ dedekindZeta_residue (Qsqrt d)
