    Primes in arithmetic progressions

    The size bounds need every unit class modulo \(W=4\prod_{p\le324}p\) to contain twenty-two primes above \(324\) below an explicit cutoff. The manuscript takes this from the explicit progression estimate of Bennett, Martin, O'Bryant and Rechnitzer. This chapter proves it instead from an effective form of Dirichlet's theorem with crude but explicit constants: for \(q\ge2^{16}\), every unit class modulo \(q\) has twenty-two primes in \((q^2,2^{q^8})\). The proof is the classical one, made quantitative; every constant is explicit, and the value of an \(L\)-function at \(s=1\) is reached as a limit from the right.

    Notation: \(\chi\) is a Dirichlet character modulo \(q\), \(\chi_0\) the principal one, \(M=\log q+3\) and \(S_\chi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)/n\).

    \(L\)-values near \(s=1\)

    Summation by parts bounds \(L(\sigma,\chi)\), its derivative and its partial sums for real \(\sigma\ge1\), and the classical product inequality gives \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge1/(256M^7)\) when \(\chi^2\ne\chi_0\).

    Real characters

    When \(\chi^2=\chi_0\), the value \(L(1,\chi)\) is tied to a quadratic field: \(\chi\) is a Legendre symbol \((d/\cdot)\) away from \(2q\), the Dedekind zeta function of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\) factors as \(\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)\) up to finitely many local factors, and the class number formula with crude bounds on the discriminant, the roots of unity and the regulator gives \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge1/(40q^{3/2})\). The splitting law and the field need only that \(d\) is not a square.

    The count

    Mertens' first theorem (with constant \(3\)), the identity relating \(L(1,\chi)S_\chi(x)\) to \(\sum_{n\le x}\chi(n)\log n/n\), and orthogonality give at least twenty-two primes of each unit class in \((q^8,q^8e^{5qB}]\), where \(B=(M^2+6q)/c\) and \(c\) is a lower bound for \(|L(1,\chi)|\) at all nonprincipal \(\chi\); here \(\log(e^{5qB})\) is of order \(q^{11/2}\).

    The theorem and its instance

    TheoremAn effective Dirichlet theorem#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Progressions.effective_dirichlet

    Let \(q\ge2^{16}\) be an integer and \(a\) an integer coprime to \(q\). Then at least \(22\) primes \(p\equiv a\pmod q\) satisfy $\(q^2<p<2^{q^8} .\)$

    Proof sketch. Put \(M=\log q+3\) and \(c=\min\bigl(1/(256M^7),1/(40q^{3/2})\bigr)\). By the lower bound at every nonprincipal character, \(|L(1,\chi)|\ge c\) for all \(\chi\ne\chi_0\), so the twenty-two primes statement gives \(22\) primes \(p\equiv a\pmod q\) with \(q^8<p\le q^8e^{5qB}\), where \(B=(M^2+6q)/c\). It remains to show \(q^8e^{5qB}<2^{q^8}\). From \(\log t\le t-1\) at \(t=\sqrt q\), \(\log q\le2\sqrt q-2\), so \(M\le3\sqrt q\) and \(M^2+6q\le15q\). Then \(1/c=\max(256M^7,40q^{3/2})\le256\cdot3^7q^{7/2}\le2^{20}q^{7/2}\), so \(B\le15\cdot2^{20}q^{9/2}\) and \(8\log q+5qB\le8q+2^{27}q^{11/2}\le2^{28}q^{11/2}\). Since \(q\ge2^{16}\), \(q^{5/2}\ge2^{40}\) and \(q^8\log2\ge q^8/2\ge2^{39}q^{11/2}\). Hence \(\log(q^8e^{5qB})<q^8\log2\), and every such prime satisfies \(q^2<q^8<p<2^{q^8}\).

    The bound is far from sharp: the proof gives such primes below \(\exp(Cq^{11/2})\) for an explicit \(C\), and the exponent \(8\) leaves room for cruder constants.

    theorem effective_dirichlet {q : ℕ} (hq : 2 ^ 16 ≤ q) {a : ℕ} (ha : a.Coprime q) :
    22 ≤ #{p ∈ Ioo (q ^ 2) (2 ^ q ^ 8) | p.Prime ∧ p % q = a % q}

    TheoremThe catalogue bound holds#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.catalogueBound8

    The catalogue bound holds: with \(P_s=\prod_{p\le324}p\) and \(W=4P_s\), for every integer \(r\) with \(0\le r<W\) and \(\gcd(r,W)=1\), at least \(22\) primes \(p\) satisfy $\(324<p<2^{W^8}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p\equiv r\pmod W .\)$

    This replaces the explicit progression estimate of Bennett, Martin, O'Bryant and Rechnitzer that the manuscript uses at the cutoff \(2^{W^3}\); the larger cutoff \(2^{W^8}\) is the range of the effective Dirichlet theorem.

    Proof sketch. By the small explicit bounds, \(W>10^5>2^{16}\). The effective Dirichlet theorem at \(q=W\) and \(a=r\) gives \(22\) primes \(p\equiv r\pmod W\) with \(W^2<p<2^{W^8}\), and \(W^2>324\). Since \(0\le r<W\), the congruence \(p\equiv r\pmod W\) says that \(p\) leaves the remainder \(r\) on division by \(W\). The constant \(W\) is never evaluated beyond these bounds.

    def CatalogueBound8 : Prop :=
