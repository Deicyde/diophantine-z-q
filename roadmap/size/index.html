    The cutoff and polynomial size

    This chapter supplies the quantitative half of input B: the fixed constants of the specification are bounded, the guard cutoff \(T\) is identified with a closed expression below \(2^{2^{2^{9013}}}\), and the specified polynomial \(P_*\) is shown to have degree below \(2\uparrow\uparrow7\) and coefficients below \(2\uparrow\uparrow8\). The constants \(D\), \(T\), \(H_{\rm cat}\), \(N_s\), \(M_0\), \(c_*\), \(\ell\) and the program literal \(c_3\) are astronomically large. None of them is ever evaluated: every statement about them is proved symbolically from their defining expressions, by monotonicity of powers and elementary logarithm estimates. The finite computations that remain are small, closed comparisons and congruences, and one traversal of the template library that computes syntax heights.

    The fixed constants are those of the specification: \(P_s=\prod_{p\le324}p\), \(W=4P_s\), the catalogue product \(H_{\rm cat}\) and maximum \(H_{\max}\), \(N_s=P_sH_{\rm cat}\), the least formal multiple \(M_0=\mathrm{LFM}(N_s)\), \(c_*=4M_0\), \(\ell=12P_s^{2916}H_{\rm cat}^{732}\), \(F_0=324!\), \(D=F_0\,3^{256F_0^4}\), \(G_0=116064000000\), \(E_0=10(DG_0)^2\), \(R=\max(D,H_{\max},c_*,10^{12})\), \(Q=R^{RE_0}\), \(B_{\rm cm}=D^2\,3^{21000D^2}+1\), \(C_{\rm cm}=2B_{\rm cm}+4096(1028+5D+84000D^2)\), \(A_{\rm ht}=2\cdot10^{13}\max(1,C_{\rm cm},Q^{17})\) and \(T=(48A_{\rm ht})^{13}\).

    The literal cutoff

    Proposition 12.1 has three ingredients. First, the prime catalogue is controlled by the catalogue bound: every unit class modulo \(W\) has twenty-two primes above \(324\) below \(X=2^{W^8}\), so \(H_{\max}<X\), \(H_{\rm cat}\le X^{22W}\) and \(\log_2N_s<2^{4613}\). The manuscript derives the same statement with the cutoff \(2^{W^3}\) from the explicit progression estimate of Bennett, Martin, O'Bryant and Rechnitzer; here the cutoff is \(2^{W^8}\), and the catalogue bound is proved from an effective Dirichlet theorem in the progressions chapter. Second, the least formal multiple has a successful index at most \(N_s^{10}\): multiplying \(P_0\) by the product of its reduction orders at the odd places above \(N_s\) puts it in the kernel of reduction there, and a further factor \(N_s^4\) pushes it deep into the filtration at every place above \(N_s\), including the place above \(2\), where \(P_0\) already starts in the third filtration subgroup. Third, a chain of symbolic estimates gives \(c_*<2^{2^{4617}}<2^{2^{8008}}<D\), hence \(R=D\), identifies the maximum in \(A_{\rm ht}\) as \(Q^{17}\), and bounds \(T\).

    The small explicit bounds and the catalogue estimates, then the constants derived from them, are in their own section.

    Degree and coefficient bounds

    Section 12.1 bounds \(P_*\) without expanding it. Integer polynomials carry the coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm and the total degree; pairs representing elements of \(\mathbb Z[X][\sqrt2]\) carry the weighted norm \(\|a\|_1+2\|b\|_1\). Both are submultiplicative, and substitution multiplies degrees and raises norms to the power of the outer degree. The division polynomials then satisfy \(\deg f_n\le(n+1)^3\) and \(\|f_n\|\le2^{100(n+1)^3}\), the coordinates of \(\iota(n)\) for \(|n|\le T\) have height at most \(2^{2^{30}(T+1)^3}\), and each of the three finite constructors has degree at most \(B^4\) and norm at most \(2^{B^8}\) for \(B=T+1\).

    • Norms, division polynomials and the finite constructors

    A syntax height is attached to every node of every template and to every recipe. At height \(h\) a polynomial has degree and logarithmic norm at most \(B^{8h}\); the library traversal gives template heights at most \(249\), the recipe rules give root height \(256\), and the final comparison with the towers uses only \(T<2^{2^{2^{9013}}}\).
