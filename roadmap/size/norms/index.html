    Norms, division polynomials and the finite constructors

    Integer polynomials are measured by total degree and coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm, and pairs \((a,b)\) representing \(a+bs\) over a polynomial ring by the weighted norm \(\|a\|_1+2\|b\|_1\). Every coefficient is bounded by the \(\ell^1\) norm, so a norm bound is a coefficient bound.

    DefinitionCoefficient norms and degrees#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Syntax.l1 ZInQ.Size.PairRing ZInQ.Size.pairNorm ZInQ.Size.pairDeg

    For a polynomial \(f=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha X^\alpha\in\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in I]\) (finitely many nonzero \(c_\alpha\)), its coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm is $\(\|f\|_1=\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha|\in\mathbb N,\)$ and \(\deg f\) is its total degree (\(\deg0=0\)).

    Let \(S=\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in I]\) and \(S[s]=S[t]/(t^2-2)\), whose elements are pairs \((a,b)\), written \(a+bs\), with \((a,b)+(c,d)=(a+c,b+d)\) and \((a,b)(c,d)=(ac+2bd,\ ad+bc)\). The weighted pair norm and degree are $\(\|a+bs\|=\|a\|_1+2\|b\|_1,\qquad\deg(a+bs)=\max(\deg a,\deg b).\)$

    TheoremElementary estimates for the coefficient norm#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.l1_estimates

    For \(f,g\in\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in I]\), \(c\in\mathbb Z\), \(i\in I\) and \(k\in\mathbb N\):

    1. \(|[X^\alpha]f|\le\|f\|_1\) for every monomial \(X^\alpha\), and \(\|f\|_1=0\) only for \(f=0\);
    2. \(\|c\|_1=|c|\) for the constant \(c\), \(\|X_i\|_1=1\), \(\|-f\|_1=\|f\|_1\), \(\|cf\|_1=|c|\,\|f\|_1\);
    3. \(\|f+g\|_1\le\|f\|_1+\|g\|_1\) and \(\|fg\|_1\le\|f\|_1\|g\|_1\), hence \(\|f^k\|_1\le\|f\|_1^k\), and the same for finite sums and products;
    4. \(\deg(f+g)\le\max(\deg f,\deg g)\), \(\deg(fg)\le\deg f+\deg g\), \(\deg f^k\le k\deg f\), \(\deg c=0\) and \(\deg X_i\le1\).

    Proof sketch. (1)-(2) from the definition. For the product, the coefficient of \(X^\gamma\) in \(fg\) is \(\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}a_\alpha b_\beta\), so \(\|fg\|_1\le\sum_\gamma\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}|a_\alpha||b_\beta|=\|f\|_1\|g\|_1\). Degrees are standard.

      l1 (-f) = l1 f ∧ l1 (C c * f) = c.natAbs * l1 f) ∧

    TheoremSubstitution and renaming bounds#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.substitution_bounds ZInQ.Size.rename_bounds ZInQ.Size.l1_rename_eq

    Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in I]\) and \(Q_i\in\mathbb Z[Y_j:j\in J]\) for \(i\in I\), and let \(P(Q)\) be the image of \(P\) under the ring homomorphism \(X_i\mapsto Q_i\). If \(d\ge1\) and \(M\ge1\) satisfy \(\deg Q_i\le d\) and \(\|Q_i\|_1\le M\) for every \(i\) such that \(X_i\) occurs in \(P\), then $\(\deg P(Q)\le d\cdot\deg P,\qquad\|P(Q)\|_1\le\|P\|_1\,M^{\deg P}.\)$ In the manuscript's notation, \(\deg P(Q)\le\deg P\cdot\max(1,\max_i\deg Q_i)\) and \(\|P(Q)\|_1\le\|P\|_1\max(1,\max_i\|Q_i\|_1)^{\deg P}\).

    For a renaming \(Q_i=Y_{\sigma(i)}\) with any map \(\sigma:I\to J\) (not necessarily injective), $\(\deg P(Y_\sigma)\le\deg P,\qquad \|P(Y_\sigma)\|_1=\sum_\beta\Bigl|\sum_{\alpha\mapsto\beta}c_\alpha\Bigr|\le\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha|=\|P\|_1 .\)$

    Proof sketch. Write \(P=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha X^\alpha\). Then \(P(Q)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\prod_iQ_i^{\alpha_i}\); by the elementary estimates each monomial image has degree at most \(\sum_i\alpha_i\deg Q_i\le d|\alpha|\le d\deg P\) and norm at most \(\prod_i\|Q_i\|_1^{\alpha_i}\le M^{|\alpha|}\le M^{\deg P}\) (as \(M\ge1\)); sum over \(\alpha\). For a renaming each monomial \(X^\alpha\) maps to a single monomial of the same degree, and coefficients of monomials with the same image add, which can only decrease the \(\ell^1\) norm.

    TheoremThe weighted pair norm#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.pair_norm_estimates

    For \(u=a+bs\) and \(u'=a'+b's\) in \(S[s]\), \(S=\mathbb Z[X_i:i\in I]\), and \(c\in\mathbb Z\):

    1. \(\|u+u'\|\le\|u\|+\|u'\|\), \(\|u-u'\|\le\|u\|+\|u'\|\) and \(\|cu\|=|c|\,\|u\|\);
    2. \(\|uu'\|\le\|u\|\,\|u'\|\), hence \(\|u^k\|\le\|u\|^k\);
    3. \(\deg(u+u')\le\max(\deg u,\deg u')\) and \(\deg(uu')\le\deg u+\deg u'\);
    4. \(\|a\|_1\le\|u\|\) and \(\|b\|_1\le\|u\|\);
    5. for integers \(e,f\), the constant \(e+fs\) has norm \(|e|+2|f|\) and degree \(0\), and the variable pair \(X_i+X_js\) (\(i\ne j\)) has norm \(3\) and degree \(1\).

    Proof sketch. (2): \(uu'=(aa'+2bb')+(ab'+a'b)s\), so by the elementary estimates \(\|uu'\|\le\|a\|\|a'\|+2\|b\|\|b'\|+2\|a\|\|b'\|+2\|a'\|\|b\|\le(\|a\|+2\|b\|)(\|a'\|+2\|b'\|)\). The rest is immediate from the scalar estimates.

    The manuscript states that both norms are submultiplicative; the companion proof adds subadditivity under the field operations.

    theorem pair_norm_estimates (u u' : PairRing σ) (c : ℤ) (k : ℕ) (e f : ℤ) (i j : σ)

      pairNorm (⟨X i, X j⟩ : PairRing σ) = 3 ∧ pairDeg (⟨X i, X j⟩ : PairRing σ) = 1)

    Used bySize of the division polynomials

    The normalized division polynomials, evaluated at the formal point \((x_0+x_1s,y_0+y_1s)\), satisfy \(\deg f_n\le(n+1)^3\) and \(\|f_n\|\le2^{100(n+1)^3}\) by strong induction: the cubic exponents leave a slack of at least nine in the even recurrence and ten in the odd one, which absorbs the fixed factors.

    TheoremSize of the division polynomials#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.normDiv_size ZInQ.Size.psiComponent_size

    Work in \(S[s]\) with \(S=\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1]\), put \(x=x_0+x_1s\), \(y=y_0+y_1s\) and \(A=-11243+7950s\), and let \(F_n=f_n(x)\in S[s]\) for the normalized division polynomials \(f_n\) of \(y^2=x^3+Ax\). Then for every \(n\ge0\) $\(\deg F_n\le(n+1)^3,\qquad\|F_n\|\le2^{100(n+1)^3}.\)$ Consequently, for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\) and \(c\in\{0,1\}\), the component polynomial \(\Psi_{n,c}\in S\) (the coefficient of \(s^c\) in \(\psi_n(x,y)\)) satisfies $\(\deg\Psi_{n,c}\le2(|n|+1)^3,\qquad\|\Psi_{n,c}\|_1\le2^{101(|n|+1)^3}.\)$

    Proof sketch. Norms: \(\|x\|=\|y\|=3\), \(\|A\|=11243+2\cdot7950=27143<2^{15}\), and \(D_0=16(x^3+Ax)^2\) has degree \(6\) and \(\|D_0\|\le16(27+3\cdot27143)^2<2^{38}\).

    Base cases. \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=F_2=1\). \(F_3=3x^4+6Ax^2-A^2\) has degree at most \(4\le64\) and norm at most \(3\cdot3^4+6\cdot27143\cdot3^2+27143^2<2^{30}\). \(F_4=2(x^6+5Ax^4-5A^2x^2-A^3)\) has degree at most \(6\le125\) and norm at most \(2(3^6+5\cdot27143\cdot3^4+5\cdot27143^2\cdot3^2+27143^3)<2^{46}\).

    Even step, \(n=2m\), \(m\ge3\): \(F_{2m}=F_m(F_{m+2}F_{m-1}^2-F_{m-2}F_{m+1}^2)\), all indices below \(2m\). By induction the first product has exponent sum \((m+1)^3+(m+3)^3+2m^3=4m^3+12m^2+30m+28\) (including the factor \(F_m\)), the second \((m+1)^3+(m-1)^3+2(m+2)^3=4m^3+12m^2+30m+16\), smaller. Since \((2m+1)^3-(4m^3+12m^2+30m+28)=4m^3-24m-27\ge9\) for \(m\ge3\), the degree is at most \((2m+1)^3-9\) and \(\log_2\|F_{2m}\|\le100((2m+1)^3-9)+1<100(2m+1)^3\) (the \(+1\) for the difference of two terms).

    Odd step, \(n=2m+1\), \(m\ge2\): \(F_{2m+1}=D_0L-R_m\) (\(m\) even) or \(L-D_0R_m\) (\(m\) odd) with \(L=F_{m+2}F_m^3\) and \(R_m=F_{m-1}F_{m+1}^3\), all indices below \(2m+1\). The exponent sums are \((m+3)^3+3(m+1)^3\) and \(m^3+3(m+2)^3\), whose differences from \((2m+2)^3\) are \(4m^3+6m^2-12m-22\ge10\) and \(4m^3+6m^2-12m-16\ge16\) for \(m\ge2\). The factor \(D_0\) costs at most \(6\) in degree and \(38\) in \(\log_2\) of the norm, the difference one more bit, and \(6\le10\), \(38+1\le1000\).

    Components. For odd \(n\ge0\), \(\psi_n=F_n\); for even \(n\ge0\), \(\psi_n=2yF_n\) with \(\|2y\|=6<2^3\) and degree one more; and \(\psi_{-n}=-\psi_n\). So \(\deg\psi_n\le(|n|+1)^3+1\le2(|n|+1)^3\) and \(\|\psi_n\|\le2^{100(|n|+1)^3+3}\le2^{101(|n|+1)^3}\) for \(n\ne0\) (\(\psi_0=0\)). Each component has \(\ell^1\) norm and degree at most those of the pair.

    These polynomials are never expanded at the astronomical indices; the bound is proved by strong induction on \(n\) from the recurrences.

    LemmaSizes under quadratic-field operations#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.QRep ZInQ.Size.QRep.exists_add ZInQ.Size.QRep.exists_sub ZInQ.Size.QRep.exists_mul ZInQ.Size.QRep.exists_two_mul ZInQ.Size.QRep.exists_div ZInQ.Size.QRep.coord_bounds

    Say that an integer triple \((a,b,c)\) with \(c\ne0\) represents \(u=(a+bs)/c\in K_0\) and has size \(\max(|a|,|b|,|c|)\). Let \(H\ge2\), and let \(u,u'\in K_0\) be represented by triples of size at most \(H\). Then:

    1. \(u+u'\), \(u-u'\), \(uu'\) and \(2u\) are represented by triples of size at most \(3H^4\);
    2. if \(u'\ne0\), then \(u/u'\) is represented by a triple of size at most \(3H^4\);
    3. if \((a,b,c)\) represents \(u=u_0+u_1s\) (\(u_0,u_1\in\mathbb Q\)) with size at most \(H\), then \(u_0=a/c\) and \(u_1=b/c\), and their reduced numerators have absolute value at most \(H\) and their positive reduced denominators are at most \(H\).

    Proof sketch. With \(u=(a+bs)/c\) and \(u'=(a'+b's)/c'\): \(u\pm u'=\bigl((ac'\pm a'c)+(bc'\pm b'c)s\bigr)/(cc')\) has size at most \(2H^2\); \(uu'=\bigl((aa'+2bb')+(ab'+a'b)s\bigr)/(cc')\) has size at most \(3H^2\); \(2u=(2a+2bs)/c\) has size at most \(2H\). For the quotient multiply by the conjugate: $\(\frac uu'=\frac{c'\bigl((aa'-2bb')+(a'b-ab')s\bigr)}{c\,(a'^2-2b'^2)},\)$ where \(a'^2-2b'^2\ne0\) because \(a'+b's\ne0\) and \(\sqrt2\) is irrational; the size is at most \(3H^3\). All these are at most \(3H^4\). For (3), reducing a fraction does not increase the absolute values of its numerator and denominator.

    TheoremHeights of the codes in the guard box#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.iota_coord_height

    Let \(T\ge0\) be an integer and \(K=2^{30}(T+1)^3\). For every integer \(n\) with \(|n|\le T\), each of the five coordinates of \(\iota(n)\) is a rational number whose reduced numerator has absolute value at most \(2^K\) and whose positive reduced denominator is at most \(2^K\).

    Proof sketch. For \(n=0\), \(\iota(0)=(1,0,0,0,0)\). Let \(1\le|n|\le T\). By the fixed point codes (which use that \(P_0\) has infinite order and the multiplication-by-\(n\) formulas), for \(n\ge1\), \(\psi_n(P_0)\ne0\) and $\(x([n]P_0)=x(P_0)-\frac{\psi_{n+1}(P_0)\psi_{n-1}(P_0)}{\psi_n(P_0)^2},\qquad y([n]P_0)=\frac{\psi_{2n}(P_0)}{2\psi_n(P_0)^4},\)$ and \(\iota(-n)\) differs from \(\iota(n)\) only in the signs of the ordinate coordinates.

    Values of \(\psi_m(P_0)\). The coordinates of \(P_0\) over the denominator \(32\) are \(x_0=6564/32\), \(x_1=-4640/32\), \(y_0=-123720/32\), \(y_1=87483/32\), with numerators below \(2^{17}\) in absolute value. For \(|m|\le2T\) put \(\delta=2(|m|+1)^3\ge\deg\Psi_{m,c}\). Then \(c_m=32^\delta\), \(a_m=32^\delta\Psi_{m,0}(P_0)\) and \(b_m=32^\delta\Psi_{m,1}(P_0)\) are integers with \(\psi_m(P_0)=(a_m+b_ms)/c_m\), and each monomial of \(\Psi_{m,c}\) contributes at most \(2^{17\delta}\) after clearing, so \(|a_m|,|b_m|\le\|\Psi_{m,c}\|_1\,2^{17\delta}\le2^{135(|m|+1)^3}\) and \(c_m=2^{10(|m|+1)^3}\).
