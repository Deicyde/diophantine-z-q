    Bounds for the fixed constants

    Only a few small facts are computed: the size of the primorial, the factorial \(324!\) to within its binary length, and \(G_0<2^{37}\).

    LemmaSmall explicit bounds#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.small_constant_bounds

    The following closed inequalities between explicit natural numbers hold, where \(P_s=\prod_{p\le324,\ p\text{ prime}}p\) and \(W=4P_s\):

    1. \(P_s<2^{433}\) and \(10^5<W<2^{435}<2^{512}\);
    2. \(2^{2000}<324!<2^{2250}\);
    3. \(G_0=116064000000<2^{37}\).

    This is a finite computation. The manuscript states \(10^5<W<2^{512}\) and \(2^{2000}<F_0<2^{2250}\); the sharper \(P_s<2^{433}\) is used for \(N_s\) and \(\ell\). The lower bound on \(W\) is used to apply the effective Dirichlet theorem at \(q=W\), which needs \(W\ge2^{16}\).

    Proof sketch. Direct evaluation: \(P_s\) and \(W\) have the decimal values of the primorial literal, with \(433\) and \(435\) binary digits; \(324!\) has \(2241\) binary digits (\(2^{2240}<324!<2^{2241}\)); and \(2^{37}=137438953472\).

    The catalogue bound puts every catalogue prime below \(X=2^{W^8}\). This bounds the catalogue maximum and product, hence \(N_s\) and the pole clearing constant \(\ell\).

    TheoremThe catalogue below the progression cutoff#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.catalogue_bounds

    Assume the catalogue bound. Let \(W=4P_s\) and \(X=2^{W^8}\). For every residue \(r\) with \(1\le r<W\) and \(\gcd(r,W)=1\) and every \(j\) with \(1\le j\le22\), the catalogue prime \(p_{r,j}\) (the \(j\)-th prime \(p>324\) with \(p\equiv r\pmod W\)) satisfies $\(324<p_{r,j}<X .\)$ Consequently $\(H_{\max}=\mathrm{catalogue\_max}(W,22,324)<X,\qquad 1\le H_{\rm cat}=\mathrm{catalogue\_product}(W,22,324)\le X^{22W}.\)$

    Proof sketch. Fix such \(r\). The hypothesis gives at least \(22\) primes \(p\) with \(324<p<X\) and \(p\bmod W=r\); these are exactly the elements below \(X\) of the set \(S_r=\{p\text{ prime}:p>324,\ p\equiv r\pmod W\}\), whose elements in increasing order are \(p_{r,1}<p_{r,2}<\cdots\). So at least \(22\) elements of \(S_r\) are below \(X\), and the \(j\)-th element for \(j\le22\) is below \(X\) (if \(\#(S_r\cap[0,X))\ge22>j-1\), then the element of index \(j-1\), counting from \(0\), is below \(X\)). It is above \(324\) by definition of \(S_r\). The maximum of finitely many numbers below \(X\) is below \(X\) (an empty maximum is \(0<X\)). The product has \(22\,\varphi(W)\le22W\) factors, each in \([1,X)\), so it lies in \([1,X^{22W}]\).

    The manuscript reaches the same conclusion at the smaller cutoff \(2^{W^3}\) through the progression estimate, which gives more than \(X/(2W^4)>22\) primes in each unit class and notes that no prime at most \(324\) lies in a unit class. Here the cutoff is \(X=2^{W^8}\), the range of the effective Dirichlet theorem, and the catalogue bound states the needed count directly.

    theorem catalogue_bounds (hCAT : CatalogueBound8) :
    (∀ r j : ℕ, 1 ≤ r → r < W → r.Coprime W → j < 22 →
      324 < Syntax.cataloguePrime W 324 r j ∧ Syntax.cataloguePrime W 324 r j < 2 ^ W ^ 8) ∧
    Hmax < 2 ^ W ^ 8 ∧ 1 ≤ Hcat ∧ Hcat ≤ (2 ^ W ^ 8) ^ (22 * W)
    def CatalogueBound8 : Prop :=
  ∀ r : ℕ, r < catalogueModulus → Nat.Coprime r catalogueModulus →
    22 ≤ ({p ∈ Finset.Ioo 324 (2 ^ catalogueModulus ^ 8) |
      p.Prime ∧ p % catalogueModulus = r}).card

    ZInQ.Size.closedT_lt

    With \(D\) and \(G_0\) as defined, $\((96\cdot10^{13})^{13}\,D^{2210G_0^2D^3}<2^{D^8}<2^{2^{2^{9013}}} .\)$ With the closed form of \(T\) this is the bound on \(T\) in the literal cutoff.
