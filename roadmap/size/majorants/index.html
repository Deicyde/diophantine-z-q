    Syntax heights and the final comparison

    The specified polynomial is a compact syntax, not an expanded monomial list: node lists share nodes, templates call earlier templates, and three constructors insert huge polynomials without expanding them. Its size is bounded by an induction on this syntax.

    9 fully proved

    Heights and majorants

    Variables have height zero, constants and fixed nodes height one, an operation or constructor one more than its highest argument, and a call one more than the callee's height plus its highest argument. Recipes add three levels for a substituted callee in a model-evaluation recipe, then two for the final sum of squares; composition recipes only rename variables, so they add two levels for a sum of squares and one for a product.

    Nodes and templates. For a template \(\tau\) with node list \(n_0,\dots,n_{k-1}\) in a library whose calls refer to earlier templates, the height \(h(n_j)\in\mathbb N\) is defined in node order:

    • \(h(\mathrm{var}\ i)=0\);
    • \(h(\mathrm{const}\ c)=1\) and \(h(\mathrm{fixed}\ e)=1\);
    • \(h(\mathrm{add}\ j\ j')=h(\mathrm{mul}\ j\ j')=1+\max(h(n_j),h(n_{j'}))\);
    • \(h(\mathrm{psi}\ e\ (a_0,\dots,a_3)\ c)=1+\max_ih(n_{a_i})\) and \(h(\mathrm{guard}\ e\ (a_1,\dots,a_{15}))=1+\max_ih(n_{a_i})\);
    • \(h(\mathrm{call}\ \tau'\ (a_0,\dots,a_{p-1})\ w)=1+H(\tau')+\max\bigl(0,\max_ih(n_{a_i})\bigr)\).

    The intrinsic height of \(\tau\) is \(H(\tau)=h(n_{\mathrm{root}})\).

    Recipes. For a model-evaluation recipe \(\rho\) whose calls have callees \(\kappa\) (the natural, raw-addition, multiplication and fixed-scalar templates and the power recipe that actually occur), $\(H(\rho)=\max\Bigl(4,\ \max_\kappa\bigl(H(\kappa)+3\bigr)\Bigr)+2 .\)$ For a composition recipe with callees \(\kappa_1,\dots,\kappa_k\), \(H(\rho)=\max_iH(\kappa_i)+2\) for a sum of squares and \(H(\rho)=\max_iH(\kappa_i)+1\) for a product.

    Majorant. For an integer \(B\ge2\), an integer polynomial \(P\) satisfies the height-\(h\) majorant, written \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(h,P)\), if $\(\deg P\le1\text{ and }\|P\|_1\le1\quad(h=0),\qquad \deg P\le B^{8h}\text{ and }\|P\|_1\le2^{B^{8h}}\quad(h\ge1).\)$

    This is a definition. The height is a majorant for the compact syntax, not the depth of an expanded expression; it deliberately overestimates (shared nodes and unused nodes are counted). For the model-evaluation wrappers the safe value four bounds the generator equations and root differences, as in the manuscript, rather than the literal height two of the companion proof.

    At height \(h\ge1\) a polynomial has degree and logarithmic norm at most \(B^{8h}\), provided \(B\ge2^{20}\) exceeds every occurring constant, constructor index and guard cutoff. The six closure rules (operations, substitution, constructors, renaming, sums of squares and products) carry this majorant through templates and recipes.

    TheoremClosure rules for the height majorant#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.Maj.mono ZInQ.Size.Maj.X ZInQ.Size.Maj.C ZInQ.Size.Maj.add ZInQ.Size.Maj.mul ZInQ.Size.Maj.bind₁ ZInQ.Size.Maj.constructor ZInQ.Size.Maj.rename ZInQ.Size.Maj.bind₁_of_maj_zero ZInQ.Size.Maj.sum_sq ZInQ.Size.Maj.prod

    Let \(B\ge2^{20}\) be an integer and write \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,P)\) for \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(h,P)\).

    1. Monotonicity and atoms. \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,P)\) and \(h\le h'\) imply \(\mathrm{Maj}(h',P)\). Every variable satisfies \(\mathrm{Maj}(0,X_i)\), and every integer constant \(c\) with \(|c|\le2^{B^8}\) satisfies \(\mathrm{Maj}(1,c)\).
    2. Operations. \(\mathrm{Maj}(h_1,P)\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(h_2,Q)\) imply \(\mathrm{Maj}(1+\max(h_1,h_2),P+Q)\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(1+\max(h_1,h_2),PQ)\).
    3. Substitution. If \(\mathrm{Maj}(H,P)\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(k,Q_i)\) for every \(i\) such that \(X_i\) occurs in \(P\), then \(\mathrm{Maj}(H+k+1,P(Q))\).
    4. Constructors. If \(\deg C\le B^4\), \(\|C\|_1\le2^{B^8}\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(k,Q_i)\) for all arguments, then \(\mathrm{Maj}(k+1,C(Q))\).
    5. Renaming. \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,P)\) implies \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,P(Y_\sigma))\) for every map \(\sigma\) of variables.
    6. Sums of squares. If \(1\le m\le B\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,c_i)\) for \(i=1,\dots,m\), then \(\mathrm{Maj}(h+2,\sum_ic_i^2)\).
    7. Products. If \(1\le m\le B\) and \(\mathrm{Maj}(h,c_i)\) for \(i=1,\dots,m\), then \(\mathrm{Maj}(h+1,\prod_ic_i)\).

    Proof sketch. Throughout, \(B^{8h}\ge B\ge2^{20}\) for \(h\ge1\), and the height-zero case has norm at most \(1\). (1) Degrees: \(\max\le B^{8m}\) and \(2B^{8m}\le B^{8m+8}\) for \(m=\max(h_1,h_2)\) (for \(m=0\), \(\deg\le2\le B^8\)). Norms: \(\|P+Q\|_1\le2\cdot2^{B^{8m}}\) and \(\|PQ\|_1\le2^{2B^{8m}}\), both at most \(2^{B^{8m+8}}\). (2) By the substitution bounds with \(d=B^{8k}\) (or \(1\)) and \(M=2^{B^{8k}}\) (or \(1\) when \(k=0\)): \(\deg\le B^{8H}B^{8k}\le B^{8(H+k+1)}\) and \(\log_2\|P(Q)\|_1\le B^{8H}+B^{8H}B^{8k}\le B^{8(H+k+1)}\); for \(k=0\) simply \(\|P(Q)\|_1\le\|P\|_1\). (3) Likewise \(\deg\le B^4B^{8k}\le B^{8k+8}\) and \(\log_2\|C(Q)\|_1\le B^8+B^4B^{8k}\le B^{8k+8}\) for \(k\ge1\); for \(k=0\) the arguments have norm at most \(1\) and \(\log_2\|C(Q)\|_1\le B^8\). (4) By the renaming bound. (5) \(\deg\le2B^{8h}\) and \(\|\sum c_i^2\|_1\le m\,2^{2B^{8h}}\le2^{B+2B^{8h}}\le2^{B^{8h+16}}\). (6) \(\deg\le mB^{8h}\le B^{8h+8}\) and \(\|\prod c_i\|_1\le2^{mB^{8h}}\le2^{B^{8h+8}}\) (for \(h=0\): degree at most \(m\le B^8\), norm at most \(1\)).

    These are the manuscript's rules: positive height \(h\) gives degree and logarithmic norm at most \(B^{8h}\); a call charges one level beyond the callee and argument heights; and a renaming retains the callee's height.

    Let \(B\ge2^{20}\) and let \(L\) be a template library, each call referring to an earlier template, such that, in every template of \(L\):

    • every node \(\mathrm{const}\ c\) has \(|c|\le B-1\);
    • every node \(\mathrm{fixed}\ e\) has \(|\mathrm{val}(e)|\le B-1\);
    • every node \(\mathrm{psi}\ e\ a\ c\) has \(|\mathrm{val}(e)|+1\le B\);
    • every node \(\mathrm{guard}\ e\ a\) has \(0\le\mathrm{val}(e)\le B-1\).

    Suppose moreover that the constructors satisfy the uniform bounds at \(B\): \(\deg\Psi_{n,c}\le B^4\) and \(\|\Psi_{n,c}\|_1\le2^{B^8}\) whenever \(|n|+1\le B\), and \(\deg G_T\le B^4\) and \(\|G_T\|_1\le2^{B^8}\) whenever \(0\le T\le B-1\). (The constructors of the specification satisfy them at every \(B\ge2^{20}\).)

    Then for every template \(\tau\) of \(L\) and every node \(n_j\) of \(\tau\), \(\mathrm{Maj}_B\bigl(h(n_j),[\![n_j]\!]\bigr)\); in particular \(\mathrm{Maj}_B\bigl(H(\tau),[\![\tau]\!]\bigr)\).

    Proof sketch. Induction over the library in stored order and, within a template, over the nodes in stored order. Variables: rule (0). Constants and fixed nodes: rule (0), since \(|c|\le B\le2^{B^8}\). Additions and multiplications: rule (1). Constructor nodes: the hypothesis on the constructors gives \(\deg\le B^4\) and \(\|\cdot\|_1\le2^{B^8}\) for \(\Psi_{\mathrm{val}(e),c}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{val}(e)}\); apply rule (3) with \(k\) the maximum argument height. Calls: the call polynomial is \([\![\tau']\!]\) with parameters replaced by the argument polynomials (height at most \(k=\max(0,\max_ih(n_{a_i}))\)) and witnesses by fresh variables (height \(0\le k\)); the induction hypothesis for the earlier template \(\tau'\) gives \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(H(\tau'),[\![\tau']\!])\), and rule (2) gives \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(H(\tau')+k+1,\cdot)\). The values \(\mathrm{val}(e)\) enter only through the four hypotheses and are never computed.

    Let \(B\ge2^{20}\).

    1. Let \(\rho\) be a model-evaluation recipe with at most \(B\) constraints, and suppose every callee \(\kappa\) occurring in its calls satisfies \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(h_\kappa,[\![\kappa]\!])\) with \(h_\kappa\ge1\). Then \(\mathrm{Maj}_B\bigl(\max(4,\max_\kappa(h_\kappa+3))+2,\ [\![\rho]\!]\bigr)\).
    2. Let \(\rho\) be a composition recipe with \(m\) calls, \(1\le m\le B\), whose callees satisfy \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(h_i,[\![\kappa_i]\!])\). Then \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(\max_ih_i+2,[\![\rho]\!])\) if \(\rho\) is a sum of squares and \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(\max_ih_i+1,[\![\rho]\!])\) if it is a product.

    Proof sketch. (1) The constraints are the three generator equations \(8X_{g_0}-1641\), \(4X_{g_1}+15465\), \(32X_{g_2}-87483\), the calls, and the five root differences. Every point reference is a variable, a constant \(0\), \(1\) or \(-145\), or the negative of a variable, so each argument polynomial has degree at most \(1\) and norm at most \(145\), and satisfies \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(2,\cdot)\). A call is a substitution of such arguments (and fresh variables) into \([\![\kappa]\!]\), so it satisfies \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(h_\kappa+3,\cdot)\) by rule (2). The generator equations and root differences have degree at most \(1\) and norm at most \(87515\), so they satisfy \(\mathrm{Maj}_B(4,\cdot)\). By monotonicity every constraint satisfies the majorant at \(\max(4,\max_\kappa(h_\kappa+3))\), and rule (5) for the sum of at most \(B\) squares adds two. (2) The arguments of every call are coordinate variables, so each constraint is a renaming of \([\![\kappa_i]\!]\) followed by moving witnesses to fresh variables, again a renaming: rule (4) keeps \(h_i\); then rule (5) or rule (6).
