    The least formal multiple

    The constant \(M_0=\mathrm{LFM}(N_s)\) is the least \(m\ge1\) for which the normalized representation \((a+bs)/c\) of \(1/x([m]P_0)\) has \(N_s^4\mid a\), \(N_s^4\mid b\) and \(\gcd(c,N_s)=1\), with inverse abscissa \(0\) at the identity. The specification only defines it; this section shows that the search succeeds at an index at most \(N_s^{10}\). The argument works for every integer \(N\ge1\) in place of \(N_s\) and does not use the catalogue bound.

    Places of \(K_0\)

    Above an odd prime \(p\) there are one or two primes of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\), each unramified with residue field of at most \(p^2\) elements, and conjugation permutes them (the primes above an odd prime, in the multiplication chapter). This lets one read off \(p\)-adic valuations of the two rational coordinates of an element from its valuations at these primes. At the prime \((s)\) above \(2\) the model chapter's formula \(v_s(c_0+c_1s)=\min(2v_2(c_0),2v_2(c_1)+1)\) does the same.

    LemmaRational coordinates at odd primes#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.padicValRat_coords_ge

    Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(k\in\mathbb Z\), and \(u=u_0+u_1s\in K_0\) with \(u_0,u_1\in\mathbb Q\). If \(v_{\mathfrak p}(u)\ge k\) for every prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(\mathbb Z[s]\) containing \(p\) (with \(v_{\mathfrak p}(0)=+\infty\)), then $\(v_p(u_0)\ge k\qquad\text{and}\qquad v_p(u_1)\ge k\)$ (with \(v_p(0)=+\infty\)).

    Proof sketch. Let \(\bar u=u_0-u_1s\). Conjugation permutes the primes above \(p\), so \(v_{\mathfrak p}(\bar u)=v_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}(u)\ge k\) for every \(\mathfrak p\mid p\). Then \(u_0=(u+\bar u)/2\) and \(u_1=(u-\bar u)/(2s)\), and \(v_{\mathfrak p}(2)=v_{\mathfrak p}(s)=0\) since \(p\) is odd, so \(v_{\mathfrak p}(u_0),v_{\mathfrak p}(u_1)\ge k\) for any one prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\). For a rational number \(c\), \(v_{\mathfrak p}(c)=v_{\mathfrak p}(p)\,v_p(c)=v_p(c)\) because \(v_{\mathfrak p}(p)=1\).

    The manuscript performs the same extraction ("both rational coefficients lie in \(N_s^4\mathbb Z_{(N_s)}\)") without spelling it out.

    Reduction and the filtration

    Reduction modulo a prime not above \(2\) is a homomorphism from the points with nonsingular reduction onto the nonsingular points of the reduced curve, with kernel the points of negative abscissa valuation. Over a finite field of odd characteristic the reduced curve has at most \(2|k|+1\) points, which replaces Hasse's bound used in the manuscript.

    TheoremReduction on the nonsingular part#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Size.E0sub ZInQ.Size.E1sub ZInQ.Size.redK ZInQ.Size.reductionHom ZInQ.Size.ker_reductionHom ZInQ.Size.E0_eq_top_of_good ZInQ.Size.zsmul_addOrderOf_mem_E1

    Let \(\mathfrak p\) be a nonzero prime of \(\mathbb Z[s]\) with \(2\notin\mathfrak p\), \(k=\mathbb Z[s]/\mathfrak p\) its (finite, odd characteristic) residue field, \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}=\mathbb Z[s]_{\mathfrak p}\subset K_0\) the local ring and \(\rho:\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\to k\) the residue map. Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[s]\), \(W:y^2=x^3+Ax\) over \(K_0\) and \(\widetilde W:y^2=x^3+\rho(A)x\) over \(k\). Let \(\widetilde W_{\rm ns}(k)\) be the group of nonsingular points of \(\widetilde W\) over \(k\) together with \(O\), under the chord-and-tangent law. Put $\(W_1(\mathfrak p)=\{O\}\cup\{(x,y)\in W(K_0):x\notin\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\},\)$ $\(W_0(\mathfrak p)=W_1(\mathfrak p)\cup\{(x,y)\in W(K_0):x\in\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p},\ (\rho(x),\rho(y))\text{ is a nonsingular point of }\widetilde W\}.\)$ (If \(x\in\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\) then \(y\in\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\), since \(y^2=x^3+Ax\).) Define \(\operatorname{red}:W_0(\mathfrak p)\to\widetilde W_{\rm ns}(k)\) by \(\operatorname{red}(Q)=O\) for \(Q\in W_1(\mathfrak p)\) and \(\operatorname{red}(x,y)=(\rho(x),\rho(y))\) otherwise. Then:

    1. \(W_0(\mathfrak p)\) is a subgroup of \(W(K_0)\) and \(\operatorname{red}\) is a group homomorphism with kernel \(W_1(\mathfrak p)\);
    2. if \(\rho(A)\ne0\), then \(\widetilde W\) is nonsingular and \(W_0(\mathfrak p)=W(K_0)\).

    Consequently, if \(Q\in W_0(\mathfrak p)\) and \(n\ge1\) is the order of \(\operatorname{red}(Q)\) in the finite group \(\widetilde W_{\rm ns}(k)\), then \([n]Q\in W_1(\mathfrak p)\), and so \([mn]Q\in W_1(\mathfrak p)\) for every integer \(m\).

    Proof sketch. This is Silverman, Proposition VII.2.1, applied after embedding \(K_0\) in its completion at \(\mathfrak p\) (the local ring \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\) is the intersection of \(K_0\) with the completed valuation ring, and the residue fields agree). The model is integral at \(\mathfrak p\), and the proof of VII.2.1 uses only integrality: the group law is given by the chord-and-tangent construction, a line through points of \(W_0(\mathfrak p)\) reduces to the line through their reductions, and the third intersection point reduces accordingly; points with \(x\notin\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}\) reduce to \(O\). For (2), the discriminant of \(y^2=x^3+ax\) is \(-64a^3\), nonzero in odd characteristic when \(a\ne0\); then every point with integral coordinates has nonsingular reduction. The final statement follows from (1) since \(W_1(\mathfrak p)\) is a subgroup.

    The manuscript cites VII.2.1 for the nonsingular reduction group at the bad place and uses the same homomorphism at the good places. The model chapter's reduction statement for split primes of good reduction is the special case of (1) with residue degree one.

    The successful index

    Let \(m_0\) be the product of the orders of the reductions of \(P_0\) at the primes above the odd prime divisors of \(N\). Each order is at most \(2|k|+1\), so \(m_0\le N^6\), and \([m_0]P_0\) lies in the kernel of reduction at every such prime. Multiplying further by \(N^4\) gives \(v(1/x)\ge2+8v_p(N)\) at those primes and \(v_s(1/x)\ge6+16v_2(N)\) at \((s)\), which is the required divisibility.
