    The alpha and power formulas

    The alpha sequence \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\) satisfies \(U_0=0\), \(U_1=1\), \(U_{n+2}=bU_{n+1}-U_n\). It grows for \(b\ge2\), its matrix powers have determinant one, and it is congruent in its parameter, with \(\alpha_2(n)=n\).

    DefinitionThe alpha sequence#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaInt ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaInt_neg ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaInt_add_two

    For a natural number \(b\) the alpha sequence \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\in\mathbb Z\), \(n\in\mathbb N\), is defined by $\(U_0=0,\qquad U_1=1,\qquad U_{n+2}=b\,U_{n+1}-U_n\quad(n\ge0).\)$ It is integer-valued for every \(b\) (its values may be negative when \(b\in\{0,1\}\)). The matrix $\(M_b=\begin{pmatrix}b&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\)$ drives the recurrence: \(M_b\,(U_n,U_{n-1})^{\mathsf T}=(U_{n+1},U_n)^{\mathsf T}\) for \(n\ge1\). When an index may be negative, the sequence is extended by \(U_{-j}=-U_j\), which is consistent with the recurrence.

    This is a definition. It is the sequence of the Archive of Formal Proofs development used by the manuscript; \(U_{n+1}\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind \(S_n\) (normalized by \(S_{n+2}=XS_{n+1}-S_n\), \(S_0=1\), \(S_{-1}=0\)) evaluated at \(b\). Only natural parameters \(b\) occur in this chapter, and every natural-valued use (a witness of the alpha formula) is justified by the growth lemma for \(b\ge2\).

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_growth ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_exponential_bounds

    Let \(b\ge2\) be a natural number and \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\). Then for every \(n\in\mathbb N\) $\(0\le U_n,\qquad U_n+1\le U_{n+1},\qquad n\le U_n .\)$ In particular the sequence is strictly increasing and \(U_n\ge1\) for \(n\ge1\). Moreover, for all natural \(c\ge1\) and \(n\), $\(c^n\le\alpha_{c+1}(n+1),\)$ and for \(b\ge3\), \(\alpha_b(n+1)\le b^n\).

    The manuscript states the first group of inequalities for \(b\ge3\); the proof works for \(b\ge2\), and \(\alpha_2(n)=n\) shows that \(b=2\) is the boundary case.

    Proof sketch. Joint induction on \(n\) for the pair \(0\le U_n\), \(U_n+1\le U_{n+1}\): the base is \(U_0=0\), \(U_1=1\); in the step, \(U_{n+2}-U_{n+1}=(b-2)U_{n+1}+(U_{n+1}-U_n)\ge0+1\) because \(b\ge2\) and \(U_{n+1}\ge0\). Summing the increments gives \(U_n\ge n\). For \(c^n\le\alpha_{c+1}(n+1)\) induct on \(n\) using \(\alpha_{c+1}(n+2)=(c+1)\alpha_{c+1}(n+1)-\alpha_{c+1}(n)\ge c\,\alpha_{c+1}(n+1)\), which holds because \(\alpha_{c+1}(n)\le\alpha_{c+1}(n+1)\). The upper bound \(\alpha_b(n+1)\le b^n\) is the same induction with \(\alpha_b(n+2)\le b\,\alpha_b(n+1)\).

    theorem alphaZ_growth (b : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (n : ℕ) :
    0 ≤ alphaZ b n ∧ alphaZ b n + 1 ≤ alphaZ b (n + 1) ∧ (n : ℤ) ≤ alphaZ b n
    theorem alphaZ_exponential_bounds (b c n : ℕ) :
    (1 ≤ c → (c : ℤ) ^ n ≤ alphaZ (c + 1) (n + 1)) ∧
      (3 ≤ b → alphaZ b (n + 1) ≤ (b : ℤ) ^ n)

    TheoremMatrix powers and the quadratic identity#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaMat_pow ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_quadratic ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_addition

    Let \(b\) be a natural number, \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\) and \(M=M_b\). Then:

    1. for every \(n\ge1\), $\(M^n=\begin{pmatrix}U_{n+1}&-U_n\\U_n&-U_{n-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det M^n=1;\)$
    2. for every \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(U_n^2+U_{n+1}^2-b\,U_nU_{n+1}=1\); equivalently, for \(n\ge1\), \(U_n^2-bU_nU_{n-1}+U_{n-1}^2=1\);
    3. for every \(n\), \(U_n\) and \(U_{n+1}\) are coprime in \(\mathbb Z\) and are not both even;
    4. (addition formula) for all \(m,n\in\mathbb N\), \(U_{m+n+1}=U_{m+1}U_{n+1}-U_mU_n\).

    Proof sketch. Item 1 by induction on \(n\) from \(M^{n+1}=M\cdot M^n\) and the recurrence; \(\det M=1\). The determinant reads \(U_n^2-U_{n+1}U_{n-1}=1\), and substituting \(U_{n+1}=bU_n-U_{n-1}\) gives item 2 (which can also be proved directly by induction, the left side being invariant under \((U_n,U_{n+1})\mapsto(U_{n+1},bU_{n+1}-U_n)\)). Item 3: item 2 is the Bezout relation \(U_n(U_n-bU_{n+1})+U_{n+1}\cdot U_{n+1}=1\), and reducing it modulo \(2\) excludes two even terms. Item 4 by two-step induction on \(n\), or from the lower-left entry of \(M^{m+n}=M^mM^n\).

    alphaMat b ^ n =
      !![alphaZ b (n + 1), -alphaZ b n; alphaZ b n, -alphaZ b (n - 1)]

    alphaZ b n ^ 2 + alphaZ b (n + 1) ^ 2 - (b : ℤ) * alphaZ b n * alphaZ b (n + 1) = 1
    theorem alphaZ_addition (b m n : ℕ) :
    alphaZ b (m + n + 1) = alphaZ b (m + 1) * alphaZ b (n + 1) - alphaZ b m * alphaZ b n

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_modEq_of_modEq ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_modEq_index

    Let \(b,b',d\) be natural numbers with \(b\equiv b'\pmod d\). Then for every \(n\in\mathbb N\) $\(\alpha_b(n)\equiv\alpha_{b'}(n)\pmod d .\)$ In particular \(\alpha_2(n)=n\) for every \(n\), and therefore \(\alpha_b(n)\equiv n\pmod d\) whenever \(b\equiv2\pmod d\).

    This is congruence in the parameter, not merely between indices: it transports residues from the parameter \(w\) of the alpha formula to the parameters \(b\) (modulo \(v\)) and \(2\) (modulo \(u\)).

    Proof sketch. Induct on \(n\) with the statements for \(n\) and \(n+1\) together: \(\alpha_b(n+2)=b\,\alpha_b(n+1)-\alpha_b(n)\) and each factor is congruent to its counterpart for \(b'\). For \(b=2\) the recurrence \(U_{n+2}=2U_{n+1}-U_n\) with \(U_0=0\), \(U_1=1\) has the solution \(U_n=n\). The case \(d=0\) is the trivial case \(b=b'\).

    TheoremNatural solutions of the alpha Pell equation#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_pell_unordered ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_pell_ordered ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_pell_converse
    1. (Unordered) Let \(b>2\) and \(x,y\in\mathbb N\) with \(x^2+y^2=1+bxy\). Then there is \(n\in\mathbb N\) with \(\{x,y\}=\{\alpha_b(n),\alpha_b(n+1)\}\); in particular \(x=\alpha_b(n')\) for some \(n'\in\mathbb N\).
    2. (Ordered) Let \(b\) be any natural number and \(r<s\) natural numbers with \(s^2+r^2=1+bsr\). Then there is \(m>0\) with \(r=\alpha_b(m-1)\) and \(s=\alpha_b(m)\).
    3. Conversely, for \(b\ge2\) every pair \((\alpha_b(n),\alpha_b(n+1))\) consists of natural numbers, satisfies the equation, and has \(\alpha_b(n)<\alpha_b(n+1)\).

    No ordering assumption on the pair in item 1 is needed.

    Proof sketch. In item 1, \(x\ne y\), since \(2x^2=1+bx^2\) is impossible for \(b\ge2\); order the pair as \(X>Y\). Item 2 is proved by strong induction on the smaller entry \(Y=r\): if \(Y=0\) then \(X^2=1\) and \((X,Y)=(1,0)=(U_1,U_0)\). Otherwise put \(Z=bY-X\in\mathbb Z\). Vieta's relation for the quadratic in \(X\) gives \(XZ=Y^2-1\); hence \(Z\ge0\) (as \(Y\ge1\)) and \(Z<Y\) (as \(X>Y\)), and \((Y,Z)\) satisfies the same equation with \(Y>Z\). By induction \((Z,Y)=(U_{m-1},U_m)\) for some \(m>0\), and then \(X=bY-Z=U_{m+1}\). Item 1 follows from item 2 applied to the ordered pair. Item 3 is the growth lemma and the quadratic identity.

    theorem alphaZ_pell_unordered {b x y : ℕ} (hb : 2 < b) (h : x ^ 2 + y ^ 2 = 1 + b * x * y) :
    ∃ n : ℕ, ((x : ℤ) = alphaZ b n ∧ (y : ℤ) = alphaZ b (n + 1)) ∨
      ((x : ℤ) = alphaZ b (n + 1) ∧ (y : ℤ) = alphaZ b n)
    theorem alphaZ_pell_ordered (b : ℕ) {r s : ℕ} (hrs : r < s)
    (h : s ^ 2 + r ^ 2 = 1 + b * s * r) :
    ∃ m : ℕ, 0 < m ∧ (r : ℤ) = alphaZ b (m - 1) ∧ (s : ℤ) = alphaZ b m

    ∃ x y : ℕ, (x : ℤ) = alphaZ b n ∧ (y : ℤ) = alphaZ b (n + 1) ∧ x < y ∧

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_dvd_alphaZ_iff ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaZ_sq_dvd_alphaZ_iff

    Let \(b>2\) be a natural number and \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\).

    1. For \(k>0\) and every \(m\in\mathbb N\): \(U_k\mid U_m\iff k\mid m\).
    2. (Equation (C.1)) For every \(k\in\mathbb N\) and every \(m>0\): $\(U_k^2\mid U_m\iff kU_k\mid m .\)$ For \(k=0\) both sides are false.

    Divisibility is in \(\mathbb Z\); all values involved are natural by the growth lemma.

    Proof sketch. (1) Reducing \(M^k\) modulo \(U_k\) gives the scalar matrix \(U_{k+1}I\) up to the entry \(-U_{k-1}\equiv U_{k+1}\) (from the recurrence \(U_{k+1}+U_{k-1}=bU_k\)), which is invertible modulo \(U_k\) by coprimality of adjacent terms. Equivalently, by the addition formula \(U_{k+n}=U_{k}U_{n+1}-U_{k-1}U_n\), so \(U_k\mid U_{k+n}\iff U_k\mid U_n\) (as \(\gcd(U_k,U_{k-1})=1\)). Removing periods of length \(k\) reduces to \(m<k\), where \(0\le U_m<U_k\) by strict growth, so \(U_k\mid U_m\) iff \(U_m=0\) iff \(m=0\). (2) For \(k=0\): \(U_0^2=0\) divides \(U_m\ge m>0\) never, and \(0\mid m\) never. For \(k>0\), both sides imply \(k\mid m\) (the left by (1) since \(U_k\mid U_m\)); write \(m=kq\) with \(q\ge1\). From \(M^k\equiv U_kM-U_{k-1}I\) entrywise (the recurrence gives \(M^k=U_kM-U_{k-1}I\) exactly), $\(M^{kq}=(U_kM-U_{k-1}I)^q\equiv(-U_{k-1})^qI+qU_k(-U_{k-1})^{q-1}M\pmod{U_k^2},\)$ whose lower-left entry gives \(U_m\equiv qU_k(-U_{k-1})^{q-1}\pmod{U_k^2}\). Since \(\gcd(U_k,U_{k-1})=1\), \(U_k^2\mid U_m\iff U_k\mid q\iff kU_k\mid kq=m\); this includes \(U_k=1\).

    alphaZ b k ^ 2 ∣ alphaZ b m ↔ (k : ℤ) * alphaZ b k ∣ (m : ℤ)

    Let \(b>2\) be a natural number, \(U_n=\alpha_b(n)\), \(m>0\), and put $\(v=U_{m+1}-U_{m-1}=\operatorname{tr}(M_b^m)=bU_m-2U_{m-1}.\)$

    1. (Folding) Every \(n\in\mathbb N\) can be written as \(n=2lm+j\), or as \(n=2lm-j\) with \(0<j<2lm\), for some \(l\in\mathbb N\) and \(0\le j\le m\).
    2. (Period and reflection) For all \(l,j\in\mathbb N\), $\(U_{j+2lm}\equiv(-1)^lU_j\pmod v,\qquad U_{2lm-j}\equiv-(-1)^lU_j\pmod v\ \ (0<j<2lm).\)$ Consequently, for \(n\) folded as in item 1, \(U_n\equiv\pm U_j\pmod v\).
    3. (Bounds) If \(b>3\) then \(2U_j<v\) for every \(0\le j\le m\); in particular \(v>2U_m\).

    The value sign in item 2 need not equal the index sign. In the manuscript's notation, \(n=2lm+\varepsilon j\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) and \(U_n\equiv(-1)^l\varepsilon U_j\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Take \(l\) with \(2lm\le n<2(l+1)m\) and fold to the nearer of \(2lm\) and \(2(l+1)m\). (2) \(M^m\) has determinant \(1\) and trace \(v\), so by Cayley-Hamilton \(M^{2m}=vM^m-I\equiv-I\pmod v\), hence \(M^{2lm}\equiv(-1)^lI\). The lower-left entry of \(M^{j+2lm}=M^jM^{2lm}\) gives the period congruence. For the reflection, the lower-left entry of \(M^{2lm}M^{-j}\), where \(M^{-j}\) is the inverse matrix (entries in \(\mathbb Z\) since \(\det M=1\)), equals \(U_{2lm-j}\) and is congruent to \((-1)^l\cdot(-U_j)\). In terms of the recurrence alone: the identity \(U_{n+2m}+U_n=vU_{n+m}\) (the addition formula) gives the period, and \(U_{N-j}=U_N(-U_{j-1})+(-U_{N-1})(-U_j)\) with \(N=2lm\), \(U_N\equiv0\), \(U_{N-1}\equiv-(-1)^l\), gives the reflection.     DefinitionThe alpha formula#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.AlphaEqns

    For natural numbers \(X,b,N\), the alpha formula \(\mathrm{Alpha}(X,b,N)\) is the statement that there are natural numbers \(r,s,t,u,v,w,x,y\) satisfying the fifteen conditions (C.2): $\(\begin{gathered} b>3,\quad u^2+t^2=1+but,\quad s^2+r^2=1+bsr,\quad r<s,\quad u^2\mid s,\quad v+2r=bs,\\ w\equiv b\pmod v,\quad w\equiv2\pmod u,\quad w>2,\quad x^2+y^2=1+wxy,\\ 2X<u,\quad 2X<v,\quad x\equiv X\pmod v,\quad 2N<u,\quad x\equiv N\pmod u . \end{gathered}\)$ Here \(p\equiv q\pmod M\) means \(p\bmod M=q\bmod M\) for natural numbers (so for \(M=0\) it means \(p=q\)), and \(p\mid q\) is divisibility in \(\mathbb N\).

    The compiler emits this system as the alpha macro: each strict inequality, divisibility and congruence is replaced by an equation with fresh natural witnesses (see the equational gadgets), and the macro introduces its eight witnesses in the order \(r,s,t,u,v,w,x,y\). The conditions are exactly those of Exp_Matrices.alpha_equations in the AFP development, with \(a=X\) and \(c=N\); equation order and the orientation of each equality are immaterial.

    This is a definition.

    def AlphaEqns (X b N : ℕ) : Prop :=

    ZInQ.AlphaPower.alphaEqns_sound

    For all natural numbers \(X,b,N\): if \(\mathrm{Alpha}(X,b,N)\) holds then \(b>3\) and \(X=\alpha_b(N)\).

    Proof sketch. Fix witnesses \(r,s,t,u,v,w,x,y\).

    1. Shapes. From \(u^2+t^2=1+but\) and \(b>2\), \(u=\alpha_b(k)\) for some \(k\) (unordered Pell classification); \(u>2X\ge0\) gives \(u\ge1\), hence \(k>0\). From \(s^2+r^2=1+bsr\) with \(r<s\), \((r,s)=(\alpha_b(m-1),\alpha_b(m))\) with \(m>0\) (ordered classification). From \(x^2+y^2=1+wxy\) and \(w>2\), \(x=\alpha_w(n)\) for some \(n\in\mathbb N\).
    2. \(u\mid m\). \(u^2\mid s\) is \(\alpha_b(k)^2\mid\alpha_b(m)\) with \(m>0\); by (C.1) \(k\,\alpha_b(k)\mid m\), so \(u\mid m\).
    3. Residues. \(v+2r=bs\) identifies \(v=\alpha_b(m+1)-\alpha_b(m-1)\) (the companion). Parameter congruence with \(w\equiv b\pmod v\) gives \(x\equiv\alpha_b(n)\pmod v\); with \(w\equiv2\pmod u\) and \(\alpha_2(n)=n\) it gives \(x\equiv n\pmod u\).
    4. Recover \(X\). Fold \(n\) as \(n=2lm\pm j\) with \(0\le j\le m\). By period and reflection, \(\alpha_b(n)\equiv\pm\alpha_b(j)\pmod v\), so \(X\equiv x\equiv\pm\alpha_b(j)\pmod v\). Both \(X\) and \(\alpha_b(j)\) are nonnegative and below \(v/2\) (\(2X<v\) by hypothesis, \(2\alpha_b(j)<v\) by the companion bound for \(b>3\), \(j\le m\)), so \(v\mid X-\alpha_b(j)\) or \(v\mid X+\alpha_b(j)\) forces \(X=\alpha_b(j)\) (in the second case both are \(0\)).
    5. Recover \(N\). \(j\le\alpha_b(j)=X\), so \(2j\le2X<u\); also \(2N<u\). Since \(u\mid m\), the folded form gives \(n\equiv\pm j\pmod u\), and \(N\equiv x\equiv n\pmod u\). Two residues below \(u/2\) that agree up to sign modulo \(u\) are equal: \(N=j\). Hence \(X=\alpha_b(N)\).

    The natural-subtraction case \(n=2lm-j\) requires \(0<j<2lm\), which the folding lemma supplies.

    Forward. By alpha soundness, \(a_0=\alpha_{Q+4}(R+1)\); then \(b=a_0+Q^2+2\ge4\), so the two other alpha formulas give \(a_1=\alpha_b(R)\), \(a_2=\alpha_b(R+1)\).     TheoremLucas's criterion for bit inclusion and disjointness#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.lucas_choose_odd_iff_masked ZInQ.lucas_choose_add_odd_iff_disjoint

    For natural numbers \(a,b\) write \(a\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}b\) for the bitwise conjunction of their binary expansions, and say \(a\) is masked by \(b\) when \(a\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}b=a\) (every set bit of \(a\) is set in \(b\)). Then:

    1. \(\binom ba\) is odd if and only if \(a\) is masked by \(b\);
    2. \(\binom{a+b}a\) is odd if and only if \(a\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}b=0\).

    Proof sketch. (1) Lucas's theorem at the prime \(2\): \(\binom ba\equiv\binom{b\bmod2}{a\bmod2}\binom{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}\pmod2\), and \(\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\) for bits \(\alpha,\beta\) is \(0\) exactly when \(\alpha=1,\beta=0\); induct on \(b\). Equivalently, \((1+t)^b=\prod_{i:\,\mathrm{bit}_i(b)=1}(1+t^{2^i})\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). (2) By (1) it suffices that \(a\) is masked by \(a+b\) iff \(a\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}b=0\). If the bits are disjoint the addition has no carries and \(a+b=a\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}b\). Otherwise, at the least position where both \(a\) and \(b\) have a set bit there is no incoming carry (below it at most one summand has each bit set, so no carry is generated), so that bit of \(a+b\) is \(0\) while the bit of \(a\) is \(1\).
