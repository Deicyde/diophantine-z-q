    Translation to rational point codes

    The natural precision polynomial speaks about natural numbers; the target polynomial has rational witnesses. This chapter translates every natural-number constraint into rational point-code constraints and proves that the translation is exact: natural variables become codes typed by the natural-code predicate, additions become raw additions, multiplications become multiplication templates, constants become fixed-scalar templates, power calls become calls of the power-value recipe, and the root becomes one equality of codes. This page follows Section 10.4; the interpreter, the lowering, the wrapper and the five-input specialization of Section 10.3 are in the natural chapter, whose equation (C.4) is the input here.

    Throughout, \(\iota(n)\) is the code of \([n]P_0\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_\tau\) is the rational relation of a template or recipe, and the multiplication statement M is used in its all-input form: \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{multiply}}(p,q,r)\) holds exactly when \(p=\iota(a)\), \(q=\iota(b)\) and \(r=\iota(ab)\) for some integers \(a,b\). The multiplication chapter proves M from the six-contact estimate, pole completeness and the twenty-two-prime pattern; the statements below assume M explicitly. The Mordell–Weil theorem, used through the generator certificate, is proved by the two-isogeny descent and is not a hypothesis.

    The typing order and the natural predicate

    The typing order does not depend on the natural predicate. Membership forces a canonical code in \(D=2E(K_0)\), raw addition on two canonical inputs forces their canonical sum, the fixed scalar templates give \([m]Q\) on \(D\), and inside \(\mathtt{multiply}\) the three root memberships are forced conjuncts of the height certificate, so every raw addition of the certificate receives canonical codes. Only once multiplication is typed on all rational inputs do the four square products and three additions of \(\mathtt{natural}\) make sense: its four untyped square roots are typed by the multiplication calls before their squares are added, and Lagrange's theorem gives exactly the codes of the natural numbers. This needs the completeness half of M. The outputs of these additions are canonical by the raw-addition contract, so no separate membership of the output is needed. The signed-encoding and exceptional templates of the assembly chapter start their addition chains at such a natural code, at the literal origin or at the fixed signed generator; the fixed generator equations are conjuncts outside the internal products of the templates that use them, and an inactive product branch is never assumed true.

    TheoremLagrange's four-square theorem#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Translation.four_squares

    For every natural number \(n\) there are natural numbers \(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3\) with $\(n=a_0^2+a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2 .\)$

    Proof sketch. Classical. By Euler's four-square identity the set of sums of four integer squares is closed under multiplication, so it suffices to treat primes; \(2=1+1+0+0\). For an odd prime \(p\) the \((p+1)/2\) values \(x^2\) and the \((p+1)/2\) values \(-1-y^2\) modulo \(p\) cannot be pairwise distinct, so \(x^2+y^2+1\equiv0\pmod p\) for some \(x,y\); hence some multiple \(mp\) with \(1\le m<p\) is a sum of four squares, and the least such \(m\) is \(1\) by Euler's descent. Replacing each integer \(a_i\) by \(|a_i|\) gives natural numbers.

    TheoremThe natural-code predicate#✓fully proved

    Assume the multiplication statement M in its all-input form: for all \(p,q,r\in\mathbb Q^5\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{multiply}}(p,q,r)\iff\exists\,a,b\in\mathbb Z:\ p=\iota(a),\ q=\iota(b),\ r=\iota(ab).\)$ Then for every \(A\in\mathbb Q^5\) (an arbitrary rational \(5\)-tuple, not assumed to be a code) $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{natural}}(A)\iff A=\iota(n)\ \text{for some }n\in\mathbb N .\)$ In words: the natural-code template accepts exactly the codes of the nonnegative indices. Its four square roots are typed by the multiplication calls before their squares are added, so the three raw additions only ever receive canonical codes.

    Proof sketch. The polynomial of \(\mathtt{natural}\) is the square of the compilation of a conjunction of seven calls with pairwise disjoint private blocks; the points \(\sigma_0,\dots,\sigma_3,\Sigma_0,\dots,\Sigma_3,\pi_1,\pi_2\) are fresh variables occurring in no block. By the private-block theorem, \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{natural}}(A)\) holds exactly when there are \(\sigma_i,\Sigma_i,\pi_1,\pi_2\in\mathbb Q^5\) with $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{multiply}}(\sigma_i,\sigma_i,\Sigma_i)\ (i=0,\dots,3),\quad \mathrm{Add}_{\rm raw}(\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1,\pi_1),\quad\mathrm{Add}_{\rm raw}(\pi_1,\Sigma_2,\pi_2),\quad \mathrm{Add}_{\rm raw}(\pi_2,\Sigma_3,A).\)$

    (\(\Rightarrow\)) By M, \(\sigma_i=\iota(a_i)=\iota(b_i)\) and \(\Sigma_i=\iota(a_ib_i)\) for integers \(a_i,b_i\). Since \(\iota\) is injective (generator certificate), \(a_i=b_i\) and \(\Sigma_i=\iota(a_i^2)\). (Injectivity can be avoided: codes are injective on points, so \([a_i]P_0=[b_i]P_0\) and \([a_ib_i]P_0=[a_i]([b_i]P_0)=[a_i]([a_i]P_0)=[a_i^2]P_0\).) The three raw additions now have canonical inputs, so by the raw-addition contract \(\pi_1=\iota(a_0^2+a_1^2)\), \(\pi_2=\iota(a_0^2+a_1^2+a_2^2)\) and \(A=\iota(a_0^2+a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2)\), and \(n=\sum_ia_i^2\in\mathbb N\).

    (\(\Leftarrow\)) Write \(n=\sum_ia_i^2\) with \(a_i\in\mathbb N\) by Lagrange's theorem and put \(\sigma_i=\iota(a_i)\), \(\Sigma_i=\iota(a_i^2)\), \(\pi_1=\iota(a_0^2+a_1^2)\), \(\pi_2=\iota(a_0^2+a_1^2+a_2^2)\). The multiplication relations hold by M (with \(a=b=a_i\)) and the three additions by the raw-addition contract; the private-block theorem supplies the witnesses of the seven calls.

    The completeness half of M is essential here; the generator certificate alone does not give this predicate.

    Natural circuits and their translation

    A natural circuit has explicit variables, constant, variable, addition and multiplication nodes, and call nodes that substitute the power template with a private block of seventy-nine natural witnesses. Its value at an integer assignment is computed node by node; a call contributes the value of the power template, which is not required to vanish.
