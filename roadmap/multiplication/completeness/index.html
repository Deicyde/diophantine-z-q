    Completeness: true triples are accepted

    Completeness says that the multiplication template accepts \((\iota(a),\iota(b),\iota(ab))\) for all integers \(a,b\). It uses the slope representation, a consequence of the twenty-two-prime pattern 22P, to choose the slopes, pole completeness PC to supply the pole witnesses, and the generator certificate (hence Mordell-Weil) through the fixed-scalar and guard semantics. It does not use the six-contact estimate.

    Slopes from the prime pattern

    Each of the nine integral homogeneous quantities of a true triple is written as a sum of three slopes \(u_j/v\) whose twenty-two linear forms take values \(\pm kp\) with \(k\) in the fixed catalogue multipliers and \(p\) a large good prime. Each slope then has bounded valuations at the old primes \(S_b\) and at most six odd contact primes outside \(S_b\), each of exact valuation one and good.

    TheoremThe two-slope pattern gives the slope representation#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Mult.slopeRepresentation_of_twoSlopePattern

    Assume the two-slope pattern. Then the slope representation holds: every integer \(A\) is the sum of the three good slopes $\(\frac uv+\Bigl(A+10-\frac uv\Bigr)+(-10)=A,\)$ with \(u,v\) given by the pattern for \(A\).

    Proof sketch. The twelve numbers \(u-cv\) and \(u-(A+10-c)v\) (\(c\in\mathcal C\)) are, up to sign, good slope primes greater than \(324\) outside \(\mathcal H\), hence outside \(S_b\); and \(v\), \(u-v\), \(u-(A+9)v\) have valuation at most \(B_q\) at every \(q\in S_b\).

    The slope \(t=u/v\). Here \(t-c=(u-cv)/v\). If \(u=v\) then \(u+15v=16v\) would be prime, so \(u-v\ne0\), and \(t\notin\mathcal C\cup\{1\}\). At \(q\in S_b\) every \(u-cv\) is a \(q\)-adic unit, so \(v_q(t-1)=v_q(u-v)-v_q(v)\) and \(v_q(F(t)/(t-1)^8)=2v_q(v)-8v_q(u-v)\) lie in \([-B_q,B_q]\) and \([-8B_q,8B_q]\). An odd contact prime \(q\notin S_b\) of \(t\) divides some \(u-cv\), so it is the prime \(|u-cv|\), a good slope prime; then \(v_q(u-c'v)\le1\) for all \(c'\), and \(v_q(t-c')>0\) forces \(v_q(v)=0\) and \(v_q(t-c')=1\). There are at most six such primes, one for each \(c\).

    The slope \(A+10-u/v=u_2/v\), with \(u_2=(A+10)v-u\). Now \(u_2-cv=-(u-(A+10-c)v)\) and \(u_2-v=-(u-(A+9)v)\), and the same argument applies with the other six primes.

    The slope \(-10\). Its contact values \(-10-c\) are \(-10,5,10,14,20,50\), whose prime factors are at most \(7\), so it has no odd contact prime outside \(S_b\). Further \(-10-1=-11\) and \(F(-10)=-7\cdot10^6=-2^6\cdot5^6\cdot7\), so at \(q\le324\) the two valuations of condition 2 are at most \(1\le4\) and \(13\le32\) in absolute value, and at \(q\in\mathcal H\) they vanish.

    The witnesses of a test group

    Each odd contact prime of a slope gets its own private test group. At such a prime, with reduced group orders \(d_1,d_2\), \(\gcd(d_1,d_2)=4\), the shift \(h\) is chosen with \(h\equiv0\pmod{\operatorname{lcm}(M_0,d_1)}\) and \(h\equiv c_*\pmod{d_2}\), and \(U_i=[n_i]H\). Then \(H,U_i\) lie in the formal group at one place and \(V_0,V_i\) at the conjugate place, and the four \(L\)-tests are congruent to \(n_i-n_i\) and \(n_1n_2-n_3=0\) modulo \(p^{28}\). Groups not assigned to a prime use \(h=2c_*\); the manuscript leaves them unspecified.
