    Multiplication and the finite false-triple guard

    The multiplication template is the arithmetic core of the construction: it must accept a triple of rational \(5\)-tuples exactly when they are the codes \(\iota(a),\iota(b),\iota(ab)\) of integer multiples of the generator \(P_0\) of \(2E(K_0)\). Its \(35{,}134\) witnesses consist of three rational constants fixing \(P_0\), four fixed scalar multiples by \(c_*=4M_0\), one height certificate of \(27\) slope certificates with six test groups each, and one inverse of a finite guard product. This chapter proves input M of the assembly lemma.

    The proof is asymmetric. Soundness (accepted triples are true) works on arbitrary rational inputs and uses the six-contact height estimate H: at every odd contact prime of every slope outside \(S_{\rm mul}\), the certificate forces \(n_1n_2\equiv n_3\) modulo \(p^{25}\), so H bounds a false triple to the box \([-T,T]^3\) with \(T=(48A_{\rm ht})^{13}\), where the guard excludes it. Completeness (true triples are accepted) uses the twenty-two-prime pattern 22P to choose slopes with controlled contact primes and pole completeness PC to supply the pole witnesses. Both directions use the generator certificate \(2E(K_0)=\mathbb ZP_0\), whose Mordell-Weil input is proved by the two-isogeny descent. The astronomically large constants \(c_*\), \(\ell_0\), \(A_{\rm ht}\) and \(T\) appear only symbolically; the finite computations of the chapter are a few small closed identities and inequalities.

    The pole obstruction

    Write \(\Phi(B)\) for the rational solubility of the pole polynomial \(Q_{\rm pole}\) with input \(B\). For \(B\ne0\) the witnesses give a signed divisor \(m\) of \(2730\) and points \((A,r_1)\in C_e\), \((Az^2,zr_2)\in C_{eH}\) on the congruent number curves \(C_\ell:y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell)\) (the manuscript has \(x+3\ell\)), and a parity argument on the valuation of the abscissa shows that \(\Phi(B)\) is impossible when \(v_p(B)\) is negative and odd at a prime \(p\notin\{2,3,5,7,13\}\). This elementary direction is what soundness uses; the converse, pole completeness, is used only for completeness, and the Route K chapter proves it from the certified rank hypothesis.

    LemmaParity of the abscissa on \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell)\)#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Mult.abscissa_val_even

    Let \(p\) be a prime, \(\ell\in\mathbb Q^\times\) with \(v_p(\ell)\) even, and \(x,y\in\mathbb Q\) with \(x\ne0\) and $\(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell).\)$ Then \(v_p(x)\) is even.

    Proof sketch. If \(v_p(x)=v_p(\ell)\) there is nothing to prove. Otherwise \(v_p(x-\ell)=v_p(x+\ell)=\min(v_p(x),v_p(\ell))\), by the equality case of the ultrametric inequality, at every prime \(p\); in particular both factors are nonzero, so the right side is nonzero, \(y\ne0\), and $\(2v_p(y)=v_p(x)+2\min\bigl(v_p(x),v_p(\ell)\bigr).\)$ Hence \(v_p(x)\) is even. (This is the manuscript's square-scaling step: after scaling \(\ell\) to a unit, the cubic has valuation \(v_p(x)\) if \(v_p(x)>0\) and \(3v_p(x)\) if \(v_p(x)<0\).) The manuscript's curve \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+3\ell)\) needs \(p\ne3\) at this step; the Route K curve needs no condition on \(p\).

    LemmaThe one-sided pole obstruction#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Mult.pole_obstruction

    Let \(S_0=\{2,3,5,7,13\}\) and let \(\Phi(B)\) be the rational solubility of the pole polynomial \(Q_{\rm pole}\) with input \(B\), as in pole completeness (that article is linked for the definition of \(\Phi\) only; the hypothesis PC is not assumed). If \(B\in\mathbb Q\), \(B\ne0\) and \(\Phi(B)\), then for every prime \(p\notin S_0\) it is not the case that \(v_p(B)\) is negative and odd.

    This is Lemma 5.1, for the Route K pole polynomial, whose curves are \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+\ell)\) instead of the manuscript's \(y^2=x(x-\ell)(x+3\ell)\); the statement and \(S_0\) are unchanged. It uses no hypothesis: it is the elementary direction of the pole predicate, and the multiplication soundness argument uses it at the odd contact primes.

    Proof sketch. Take a rational zero \((b_0,\dots,b_5,e,z,r_1,r_2,w)\) of \(Q_{\rm pole}\) with input \(B\ne0\). Since \(B\ne0\) the sum of squares in \(Q_{\rm pole}\) vanishes, so every summand does: \(b_i^2=b_i\), hence \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\); \(eHzw=1\), hence \(e,H,z\ne0\); \(r_1^2=T_1\) and \(r_2^2=T_2\). The multiplier \(m\) is a product of factors in \(\{\pm1\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{1,5\},\{1,7\},\{1,13\}\), so it is a signed divisor of \(2730=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot13\) and \(v_p(m)=0\) for \(p\notin S_0\).

    Suppose \(k=v_p(B)<0\) is odd. Then \(v_p(A)=v_p(mB)=k\); since \(2k<k\), \(v_p(A-B^2)=2k\), so \(v_p(H)=3k\). The equations say that \((A,r_1)\) lies on \(C_e:y^2=x(x-e)(x+e)\) and, multiplying \(r_2^2=T_2\) by \(z^2\), that \((Az^2,zr_2)\) lies on \(C_{eH}\): \((zr_2)^2=z^2T_2=Az^2(Az^2-eH)(Az^2+eH)\). Both abscissas are nonzero (\(m\ne0\) because \(1-2b_0=\pm1\) and the other factors are positive, and \(z\ne0\)), and both parameters \(e,eH\) are nonzero. The valuations of \(e\) and \(eH\) differ by the odd number \(3k\), so exactly one of them is even. If \(v_p(e)\) is even, the parity lemma applied to \((A,r_1)\) makes \(v_p(A)=k\) even; if \(v_p(eH)\) is even, it makes \(v_p(Az^2)=k+2v_p(z)\) even. Both contradict \(k\) odd.

    TheoremCorrectness of the multiplication template#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Mult.multiplication_exact ZInQ.Mult.multiplication_exact_unique ZInQ.Mult.multiplication_existsUnique

    Assume the minimal height hypothesis, pole completeness and the slope representation. For all \(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3\in\mathbb Q^5\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{multiply}}(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)\iff\exists\,a,b\in\mathbb Z:\quad (Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)=\bigl(\iota(a),\iota(b),\iota(ab)\bigr),\)$ where \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{multiply}}(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)\) means that the polynomial of the multiplication template, which has \(15\) parameters and \(35{,}134\) witnesses, vanishes at \((Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)\) and some \(w\in\mathbb Q^{35134}\). The inputs are arbitrary rational \(5\)-tuples, not assumed to be codes; when the relation holds, \(a\) and \(b\) are unique.

    This is input M of the assembly lemma. The two directions use different hypotheses:

    • soundness (\(\Rightarrow\)) uses the six-contact estimate H in its minimal form (through the finite-box deduction) and the generator certificate, but not pole completeness and not the slope representation;
    • completeness (\(\Leftarrow\)) uses pole completeness PC and the slope representation, which the prime pattern 22P gives (to build the slope certificates of a true triple), and the generator certificate (through the fixed-scalar and guard semantics), but not the six-contact estimate.

    Neither direction uses the catalogue bound. The Mordell–Weil input of the generator certificate is proved by the two-isogeny descent, so it is not a hypothesis. No constant of the template (\(c_*\), \(\ell_0\), \(T\)) is evaluated. The template's three parameter points, written \(P_0,P_1,P_2\) in the specification, are written \(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3\) in this chapter, since \(P_0\) denotes the generator.

    Proof sketch. Combine soundness of the multiplication template with completeness of the multiplication template. Uniqueness of \(a,b\) is injectivity of \(\iota\) (the generator certificate).
