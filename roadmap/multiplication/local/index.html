    Local conventions: the formal group and the truncated logarithm

    The multiplication certificate reads arithmetic information off points of \(2E(K_0)\) at finite places. This section fixes the local conventions of the manuscript and proves the local facts that both directions of the multiplication statement use. Throughout, \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\) with \(d=75-53s\), a point of \(E(K_0)\) is regarded as a point over every completion \(K_{\mathfrak p}\) of \(K_0\), \(z=-x/y\) and \(w=1/x\) with \(z(O)=w(O)=0\), and \(E_1(K_{\mathfrak p})\) is the set of points with \(v_{\mathfrak p}(x)<0\) together with \(O\). For a point \(P\) of \(E(K_0)\) with \(x(P)\ne0\), \(P\in E_1(K_{\mathfrak p})\) exactly when \(v_{\mathfrak p}(1/x(P))\ge1\); this is how the inverse-abscissa tests detect the formal group.

    Primes of \(K_0\)

    An odd prime \(p\) is unramified in \(K_0\); it splits into two conjugate primes when \(2\) is a square modulo \(p\) and is inert otherwise. Conjugation exchanges the valuations at conjugate primes, which is how the conjugated tests of a test group refer to the other place above \(p\).

    Let \(p\) be an odd prime and \(\sigma\) the conjugation \(s\mapsto-s\) of \(K_0\).

    1. If \(2\) is a square modulo \(p\), choose \(r\in\mathbb Z\) with \(r^2\equiv2\pmod p\). Then \(\mathfrak p_r=(p,s-r)\) and \(\mathfrak p_{-r}=(p,s+r)\) are distinct prime ideals of \(\mathbb Z[s]\), \(a+bs\mapsto(a+br)\bmod p\) induces \(\mathbb Z[s]/\mathfrak p_r\cong\mathbb F_p\), \(p\mathbb Z[s]=\mathfrak p_r\mathfrak p_{-r}\) and \(\sigma(\mathfrak p_r)=\mathfrak p_{-r}\). Otherwise \(p\mathbb Z[s]\) is prime, with residue field of \(p^2\) elements, and \(\sigma(p\mathbb Z[s])=p\mathbb Z[s]\). In both cases these are all the prime ideals of \(\mathbb Z[s]\) containing \(p\).
    2. For a prime \(\mathfrak p\ni p\) let \(v_{\mathfrak p}\) be the normalized valuation of \(K_0\) (\(v_{\mathfrak p}(K_0^\times)=\mathbb Z\), \(v_{\mathfrak p}(0)=+\infty\)). Then \(p\) is unramified: \(v_{\mathfrak p}(q)=v_p(q)\) for every \(q\in\mathbb Q\).
    3. \(v_{\sigma\mathfrak p}(y)=v_{\mathfrak p}(\sigma y)\) for every \(y\in K_0\).
    4. For \(y\in K_0\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z\), if \(v_{\mathfrak p}(y)\ge m\) and \(v_{\sigma\mathfrak p}(y)\ge m\), then \(v_p(\operatorname{Tr}y)\ge m\), where \(\operatorname{Tr}y=y+\sigma y\in\mathbb Q\).

    At the prime \(2\) the corresponding facts are those of the prime \((s)\).

    Proof sketch. (1) is Dedekind-Kummer for \(\mathbb Z[s]=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^2-2)\), whose discriminant \(8\) is prime to \(p\): \(X^2-2\) is separable modulo \(p\) and factors as \((X-r)(X+r)\) or is irreducible.     The formal group and its logarithm

    For the integral equation \(y^2=x^3+Ax\) over a discretely valued field, the points with \(v(x)<0\) form a subgroup on which \(z\) is injective, with \(v(w)=2v(z)\), filtered by the subgroups \(v(z)\ge k\), so multiplication by an integer never lowers \(v(z)\); this part has an elementary chord-and-tangent proof. Over a complete field, \(z\) turns the group law into a formal group law, whose formal logarithm converges on \(v(z)>0\), is a homomorphism, and is an isometry where \(v(z)>e/(p-1)\); at an odd unramified prime this is the whole formal group. These are classical (Silverman IV and VII.2); here they are background statements, the formal group law and the logarithm being the deepest inputs of the chapter. The manuscript's remark that identifying the formal neighbourhood with the reduction kernel of a minimal model needs Silverman VII.2.1-2.2 is not used: every argument below works with the integral equation of \(E\) itself.

    Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic \(0\) with a discrete valuation \(v\) normalized by \(v(K^\times)=\mathbb Z\) (for instance \(K_0\) with \(v_{\mathfrak p}\), or a completion of \(K_0\)). Let \(A\in K^\times\) with \(v(A)\ge0\), and \(W:y^2=x^3+Ax\) over \(K\). Put $\(W_1(K)=\{O\}\cup\{(x,y)\in W(K):v(x)<0\},\)$ and for \(P=(x,y)\in W_1(K)\) put \(z(P)=-x/y\), \(w(P)=1/x\) and \(\omega(P)=-1/y\), with \(z(O)=w(O)=\omega(O)=0\). Then:

    1. for every affine \(P=(x,y)\in W_1(K)\) there is an integer \(N\ge1\) with \(v(x)=-2N\) and \(v(y)=-3N\); hence \(y\ne0\), \(v(z(P))=N\), \(v(w(P))=2N=2v(z(P))\) and \(v(\omega(P))=3N\);
    2. for every integer \(k\ge1\) the set \(W_k(K)=\{P\in W_1(K):v(z(P))\ge k\}\) (containing \(O\)) is a subgroup of \(W(K)\); in particular \(W_1(K)\) is a subgroup;
    3. \(z\) is injective on \(W_1(K)\);
    4. for every \(P\in W_1(K)\) and \(n\in\mathbb Z\), \([n]P\in W_1(K)\) and \(v(z([n]P))\ge v(z(P))\).

    In part 1, \(w=1/x\) is the manuscript's parameter; it differs from the auxiliary variable \(\omega=-1/y\) of Silverman, which is used only inside the proof. This is the elementary part of Silverman VII.2.2 (there stated for a minimal equation over a complete field; only the integrality of \(A\) is used). The formal group law itself is a separate statement.

    Proof sketch. (1) If \(v(x)<0\) then \(v(Ax)\ge v(x)>3v(x)\), so \(2v(y)=v(x^3+Ax)=3v(x)\); thus \(v(x)\) is even, \(v(x)=-2N\) with \(N\ge1\), and \(v(y)=-3N\).

    Coordinates. For affine \(P\in W_1(K)\), \(x=z/\omega\) and \(y=-1/\omega\), and dividing the equation by \(-y^3\) gives $\(\omega=z^3+Az\omega^2 .\tag{\)*\(}\)$ Conversely a pair \((z,\omega)\) with \(\omega\ne0\) satisfying \((*)\) is the image of the affine point \((z/\omega,-1/\omega)\). If \(v(z)\ge1\), \(v(\omega)\ge1\) and \((z,\omega)\) satisfies \((*)\), then \(v(Az\omega^2)>v(\omega)\), so \(v(\omega)=3v(z)\); in particular \(\omega=0\) iff \(z=0\). The map \((x,y)\mapsto(z,\omega)\) is the projective change of coordinates \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[-X:-Z:Y]\), which sends \(O=[0:1:0]\) to \((0,0)\) and lines to lines.

    (2)-(3) Negation is \((x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)\), i.e. \((z,\omega)\mapsto(-z,-\omega)\), which preserves \(v(z)\). Let \(P_1,P_2\in W_k(K)\) be affine with coordinates \((z_i,\omega_i)\), so \(v(z_i)\ge k\) and \(v(\omega_i)=3v(z_i)\ge3k\). Subtracting the two instances of \((*)\) gives the identity $\((\omega_2-\omega_1)\bigl(1-Az_1(\omega_1+\omega_2)\bigr)=(z_2-z_1)\bigl(z_1^2+z_1z_2+z_2^2+A\omega_2^2\bigr),\)$ whose left factor \(u=1-Az_1(\omega_1+\omega_2)\) is a unit. Hence \(z_1=z_2\) forces \(\omega_1=\omega_2\), i.e. \(P_1=P_2\); this is (3). Put $\(\lambda=\frac{z_1^2+z_1z_2+z_2^2+A\omega_2^2}{1-Az_1(\omega_1+\omega_2)},\qquad \nu=\omega_1-\lambda z_1 .\)$ If \(P_1\ne P_2\), \(\omega=\lambda z+\nu\) is the line through them; if \(P_1=P_2\) the same formula gives the tangent, by implicit differentiation of \((*)\): \(\omega'(1-2Az\omega)=3z^2+A\omega^2\). In both cases \(v(\lambda)\ge2k\) and \(v(\nu)\ge3k\). Substituting \(\omega=\lambda z+\nu\) in \((*)\) gives the cubic $\((1+A\lambda^2)z^3+2A\lambda\nu z^2+(A\nu^2-\lambda)z-\nu=0,\)$ whose leading coefficient is a unit and whose roots, with multiplicity, are \(z_1\), \(z_2\) and the \(z\)-coordinate \(z_3\) of the third intersection point \(Q\) of the line with \(W\); by the chord construction of the group law, \(P_1+P_2=-Q\). By Vieta, $\(z_3=-z_1-z_2-\frac{2A\lambda\nu}{1+A\lambda^2},\qquad v(z_3)\ge k .\)$ If \(z_3=0\) then \(\omega_3=\nu\) and \((*)\) gives \(\nu=0\), so \(Q=O\) and \(P_1+P_2=O\in W_k(K)\). Otherwise \(\omega_3=\lambda z_3+\nu\) satisfies \(v(\omega_3)\ge3k\ge1\), hence \(v(\omega_3)=3v(z_3)\) and \(\omega_3\ne0\), so \(Q=(z_3/\omega_3,-1/\omega_3)\) is affine with \(v(x(Q))=-2v(z_3)\le-2k\), and \(P_1+P_2=-Q\in W_k(K)\) with \(z(P_1+P_2)=-z_3\). Sums involving \(O\) are trivial. (Alternatively, \(z(P_1+P_2)=-z_3\) can be verified directly from the Weierstrass addition formulas: the \((x,y)\)-line \(y=\ell x+m\) is \(\omega=\lambda z+\nu\) with \(\lambda=-\ell/m\), \(\nu=-1/m\) when \(m\ne0\), and the case \(m=0\) is the line through \(O\).)

    (4) By (2) with \(k=v(z(P))\) (or \(P=O\)), \(W_k(K)\) is a subgroup containing \(P\), hence \([n]P\).
