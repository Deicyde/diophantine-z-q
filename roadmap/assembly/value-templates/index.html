    The value templates and their relations

    The value relation is assembled from the membership template, three value templates (signed encoding, coordinate window, value test), the exceptional-values template and the precision recipe, combined by five composition recipes. This section proves what each template expresses on arbitrary rational inputs. Every proof has the same shape: the template polynomial is the square of a compiled formula whose calls have disjoint private blocks, so the private-witness lemma replaces each call by its relation, and the model statements identify the relations on codes.

    Nonzero multiples of \(P_0\) are affine with nonzero coordinates (nonzero multiples of the generator, in the model chapter); this makes every local parameter and inverse abscissa below well-defined.

    Natural and signed codes

    The natural-code template types four untyped tuples by multiplication before adding their squares, so it accepts exactly \(\iota(\mathbb N)\) by Lagrange's theorem; this is the natural-code predicate of the compiler chapter, with M supplied by the multiplication template. The signed encoding links a signed code \(\iota(m)\) to the natural code \(\iota(\operatorname{enc}m)\), in which form the integer enters the precision relation.

    TheoremSemantics of the signed encoding#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.signedEncode_rel_iff

    Assume the six-contact estimate, pole completeness and the twenty-two-prime pattern. Then for all \(N,C\in\mathbb Q^5\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value.signed\_encode}}(N,C)\iff\exists\,m\in\mathbb Z:\ N=\iota(m)\ \text{and}\ C=\iota(\operatorname{enc}(m)),\)$ equivalently, \(C=\iota(c)\) for some \(c\in\mathbb N\) and \(N=\iota(\operatorname{dec}(c))\).

    Proof sketch. The template polynomial is the sum of the squares of the three generator equations and of \(\kappa(\varphi)\). By the rational-constant lemma the generator equations force \((c_1,c_2,c_3)=(1641/8,-15465/4,87483/32)\), so \(G=(0,c_1,-145,c_2,c_3)=\iota(1)\); they are outside the internal disjunction and constrain both branches. By the private-witness lemma, \(\mathrm{Rel}(N,C)\) holds exactly when there are points \(Q,Q_2,Q_+\) with \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{member}}(N)\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{member}}(C)\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{natural}}(Q)\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{raw\_add}}(Q,Q,Q_2)\) and one of the branches (i) \(N=Q\), \(C=Q_2\), or (ii) \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{raw\_add}}(Q,G,Q_+)\), \(N=\operatorname{neg}(Q_+)\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{raw\_add}}(Q_2,G,C)\).

    (\(\Rightarrow\)) The natural-code predicate, with M from the multiplication template, gives \(Q=\iota(q)\) with \(q\in\mathbb N\), and raw addition on canonical codes gives \(Q_2=\iota(2q)\). In branch (i), \(N=\iota(q)\) and \(C=\iota(2q)=\iota(\operatorname{enc}q)\). In branch (ii), \(Q_+=\iota(q+1)\), \(N=\operatorname{neg}(\iota(q+1))=\iota(-(q+1))\) and \(C=\iota(2q+1)\); since \(-(q+1)<0\), \(\operatorname{enc}(-(q+1))=2q+1\).

    (\(\Leftarrow\)) For \(m\ge0\) take \(Q=\iota(m)\), \(Q_2=\iota(2m)\) and branch (i). For \(m<0\) take \(Q=\iota(-m-1)\), \(Q_2=\iota(-2m-2)\), \(Q_+=\iota(-m)\) and branch (ii); then \(\operatorname{neg}(Q_+)=\iota(m)\) and the last raw addition gives \(\iota(-2m-1)=\iota(\operatorname{enc}m)\). The membership, natural and raw-addition relations hold by the accepted-indices statement, the natural-code predicate (with M from the multiplication template) and raw addition; an inactive branch takes arbitrary witnesses.

    The coordinate window

    For nonzero \(a,l\) the coordinate-window template expresses the strict window \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\), with \(r(a,j)=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(z_j/z_a)\) and \(\epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|\).

    TheoremSemantics of the coordinate window#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.closeCoordinates_rel_iff

    For all \(A,L,J\in\mathbb Q^5\) and \(t\in\mathbb Q\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value.close\_coordinates}}(A,L,J,t)\) holds exactly when there are \(a,l,j\in\mathbb Z\) with $\(A=\iota(a),\quad L=\iota(l),\quad J=\iota(j),\quad a\ne0,\quad l\ne0,\quad |t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l ,\)$ with \(r(a,j)=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(z_j/z_a)\) and \(\epsilon_l\) formed from the local parameter \(z\) on multiples of the generator, extended by \(z_0=0\) (so \(r(a,0)=0\)); \(a,l\) and every nonzero \(j\) are admissible by the nonzero-multiples lemma.

    Proof sketch. The template is \(\kappa(\varphi)^2\); its calls (\(\mathtt{member}\) three times, \(\mathtt{value.abs}\) three times) have disjoint blocks occurring in no parameter, so by the private-witness lemma the relation holds exactly when there are pairs \(\zeta_A,\zeta_L,\zeta_J,q\) and rationals \(v,a_0,a_1,\delta,u_\epsilon,z_1,\dots,z_4\) and inverse witnesses making the following true: \(A,L,J\) are accepted by membership, \(A[0]=L[0]=0\) (first code coordinates), the gadgets \(Z(A,\zeta_A)\), \(Z(L,\zeta_L)\), \(Z(J,\zeta_J)\), \(\operatorname N(\zeta_A)v=1\) and \(\zeta_A\cdot q=\zeta_J\), \(a_0=|(\zeta_L)_0|\), \(a_1=|(\zeta_L)_1|\), \((a_0+a_1)u_\epsilon=1\), \(\delta=|t-q_0|\) and \((a_0+a_1-\delta)(z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2+z_4^2)=1\).

    By the accepted-indices statement, membership means \(A=\iota(a)\), \(L=\iota(l)\), \(J=\iota(j)\). The conditions \(A[0]=L[0]=0\) say \(a\ne0\) and \(l\ne0\) (first code coordinate). By the nonzero-multiples lemma, every nonzero multiple of \(P_0\) has nonzero abscissa and ordinate, so by pair arithmetic the gadget \(Z(\iota(m),\zeta)\) is satisfiable and forces \([\zeta]=z_m\) (also for \(m=0\), where \(z_0=0\)). Then \(\operatorname N(\zeta_A)\ne0\) holds because \(z_a\ne0\), and \([q]=z_j/z_a\), so \(q_0=r(a,j)\). The absolute-value template gives \(a_0+a_1=\epsilon_l\), which is nonzero because \(z_l\ne0\), so its inverse witness exists. Finally \(\delta=|t-r(a,j)|\), and the four-square positivity statement turns the last equation into \(\epsilon_l-|t-r(a,j)|>0\). Conversely, for \(a,l\ne0\) and the strict window, all these witnesses exist.

    The exceptional values

    Double-and-add chains of raw additions, started at the literal origin and the fixed signed generator, produce the codes of the six contact values.

    LemmaDouble-and-add chains#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.chain_value ZInQ.Assembly.chain_satisfiable ZInQ.Assembly.chainBuild_binDigits_value ZInQ.Assembly.chainCount_contacts

    Let \(m\in\mathbb Z\), \(k\in\mathbb N\), and consider the double-and-add chain for \(k\) on the point \(p=\iota(m)\), as defined for the exceptional-values template: starting from \(\mathrm{out}=O\), \(\mathrm{base}=p\), it takes for each odd bit a fresh point \(\sigma_i\) with the call \(\langle\mathtt{raw\_add}[\mathrm{out},\mathrm{base},\sigma_i]\rangle\) and for each further bit a fresh point \(\delta_i\) with \(\langle\mathtt{raw\_add}[\mathrm{base},\mathrm{base},\delta_i]\rangle\). Then:

    1. for every assignment of rational \(5\)-tuples to the fresh points such that all raw-addition relations of the chain hold, the value of the chain (its final \(\mathrm{out}\)) is \(\iota(km)\);
    2. there is such an assignment.

    The number of raw additions is the number of one bits of \(k\) plus the bit length of \(k\) minus one (\(0\) for \(k=0\)). For \(k=0,15,20,24,30,60\) this gives \(0,7,6,6,8,9\).

    Proof sketch. Induction along the chain with the invariant that after \(i\) steps \(\mathrm{base}=\iota(2^im)\) and \(\mathrm{out}=\iota(k_im)\), where \(k_i=k\bmod2^i\). Both inputs of every raw addition are then canonical codes, so raw addition forces the fresh output to be \(\iota(k_im+2^im)\) or \(\iota(2^{i+1}m)\), which preserves the invariant; at the end \(k_i=k\). For 2, assign each fresh point the code forced in this way; raw addition is complete on canonical codes. Initially \(\mathrm{out}=O=\iota(0)\) is the literal code. No property of \(P_0\) beyond lying on \(E\) is used.

    Proof sketch. Each outer recipe is a composition recipe: its polynomial is the sum of the squares (for \(\mathtt{value\_union}\), the product) of its call polynomials, which is \(\kappa\) of the conjunction (disjunction) of the call atoms. By the witness accounting and the witness-scope theorem, the call blocks lie inside the recipe's variables, are pairwise disjoint, are disjoint from the fresh ranges, and occur in no parameter. The private-witness lemma then replaces each call by its callee relation, and the fresh ranges (\(A,L,J,E_0,\dots,E_4\) for \(\mathtt{close}\), \(K\) for \(\mathtt{main}\)) become existential quantifiers. For the product recipe, the block of an inactive factor takes arbitrary values; this does not discard any fixed equation of the active factor.
