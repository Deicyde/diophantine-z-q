    Assembly and the ordinary-value relation

    This chapter proves that the rational zero projection of the specified polynomial \(P_*\) is exactly \(\mathbb Z\), and assembles the target statement. It follows Section 11 of the manuscript (the variable-shift relation for ordinary values) and Lemma 1.2 (assembly).

    The polynomial never sees an integer directly. A rational input \(t\) comes with a code \(N\in\mathbb Q^5\), which membership types as \(N=\iota(n)\) for an integer index \(n\); the value relation must then force \(t=n\). The point coordinates and the integer index play distinct roles throughout: the index is what the precision computation and the height estimate reason about, the coordinates are what the polynomial sees. For the six contact values \(n\in\mathcal C=\{0,-15,-20,-24,-30,-60\}\) an exceptional branch states \(t=n\) outright. For every other \(n\), the main branch chooses one shift \(\kappa\ne0\), a five-coordinate code shared by two relations: Close, which says that \(t\) lies within \(e^{-B(n,\kappa)}\) of \(n\) with \(B(n,\kappa)=H_6(S_{\rm ht})|\kappa F(n)|^{24}\), and Test, which through the height estimate bounds \(h(t)\le B(n,\kappa)\). A rational number of height at most \(B\) that is so close to an integer is that integer.

    The constants \(D\), \(H_6(S_{\rm ht})\) and \(B(n,\kappa)\) are astronomically large. Every argument here treats them symbolically through their defining expressions; none is ever evaluated.

    Approximation for every fixed shift

    The relation Close links the five signed codes \(N,\mathcal K,A,L,J\) to the inputs of the compiled precision relation (input C) and imposes the coordinate window \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\). It is sound, \(|t-n|<e^{-B(n,\kappa)}\), and complete for every fixed nonzero shift, because positive multiples of \(P_0\) approach the identity at both real places and the derivative of \([n]\) at the identity is \(n\).

    The six local tests

    The relation Test forms six field-valued tests from the shift, puts them over one common denominator \(F(t)\) into one product \(W\), one trace \(\Theta\) and one instance \(\Phi(R/\Theta^2)\) of the pole predicate. Soundness (Lemma 11.1) uses the pole obstruction and the formal groups at the primes above each contact prime to show \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\mid\kappa F(n)\). Completeness (Lemma 11.2) chooses \(\kappa\) deep in the formal groups above the primes of \(2730^6F(n)\) and applies pole completeness (PC).

    Exact ordinary value

    In an accepting main branch, the divisibility lemma and the six-contact estimate (H) give \(h(t)\le B(n,\kappa)\), and soundness of Close gives \(|t-n|<e^{-B(n,\kappa)}\); a reduced fraction \(a/b\ne n\) has \(|t-n|\ge1/b\ge e^{-h(t)}\), so \(t=n\).

    TheoremAn accepting main branch forces the exact value#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.main_branch_eq

    Assume the minimal height hypothesis, pole completeness and the slope representation. Let \(n,\kappa\in\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\mathbb Q\). If \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),t)\) and \(\operatorname{Test}(n,\kappa,t)\), then \(t=n\).

    Proof sketch. Soundness of Close gives \(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\) and \(|t-n|<e^{-B(n,\kappa)}\). The test gives \(t\notin\mathcal C\) and, by the divisibility lemma, \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\mid\kappa F(n)\); as \(\kappa F(n)\ne0\), \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\le|\kappa F(n)|\). Apply the minimal height hypothesis at \(u=t\notin\mathcal C\); since \(S_{\rm ht}\subseteq S_{\rm mul}\), \(M_{S_{\rm mul}}(t)\) divides \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\), so $\(h(t)\le H_6(D)\,M_{S_{\rm mul}}(t)^{24}\le H_6(D)\,M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)^{24}\le A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}\,|\kappa F(n)|^{24}=B(n,\kappa),\)$ where \(H_6(D)=H_6(S_{\rm ht};D)=A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}\) is the precision height constant. Suppose \(t=a/b\ne n\) in lowest terms, \(b>0\). Then \(|a-nb|\ge1\), so $\(|t-n|\ge1/b\ge e^{-h(t)}\ge e^{-B(n,\kappa)},\)$ contradicting soundness of Close. Hence \(t=n\). No numerical value of \(A^{(0)}_{\rm ht}\) or \(D\) is needed.

    TheoremThe exact ordinary-value relation#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.value_relation_iff ZInQ.Assembly.exists_value_relation_iff

    Assume the minimal height hypothesis, pole completeness and the slope representation. Then for every \(N\in\mathbb Q^5\) and every \(t\in\mathbb Q\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value}}(N,t)\iff\exists\,n\in\mathbb Z:\ N=\iota(n)\ \text{and}\ t=n .\)$ Consequently, for every \(t\in\mathbb Q\), $\(\bigl(\exists\,N\in\mathbb Q^5:\ \mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value}}(N,t)\bigr)\iff t\in\mathbb Z .\)$

    In the manuscript's notation, \(\operatorname{Val}(N,t)\) is membership of \(N\) together with \(\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}(N=\iota(c)\wedge t=c)\ \vee\ \exists\mathcal K\,(\operatorname{Close}(N,\mathcal K,t)\wedge\operatorname{Test}(N,\mathcal K,t))\), with the same five coordinates \(\mathcal K\) in both calls, and \(\operatorname{Val}(\iota(n),t)\) is exactly the graph \(t=n\).

    Proof sketch. By the relations of the outer recipes, \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value}}(N,t)\) holds exactly when \(N\) is accepted by membership and either the exceptional relation holds or there is \(K\) with \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(N,K,t)\) and \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value.test}}(N,K,t)\).

    (\(\Rightarrow\)) Membership gives \(N=\iota(n)\). In the exceptional branch, \(N=\iota(c)\) and \(t=c\) for some \(c\in\mathcal C\), and \(c=n\) by injectivity of \(\iota\). In the main branch, the value-test semantics gives \(N=\iota(n')\), \(K=\iota(\kappa)\) and \(\operatorname{Test}(n',\kappa,t)\) with \(n'=n\); then \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),t)\) and \(\operatorname{Test}(n,\kappa,t)\) force \(t=n\).

    (\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(N=\iota(n)\) and \(t=n\); \(N\) is accepted by membership. If \(n\in\mathcal C\), the exceptional relation holds. If \(n\notin\mathcal C\), choose \(\kappa\ne0\) with \(\operatorname{Test}(n,\kappa,n)\) (one suitable shift), then \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),n)\) for that same shift (completeness of Close), and \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{value.test}}(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),n)\); the main relation holds with \(K=\iota(\kappa)\). The two calls have disjoint private blocks, so they are satisfied simultaneously, and the block of the inactive factor of the product takes arbitrary values.

    For the consequence, project to \(t\): the right side becomes \(\exists n\in\mathbb Z,\ t=n\). For \(n\in\mathcal C\) the main branch is never accepted (Close requires \(n\notin\mathcal C\)), so the six exceptional integers are given exactly by their displayed branches.

    Assembly

    The root recipe squares one call of the value relation with a fresh code, so the rational zero projection of \(P_*\) is the projection of the value relation; with the witness accounting and the size bounds of the specification and size chapters this proves the target statement for \(P_*\) under the four hypotheses. The main theorem follows directly.

    TheoremThe assembly lemma#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.Pstar_spec ZInQ.Assembly.assembly

    Assume the minimal height hypothesis, pole completeness and the slope representation. Then the specified polynomial \(P_*\in\mathbb Z[X_0,\dots,X_{292890231904}]\) satisfies:

    1. \(\deg P_*<2\uparrow\uparrow7\);
    2. every coefficient of \(P_*\) has absolute value less than \(2\uparrow\uparrow8\);
    3. for every \(q\in\mathbb Q\), $\(\bigl(\exists\,w\in\mathbb Q^{292890231904}:\ P_*(q,w)=0\bigr)\iff q\in\mathbb Z .\)$

    In particular \(P_*\) witnesses the target statement, from which the main theorem follows at once.
