    The six local tests

    The relation Test (defined with the value templates) puts the six tests \(D_1,\dots,D_6\) over the common denominator \(F(t)\), forms \(W=\prod(1+RC_j^2)\), the trace \(\Theta=\operatorname{Tr}(W^{-1})\) and one pole instance \(\Phi(R/\Theta^2)\). Both lemmas about it are local: soundness works at the primes above one contact prime \(p>324\), completeness at the primes above \(2730^6F(n)\) and at the two real places.

    Valuations and formal groups

    Odd primes are unramified in \(\mathbb Z[s]\) and the trace of an element that is small at the primes above \(p\) is small at \(p\) (the primes above an odd prime); \(2\) is ramified, and at \((s)\) the trace gains one more power of \(2\).

    Let \(v_s\) be the valuation of \(K_0\) at the prime \((s)\), normalized by \(v_s(s)=1\) (so \(v_s(2)=2\)), \(\sigma\) the conjugation \(s\mapsto-s\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}x=x+\sigma x\in\mathbb Q\). Then:

    Soundness

    At a prime above a contact prime \(p\), the pole obstruction forces the pole input to have positive valuation, which makes all six tests small at one prime above \(p\). The first four put the four points in formal groups, and the last two, through the multiplication estimate, give \(s_0/h\equiv t\) and \((s_0-\kappa n)/(h-\kappa)\equiv t\), hence \(p\mid\kappa(n-c)\).

    LemmaDivisibility from the six local tests#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.test_dvd

    Let \(n,\kappa\in\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\mathbb Q\). If \(\operatorname{Test}(n,\kappa,t)\) holds, then $\(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\ \big|\ \kappa F(n),\)$ where \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\) is the product of the primes \(p>324\) for which \(v_p(t-c)\) is positive and odd for some \(c\in\mathcal C\). No unit assumption on \(\kappa\) is used, and no hypothesis is needed.

    Proof sketch. Fix the data \(h,s_0,\eta,q\) of the test. Since \(M_{S_{\rm ht}}(t)\) is squarefree, it suffices to show \(p\mid\kappa F(n)\) for every prime \(p>324\) with \(\delta=v_p(t-c)>0\) odd for some \(c\in\mathcal C\). For \(c'\ne c\) in \(\mathcal C\), \(0<|c-c'|\le60<p\), so \(v_p(t-c')=0\); hence \(v_p(F(t))=\delta\), and \(t\) is \(p\)-integral. The primes above \(p\) have \(v_\pi(p)=1\), and \(v_\pi\) restricts to \(v_p\) on \(\mathbb Q\).

    Valuation of \(R\). \(\eta\) and \(2730^6\) are \(p\)-units, so \(v_p(R_0q^2)=\delta+2v_p(q)\) when \(q\ne0\). Put \(b=v_p(a_0)\). If \(q=0\) then \(b=0\); otherwise \(\delta+2v_p(q)\) is odd, hence nonzero, and \(b=\min(0,\delta+2v_p(q))\le0\). So \(\alpha=v_p(R)=\delta+2b\) is odd.

    Valuation of \(W\). At each prime \(\pi\) above \(p\), \(v_\pi(RC_j^2)=\alpha+2(v_\pi(D_j)-\delta)\) is odd, hence nonzero, so \(v_\pi(1+RC_j^2)=\min\bigl(0,\alpha+2(v_\pi(D_j)-\delta)\bigr)\) (this is \(0\) if \(D_j=0\)). Let \(\lambda=\max_{\pi\mid p}v_\pi(W)\le0\). Since \(v_\pi(W^{-1})\ge-\lambda\) at every \(\pi\) above \(p\), the trace statement gives \(v_p(\Theta)\ge-\lambda\ge0\).

    The pole input. \(B_{\rm pole}=R/\Theta^2\ne0\) has \(v_p(B_{\rm pole})=\alpha-2v_p(\Theta)\), which is odd; since \(\Phi(B_{\rm pole})\) and \(p\notin\{2,3,5,7,13\}\), the one-sided pole obstruction says it is not negative, so it is positive. Hence \(\alpha>2v_p(\Theta)\ge-2\lambda\ge0\): \(\alpha>0\) and \(\lambda>-\alpha/2\).

    All six tests are small at one prime. Take \(\pi\) above \(p\) with \(v_\pi(W)=\lambda\). The six terms \(\min(0,\alpha+2(v_\pi(D_j)-\delta))\) are \(\le0\) and sum to \(\lambda\), so each is \(\ge\lambda>-\alpha/2\); hence \(v_\pi(D_j)>\delta-3\alpha/4=\delta/4-3b/2>0\), so \(v_\pi(D_j)\ge1\) for \(j=1,\dots,6\). Let \(\pi'=\sigma(\pi)\), so \(v_\pi(\sigma(x))=v_{\pi'}(x)\).

    Formal groups. \(v_\pi(D_1)\ge1\) and \(v_\pi(D_2)\ge1\) put \(H\ne O\) and \(U\) in \(E_1(\pi)\); \(v_\pi(D_3)\ge1\) and \(v_\pi(D_4)\ge1\) put \(V_0\ne O\) and \(V_1\) in \(E_1(\pi')\). The fifth test makes \(z(U)/z(H)\) integral at \(\pi\) and congruent to \(t\) modulo \(\pi\). Let \(g=\gcd(h,s_0)>0\) and \(Q=[g]P_0\). The set of \(m\) with \([m]P_0\in E_1(\pi)\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\) containing \(h\) and \(s_0\), so \(Q\in E_1(\pi)\), and \(Q\ne O\) because \(H=[h/g]Q\ne O\). By the multiplication statement (\(p\) odd), \(z(H)=(h/g)z(Q)u\) and \(z(U)=(s_0/g)z(Q)u'\) with \(u,u'\equiv1\pmod\pi\) (for \(s_0=0\) take \(u'=1\)). Hence \(s_0/h=(z(U)/z(H))(u/u')\) is \(\pi\)-integral and congruent to \(t\), so \(v_p(s_0/h-t)\ge1\) and, \(h\) being an integer, \(s_0\equiv th\pmod p\) in \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\). The same argument at \(\pi'\) with \(V_0=[h-\kappa]P_0\), \(V_1=[s_0-\kappa n]P_0\) and the sixth test gives \(s_0-\kappa n\equiv t(h-\kappa)\pmod p\).
