    Approximation for every fixed shift

    The precision computation of the compiler chapter decides, for five integers \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\) given by their signed encodings, whether \(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(a,l\ge1\), \(j=na\) and the two strict tests \(|r(a,j)-n|<\epsilon_l\) and \(4\epsilon_l<4^{-B(n,\kappa)}\) hold. All quantities are exact rationals and integers. The compiler chapter defines the precision relation, the local parameter and the window quantities, the signed encoding and the precision height constant, and proves that this constant is the six-contact constant \(H_6(S_{\rm ht})\), so that its bound is the \(B(n,\kappa)\) of the ordinary-value relation (identification). Its admissibility clause is automatic for nonzero indices, by the nonzero-multiples lemma.

    Close chooses codes \(A,L,J\), links the five signed codes to the precision inputs in the order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\) and imposes \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\) on the same coordinates.

    ZInQ.Assembly.close_relation_iff

    Assume the six-contact estimate, pole completeness and the twenty-two-prime pattern. Then for all \(N,K\in\mathbb Q^5\) and \(t\in\mathbb Q\), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(N,K,t)\) holds exactly when there are \(n,\kappa,a,l\in\mathbb Z\) with $\(N=\iota(n),\quad K=\iota(\kappa),\quad\operatorname{Prec}(n,\kappa,a,l,na),\quad |t-r(a,na)|<\epsilon_l ,\)$ where, for integers \(n,\kappa,a,l,j\), \(\operatorname{Prec}(n,\kappa,a,l,j)\) abbreviates the precision relation \(\operatorname{accepts}(\operatorname{enc}n,\operatorname{enc}\kappa,\operatorname{enc}a,\operatorname{enc}l,\operatorname{enc}j)\) at the signed encodings, and \(r(a,j)\) and \(\epsilon_l\) are the quantities of the local parameter \(z\), defined because \(\operatorname{Prec}\) includes the admissibility of \(l,a,j\). This is the manuscript's \(\operatorname{Close}(N,\mathcal K,t)\): choose \(A,L,J\), link the five signed codes \(N,\mathcal K,A,L,J\) to the precision inputs in the order \((n,\kappa,a,l,j)\), and impose \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\) on the same coordinates.

    Proof sketch. By the relations of the outer recipes, \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}(N,K,t)\) holds exactly when there are \(A,L,J,E_0,\dots,E_4\) with five signed-encoding relations, the precision relation and the coordinate window. Input C is used as a chain of three equivalences. Let \(C\) be the natural precision polynomial. For all \(E_0,\dots,E_4\in\mathbb Q^5\):

    • by the semantics of the rational translation (which uses the multiplication statement M and so the three hypotheses of M), \(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{precision}}(E_0,\dots,E_4)\) holds exactly when there is \(c\in\mathbb N^5\) with \(E_i=\iota(c_i)\) for \(0\le i\le4\) and \(C(c,\mathbf z)=0\) for some \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb N^{1305806}\);
    • by the semantics of the natural precision polynomial, the existence of such \(\mathbf z\) is \(\operatorname{accepts}(c)\);
    • since \(\operatorname{enc}\circ\operatorname{dec}\) is the identity, \(\operatorname{accepts}(c)\) is \(\operatorname{Prec}(\operatorname{dec}c_0,\dots,\operatorname{dec}c_4)\).

    Hence $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{precision}}(E_0,\dots,E_4)\iff\exists\,c\in\mathbb N^5:\ E_i=\iota(c_i)\ (0\le i\le4)\ \text{and}\ \operatorname{Prec}(\operatorname{dec}c_0,\dots,\operatorname{dec}c_4).\)$

    (\(\Rightarrow\)) The signed encodings give \(\Sigma_i=\iota(m_i)\) and \(E_i=\iota(\operatorname{enc}m_i)\) with \((m_0,\dots,m_4)=(n,\kappa,a,l,j)\). By input C and injectivity of \(\iota\), \(c_i=\operatorname{enc}m_i\), so \(\operatorname{Prec}(n,\kappa,a,l,j)\), in particular \(j=na\). The coordinate window gives indices \(a',l',j'\) with \(A=\iota(a')\) etc. and \(|t-r(a',j')|<\epsilon_{l'}\); injectivity gives \((a',l',j')=(a,l,j)\).

    (\(\Leftarrow\)) Put \(j=na\), \(A=\iota(a)\), \(L=\iota(l)\), \(J=\iota(j)\) and \(E_i=\iota(\operatorname{enc}m_i)\). The signed encodings hold, the precision relation holds by input C since \(\operatorname{dec}\circ\operatorname{enc}\) is the identity, and the coordinate window holds because \(a,l\ge1\) are nonzero.

    LemmaCompactness and continuity of subtraction on a real curve#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.realCurve ZInQ.Assembly.RealCurve.TendstoPt ZInQ.Assembly.RealCurve.exists_subseq_tendstoPt ZInQ.Assembly.RealCurve.tendstoPt_sub_zero

    Let \(e\in\mathbb R\), \(e\ne0\), and let \(E_e(\mathbb R)\) be the group of real points of \(y^2=x^3-e^2x\) (chord-and-tangent law, identity \(O\)). For a sequence \((P_m)\) in \(E_e(\mathbb R)\) and \(Q\in E_e(\mathbb R)\) write \(P_m\to Q\) when

    • \(Q=(x_Q,y_Q)\) is affine, \(P_m\) is affine for all large \(m\), and \(x(P_m)\to x_Q\), \(y(P_m)\to y_Q\); or
    • \(Q=O\) and for every \(M\in\mathbb R\), for all large \(m\), either \(P_m=O\) or \(P_m\) is affine with \(x(P_m)>M\).

    Then:

    1. every sequence in \(E_e(\mathbb R)\) has a subsequence converging to some point of \(E_e(\mathbb R)\);
    2. if \(P_m\to Q\) and \(P'_m\to Q\) for the same \(Q\), then \(P'_m-P_m\to O\).

    Proof sketch. Write \(f(x)=x^3-e^2x=(x+e)x(x-e)\); its roots \(0,\pm e\) are simple, with \(f'(c)=3c^2-e^2\ne0\) at each root. Real points have \(f(x)\ge0\), so \(x\ge-|e|\).

    (1) If infinitely many terms are \(O\), or the abscissas of the affine terms are unbounded above, a subsequence converges to \(O\). Otherwise the affine terms eventually lie in \([-|e|,M]\times[-\sqrt{\max f},\sqrt{\max f}]\), and Bolzano–Weierstrass gives a convergent subsequence; its limit satisfies \(y^2=f(x)\) by continuity.

    (2) Discard the indices with \(P'_m=P_m\), where the difference is \(O\). Case \(Q\) affine with \(y_Q\ne0\): eventually \(y'+y\to2y_Q\ne0\), so \(P'\ne-P\); if \(x'=x\) then \(P'=P\); otherwise \(P'-P=P'+(-P)\) has abscissa \(\lambda^2-x-x'\) with chord slope \(\lambda=(y'+y)/(x'-x)\), and \(|\lambda|\to\infty\). Case \(Q=(c,0)\) with \(f(c)=0\): if \(x'=x\) then \(P'=-P\) and \(P'-P=-[2]P\) has abscissa \(\bigl(f'(x)/(2y)\bigr)^2-2x\to\infty\) since \(f'(x)\to f'(c)\ne0\) and \(y\to0\); otherwise \(y'^2-y^2=(x'-x)\,g\) with \(g=x'^2+xx'+x^2-e^2\to f'(c)\ne0\), so eventually \(y'\ne y\) and \(\lambda=(y'+y)/(x'-x)=g/(y'-y)\) has \(|\lambda|\to\infty\). Case \(Q=O\): translate by \(T=(0,0)\). For affine \(P=(x,y)\) with \(x\ne0\), \(P+T=(-e^2/x,\ e^2y/x^2)\), and \(|e^2y/x^2|^2=e^4f(x)/x^4\to0\) as \(x\to\infty\), while \(O+T=T\); hence \(P_m+T\to T\) and \(P'_m+T\to T\), and \((P'_m+T)-(P_m+T)=P'_m-P_m\to O\) by the previous case. In every case the abscissa of \(P'_m-P_m\) tends to \(+\infty\) (or the difference is \(O\)).

    This is the elementary content of "\(E(\mathbb R)\) is a compact topological group"; the manuscript uses compactness of \(E(\mathbb R)\times E(\mathbb R)\) directly.

    TheoremMultiples of the generator approach the identity at both real places#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.exists_multiple_large_unconditional

    For every \(M\in\mathbb R\) there is an integer \(k\ge1\) such that \([k]P_0=(x_k,y_k)\) is affine and $\(\sigma_+(x_k)>M\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_-(x_k)>M .\)$ That is, positive multiples of \(P_0\) approach the identity simultaneously in the two real embeddings.

    Proof sketch. Each real embedding \(\sigma_\pm\) maps \(E\) to the real curve \(y^2=x^3-e_\pm^2x\) with \(e_\pm=\sigma_\pm(d)=75\mp53\sqrt2\ne0\), and induces a group homomorphism \(E(K_0)\to E_{e_\pm}(\mathbb R)\), \((x,y)\mapsto(\sigma_\pm x,\sigma_\pm y)\), \(O\mapsto O\) (the chord-and-tangent formulas are rational and commute with ring homomorphisms). Apply the compactness statement to the sequence \(\sigma_+([k]P_0)\), \(k\ge1\), and then to \(\sigma_-([k]P_0)\) along the resulting subsequence: there are \(k_1<k_2<\cdots\) and points \(Q_\pm\) with \(\sigma_\pm([k_i]P_0)\to Q_\pm\). By continuity of subtraction, \(\sigma_\pm([k_{i+1}-k_i]P_0)=\sigma_\pm([k_{i+1}]P_0)-\sigma_\pm([k_i]P_0)\to O\) in both embeddings. For \(i\) large, \(k=k_{i+1}-k_i\ge1\) has both embedded points equal to \(O\) or with abscissa \(>M\); since \(P_0\) has infinite order, \([k]P_0\) is affine, so both abscissas exceed \(M\).

    The manuscript's argument (partition the compact closure of the cyclic subgroup into small neighbourhoods and subtract two close multiples) is the same pigeonhole principle.

    LemmaThe derivative of multiplication at a real place#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.Assembly.zCoord ZInQ.Assembly.z_ratio_tendsto

    Let \(\tau\in\{\sigma_+,\sigma_-\}\) be a real embedding of \(K_0\) and \(n\) a nonzero integer. For every \(\eta>0\) there is \(M\in\mathbb R\) such that for every affine point \(Q=(x,y)\in E(K_0)\) with \(\tau(x)>M\):

    1. \(x\ne0\), \(y\ne0\), and \([n]Q\) is affine with nonzero abscissa and ordinate;
    2. \(\bigl|\tau\bigl(z([n]Q)/z(Q)\bigr)-n\bigr|<\eta\).

    This is the statement that the derivative of \([n]\) at the identity is \(n\), read in the coordinate \(z=-x/y\) near \(O\) at the real place \(\tau\).

    Proof sketch. Write \(f_m\) for the normalized division polynomials of \(y^2=x^3+Ax\), \(A=-d^2\), and \(\psi_m\) for the division polynomials. Since \(z(-P)=-z(P)\), it suffices to treat \(n\ge1\). By the multiplication-by-\(n\) formulas, if \(\psi_n(Q)\ne0\) then \([n]Q\) is affine with $\(\frac{x([n]Q)}{x}=\frac{\phi_n(x)}{x\,\psi_n^2(x)},\qquad \frac{y([n]Q)}{y}=\frac{f_{2n}(x)}{\psi_n^4(x)},\)$ where \(\phi_n=x\psi_n^2-\psi_{n+1}\psi_{n-1}\), and \(\psi_n^2\), \(\psi_n^4\) and \(\phi_n\) are polynomials in \(x\) after substituting \(y^2=x^3+Ax\) (the second identity is \(y([n]Q)=\psi_{2n}/(2\psi_n^4)\) with \(\psi_{2n}=2yf_{2n}\)). By the agreement with the classical division polynomials, the degrees and leading coefficients are: \(\phi_n\) degree \(n^2\), leading coefficient \(1\); \(\psi_n^2\) degree \(n^2-1\), leading coefficient \(n^2\); \(f_{2n}\) degree \(2n^2-2\), leading coefficient \(n\); \(\psi_n^4\) degree \(2n^2-2\), leading coefficient \(n^4\) (for odd \(n\) it is \(f_n^4\), for even \(n\) it is \(16(x^3+Ax)^2f_n^4\), and \(f_n\) has leading coefficient \(n\) or \(n/2\)). Apply \(\tau\) to the coefficients: as \(\tau(x)\to+\infty\) the two quotients tend to \(1/n^2\) and \(1/n^3\), and \(\tau(\psi_n(x)^2)\ne0\), \(\tau(y^2)=\tau(x)(\tau(x)^2-\tau(d)^2)>0\) for \(\tau(x)\) large. Hence for \(\tau(x)>M\) all quantities are nonzero, and $\(\tau\Bigl(\frac{z([n]Q)}{z(Q)}\Bigr)=\tau\Bigl(\frac{x([n]Q)}{x}\Bigr)\Big/\tau\Bigl(\frac{y([n]Q)}{y}\Bigr)\longrightarrow\frac{n^3}{n^2}=n .\)$ The polynomials depend only on \(n\) and \(A\), so \(M\) depends only on \(n\), \(\tau\) and \(\eta\).

    Assume the six-contact estimate, pole completeness and the twenty-two-prime pattern. For every \(n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\mathcal C\) and every nonzero \(\kappa\in\mathbb Z\), $\(\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathtt{close}}\bigl(\iota(n),\iota(\kappa),n\bigr).\)$ The shift \(\kappa\) is arbitrary: this holds for every already fixed nonzero \(\kappa\), whatever its size.

    Proof sketch. Put \(B=B(n,\kappa)\in\mathbb N\) and \(\theta=4^{-B}/4>0\). By the Close relation it suffices to find \(a,l\ge1\) with \(\operatorname{Prec}(n,\kappa,a,l,na)\) and \(|n-r(a,na)|<\epsilon_l\); the latter is one of the conditions of \(\operatorname{Prec}\).

    Choice of \(l\). For an affine point with abscissa \(x\) and \(\tau(x)>|\tau(d)|\) in a real embedding \(\tau\), \(\tau(z)^2=\tau(x)/(\tau(x)^2-\tau(d)^2)\), which tends to \(0\) as \(\tau(x)\to\infty\). Choose \(M_1\) such that \(\tau(x)>M_1\) gives \(|\tau(z)|<\theta/2\) for both \(\tau=\sigma_\pm\), and by the accumulation statement choose \(l\ge1\) with \(\sigma_\pm(x([l]P_0))>M_1\). Since \(u_l=(\sigma_+z_l+\sigma_-z_l)/2\) and \(v_l=(\sigma_+z_l-\sigma_-z_l)/(2\sqrt2)\), $\(0<\epsilon_l=|u_l|+|v_l|\le|\sigma_+z_l|+|\sigma_-z_l|<\theta,\)$ positivity because \(z_l\ne0\). Hence \(4\epsilon_l\cdot4^{B}<1\).

    Choice of \(a\). Apply the real derivative statement to \(n\) with \(\eta=\epsilon_l\) in both embeddings, obtaining \(M_2\), and choose \(a\ge1\) by accumulation with \(\sigma_\pm(x([a]P_0))>M_2\). With \(Q=[a]P_0\) and \([n]Q=[na]P_0\), $\(|r(a,na)-n|=\Bigl|\tfrac12\sum_\pm\bigl(\sigma_\pm(z_{na}/z_a)-n\bigr)\Bigr|<\epsilon_l .\)$

    With \(j=na\) all conditions of \(\operatorname{Prec}(n,\kappa,a,l,j)\) hold (\(n\notin\mathcal C\), \(\kappa\ne0\), \(a,l\ge1\), and \(l,a,j\) are admissible because they are nonzero), and the window \(|t-r(a,j)|<\epsilon_l\) holds for \(t=n\). The order of choices matters: \(l\) is fixed first, then \(a\) depending on \(\epsilon_l\).
