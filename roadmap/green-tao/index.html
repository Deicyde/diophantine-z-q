    Szemerédi's theorem in the primes (Green–Tao)

    Route AP replaces the twenty-two-prime pattern by long arithmetic progressions of good slope primes. They come from two theorems: the good slope primes have positive relative density (Proposition D), and every set of primes of positive relative upper density contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length (Green and Tao, Theorem 1.2). This chapter proves the second in Lean. It is the only part of the formalization that rests on code not written in this project: the Lean proof of the Green–Tao theorem for the set of all primes by Vasily Ilin, from the repository Vilin97/Autoformalization (directory green-tao/), distributed under the Apache License 2.0 and vendored in this repository with its license and a notice of the changes made to port it. The chapter extends that proof to dense sets of primes without changing any vendored file.

    Notation: \(\lambda_{\alpha,W,b}\) is the W-tricked prime weight on \(\mathbb Z/N\) and \(\lambda^A_{\alpha,W,b}\) its restriction to a set \(A\); \(\Lambda_k(f)\) is the normalized count of \(k\)-term progressions in \(\mathbb Z/N\) weighted by \(f\).

    DefinitionSzemerédi's theorem in the primes#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.GreenTaoDense ZInQ.ContainsArbitraryAPs ZInQ.PrimesRelUpperDensityPos

    A set \(A\) of natural numbers contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length if for every \(k\) there are natural numbers \(a\) and \(b\ge1\) with \(a+bj\in A\) for every \(j<k\). A set \(A\) has positive relative upper density in the primes if there is a real \(c>0\) such that $\(c\,\pi(N)\le\#\bigl(A\cap[0,N]\bigr)\)$ for infinitely many \(N\), where \(\pi\) is the prime counting function; for a set of primes this says \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\#(A\cap[1,N])/\pi(N)>0\).

    Szemerédi's theorem in the primes, in its dense form, is the assertion that every set of primes of positive relative upper density contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length.

    This is Theorem 1.2 of Green and Tao, with "infinitely many progressions" weakened to "one". Green and Tao prove it in outline; Tao and Ziegler give a complete proof. Route AP needs it for the good slope primes above \(\max S_b\). It was first a hypothesis of the route AP variants of the main theorem; it is proved in this chapter (Szemerédi's theorem in the primes holds).

    TheoremThe Green–Tao theorem#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.greenTao_primes Submission.green_tao

    The set of primes contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length: for every \(k\) there are natural numbers \(a\) and \(b\ge1\) such that \(a+bj\) is prime for every \(j<k\).

    This is Theorem 1.1 of Green and Tao. Its Lean proof is not written in this project: it is Vasily Ilin's proof from the repository Vilin97/Autoformalization (directory green-tao/, commit 0dc67d87416c19690dd641a178ada32cca95c651), produced under Ilin's direction as a submission to the green_tao target of the lean-eval benchmark and distributed under the Apache License 2.0. It is vendored in this repository with its license and a notice, and ported to the project's Lean and Mathlib versions with proof edits only; its statement is unchanged, and it is the statement here, since the two definitions of "contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length" agree word for word.

    Proof sketch. The vendored proof follows Green and Tao. After the W-trick (restricting to a reduced residue class \(b\) modulo \(W=\prod_{p\le w}p\) and weighting the primes \(Wn+b\) by \(\log(Wn+b)\)), the weighted primes in a cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/N\) of prime order are bounded by a Selberg-sieve majorant \(\nu\) that satisfies the linear forms condition. The relative Szemerédi theorem (transference: a weight \(0\le f\le\nu\) of positive mean has a dense model) and the dense Szemerédi theorem, from the hypergraph removal lemma, give a positive count of \(k\)-term progressions weighted by \(f\); the diagonal progressions are negligible, so some progression with nonzero difference has positive weight at every term, and a short-interval lift turns it into a progression of primes. Lengths \(k\le2\) come from the progression \(2,3\).

    From density to windows

    The density hypothesis counts all of \([0,N]\) at infinitely many \(N\); the W-trick samples a window \((X/4,X]\) at a scale tied to the cyclic modulus. A dyadic decomposition and Chebyshev's lower bound put a fixed share of the mass into some window of ratio \(2\), and Bertrand's postulate chooses a prime modulus whose sampled window contains most of it.

    TheoremThe window lemma#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.frequently_window ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.frequently_window_of ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.card_filter_range_le_add_Ioc ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.card_filter_range_le_sum_windows ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.isLittleO_sqrt_mul_log ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.eventually_window_error

    Let \(A\) be a set of natural numbers of positive relative upper density in the primes, with \(c\,\pi(N)\le\#(A\cap[0,N])\) for infinitely many \(N\), where \(c>0\). Put \(c_1=c\log2/24\). Then for infinitely many \(Z\), $\(\#\bigl(A\cap(\lfloor Z/2\rfloor,Z]\bigr)\ge\frac{c_1Z}{\log Z}.\)$ In particular some \(c_1>0\) has this property.

    The set \(A\) need not consist of primes. The point is that the density hypothesis, which counts all of \([0,N]\), yields mass in a window of ratio \(2\), which is what the sampling of the W-trick sees.

    Proof sketch. Let \(N\) be large with \(c\,\pi(N)\le\#(A\cap[0,N])\), and let \(J\) be the largest integer with \(2^J\le\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\). Cut \([0,N]\) into the tail \([0,\lfloor N/2^J\rfloor]\) and the windows \((\lfloor N/2^{j+1}\rfloor,\lfloor N/2^j\rfloor]\) for \(j<J\). The tail has at most \(2\sqrt N+1\) elements. Every window has \(Z_j=\lfloor N/2^j\rfloor\ge\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\), so \(\log Z_j\ge\frac13\log N\) for large \(N\), and \(Z_j\) tends to infinity with \(N\). If every window had fewer than \(c_1Z_j/\log Z_j\) elements of \(A\), the windows together would hold fewer than \(\sum_j 3c_1Z_j/\log N\le6c_1N/\log N=\frac c4\log2\cdot N/\log N\) elements. Chebyshev's lower bound \(\pi(N)\log N\ge N\log2-\log(N+1)\) then gives \(c\,\pi(N)>\#(A\cap[0,N])\) once the error terms \((2\sqrt N+2)\log N+c\log(N+1)\) are below \(\frac34c\log2\cdot N\), which holds for large \(N\) because \(\sqrt x\log x=o(x)\). So for every large such \(N\) some window, with \(Z_j\ge\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\), is good. Only the lower bound for \(\pi\) is used.

    The weight of a set of primes

    The weight of \(A\) is the prime weight times the indicator of \(A\). It lies below the prime weight, so majorization and the height bound carry over; positivity now means membership in \(A\), and the residue pigeonhole runs over the elements of \(A\) only.

    DefinitionThe W-tricked weight of a set of primes#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.wTrickedSetWeight ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.wTrickedSetWeight_nonneg ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.wTrickedSetWeight_le ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.wTrickedSetWeight_pos_iff

    Let \(N\ge1\), let \(\alpha\) be real and \(W,b\) natural numbers, and for \(n\in\mathbb Z/N\) let \(\bar n\) be its representative in \([0,N)\). The W-tricked prime weight of the Green–Tao proof is $\(\lambda_{\alpha,W,b}(n)=\begin{cases}\alpha\,\dfrac{\varphi(W)}{W}\log(W\bar n+b)&\text{if } \lfloor N/64\rfloor\le\bar n\le\lfloor N/4\rfloor\text{ and }W\bar n+b\text{ is prime},\\[1ex] 0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\)$ For a set \(A\) of natural numbers, the W-tricked weight of \(A\) is \(\lambda^A_{\alpha,W,b}(n)=\lambda_{\alpha,W,b}(n)\) if \(W\bar n+b\in A\) and \(0\) otherwise. If \(\alpha\ge0\), then \(0\le\lambda^A_{\alpha,W,b}\le\lambda_{\alpha,W,b}\); if \(\alpha>0\) and \(W>0\), then \(\lambda^A_{\alpha,W,b}(n)>0\) exactly when \(\lfloor N/64\rfloor\le\bar n\le\lfloor N/4\rfloor\) and \(W\bar n+b\) is a prime in \(A\).

    The bound \(\lambda^A\le\lambda\) is what lets the weight of \(A\) inherit, unchanged, the majorization of the prime weight by the Selberg-sieve majorant and its pointwise height bound.

    TheoremExtraction of a progression in the set#✓fully proved

    ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.containsAP_of_wTrickedSetWeight_offDiagMass_pos

    Let \(A\) be a set of natural numbers, \(k\ge2\), \(N\ge1\), \(\alpha>0\), \(W>0\) and \(b\) natural. If the off-diagonal mass of the weight of \(A\) is positive, $\(\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z/N}\ \sum_{d\in\mathbb Z/N,\ d\ne0}\ \prod_{j<k}\lambda^A_{\alpha,W,b}(a+jd)>0,\)$ then \(A\) contains a \(k\)-term arithmetic progression: there are natural numbers \(a'\) and \(d'>0\) with \(a'+jd'\in A\) for every \(j<k\).

    Proof sketch. Some \(a\) and \(d\ne0\) have \(\lambda^A(a+jd)>0\) for every \(j<k\). The short-interval lift of the vendored proof turns a cyclic progression inside such an interval into an integer progression with positive difference in \(\{n:Wn+b\in A\}\), and the affine map \(n\mapsto Wn+b\) carries it into \(A\). This is the vendored extraction for the primes, with the positivity criterion of the weight of \(A\) in place of that of the prime weight.

    Let \(A\) be a set of natural numbers, \(w\) and \(N\ge1\) natural numbers, \(\alpha\ge0\), \(W=\prod_{p\le w}p\) and \(X=W\lfloor N/8\rfloor\), and suppose \(w\le\lfloor X/4\rfloor\).     Proof sketch. This is the vendored density selection with the primes of \((\lfloor X/4\rfloor,X]\) replaced by the elements of \(A\) among them; the original pigeonhole is already stated for an arbitrary finite set of primes above \(w\).

    ZInQ.GreenTaoDense.containsAP_of_frequently_window

    Let \(A\) be a set of primes and \(c_1>0\) such that \(\#(A\cap(\lfloor Z/2\rfloor,Z])\ge c_1Z/\log Z\) for infinitely many \(Z\). Then for every \(k\) the set \(A\) contains a \(k\)-term arithmetic progression: there are natural numbers \(a\) and \(d>0\) with \(a+jd\in A\) for every \(j<k\).

    Proof sketch. For \(k\le2\) truncate a progression of length \(3\); so let \(k\ge3\) and \(\alpha=\alpha_k\). Put \(c_2=c_1/6\) and \(\delta=\alpha c_2/32\), fixed before \(w\). Transference for every large prime modulus gives \(w\), \(c>0\) and a threshold; put \(W=\prod_{p\le w}p\). The modulus selection gives a prime \(q=M+1\) beyond the transference threshold, the threshold of the height bound \((2\alpha\log q)^k<qc\) and \(2W+4w+16\), such that \(A\) has at least \(c_2X/\log X\) elements in \((\lfloor X/4\rfloor,X]\), \(X=W\lfloor q/8\rfloor\); these are primes, so they form the set \(S\) of the residue pigeonhole. The pigeonhole gives a reduced class \(b\) with \(\mathbb E\lambda^A\ge\frac\alpha W\#S\log(\lfloor X/4\rfloor+1)/q\), and the vendored Chebyshev arithmetic, with the constant \(\log2/4\) replaced by \(c_2\), bounds this below by \(\alpha c_2/32=\delta\). Since \(0\le\lambda^A\le\lambda\), transference gives \(\Lambda_k(\lambda^A)\ge c\). The weight is at most \(2\alpha\log q\), so the diagonal progressions (\(d=0\)) carry less than the count, and the off-diagonal mass is positive. Extraction gives the progression in \(A\).

    ZInQ.greenTaoDense

    Every set of primes of positive relative upper density contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length.

    This is Theorem 1.2 of Green and Tao (in the weakened form of Szemerédi's theorem in the primes), proved here by extending the vendored Lean proof of the Green–Tao theorem from the set of all primes to dense subsets. No vendored file is changed: the steps that use a property of the set of all primes are redone for the subset in new files, those adapted from vendored proofs under the vendored proof's Apache License.

    Proof sketch. Let \(A\) be a set of primes of positive relative upper density and \(k\) a length. The window lemma gives \(c_1>0\) with \(\#(A\cap(\lfloor Z/2\rfloor,Z])\ge c_1Z/\log Z\) for infinitely many \(Z\). Transference holds for every large prime modulus and every weight below the W-tricked prime weight, so the theorem on progressions in a set with mass in dyadic windows applies and gives a \(k\)-term progression in \(A\) with positive difference.

    The proof for all primes uses three properties that a dense set lacks: Chebyshev's lower bound in the fixed window \((X/4,X]\) at every large scale, a modulus chosen freely after the density, and the progression \(2,3\) for the small lengths. They are replaced by the window lemma, the Bertrand modulus selection with transference at every large prime modulus, and truncation of a progression of length \(3\).
