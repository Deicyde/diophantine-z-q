## The method

**The checking builder `HP`** (`Core.lean`, Init only) transcribes `FM` and all of its macros,
items 1–7, the buckets and `constructionK`. Its state keeps no table. It keeps:

- the counters;
- a complete table of the at most 6 constants;
- a *window* of records `2·key + flag` of the newest nodes, pinned at 20 entries;
- a *hot list* of `(key, index)` pairs. It only ever holds nodes that exist.

Pinned oracle data drives every addition and multiplication call:

- **hit**: the key is taken from the hot list, optionally moving the entry to the front or
  dropping it;
- **local miss**: the node is new. The run checks that no record between its youngest child
  `y` and the end has the same key, and that `y` is not a hub. This needs a window of at most
  16 records;
- **far miss in lane ℓ**: the node is new. Its entry `y·2^42 + key` must be the head of lane ℓ.

Hubs (`8·index + label`) are read in index order; the run checks `len < lim` (the chunk end)
so that no hub is skipped. A failed check clears `ok`.

**Why it is sound** (`Sim/Basic.lean`, `Sim/Prims.lean`). The relation `Rel` holds between an
`FM` state and an `HP` state. Its key invariant, `hist`, says that every node of a hub group
has its lane entry no longer among the remaining entries.

- Far misses are distinct because each lane is duplicate-free, and lanes carry disjoint hub
  labels. This is checked globally in `Static.lean`.
- A local miss cannot repeat an older node: nodes before `y` cannot have `y` as a child, and
  the nodes after `y` were compared by key. It also cannot repeat a far node, since `y` would
  then be a hub.
- Hits are correct because the hot list only holds existing nodes, and the tabled nodes of the
  fast run are distinct.

So the fast builder's table answers exactly as the oracle says, and the counters of `HP` equal
`countKind` of the fast node list.

**Chunks.** `Data/` is generated by `gen/gen.py`, which mirrors `HP` in Python and checks
every chunk before writing:

- the program literal `interpreterL`;
- the hubs and lanes per chunk, packed into big numbers and unpacked by the kernel;
- the pinned states;
- 16 files `Chunks*.lean` with the codes, the expected results and 89 theorems
  `chunk_k : chunkOK beq (piece st_k) st_{k+1} out_k = true`.

The pieces are the prelude, item 1, item 2, items 3–5 split into ranges, items 6 and 7, the
encoding of each construction chunk's formulas, the squares per encoding chunk, and the final
sum. `Data/StaticChecks.lean` checks the hub list (sorted, bounded by each chunk's `lim`) and
each lane (natural merge sort, strictly increasing, every entry carries its lane's hub label).

**Assembly** (`Sim/Assembly.lean`, generated `Final.lean`, `Main.lean`):

- `fast_counts_of` walks the bind structure of `constructionK interpreter 204 2 >>= encode`,
  splitting with `items3_seq`, `seqConcat_seq`, `subBranchesK_seq`, `encodeList_seq` and
  `squares_seq`, and applies `cutS` with the chunk theorems.
- `rel_init` relates the initial states; `cut_end` reads off the counts.
- `Main.lean` transfers the counts to `haltingCircuit` by `compiled_nodes` and
  `fastNodesRevK_eq`.

## Problems

- Kernel and elaborator pitfall (cost me most of the debugging). The two programs being
  compared can differ only by a nested beta-redex or projection under `>>=`: `rewrite` with a
  continuation lemma, `dsimp` or `beta_reduce` all produce this. Then both the kernel and Meta
  unfold `Bind.bind` (an instance projection) and run the fast builder symbolically: 36 GB,
  deep recursion, whnf timeouts. The fix in `Sim/Assembly.lean` has three parts:
  - all splitting lemmas create binds with `@[irreducible] def Seq`, a regular head that is
    compared argument by argument;
  - goals are never dsimp'ed;
  - the initial state is kept abstract (`fast_counts_of`).
- `ZInQFinite` (`haltingNodeCheck_native`, `haltingAddMulCheck_native`) is now redundant.
- I did not merge `origin/main`; the interfaces I use (`HaltingAddMulCheck`, `compiled_nodes`,
  `fastNodesRevK_eq`) are unchanged there.
