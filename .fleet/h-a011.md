## Summary

In upstream's Version 14, the modularity input of the proof of Theorem 4.1 is Lemma
`lem:qm-modularity-descent`. Its proof cites a survey sentence as its supplier (Calegari, ICM 2022,
p. 624), and an unpublished supplement (`direct-p5-modularity`) for the "source-to-object map".
The record applies Pan's numbered theorem directly to the lemma's `r₅`. It discharges every
hypothesis by a Version 14 line or a numbered published statement, and uses neither the survey nor
the supplement.

- **Statement used.** Pan, Theorem 1.0.4, with `ρ` as in Conjecture 1.0.1. Statement STANDARD:
  read in arXiv:1901.07166v2, FM.tex L167-177 and L226-233; the theorem is on p. 5 of the PDF
  that arXiv compiles. Its hypotheses: `ρ : G_ℚ → GL₂(Q̄_p)` continuous, irreducible, finitely
  ramified, potentially semistable at `p`, odd; `p` odd; distinct Hodge–Tate weights; a residual
  condition only at `p = 3`.
  - Published: J. Amer. Math. Soc. 35 (2022), no. 4, 1031-1169, DOI `10.1090/jams/991`
    (Crossref).
  - The published abstract is word for word the arXiv abstract, including the `p ≥ 5`
    sentence (VERIFIED).
  - **The JAMS numbering "1.0.4" is UNCHECKED**: the journal text could not be read here.
    Evidence that it is the same: arXiv v2 is the "revised version" of 3 September 2021 and
    thanks the referees, and Version 14's bibliography cites it as "Theorem 1.0.4, p. 5".

  - *A new check on the weights* (VERIFIED as an argument). `det r₅ = (finite)·ε₅` on `G_F`,
    from `det V₅(A) = ε₅²`, so the two weights sum to `−1` and are distinct. This needs no Hodge
    numbers. The exact values `{0, −1}` are still needed for the weight-two step.
  - **(b), recommended.** Silverberg–Zarhin Thm 3.5 gives semistable reduction over `F_w(A[3])`;
    Version 14 makes the same application at L1501-1503. Then **Breuil, Ann. of Math. 152
    (2000), Corollaire 5.3.4, p. 547**: for `p ≠ 2` and residue field in `F̄_p`, semistable
    (resp. good) reduction ⇔ `V_p(A)` semistable (resp. crystalline). Read in the typeset Annals
    text on arXiv: STANDARD. Then an elementary summand lemma: `D_st` is additive, and the
    summands are identical. Route (b) needs no Lemma PG, no endomorphism hypothesis and no
    Berger, and it holds for the lemma in full generality.
  - **(a), the h-v14 route.** Lemma PG (good reduction over `F_w(A[3])` for `B = (2,−3)`; no
    NoCM, since the lemma's own hypothesis supplies `End⁰ = B`), then the crystalline half of the
    same corollary. This gives potentially crystalline.
  - **(c), fallback.** The de Rham paragraph V14 L532-547, then Berger's theorem dR ⇒ pst:
    Théorème 0.6 = 5.19 of arXiv:math/0102179v3, the revised version for *Inventiones*. STANDARD
    for that version; journal numbering UNCHECKED.
  - *Caveat* (VERIFIED wording). Breuil states the direction used (reduction ⇒ representation)
    but calls it "classique … due à Fontaine" and does not prove it. Its primary source,
    Fontaine 1982, is UNCHECKED.
- **The conclusion.** Pan gives only "comes from a cuspidal eigenform up to twist", so Version

  - The argument also needs `r₅` de Rham over `ℚ₅` with weights `{0, −1}`. So the de Rham
