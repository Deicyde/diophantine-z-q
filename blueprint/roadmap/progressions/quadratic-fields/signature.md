# The signature of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$

Let $d$ be a squarefree integer with $d\ne1$ and $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$, with $r_1$ real and
$r_2$ complex places. If $d<0$, then $r_1=0$ and $r_2=1$; if $d>0$, then $r_1=2$ and $r_2=0$.

**Proof sketch.** The two embeddings of $K$ into $\mathbb C$ send $\sqrt d$ to the two square
roots of $d$. They are real exactly when $d>0$; when $d<0$ they are complex conjugate and
form one complex place. In both cases $r_1+2r_2=2$.
