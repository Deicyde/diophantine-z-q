# The quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$

Let $d$ be a squarefree integer with $d\ne1$. The quadratic field
$K_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)=\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-d)$ is the $\mathbb Q$-algebra with basis
$1,\sqrt d$ and $(\sqrt d)^2=d$. It is a number field of degree $[K_d:\mathbb Q]=2$.

**Proof sketch.** A squarefree $d\ne1$ is not the square of a rational number ($d\ne0$, and
$d=a^2/b^2$ in lowest terms forces $b=1$ and $d=a^2$, so $d=1$). Hence $X^2-d$ is irreducible
over $\mathbb Q$, the algebra is a field, and it has characteristic $0$ and dimension $2$
over $\mathbb Q$.
