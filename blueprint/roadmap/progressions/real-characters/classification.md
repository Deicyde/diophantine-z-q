# Real characters are Legendre symbols away from $2q$

Let $q\ge1$ and let $\chi$ be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo $q$ with
$\chi^2=\chi_0$. Then there is a squarefree integer $d\ne1$ with $|d|\le q$, every prime
factor of which divides $2q$, such that
$$\chi(p)=\Bigl(\frac dp\Bigr)\qquad\text{for every prime }p\nmid2q .$$

**Proof sketch.** Write $q=2^e\prod_ip_i^{k_i}$ with distinct odd primes $p_i$. By the
Chinese remainder theorem $\chi=\chi_2\prod_i\chi_i$ with $\chi_2$ a character modulo $2^e$
and $\chi_i$ modulo $p_i^{k_i}$, all real. The group $(\mathbb Z/p_i^{k_i})^\times$ is
cyclic of even order, so it has exactly one nontrivial real character, the Legendre symbol
$(\cdot/p_i)$ of the reduction modulo $p_i$; thus $\chi_i$ is trivial or $(\cdot/p_i)$. The
real characters modulo $2^e$ are $1$, $\chi_4$ (for $e\ge2$), $\chi_8$ and $\chi_4\chi_8$ (for
$e\ge3$), since $(\mathbb Z/2^e)^\times/(\cdot)^2$ is generated by $-1$ and $5$. For a prime
$p\nmid2q$, the supplementary laws give $\chi_4(p)=(-1/p)$, $\chi_8(p)=(2/p)$,
$\chi_4\chi_8(p)=(-2/p)$, and reciprocity gives $(p/p_i)=(p_i^*/p)$ with
$p_i^*=(-1)^{(p_i-1)/2}p_i$. Hence $\chi(p)=(d/p)$ with $d=d_2\prod_{i\in S}p_i^*$, where
$S$ is the set of $i$ with $\chi_i$ nontrivial and $d_2\in\{1,-1,2,-2\}$ corresponds to
$\chi_2$. This $d$ is squarefree, its odd prime factors divide $q$, $2\mid d$ only if $8\mid q$,
and $|d|\le q$ (if $d_2=\pm2$ then $|d|\le2q/8$; if $d_2=-1$ then $|d|\le q/4$). Distinct
choices of $(\chi_2,S)$ give distinct $d$, and the trivial choice gives $d=1$; since $\chi$ is
nonprincipal, $d\ne1$.
