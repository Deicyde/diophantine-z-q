# Raynaud's criterion for semistable reduction

The *Raynaud statement* is the following assertion. Let $\mathcal O$ be a strictly henselian
discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$, $A$ an abelian variety over $K$ and $m\ge3$ an
integer invertible in $\mathcal O$. If every point of $A$ of order dividing $m$ is rational over
$K$, then $A$ has semistable reduction at $\mathcal O$ in one of the two readings of the term: the
identity component of the special fibre of every Néron model of $A$ is an extension of an abelian
variety by a torus over the residue field, or the same holds on the geometric special fibre.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
Whichever of the two readings the print uses, it gives the statement directly. Other readings remain
open: the print may assume an algebraically closed residue field or require $m>3$. It is a
hypothesis, not proved here.
