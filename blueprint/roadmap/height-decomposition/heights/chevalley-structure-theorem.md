# Chevalley's structure theorem over a perfect field

The *Chevalley statement* is the following assertion. Let $k$ be a perfect field and $G$ a smooth
connected group scheme of finite type over $k$. Then there are an abelian variety $B$ over $k$ and
a faithfully flat homomorphism $\pi:G\to B$ whose kernel is smooth, connected and affine; and any
two such homomorphisms have the same kernel.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
The print says that $G$ has a unique normal smooth connected affine subgroup $H$ with $G/H$ an
abelian variety; the statement reads "$G/H$ is an abelian variety" as "$G\to B$ is a faithfully flat
homomorphism with kernel $H$". The statement has not been read against the source. It is used where
the project's coordinate form of the stable Faltings height is compared with the printed one, to
bring the semi-abelian identity component of a special fibre down from the algebraic closure of a
perfect residue field. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
