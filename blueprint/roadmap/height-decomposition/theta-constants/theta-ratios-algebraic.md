# Ratios of theta constants are algebraic

The *theta-ratio statement* is the following assertion. Let $A$ be an abelian surface over a number
field $K$, $\sigma:K\to\mathbb C$ an embedding, $\Phi:\mathbb C^2\to A_\sigma(\mathbb C)$ a
uniformization with kernel $\Lambda$, and $\Omega\in\mathfrak H_2$ a period matrix of $\Lambda$.
Then some theta constant $\theta_{m_0}(0,\Omega)$ is nonzero, and every ratio
$\theta_m(0,\Omega)/\theta_{m_0}(0,\Omega)$ is an algebraic number.

No single source prints it. It combines Shimura's theorem that the values of Siegel modular
functions with algebraic Fourier coefficients at a point whose principally polarized abelian variety
is defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ are algebraic (for genus $2$ also Thomae's formula), the
equality of the Néron–Severi groups of $A$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and over $\mathbb C$ for the
polarization that $\Omega$ defines, and Igusa's theorem that at most one of the ten even theta
constants vanishes in genus $2$. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
