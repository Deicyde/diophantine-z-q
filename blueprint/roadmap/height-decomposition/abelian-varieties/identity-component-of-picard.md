# The identity component of the Picard scheme is an abelian variety

The *dual statement* is the following assertion. Let $A$ be an abelian variety over a field $K$. For
every scheme representing the relative Picard functor of $A$, its identity component, with the
induced group law, is an abelian variety $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{A/K}$.

It is not printed in this form but follows from published theorems; reaching it from the print takes
further theorems, which are not proved here. Mumford builds the dual abelian variety over an
algebraic closure of $K$; identifying it with the identity component of $\operatorname{Pic}_{A/K}$
and descending to $K$ are theorem steps (Kleiman 9.5.1; SGA 3, Exposé VI_A, 2.4). One printed
statement over an arbitrary field in this form would make it a published statement (candidates:
Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud 8.4; *FGA Explained* 9.5; Milne 1986, §10). It is a hypothesis, not
proved here.
