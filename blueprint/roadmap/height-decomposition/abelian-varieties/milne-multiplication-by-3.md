# Multiplication by 3 on an abelian scheme is finite étale

For every scheme $S$ over $\mathbb Z[1/30]$ and every abelian scheme $\mathcal A$ of relative
dimension $2$ over $S$ with a ring homomorphism
$\iota:\mathcal O\to\operatorname{End}_S(\mathcal A)$, the endomorphism $\iota(3)$ of $\mathcal A$
is finite and étale.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is the case $g=2$, $n=3$ of Milne's Proposition 20.7, which states for an abelian scheme of
relative dimension $g$ that multiplication by $n$ is flat, surjective and finite, and étale exactly
when $n$ is divisible by no residue characteristic of the base; no residue characteristic of a
$\mathbb Z[1/30]$-scheme divides $3$. That $\iota(3)$ is multiplication by $3$ is proved here, and
the action of $\mathcal O$ plays no other part. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
