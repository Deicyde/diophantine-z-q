# The n-torsion of an abelian surface has n⁴ points

For every scheme $S$ over $\mathbb Z[1/30]$, every abelian scheme $\mathcal A$ of relative dimension
$2$ over $S$ with a ring homomorphism $\iota:\mathcal O\to\operatorname{End}_S(\mathcal A)$, every
geometric point $s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to S$ and every positive integer $n$ not divisible by the
characteristic of $k$, exactly $n^4$ points of $\mathcal A_s(k)$ are killed by $n$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is Milne's Remark 8.4 at $g=2$: over a separably closed field whose characteristic does not
divide $n$, the $n$-torsion of an abelian variety of dimension $g$ has $n^{2g}$ elements. The fibre
$\mathcal A_s$ is an abelian surface over $k$ (Milne, §20, and the relative dimension). The action
of $\mathcal O$ plays no part. The counts at $n=3$ and $n=5$ are proved from it here, and with
Milne's Proposition 20.7 at $n=5$ they give the representability of full level-$5$ structures by an
étale torsor, which Buzzard prints without proof. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
