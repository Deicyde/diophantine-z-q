# Hecke operators on J₁(N) over ℚ

The *Hecke statement* is the following assertion. Let $N\ge2$ and let $X$ be a proper smooth
geometrically connected integral curve over $\mathbb Q$ whose function field is generated by two
functions $j_X,t_X$ with exactly the polynomial relations over $\mathbb Z$ of $j(\tau)$ and the
Weber function $f_1(\tau)=\frac{g_2(\tau)g_3(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau)}\wp_\tau(1/N)$ on the upper
half-plane, $\wp_\tau$ being the Weierstrass function of $\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$; such a curve is
a model of $X_1(N)$ over $\mathbb Q$. Write $x_\tau$ for a point of $X(\mathbb C)$ at $\tau$, that
is, a point at which every rational function of $j_X,t_X$ whose counterpart in $j,f_1$ has a limit
at $\tau$ is regular with that value. Let $P_0$ be a rational point of $X$ and $J$ a Jacobian of $X$
with its Abel–Jacobi map based at $P_0$. Then there are endomorphisms $T_p$ ($p$ prime) and
$\langle d\rangle$ ($d\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$) of $J$ over $\mathbb Q$ that act on the classes of
degree-zero divisors supported on such points by
$$T_p[x_\tau]=\sum_{i<p}[x_{(\tau+i)/p}]+[x_{g(p\tau)}],\qquad\langle d\rangle[x_\tau]=[x_{g\tau}],$$
for every choice of these points, where the last term of $T_p$ occurs only for $p\nmid N$, and $g$
is any element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ with lower row $(c,d')$, $N\mid c$ and $d'\equiv p$,
respectively $d'\equiv d$, modulo $N$.

It is not printed in this form but follows from published theorems; reaching it from the print takes
further theorems, which are not proved here. Shimura's 1973 paper works on his standard model of
$X_1(N)$ over $\mathbb Q$, which it defines by citing his 1971 book, with the Hecke correspondences
$\xi_n$ acting on the Jacobian, and states, in the proof of its Theorem 1, that the elements of its
Hecke ring lying in $\operatorname{End}(J)$ are rational over $\mathbb Q$; that a correspondence
rational over $\mathbb Q$ induces an endomorphism of the Jacobian over $\mathbb Q$ is standard. Two
steps are made on the spot and are not printed there: that a curve with these coordinates is
Shimura's standard model, with the same points $x_\tau$, and that $\xi_p$ and $\langle d\rangle$ act
on these points by Diamond and Shurman's double-coset formulas above, a matter of conventions. The
pages read do not name the diamond operators; they lie in the Hecke ring tensored with $\mathbb Q$,
and so are rational over $\mathbb Q$, by the Hecke relation $p\langle p\rangle=\xi_p^2-\xi_{p^2}$
for $p\nmid N$ and Dirichlet's theorem. The statement asserts the operators on every model with
these coordinates. A review of this statement is pending. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
