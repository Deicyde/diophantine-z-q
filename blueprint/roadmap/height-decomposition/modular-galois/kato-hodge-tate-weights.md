# Hodge–Tate weights of the representation of a newform

The *Hodge–Tate weight statement* is the following assertion. Let $\ell$ be a prime and $f$ a
newform of weight $k\ge2$ and level $N$; $\ell$ may divide $N$. Then there is a continuous
representation $\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$, unramified outside
$N\ell$, for which one of the following holds: the trace of an arithmetic Frobenius at every prime
$p\nmid N\ell$ is $a_p(f)$ and the Hodge–Tate weights of $\rho$ at $\ell$ are $0$ and $1-k$; or the
trace of a geometric Frobenius at every such prime is $a_p(f)$ and the Hodge–Tate weights are $0$
and $k-1$. The cyclotomic character has weight $-1$.

The project classes it as a published statement; its reading against the print is still pending.
The two alternatives are the two normalizations in which the literature states the theorem (Deligne
for the representation; Faltings, Saito and Kato for its Hodge–Tate weights); the surveys that
record it state it for automorphic representations, and the statement is its translation to
newforms. The project's earlier
form, for every semisimple representation attached to $f$, is proved here from this one, with the
Chebotarev density theorem and the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
