# Deligne's Galois representations of eigenforms

The *Deligne statement* is the following assertion. Let $f\in S_k(N,\chi)$ be a normalized eigenform
of weight $k\ge2$ whose coefficient field $K_f$ is a number field, and $\lambda$ a prime of $K_f$
above a prime $\ell$, with completion $K_{f,\lambda}$. Then there is a continuous representation
$\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$ such that for every prime $p\nmid N\ell$: $\rho$
is unramified at $p$, and for every arithmetic Frobenius $\sigma$ at $p$ either the characteristic
polynomial of $\rho(\sigma)$ is $x^2-a_p(f)x+\chi(p)p^{k-1}$ or $\rho(\sigma)$ satisfies this
polynomial.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
Either wording of Diamond and Shurman's theorem (the characteristic polynomial, or "satisfies")
gives it directly. That $K_f$ is a number field, which the theorem presupposes, is the
[coefficient-field statement](coefficient-field-finite.md). The statement has not been read against
the book. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
