# Deligne's Galois representations of eigenforms

The *Deligne statement* is the following assertion. Let $\ell$ be a prime and $f\in S_k(N,\chi)$ a
normalized eigenform of weight $k\ge2$. Then there is a continuous representation
$\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$ such that for every prime
$p\nmid N\ell$: $\rho$ is unramified at $p$, $a_p(f)$ and $\chi(p)p^{k-1}$ are the images under
$\iota_{\mathbb C}$ of algebraic numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and for every arithmetic Frobenius
$\sigma$ at $p$ either the characteristic polynomial of $\rho(\sigma)$ is
$x^2-\iota_\ell(\alpha)x+\iota_\ell(\beta)$ or $\rho(\sigma)$ satisfies this polynomial.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
Diamond and Shurman's theorem gives this under either of its two possible wordings (the
characteristic polynomial, or "satisfies"). That $a_p(f)$ is algebraic rests on their printing the
theorem with the number field $K_f$ of the coefficients, which still needs a page check; the
convention by which the coefficients are carried from $\mathbb C$ to the $\ell$-adic field was
flagged for a human check. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
