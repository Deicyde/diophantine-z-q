# The Deligne–Serre theorem

The *Deligne–Serre statement* is the following assertion. Let $N\ge1$, let $\varepsilon$ be a
Dirichlet character modulo $N$ with $\varepsilon(-1)=-1$, and let $f$ be a nonzero cusp form of
weight $1$ on $\Gamma_1(N)$ on which every diamond operator $\langle d\rangle$ acts by
$\varepsilon(d)$ and which is an eigenvector of every Hecke operator $T_n$ with $n$ prime to $N$,
with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$. Then there is a continuous representation
$\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with finite image, unramified at every prime
$p\nmid N$, such that every arithmetic Frobenius at such a prime $p$ has trace $\lambda_p$ and
determinant $\varepsilon(p)$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is Theorem 4.1 of Deligne and Serre with their hypotheses: a nonzero form of type
$(1,\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon(-1)=-1$ that is an eigenvector of the Hecke operators away from
the level, with no normalization of its coefficients; the statement takes the cusp forms among their
modular forms. The condition $\varepsilon(-1)=-1$, which every nonzero form of weight $1$ and
character $\varepsilon$ satisfies, is kept as in the print. The project's earlier form, for
normalized eigenforms with trace $a_p(f)$, is proved here from this one. Only AI agents have read it
against the paper, which is not in the manuscript's bibliography. It is a hypothesis, not proved
here.
