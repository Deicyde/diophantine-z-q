# A model on which Galois acts as on the moduli objects is canonical

For every proper smooth curve $M$ over $\mathbb Q$ with a uniformization $\psi$ of $M(\mathbb C)$ by
$B^\times\backslash(\mathbb H^\pm\times(B\otimes\mathbb A_f)^\times/K_3)$, holomorphic in the first
variable, on which every automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathbb C$ acts as on the level-$3$ QM surfaces
of the points, there is a sign $\varepsilon$ such that, at every CM point $z$ of an imaginary
quadratic field $K$, the automorphism $\sigma=\mathrm{rec}_K(\lambda)$ acts by
$\sigma\,\psi(z,g)=\psi(z,\hat q(\lambda)^{\varepsilon}g)$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is Milne's statement in the PEL case of type A or C, that a model over the reflex field on which
$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb C/E)$ acts as on the moduli quadruples is canonical, with his definition of a
canonical model at one level (Definition 12.8 and (54)). The reflex field is $\mathbb Q$ and the
datum is of type C (proved in Lean). Milne's quadruples are read on the integral objects: the
polarization is the unique compatible principal one, and the level structure is the level-$3$
structure of the Tate module. Milne's Artin map is the reciprocal of the arithmetic one ((51), p.
341). The source is a survey that states the result with an outline. It is a hypothesis, not proved
here.
