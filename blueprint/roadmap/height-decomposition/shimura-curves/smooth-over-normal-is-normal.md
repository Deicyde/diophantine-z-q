# A scheme smooth over a normal base is normal

For every smooth morphism of schemes $f:X\to Y$ and every point $x$ of $X$, if the local ring of $Y$
at $f(x)$ is an integrally closed domain, then so is the local ring of $X$ at $x$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
Its reading against the print or its review is not complete, and until both are the project's strict
count does not count it as published. It is Grothendieck's Proposition (17.5.7) of EGA IV, in its
"intégralement clos" case, in the direction from the base to the source, for a morphism smooth at
every point. A smooth morphism is locally of finite presentation and formally smooth, which are
EGA's hypotheses: formal smoothness in EGA's sense is the lifting property along nilpotent ideals,
which smooth ring maps have. In EGA's usage "intégralement clos" for a local ring includes
"intègre". It shows that the moduli curve at level $V_1(5)$, smooth over $\mathbb Z[1/30]$, has
integrally closed local rings, which the projectivity statement below needs. It is a hypothesis, not
proved here.
