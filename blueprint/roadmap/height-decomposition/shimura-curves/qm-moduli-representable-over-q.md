# The level-three QM moduli problem is representable over the rationals

There is a scheme $S$ over $\mathbb Q$, proper and smooth of relative dimension $1$, whose points
with values in every field $K\supseteq\mathbb Q$ are in bijection, compatibly with extension of
fields, with the isomorphism classes of abelian surfaces over $K$ with an action of $\mathcal O$ and
a full level-$3$ structure, carrying a universal family; and every such family over a proper smooth
curve $T$ over $\mathbb C$ is classified by a morphism $T\to S$ over $\mathbb Q$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is the first sentence of Boutot and Carayol's 1.4, that their moduli problem $\mathcal M_U$ is
defined and representable over $\mathbb Q$, at the level $U(3)$, the units of
$\mathcal O\otimes\hat{\mathbb Z}$ congruent to $1$ modulo $3$, which is small enough by their 1.3.
Properness and smoothness come from the second sentence of 1.4, the proper smooth curve over
$\mathbb Z[1/\delta N]$ (here $\delta=6$) that it recalls from Milne and Boutot. Only the
consequences listed here are used: field-valued points, the universal family, and classifying maps
for families over proper smooth complex curves. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
