# Complex abelian surfaces with quaternionic multiplication, in lattice form

Let $B$ be the quaternion algebra over $\mathbb Q$ of discriminant $6$, $\mathcal O$ its maximal
order, $\iota:B\to M_2(\mathbb R)$ the fixed splitting, and for $\tau$ in the upper half-plane let
$\Lambda_\tau=\iota(\mathcal O)\binom{\tau}{1}\subset\mathbb C^2$. (i) For every $\tau$ there is a
complex abelian surface $A$ with an injective ring homomorphism $\mathcal O\to\operatorname{End}(A)$
and an $\mathcal O$-equivariant analytic isomorphism $\mathbb C^2/\Lambda_\tau\cong A(\mathbb C)$.
(ii) Every complex abelian surface with such an action and a principal polarization whose Rosati
involution induces $x\mapsto\mu^{-1}\bar x\mu$ on $\mathcal O$, $\mu=2j+ij$, is of this form for
some $\tau$. (iii) If one such surface is of this form for $\tau$ and for $\tau'$, then
$\tau'=\gamma\tau$ for some $\gamma\in\mathcal O$ of reduced norm $1$, acting by Möbius
transformations through $\iota$.

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
It is Voight's construction 43.6.12, Main Theorem 43.6.14 and Proposition 43.6.28, with the
polarization kept only as the hypothesis of (ii) and dropped from the conclusions. A complex abelian
surface is read as an algebraic one through Riemann's theorem (43.4.6) and GAGA. The proof of
43.6.28 cites Lemma 43.4.17 to put $\tau$ in the upper half-plane, which that lemma does not give; a
unit of norm $-1$ (43.7.1) repairs the step, and the statement is unaffected. The author's corrected
edition was read; the quotations agree with the printed book. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
