# Berger's theorem: de Rham representations are potentially semistable

The *Berger statement* is the following assertion. Let $E$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$
inside $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$ and $\rho:G_{\mathbb Q_p}\to\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ a continuous
representation, and let $V=E^n$, viewed as a representation of $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ over $\mathbb Q_p$
of dimension $d=\dim_{\mathbb Q_p}V$. If $V$ is de Rham, that is,
$\dim_{\mathbb Q_p}(B_{\rm dR}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}V)^{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}=d$, then $V$ is potentially
semistable: there is a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb Q_p$ inside $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$ with
$\dim_{B_{\rm st}^{G_K}}(B_{\rm st}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}V)^{G_K}=d$. Here $B_{\rm st}$ is the
$\mathbb Q_p$-subalgebra of $B_{\rm dR}$ generated by $A_{\rm cris}$, $1/t$ and $\log[\tilde p]$:
$A_{\rm cris}$ is the set of sums $\sum_{m\ge0}a_m\omega^m/m!$ in $B_{\rm dR}^+$ with
$a_m\in A_{\rm inf}=W(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}^\flat)$ tending to $0$ $p$-adically, where $\omega$
generates the kernel of $\theta:A_{\rm inf}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$; $t=\log[\varepsilon]$,
where $\varepsilon=(1,\zeta_p,\zeta_{p^2},\dots)\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}^\flat$ is a compatible
system of primitive $p$-power roots of unity; and $\log[\tilde p]=-\sum_{m\ge1}(1-[\tilde p]/p)^m/m$
for $\tilde p=(p,p^{1/p},\dots)$, Fontaine's $\log[\tilde p]$ with $\log_p(p)=0$. The assertion is
made for every such $\omega$ and $\varepsilon$.

It is not printed in this form but follows from published theorems; reaching it from the print takes
further theorems, which are not proved here. It is Berger's theorem, from his Inventiones paper
(Théorème 0.6 in the arXiv version; the journal's theorem number is unverified), at the base field
$\mathbb Q_p$ for representations of the form $E^n$, with potential semistability in Fontaine's
sense: the representation becomes $B_{\rm st}$-admissible over a finite extension, admissibility
meaning the equality of dimensions, with $B_{\rm st}$ taken as its image in $B_{\rm dR}$, as
Fontaine describes it. Berger works over his own variant of $B_{\rm st}$ and states that it has the
same $D_{\rm st}$ as Fontaine's; reaching Fontaine's ring from his is a step of the reading, not a
theorem proved here, so the statement counts as a consequence of the print. Its de Rham condition is
the one above because $B_{\rm dR}^{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}=\mathbb Q_p$, which is proved here. Colmez
prints the conclusion in the stronger form of a basis (Théorème 0.1 of his 2008 paper); that form
implies this statement, which is proved here, and the converse is proved here from two properties of
$B_{\rm st}$ that make it $G_K$-regular, for every finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb Q_p$ in
$\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$: the $G_K$-invariants of its field of fractions lie in $B_{\rm st}$, and a
nonzero element $b$ of $B_{\rm st}$ with $\sigma(b)\in\mathbb Q_pb$ for every $\sigma\in G_K$ is a
unit of $B_{\rm st}$. Both are in Fontaine's account of $B_{\rm st}$ (the locator is unverified);
neither is proved here. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
