# CM elliptic curves have potentially good reduction

The *potential good reduction statement* is the following assertion. Let $L$ be a number field and
$E$ an elliptic curve over $L$ whose ring of endomorphisms over an algebraic closure of $L$ is
isomorphic to an order of an imaginary quadratic field. Then there is a number field $L_1$
containing $L$ over which $E$ has good reduction everywhere: some smooth separated group scheme of
finite type over $\mathcal O_{L_1}$, proper over $\mathcal O_{L_1}$, has generic fibre isomorphic to
$E_{L_1}$ as a group scheme.

It is not printed in this form but follows from published theorems; reaching it from the print takes
further theorems, which are not proved here. The manuscript uses the fact without a citation: "After
a finite extension $E$ has everywhere good reduction, since its $j$-invariant is integral." It is
classical. The $j$-invariant of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication is an algebraic
integer, and an elliptic curve over a local field with integral $j$-invariant acquires good
reduction over a finite extension (Silverman, Proposition VII.5.5); Serre and Tate prove that
abelian varieties with complex multiplication have potentially good reduction everywhere, and
Barquero-Sanchez, Cadwallader, Cannon, Genao and Masri prove it for elliptic curves with complex
multiplication in the proof of their Theorem 1.3. The statement asks for one finite extension that
gives good reduction at all finite places at once, in the form of an abelian scheme over its ring of
integers; the passage from the local statements to this form is standard and is not proved here. The
locators in Serre–Tate and Silverman are unverified, and a review of the statement is pending. It is
a hypothesis, not proved here.
