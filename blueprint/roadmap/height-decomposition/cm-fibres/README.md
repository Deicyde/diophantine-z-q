# Complex multiplication

Let $k$ be an imaginary quadratic field, with ring of integers $\mathcal O_k$ and discriminant
$d_k$. An *order* of $k$ is a subring $R$ of $\mathcal O_k$ of finite index $f=[\mathcal O_k:R]$,
the conductor of $R$; its discriminant is $\Delta_R=f^2d_k$, and its class number is the order of
its Picard group $\operatorname{Pic}(R)$, the group of isomorphism classes of invertible $R$-modules
under the tensor product. An elliptic curve $E$ over a field $L$, an abelian variety of dimension
$1$, has *complex multiplication by* $R$ when its ring of endomorphisms over an algebraic closure of
$L$ is isomorphic to $R$. Over a number field $L$, $E$ has *good reduction everywhere* when it is,
as a group scheme, the generic fibre of an abelian scheme over $\mathcal O_L$: a smooth separated
group scheme of finite type over $\mathcal O_L$ that is proper over $\mathcal O_L$. For an embedding
$\sigma:L\to\mathbb C$, a *period* of $E$ at $\sigma$ is a complex number $\tau$ such that some
surjective holomorphic homomorphism $\mathbb C\to E_\sigma(\mathbb C)$ has kernel
$c(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)$ for some $c\ne0$; on the upper half-plane,
$\Delta(\tau)=(2\pi)^{12}\eta(\tau)^{24}$ is the modular discriminant, with Dedekind's
$\eta(\tau)=e^{\pi i\tau/12}\prod_{n\ge1}(1-e^{2\pi in\tau})$. The manuscript's proof of its Theorem
4.1, the six-contact estimate, meets these curves at its CM fibres: abelian surfaces with
quaternionic multiplication whose geometric endomorphism algebra is $\mathrm M_2(k)$ rather than the
quaternion algebra, which the manuscript splits, up to isogeny, as the square of an elliptic curve
with complex multiplication by an order of $k$.

It is not proved here.
