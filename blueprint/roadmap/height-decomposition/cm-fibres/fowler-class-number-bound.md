# Fowler's bound for discriminants of bounded class number

The *Fowler statement* is the following assertion. Let $k$ be an imaginary quadratic field, with
ring of integers $\mathcal O_k$ and discriminant $d_k$, and $R$ an order of $k$, a subring of
$\mathcal O_k$ of finite index $f=[\mathcal O_k:R]$; put $\Delta_R=f^2d_k$, and let
$\operatorname{Pic}(R)$ be the Picard group of $R$, the group of isomorphism classes of invertible
$R$-modules. Then for every integer $n\ge1$ with $\#\operatorname{Pic}(R)\le n$,
$$\sqrt{|\Delta_R|}\le2n^2\exp\bigl(21000\,n^2\bigr).$$

It is a published statement: the printed theorem, or a direct instance of it that needs no argument.
Its reading against the print or its review is not complete, and until both are the project's strict
count does not count it as published. Fowler's Proposition 2.7 gives
$\sqrt{|\Delta|}\le2n^2\exp(21000n^2)$ for every negative discriminant $\Delta$ of an imaginary
quadratic order with class number $h(\Delta)\le n$, with no exceptional field; the manuscript cites
it and applies it at $n=D$. The statement reads the print on orders, with $\Delta=\Delta_R=f^2d_k$
and $h(\Delta)=\#\operatorname{Pic}(R)$; both identifications are standard (Cox, Lemma 7.2 and
Theorem 7.7) and are not proved here. The upstream review of the manuscript's CM argument read the
proposition in the published article; the reading has not been repeated for this statement, and its
review is pending. It is a hypothesis, not proved here.
