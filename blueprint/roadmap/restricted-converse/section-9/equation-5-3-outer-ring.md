# (5.3): the outer valuation ring

There are no inputs: the theorem is unconditional.

Let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter on the ring instances and let $\mathscr O_j=\mathscr O$ for each $j$.
The quotient
$$\mathscr O_\infty=\Bigl(\prod_{\mathcal U}\mathscr O_j\Bigr)\Big/\bigl\{(x_j):v_2(x_j)\to_{\mathcal U}\infty\bigr\}\tag{5.3}$$
is a discrete valuation ring whose maximal ideal is generated by $2$. The map $\mathscr O\to\mathscr O_\infty$
is injective, and $\mathscr O_\infty$ and its fraction field have characteristic zero. Completeness is
not claimed, here or in the manuscript.

The manuscript forms (5.3) for a sequence of ring instances with $\omega(k_j)\le A$ along its fixed
nonprincipal ultrafilter; Lean takes any index set and any ultrafilter, and divides the full product
rather than the ultraproduct, which gives the same ring (`ultraproductQuotientEquiv`).

The reduction of Theorem 5.3 does not cite this theorem, but its proof of
[(3.5)](../section-7/equation-3-5-arithmetic-lower-bound.md) works in the same quotient `OInf`, where
the switch argument of [Proposition 9.6](proposition-9-6-uniform-lower-bound.md) takes its outer
matrices.

**Proof sketch.** A class that is not infinitesimal has one valuation $n$ on a set in $\mathcal U$, so
it is $2^n$ times a unit. Every nonzero ideal is then generated by a power of $2$.
