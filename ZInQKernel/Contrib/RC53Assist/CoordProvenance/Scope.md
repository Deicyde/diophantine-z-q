# The residual scalar dictionary

## The coordinate that must be identified

For a retained finite model of a genuine Selmer complex, the class-functional coordinate is the coordinate of the same cohomology tensor in the original determinant basis. At a rank-one constituent, write the transported free-line coefficients as α and β and the three retained Smith determinants as δ₀, δ₁ and δ₂. For the degree-two-to-three cancellation model, the coordinate is

u = −κ α β δ₀ δ₁⁻¹ δ₂.

At a generically acyclic constituent it is zero. These are the conventions of Definition ex:arith-coordinate, TeX 3877–3892, and ex:limits-fixed-field-clearing, TeX 5509–5516 and 5586–5617.

## The genuine class and functional

Let ψ be the specified quasi-isomorphism from the finite model to the genuine continuous-cochain Selmer complex, let zₛ be the given Selmer cycle, and let L be the given integral closed functional. The model class must represent zₛ through ψ: its image minus zₛ is a retained boundary. The model functional is the pullback of L; its row entries are precisely its values on the original degree-two basis vectors. There is no new scalar multiplying either object.

The cochain change of local conditions uses the actual maps: primitive to full at selected places, zero to primitive on the paired side, and identity at old places. Given the primitive isotropy homotopy, the relaxed isotropy homotopy is its unchanged old part and zero at the selected places. Hence the retained-homotopy equality is derived. Naturality then gives the second norm factor on cocycles, with the boundary of the paired strict lift retained explicitly. This is the cochain content of TeX 4159–4177; it does not determine a determinant-basis normalization by itself.

## What still has to be constructed

For the manuscript's actual family, let uₘ denote the relaxed scalar supplied by its support-triangle and correction construction. The unresolved equality is

uₘ(x) = u([zₛ], L; the original retained basis), whenever Δ(x) ≠ 0.

The right side must be formed in the same scalar realization as the left side. In particular:

1. The given coefficient module and local conditions must be the manuscript's arithmetic family, and its bounded model must carry the manuscript's retained determinant basis. A bare quasi-isomorphism does not supply a retained-basis comparison.
2. The cycle must be the retained arithmetic class, and the dual cycle defining L must be the specified conjugate-orthogonal transport of that class. The required integral orthogonal comparison and its inverse homotopies must be retained through the coefficient diagrams.
3. A common coefficient model must realize the scalar in the support-triangle construction and the original determinant coordinate. The coefficient diagrams must carry the same class, functional, and basis. Completed-ring integrality is not a power-series realization.

The support-triangle and correction identities alone do not prove this equality. Defining a new scalar to be a reconstructed coordinate is not an identification of uₘ. No proposition asserting this equality is treated as a printed fact.

## The grading must also agree

The manuscript's full-condition singular quotients occupy degrees one and two, as stated at TeX 3479–3481 and 5398–5403. Cancelling such a disk contributes det(B)⁻¹. The degree-two-to-three model instead contributes det(B). These are different graded models, not interchangeable descriptions of the same retained coordinate.

Consequently a construction through the full-condition identification must use its corresponding original-coordinate interface and inverse determinant factor, or supply an actual graded, retained comparison to the other model. No such comparison follows merely from the existence of the two interfaces. Taking empty selected places, zero coefficients, or an unrelated class does not identify the manuscript's family.

The finite free model construction for a total differential module and the minimal-model construction for an already graded finite matrix are useful prerequisites. The remaining application requires a degree-preserving retained finite diagram of the genuine arithmetic cochains, compatible with its scalar realizations and determinant bases.

No additional printed proposition is introduced. In particular, no premise that the correction is a power-series unit is required by the scalar dictionary or by the retained-coordinate comparison.
