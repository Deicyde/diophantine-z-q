# Native supporting geometry for wild inertia

This additive checkpoint extends the local supporting results. It does **not** prove `SZRaynaud.ElliotWildInertiaTrivial`, change any assembly premise, or prove the full Z-in-Q statement.

## Mathematical scope

- Inertia-fixed points acquire actual finite unramified coordinate fields. Their integral-center compatibility is proved, and native Néron mapping-property data give unique integral torsion sections and compatible Tate sections without a supplied unramified-descent premise.
- Prime-to-characteristic residual torsion lifts over the original valuation-ring point domains. Reduction is a literal torsion-group isomorphism. This is not a claim that every geometric torsion point extends integrally.
- Actual Frobenius-fixed primary torsion is finite, using Néron sections, reduction injectivity and finite fixed special-fibre points. The intrinsic model and generic-fibre identifications remain explicit.
- The scheme-theoretic kernel of a natural semiabelian presentation is reconstructed as a split torus. Laurent-coordinate geometry supplies its identity-stalk dimension. The unbundled generic/special dimension relation is obtained from proved relative-dimension and group-scheme dimension theorems, not from assumed equal dimensions.
- Native comparison-torsion injectivity gives the toric-rank inequality. The finite-intermediate restriction version derives its DVR class. Source/target model data, torus presentations, comparison compatibility and target semistability remain genuine inputs; equal rank is not proved here.
- Finite residue-Frobenius descent produces a common torus power and the action on its actual points. Residue-field, presentation and original-model bindings must still be checked where needed.
- The ambient Krull-density generator is bound to `FrobLift.TameGeneratorDense` at the original number-field objects. The commuting-image consumer remains conditional on the actual Frobenius–tame commutation, finite wild image and continuity assumptions.
- Divisibility and finite torsion of the original algebraically closed-field point group are derived. The degree-free left-nondegeneracy consumer still requires an actual finite-kernel map and right-perfect pairing.
